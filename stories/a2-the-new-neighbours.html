همسایه‌های جدیدThe new neighbours

hamsāye-hā-ye jadid

Leila and Dariush move into the flat across the landing, and Mina sends Sara over with a dish of sholeh zard to welcome them. A few days later the dish comes back – but not empty. The story shows one of the friendliest Iranian customs and practises the past continuous, the present perfect (پخته‌ام 'I have baked'), رسم است که + subjunctive 'it is the custom that…', and the thank-you phrase every learner needs: دست مادرت درد نکند.

Elementary (A2), ۱۹۰ کلمه (190 words)

Show

یک روز صبح، کامیونی جلوی ساختمان مینا و رضا ایستاد.

yek ruz sobh, kāmyun-i jelo-ye sākhtemān-e Minā o Rezā istād.

One morning a lorry stopped in front of Mina and Reza's building.

خانواده‌ی جدیدی به آپارتمان روبه‌رو آمد.

khānevāde-ye jadid-i be āpārtemān-e ru-be-ru āmad.

A new family was moving into the flat opposite.

اسم آن‌ها لیلا و داریوش بود و یک پسر کوچک داشتند.

esm-e ān-hā Leylā o Dāriush bud o yek pesar-e kuchak dāshtand.

They were called Leila and Dariush, and they had a little boy.

آن‌ها تمام روز وسایلشان را بالا می‌بردند و خیلی خسته بودند.

ān-hā tamām-e ruz vasāyel-eshān rā bālā mi-bordand o kheyli khaste budand.

All day they were carrying their things upstairs, and they were very tired.

عصر، مینا یک ظرف شله‌زرد درست کرد و روی آن با دارچین گل کشید.

asr, Minā yek zarf shole-zard dorost kard o ru-ye ān bā dārchin gol keshid.

In the afternoon Mina made a dish of sholeh zard and drew a flower on it with cinnamon.

او به سارا گفت: «این را برای همسایه‌های جدید ببر و بگو خوش آمدید.»

u be Sārā goft: «in rā barā-ye hamsāye-hā-ye jadid bebar o begu khosh āmadid.»

She said to Sara: "Take this to the new neighbours and tell them 'welcome'."

سارا زنگ در را زد و لیلا در را باز کرد.

Sārā zang-e dar rā zad o Leylā dar rā bāz kard.

Sara rang the doorbell and Leila opened the door.

لیلا ظرف را گرفت و گفت: «وای، چه مهربان! دست مادرت درد نکند.»

Leylā zarf rā gereft o goft: «vāy, che mehrabān! dast-e mādar-at dard na-konad.»

Leila took the dish and said: "Oh, how kind! Please thank your mother for me."

سه روز بعد، زنگ خانه‌ی مینا به صدا درآمد.

se ruz ba'd, zang-e khāne-ye Minā be sedā dar-āmad.

Three days later the bell rang at Mina's flat.

لیلا پشت در بود و همان ظرف در دستش بود، اما ظرف خالی نبود.

Leylā posht-e dar bud o hamān zarf dar dast-ash bud, ammā zarf khāli na-bud.

Leila was at the door with the same dish in her hand – but the dish wasn't empty.

حالا داخل آن، شیرینی خانگی بود.

hālā dākhel-e ān, shirini-ye khānegi bud.

Now there were homemade biscuits in it.

لیلا گفت: «ممنون از شله‌زرد خوشمزه‌تان. این شیرینی‌ها را خودم پخته‌ام.»

Leylā goft: «mamnun az shole-zard-e khoshmaze-tān. in shirini-hā rā khod-am pokhte-am.»

Leila said: "Thank you for your delicious sholeh zard. I baked these biscuits myself."

بعد از رفتن لیلا، سارا پرسید: «مامان، چرا ظرف را خالی پس نداد؟»

ba'd az raftan-e Leylā, Sārā porsid: «māmān, cherā zarf rā khāli pas na-dād?»

After Leila had gone, Sara asked: "Mum, why didn't she give the dish back empty?"

مینا گفت: «در ایران رسم است که ظرف همسایه را خالی پس ندهیم.»

Minā goft: «dar Irān rasm ast ke zarf-e hamsāye rā khāli pas na-dahim.»

Mina said: "In Iran it's the custom not to give a neighbour's dish back empty."

«همسایه‌ها این‌طوری با هم دوست می‌شوند.»

«hamsāye-hā in-towri bā ham dust mi-shavand.»

"That's how neighbours become friends."

سارا یک شیرینی برداشت و گفت: «پس دفعه‌ی بعد، ظرف را با چه چیزی پر کنیم؟»

Sārā yek shirini bar-dāsht o goft: «pas daf'e-ye ba'd, zarf rā bā che chiz-i por konim?»

Sara took a biscuit and said: "So next time, what shall we fill the dish with?"

مینا خندید: «با یک ظرف آش! و این بازی هیچ‌وقت تمام نمی‌شود.»

Minā khandid: «bā yek zarf āsh! va in bāzi hich-vaght tamām nemi-shavad.»

Mina laughed: "With a bowl of ash! And this game never ends."

About this story

Sending food to new neighbours – and to anyone who is moving house, ill or has guests coming – is an old Iranian custom, and a dish is never returned empty: you send it back with something you have made yourself, so the friendship goes on. شله‌زرد, a saffron and rice pudding decorated with cinnamon patterns, is also one of the classic نذری foods, cooked to fulfil a vow and shared with the whole street. دست شما درد نکند 'may your hand not hurt' is the everyday thank-you for something someone has made or done for you; the polite answer is سرتان درد نکند 'may your head not hurt'. Notice رسم است که + subjunctive (رسم است که ظرف را خالی پس ندهیم 'it is the custom that we don't give the dish back empty') and the present perfect for something just done: خودم پخته‌ام 'I baked them myself'.
