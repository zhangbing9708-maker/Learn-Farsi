چای مادربزرگGrandmother's tea

chāy-e mādar-bozorg

Tom is a guest at the house of Mina's grandmother, and he learns two things every visitor to Iran needs to know: how to drink tea with a sugar cube, and how to say 'no, thank you' – which in Iran is never the end of the conversation. The story uses the present tense, polite phrases like بفرمایید and دست شما درد نکند, and gives you a first taste of تعارف (ta'ārof), the Persian art of politeness.

امروز تام مهمان است.

emruz Tām mehmān ast.

Today Tom is a guest.

او به خانه‌ی مادربزرگ مینا می‌رود.

u be khāne-ye mādar-bozorg-e Minā mi-ravad.

He goes to the house of Mina's grandmother.

مادربزرگ خیلی مهربان است.

mādar-bozorg kheyli mehrabān ast.

Grandmother is very kind.

او یک سماور بزرگ دارد.

u yek samāvar-e bozorg dārad.

She has a big samovar.

مادربزرگ چای درست می‌کند.

mādar-bozorg chāy dorost mi-konad.

Grandmother makes tea.

چای در استکان‌های کوچک است.

chāy dar estekān-hā-ye kuchak ast.

The tea is in small glasses.

چای خیلی خوش‌رنگ است.

chāy kheyli khosh-rang ast.

The tea is a lovely colour.

مادربزرگ می‌گوید: «بفرمایید، تام جان!»

mādar-bozorg mi-guyad: «befarmāyid, Tām jān!»

Grandmother says: "Please, have some, Tom dear!"

تام قند را در استکان می‌گذارد.

Tām ghand rā dar estekān mi-gozārad.

Tom puts the sugar cube in his glass.

مینا می‌خندد و می‌گوید: «نه، تام! قند در دهان!»

Minā mi-khandad o mi-guyad: «na, Tām! ghand dar dahān!»

Mina laughs and says: "No, Tom! The sugar goes in your mouth!"

تام یک قند در دهان می‌گذارد و چای می‌خورد.

Tām yek ghand dar dahān mi-gozārad o chāy mi-khorad.

Tom puts a sugar cube in his mouth and drinks his tea.

مادربزرگ یک استکان دیگر می‌آورد.

mādar-bozorg yek estekān-e digar mi-āvarad.

Grandmother brings another glass.

تام می‌گوید: «نه، ممنون. دست شما درد نکند.»

Tām mi-guyad: «na, mamnun. dast-e shomā dard na-konad.»

Tom says: "No, thank you. Thank you for your trouble."

Literally: No, thanks. May your hand not hurt.

مادربزرگ می‌گوید: «تعارف نکن، پسرم! نوش جان!»

mādar-bozorg mi-guyad: «ta'ārof na-kon, pesar-am! nush-e jān!»

Grandmother says: "Don't stand on ceremony, my boy! Enjoy it!"

تام این چای را هم می‌خورد.

Tām in chāy rā ham mi-khorad.

Tom drinks this tea too.

About this story

In many Iranian homes tea is still made in a samovar and served in small glasses (استکان) on a tray. Many people do not stir sugar into their tea: they hold a sugar cube (قند) in the mouth – or dip it in the tea first – and drink through it. Good tea is خوش‌رنگ, 'of a lovely colour': deep, clear red. The end of the story is pure ta'ārof: the guest politely refuses, the host insists with تعارف نکن 'don't stand on ceremony', and the guest gives in. Saying 'no' once is just being polite. دست شما درد نکند, literally 'may your hand not hurt', thanks someone for the trouble they took; نوش جان, 'may it nourish your soul', is what you say to someone who is eating or drinking; and جان after a name, as in تام جان, means 'dear'.
