خاله سوسکهAuntie Beetle

khāle suske

Every Iranian child knows Khale Suske, 'Auntie Beetle', who dresses up in an onion-skin chador and sets off for Hamadan to find a husband. On the road, one suitor after another asks for her hand, and she asks each of them the same test question. Read it for the past tenses, the subjunctive after تا 'in order to' and اگر 'if', the past perfect نشسته بود, and a relative clause with که.

Elementary (A2), ۲۱۸ کلمه (218 words)

Show

روزی روزگاری، سوسک کوچکی بود که همه به او «خاله سوسکه» می‌گفتند.

ruz-i ruzgāri, susk-e kuchak-i bud ke hame be u «khāle suske» mi-goftand.

Once upon a time there was a little beetle whom everyone called "Auntie Beetle".

یک روز پدرش گفت: «تو دیگر بزرگ شده‌ای. باید شوهر کنی.»

yek ruz pedar-ash goft: «to digar bozorg shode-i. bāyad showhar koni.»

One day her father said: "You're grown up now. You must get married."

خاله سوسکه از پوست پیاز یک چادر درست کرد و از پوست بادمجان کفش.

khāle suske az pust-e piyāz yek chādor dorost kard o az pust-e bādemjān kafsh.

Auntie Beetle made a chador out of onion skin and a pair of shoes out of aubergine skin.

بعد کفش‌ها را پوشید و به طرف همدان راه افتاد.

ba'd kafsh-hā rā pushid o be taraf-e Hamedān rāh oftād.

Then she put on the shoes and set off for Hamadan.

در راه، یک قصاب او را دید و پرسید: «خاله سوسکه، کجا می‌روی؟»

dar rāh, yek ghassāb u rā did o porsid: «khāle suske, kojā mi-ravi?»

On the way a butcher saw her and asked: "Auntie Beetle, where are you going?"

خاله سوسکه گفت: «به همدان می‌روم تا شوهر کنم.»

khāle suske goft: «be Hamedān mi-ravam tā showhar konam.»

Auntie Beetle said: "I'm going to Hamadan to find a husband."

قصاب گفت: «زن من می‌شوی؟»

ghassāb goft: «zan-e man mi-shavi?»

The butcher said: "Will you be my wife?"

خاله سوسکه پرسید: «اگر روزی با هم دعوا کنیم، مرا با چه می‌زنی؟»

khāle suske porsid: «agar ruz-i bā ham da'vā konim, ma-rā bā che mi-zani?»

Auntie Beetle asked: "If one day we quarrel, what will you hit me with?"

قصاب گفت: «با کارد بزرگم!»

ghassāb goft: «bā kārd-e bozorg-am!»

The butcher said: "With my big knife!"

خاله سوسکه ترسید و گفت: «نه، نه!» و راهش را ادامه داد.

khāle suske tarsid o goft: «na, na!» o rāh-ash rā edāme dād.

Auntie Beetle was frightened, said "No, no!" and went on her way.

بعد یک نانوا و یک بقال هم از او خواستگاری کردند.

ba'd yek nānvā o yek bagghāl ham az u khāstegāri kardand.

Then a baker and a grocer also asked her to marry them.

نانوا گفت: «با پارو!» و بقال گفت: «با سنگ ترازو!»

nānvā goft: «bā pāru!» o bagghāl goft: «bā sang-e tarāzu!»

The baker said: "With my oven paddle!" and the grocer said: "With the weights of my scales!"

نزدیک همدان، آقا موشه را دید که کنار جوی آب نشسته بود.

nazdik-e Hamedān, āghā mushe rā did ke kenār-e ju-ye āb neshaste bud.

Near Hamadan she saw Mr Mouse, who was sitting beside a stream.

آقا موشه با ادب پرسید: «خانم سوسکه، با من ازدواج می‌کنید؟»

āghā mushe bā adab porsid: «khānom suske, bā man ezdevāj mi-konid?»

Mr Mouse asked politely: "Miss Beetle, will you marry me?"

خاله سوسکه پرسید: «اگر با هم دعوا کنیم، مرا با چه می‌زنی؟»

khāle suske porsid: «agar bā ham da'vā konim, ma-rā bā che mi-zani?»

Auntie Beetle asked her question again: "If we quarrel, what will you hit me with?"

آقا موشه خندید و گفت: «با دم نرم و نازکم!»

āghā mushe khandid o goft: «bā dom-e narm o nāzok-am!»

Mr Mouse laughed and said: "With my soft, thin tail!"

خاله سوسکه خوشحال شد و گفت: «تو همان شوهری هستی که من می‌خواستم!»

khāle suske khoshhāl shod o goft: «to hamān showhar-i hasti ke man mi-khāstam!»

Auntie Beetle was delighted and said: "You are just the husband I wanted!"

آن‌ها در همدان عروسی گرفتند و همه‌ی حیوانات به جشن آمدند.

ān-hā dar Hamedān arusi gereftand o hame-ye heyvānāt be jashn āmadand.

They had their wedding in Hamadan, and all the animals came to the party.

آقا موشه هر روز برای خاله سوسکه نان تازه می‌آورد و آن‌ها هیچ‌وقت دعوا نکردند.

āghā mushe har ruz barā-ye khāle suske nān-e tāze mi-āvard o ān-hā hich-vaght da'vā na-kardand.

Every day Mr Mouse brought Auntie Beetle fresh bread, and they never once quarrelled.

قصه‌ی ما به سر رسید، کلاغه به خانه‌اش نرسید!

ghesse-ye mā be sar resid, kalāghe be khāne-ash na-resid!

Our story has come to an end, but the crow never got home!

About this story

Khale Suske is told in many versions all over Iran; in the longest ones she later falls into a pool and Mr Mouse rescues her with a ladder. The names end in a little -e (سوسکه, موشه, کلاغه): in spoken Persian this ending makes a noun 'the one we know', and in tales it sounds friendly, like 'Mousey'. Iranian stories traditionally open with یکی بود یکی نبود or روزی روزگاری and close with the rhyme قصه‌ی ما به سر رسید، کلاغه به خانه‌اش نرسید 'our story came to an end, the crow did not reach home'. Notice the subjunctive after تا 'in order to' (تا شوهر کنم) and after اگر (اگر دعوا کنیم), while the main clause stays in the present (می‌زنی). شوهر کردن 'to take a husband' is said of a woman; for a man it is زن گرفتن 'to take a wife', and for both, ازدواج کردن.
