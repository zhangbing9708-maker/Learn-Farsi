جهان با «ثانیه‌ی کبیسه» خداحافظی می‌کندThe world says goodbye to the leap second

jahān bā «sāniye-ye kabise» khodāhāfezi mi-konad

Science, level A2. Source: Euronews / CTV News

Show

زمین هر روز یک بار دور خودش می‌چرخد، اما سرعتش همیشه یکسان نیست.

zamin har ruz yek bār dowr-e khod-ash mi-charkhad, ammā sor'at-ash hamishe yeksān nist.

The Earth turns once on its axis every day, but its speed is not always the same.

ساعت‌های اتمی زمان را بسیار دقیق اندازه می‌گیرند.

sā'at-hā-ye atomi zamān rā besyār daghigh andāze mi-girand.

Atomic clocks measure time very precisely.

گاهی یک «ثانیه‌ی کبیسه» به ساعت جهانی اضافه می‌شد تا ساعت‌ها با زمین هماهنگ بمانند.

gāhi yek «sāniye-ye kabise» be sā'at-e jahāni ezāfe mi-shod tā sā'at-hā bā zamin hamāhang bemānand.

From time to time a "leap second" was added to world time, so that the clocks stayed in step with the Earth.

از سال ۱۹۷۲ تا حالا، این کار ۲۷ بار انجام شده است.

az sāl-e hezār-o-nohsad-o-haftād-o-do tā hālā, in kār bist-o-haft bār anjām shode ast.

Since 1972 this has been done 27 times.

روز پنج‌شنبه، نمایندگان کشورها در ورسای فرانسه رأی دادند که این کار تمام شود.

ruz-e panj-shanbe, namāyandegān-e keshvar-hā dar Versāy-e Farānse ra'y dādand ke in kār tamām shavad.

On Thursday, representatives of many countries voted in Versailles, France, to end the practice.

از ماه مه ۲۰۲۷ دیگر ثانیه‌ی کبیسه اضافه نمی‌شود.

az māh-e meh-e do-hezār-o-bist-o-haft digar sāniye-ye kabise ezāfe nemi-shavad.

From May 2027 no more leap seconds will be added.

به گفته‌ی کارشناسان، ثانیه‌ی کبیسه برای رایانه‌ها، شبکه‌ها و ماهواره‌ها مشکل درست می‌کرد.

be gofte-ye kārshenāsān, sāniye-ye kabise barā-ye rāyāne-hā, shabake-hā o māhvāre-hā moshkel dorost mi-kard.

According to experts, the leap second caused problems for computers, networks and satellites.

حالا اختلاف ساعت‌ها با چرخش زمین می‌تواند تا یک ساعت بزرگ شود، اما رسیدن به این اختلاف شاید هزاران سال طول بکشد.

hālā ekhtelāf-e sā'at-hā bā charkhesh-e zamin mi-tavānad tā yek sā'at bozorg shavad, ammā residan be in ekhtelāf shāyad hezārān sāl tul bekeshad.

Now the gap between the clocks and the Earth's rotation may grow to as much as an hour, but reaching that could take thousands of years.

Background

Clock time (UTC) is kept by atomic clocks, but the length of a day depends on the Earth's spin, which changes slightly. Since 1972, 27 leap seconds have been added to keep the two together, the last at the end of 2016. Lately the Earth has been spinning a little faster, which raised the possibility of the first-ever 'negative' leap second – and since that has never been done, nobody knew how well computer systems would cope. On 15 October the General Conference on Weights and Measures, meeting in Versailles, voted to stop adding leap seconds from 20 May 2027 and to let the gap grow, up to an hour. In Persian, سال کبیسه (sāl-e kabise) is a leap year – in the Iranian calendar, the year in which Esfand has 30 days instead of 29.
