# Persepolis and Pasargadae
## تخت جمشید و پاسارگاد
*Takht-e Jamshid o Pāsārgād* — [read on the website with audio](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/culture/places-persepolis-and-pasargadae.html)

_A visitor's guide to the heartland of the first Persian empire, north of Shiraz: the terraces of Persepolis, the royal tombs of Naqsh-e Rostam and the lonely tomb of Cyrus at Pasargadae._

### A day in the Achaemenid heartland

North of Shiraz, in the plains of Fars, lie the ruins of the first Persian empire. In one long day from Shiraz you can see تخت جمشید (*Takht-e Jamshid*, ‘Persepolis’), the ceremonial capital of Darius the Great and his successors; نقش رستم (*Naghsh-e Rostam*, ‘Naqsh-e Rostam’), the cliff of royal tombs a few kilometres to the north; the small Sasanian reliefs of نقش رجب (*Naghsh-e Rajab*, ‘Naqsh-e Rajab’) on the way; and, an hour's drive further north, پاسارگاد (*Pāsārgād*, ‘Pasargadae’), the capital of Cyrus the Great. Many visitors split the trip in two and give Persepolis a whole morning.

### Persepolis: what to see

![A column at Persepolis](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Perspolis.jpg/960px-Perspolis.jpg)
<sub>A column at Persepolis. Photo: [Luis Argerich](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Perspolis.jpg), CC BY 2.0.</sub>

Start early, before the heat and the tour groups. A broad double staircase with low, shallow steps leads up to the terrace and the دروازه‌ی ملل (*darvāze-ye melal*, ‘Gate of All Nations’), guarded by winged bulls. Beyond it, the staircases of the آپادانا (*Āpādānā*, ‘Apadana’), the great audience hall, carry the famous procession of delegations bringing gifts to the king: look for the two-humped Bactrian camel, the lion attacking a bull and the guards in their long robes.

Then walk through the تچر (*Tachar*, ‘Tachara’), the palace of Darius, to the Hall of a Hundred Columns, and climb the hill behind the site to the rock tombs of two later kings for the best view over the ruins. The small museum is housed in a rebuilt part of the so-called Harem of Xerxes.

### Naqsh-e Rostam

![The royal tombs of Naqsh-e Rostam near Persepolis](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Naqsh-e_Rustam_necropolis_in_Iran.jpg/960px-Naqsh-e_Rustam_necropolis_in_Iran.jpg)
<sub>The royal tombs of Naqsh-e Rostam near Persepolis. Photo: [Maasaak](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Naqsh-e_Rustam_necropolis_in_Iran.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

At Naqsh-e Rostam, four royal tombs are cut high into the cliff in the shape of a cross. An inscription identifies the tomb of Darius I; the others are thought to belong to Xerxes, Artaxerxes I and Darius II. Centuries later the Sasanian kings carved their own reliefs below them. The most famous shows Shapur I on horseback, celebrating his victory over the Roman emperor Valerian, whom he captured in AD 260.

Facing the cliff stands a square stone tower, the کعبه‌ی زرتشت (*Ka'be-ye Zartosht*, ‘Cube of Zoroaster’), whose purpose is still debated. Later Iranians named the whole site after رستم (*Rostam*, ‘Rostam’), the hero of the Shahnameh, believing that the reliefs showed him.

### Pasargadae and the tomb of Cyrus

![The tomb of Cyrus the Great at Pasargadae](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/Pasargad_Tomb_Cyrus3.jpg/960px-Pasargad_Tomb_Cyrus3.jpg)
<sub>The tomb of Cyrus the Great at Pasargadae. Photo: [Bernd81](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pasargad_Tomb_Cyrus3.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

At Pasargadae there is less to see, but the atmosphere is unforgettable. Alone on the windy plain stands the آرامگاه کوروش (*ārāmgāh-e Kurosh*, ‘tomb of Cyrus’), a gabled stone chamber on six great steps, a little over 11 metres high. After the coming of Islam local people called it the tomb of مادر سلیمان (*mādar-e Soleymān*, ‘Solomon's mother’), and that belief helped to protect it.

Nearby are the ruins of Cyrus's palaces, a gate pillar carved with a four-winged figure, and the outline of his palace garden – the earliest known example of the four-part Persian garden.

### Tips and words for the visit

Bring water, a hat and good shoes: there is little shade on the terrace. Ask a driver for 'Persepolis' and he may think you mean Tehran's most popular football club – ask instead for *Takht-e Jamshid*, 'the throne of Jamshid', named after the mythical king of the Shahnameh. Useful words on the signs are کاخ (*kākh*, ‘palace’), ستون (*sotun*, ‘column’), کتیبه (*katibe*, ‘inscription’) and سنگ‌نگاره (*sang-negāre*, ‘rock relief’).

The best seasons are spring and autumn. The Nowruz holiday is the busiest time of the year, and in summer the midday heat often passes 40°C.

### Facts

- Persepolis was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 1979, and Pasargadae in 2004.
- The tomb of Cyrus stands a little over 11 metres high on a platform of six stone steps.
- The palace garden at Pasargadae is the earliest known example of the chahar bagh, the four-part Persian garden.
- Of the four royal tombs at Naqsh-e Rostam, only that of Darius I is identified by an inscription.
- Valerian, captured by Shapur I in AD 260, was the only Roman emperor ever taken prisoner.
- Naqsh-e Rostam means 'picture of Rostam': later Iranians thought the reliefs showed the hero of the Shahnameh.
- The Persepolis museum occupies part of the so-called Harem of Xerxes, rebuilt by archaeologists in the 1930s.

### Say it in Persian

- یک دربست برای تخت جمشید و نقش رستم می‌خواهیم.  
  *yek darbast barā-ye Takht-e Jamshid o Naghsh-e Rostam mi-khāhim.* — We'd like a private taxi to Persepolis and Naqsh-e Rostam.  
  Spoken: یه دربست برای تخت جمشید و نقش رستم می‌خوایم. *ye darbast barā-ye Takht-e Jamshid o Naghsh-e Rostam mi-khāym.*
- بلیت تخت جمشید را کجا می‌فروشند؟  
  *belit-e Takht-e Jamshid rā kojā mi-forushand?* — Where do they sell tickets for Persepolis?
- صبح زود بیایید؛ ظهر خیلی گرم می‌شود.  
  *sobh-e zud bi-yāyid; zohr kheyli garm mi-shavad.* — Come early in the morning; it gets very hot at midday.
- این پلکان به دروازه‌ی ملل می‌رسد.  
  *in pellekān be Darvāze-ye Melal mi-resad.* — This staircase leads to the Gate of All Nations.
- روی این سنگ‌نگاره نمایندگان ملت‌ها هدیه می‌آورند.  
  *ru-ye in sang-negāre namāyandegān-e mellat-hā hedye mi-āvarand.* — On this relief the delegates of the nations are bringing gifts.
- چهار آرامگاه شاهان هخامنشی در نقش رستم است.  
  *chahār ārāmgāh-e shāhān-e Hakhāmaneshi dar Naghsh-e Rostam ast.* — Four tombs of Achaemenid kings are at Naqsh-e Rostam.
- شاپور در این نقش برجسته سوار بر اسب است.  
  *Shāpur dar in naghsh-e barjaste savār bar asb ast.* — In this relief Shapur is on horseback.
- آرامگاه کوروش شش پله دارد.  
  *ārāmgāh-e Kurosh shesh pelle dārad.* — The tomb of Cyrus has six steps.
- مردم قدیم فکر می‌کردند این آرامگاه مادر سلیمان است.  
  *mardom-e ghadim fekr mi-kardand in ārāmgāh-e mādar-e Soleymān ast.* — People in the old days thought this was the tomb of Solomon's mother.
- باغ پاسارگاد قدیمی‌ترین باغ ایرانی شناخته‌شده است.  
  *bāgh-e Pāsārgād ghadimi-tarin bāgh-e irāni-ye shenākhte-shode ast.* — The garden at Pasargadae is the oldest known Persian garden.
- در ایران این‌جا را «تخت جمشید» می‌نامند.  
  *dar Irān in-jā rā «Takht-e Jamshid» mi-nāmand.* — In Iran this place is called 'Takht-e Jamshid'.
- آب و کلاه یادتان نرود.  
  *āb o kolāh yād-etān na-ravad.* — Don't forget water and a hat.
