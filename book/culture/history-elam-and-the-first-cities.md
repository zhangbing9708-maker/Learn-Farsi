# Elam and the first cities
## ایلام و نخستین شهرها
*Ilām o nokhostin shahr-hā* — [read on the website with audio](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/culture/history-elam-and-the-first-cities.html)

_Long before there were Persians, the Iranian plateau was home to cities, ziggurats and writing. The story of Iran begins with Elam, Jiroft and the Burnt City – five thousand years ago._

### Before the Persians

When people think of ancient Iran they think of Cyrus and Persepolis. But the Persians were latecomers. For three thousand years before them, the land between the Zagros mountains and the Persian Gulf was already full of towns, temples and traders. Archaeologists call this period پیش از تاریخ (*pish az tārikh*, ‘prehistory’) and the early عصر برنز (*asr-e boronz*, ‘Bronze Age’), and every decade of digging pushes the story further back.

The oldest villages on the plateau – places like تپه سیلک (*Tappe Sialk*, ‘Tepe Sialk’) near Kashan – date to around 6000 BC. Their people farmed wheat and barley, kept goats, and painted pottery with running ibexes and birds. By 3500 BC the first real towns appeared, and with them the two inventions that make history possible: خط (*khatt*, ‘writing’) and the شهر (*shahr*, ‘city’).

### Elam: the kingdom of Susa

![The ancient water mills and channels of Shushtar](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Shushtar_Historical_Hydraulic_System_Panorama.jpg/960px-Shushtar_Historical_Hydraulic_System_Panorama.jpg)
<sub>The ancient water mills and channels of Shushtar. Photo: [Iman Yari](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shushtar_Historical_Hydraulic_System_Panorama.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

The great power of early Iran was ایلام (*Ilām*, ‘Elam’), centred on the city of شوش (*Shush*, ‘Susa’) in what is now Khuzestan. The Elamites were not Persians and their language is related to nothing we know. They wrote first in a picture-script called Proto-Elamite (around 3100 BC, still undeciphered), then in cuneiform borrowed from their Mesopotamian neighbours.

For two thousand years Elam fought, traded and married with Sumer, Akkad and Babylon. Around 1250 BC the Elamite king Untash-Napirisha built a new holy city, چغازنبیل (*Choghā Zanbil*, ‘Chogha Zanbil’), whose ziggurat – a stepped temple-tower – still stands 25 metres high in the plain. It is the best-preserved ziggurat in the world and in 1979 became one of the first three Iranian sites on the UNESCO World Heritage list.

Elam's end came in 646 BC when the Assyrian king Ashurbanipal sacked Susa. But the Elamites did not vanish: their scribes went on working for the Persians, and Elamite became one of the three official languages carved on the walls of Persepolis.

### Jiroft and the Burnt City

![Excavations at Shahr-e Sukhteh, the Burnt City](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/C20R1002.jpg/960px-C20R1002.jpg)
<sub>Excavations at Shahr-e Sukhteh, the Burnt City. Photo: [Amirkhan33](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:C20R1002.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

Two discoveries of recent decades have rewritten the map. In 2001 floods in the Halil river valley, in Kerman province, uncovered thousands of carved stone vessels: the تمدن جیرفت (*tamaddon-e Jiroft*, ‘Jiroft civilisation’), a Bronze Age culture of around 2500 BC with its own art of scorpions, eagles and palm trees, and possibly its own writing.

Far to the east, in Sistan, lies شهر سوخته (*Shahr-e Sukhte*, ‘the Burnt City’). Founded about 3200 BC, it was burned three times and abandoned around 1800 BC. Its dry soil preserved astonishing things: the world's oldest known artificial eye (made of bitumen paste and gold wire, worn by a tall woman), a backgammon-like board game, and a goblet painted with a goat that seems to leap when the cup is turned – perhaps the oldest animation ever made.

### The arrival of the Iranians

Around 1500–1000 BC new peoples speaking Indo-European languages moved onto the plateau from the north-east. They called themselves آریا (*Āriyā*, ‘Aryans, 'the noble ones'’), and the land eventually took their name: ایران (*Irān*, ‘Iran’), 'the land of the Aryans'. Among them were the Medes in the north-west and the Persians in the south-west, in the province the Elamites called Anshan and the Persians پارس (*Pārs*, ‘Persia’) – today's Fars.

The Medes built the first Iranian empire, with its capital at Ecbatana (modern همدان (*Hamedān*, ‘Hamadan’)), and helped destroy Assyria in 612 BC. Their Persian cousins were their vassals – until a young Persian prince named Cyrus changed everything.

### Why it matters for a learner

Modern Persian carries almost no Elamite words, but the land's deep past is everywhere in Iranian conversation. Iranians speak proudly of تمدن هفت‌هزارساله (*tamaddon-e haft-hezār-sāle*, ‘a seven-thousand-year civilisation’); the National Museum in Tehran opens with Sialk pottery; and the word باستان (*bāstān*, ‘ancient’) – as in ایران باستان (*Irān-e bāstān*, ‘ancient Iran’) and باستان‌شناسی (*bāstān-shenāsi*, ‘archaeology’) – is one you will meet in every museum and documentary.

### Facts

- Chogha Zanbil (c. 1250 BC) is the best-preserved ziggurat in the world and one of Iran's first three UNESCO World Heritage sites (all inscribed in 1979, with Persepolis and Naqsh-e Jahan Square).
- Susa was continuously inhabited for about 6,000 years, from c. 4200 BC until the 13th century AD.
- Proto-Elamite, written around 3100 BC, is one of the world's oldest scripts and is still undeciphered.
- The Burnt City's painted goblet (c. 3000 BC) shows a goat leaping at a tree in five frames – often called the oldest animation.
- Elamite was one of the three languages of the Achaemenid royal inscriptions, with Old Persian and Babylonian.
- The word Iran comes from Old Persian 'Ariya'; 'Persia' comes from the province of Pārs (Fars).

### Say it in Persian

- ایلام یک پادشاهی قدیمی در جنوب غرب ایران بود.  
  *Ilām yek pādshāhi-ye ghadimi dar jonub-e gharb-e Irān bud.* — Elam was an ancient kingdom in the south-west of Iran.
- شوش یکی از قدیمی‌ترین شهرهای جهان است.  
  *Shush yek-i az ghadimi-tarin shahr-hā-ye jahān ast.* — Susa is one of the oldest cities in the world.
- زیگورات چغازنبیل بیش از سه هزار سال پیش ساخته شد.  
  *zigurāt-e Choghā-zanbil bish az se hezār sāl pish sākhte shod.* — The ziggurat of Chogha Zanbil was built more than three thousand years ago.
- باستان‌شناسان در جیرفت ظرف‌های سنگی زیبایی پیدا کردند.  
  *bāstān-shenāsān dar Jiroft zarf-hā-ye sangi-ye zibā-yi peydā kardand.* — Archaeologists found beautiful stone vessels in Jiroft.
- شهر سوخته در سیستان سه بار در آتش سوخت.  
  *Shahr-e Sukhte dar Sistān se bār dar ātash sukht.* — The Burnt City in Sistan burned three times.
- قدیمی‌ترین چشم مصنوعی جهان در آن‌جا پیدا شد.  
  *ghadimi-tarin cheshm-e masnu'i-ye jahān dar ān-jā peydā shod.* — The world's oldest artificial eye was found there.
- آریایی‌ها حدود سه هزار سال پیش به ایران آمدند.  
  *āriyāyi-hā hodud-e se hezār sāl pish be Irān āmadand.* — The Aryans came to Iran about three thousand years ago.
- نام ایران یعنی «سرزمین آریایی‌ها».  
  *nām-e Irān ya'ni «sarzamin-e āriyāyi-hā».* — The name Iran means 'the land of the Aryans'.
- موزه‌ی ملی ایران در تهران ظرف‌های سیلک را نشان می‌دهد.  
  *muze-ye melli-ye Irān dar Tehrān zarf-hā-ye Sialk rā neshān mi-dahad.* — The National Museum of Iran in Tehran shows the pottery of Sialk.
- ایرانی‌ها به تاریخ کهن کشورشان افتخار می‌کنند.  
  *irāni-hā be tārikh-e kohan-e keshvar-eshān eftekhār mi-konand.* — Iranians are proud of their country's ancient history.
