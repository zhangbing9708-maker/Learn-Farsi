# The Caspian coast
## گیلان و مازندران
*Gilān o Māzandarān* — [read on the website with audio](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/culture/places-caspian-coast.html)

_Over the mountains from Tehran lies a different Iran: rain, rice paddies, tea gardens and ancient forests along the Caspian Sea. Rasht and its food, the stepped village of Masuleh, the Anzali lagoon and the roads north._

### Shomal: the green north

Cross the البرز (*Alborz*, ‘Alborz’) mountains from Tehran and within a few hours the desert gives way to rice paddies, tea gardens and forest. The narrow strip between the mountains and the دریای خزر (*daryā-ye Khazar*, ‘Caspian Sea’) – the provinces of گیلان (*Gilān*, ‘Gilan’) and مازندران (*Māzandarān*, ‘Mazandaran’), with Golestan further east – is what Iranians simply call شمال (*shomāl*, ‘the north’), and 'going north' means a holiday by the sea.

This is the wettest part of Iran. Clouds from the sea drop their rain on the wall of the mountains, so everything is green, and summers are warm and humid.

### Rasht and Anzali

![The municipality building of Rasht on an autumn night](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Rasht_Municipality_Mansion_in_an_Autumn_night.jpg/960px-Rasht_Municipality_Mansion_in_an_Autumn_night.jpg)
<sub>The municipality building of Rasht on an autumn night. Photo: [AlexTrn1991](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rasht_Municipality_Mansion_in_an_Autumn_night.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

رشت (*Rasht*, ‘Rasht’), the capital of Gilan, is known as شهر باران (*shahr-e bārān*, ‘the city of rain’), and in 2015 it joined UNESCO's network of Creative Cities as a City of Gastronomy. Its market is a feast of fish, olives, herbs, citrus fruit and pickles, and in the evening people stroll in the square around the old municipality building.

Half an hour away is the port of بندر انزلی (*Bandar-e Anzali*, ‘Bandar Anzali’) on the تالاب انزلی (*tālāb-e Anzali*, ‘Anzali lagoon’), where boats take you out among the reeds and, in summer, fields of lotus flowers; in winter the lagoon fills with migrating birds.

In the hills west of Rasht, the village of ماسوله (*Māsule*, ‘Masuleh’) climbs a steep slope at about 1,050 metres. Its yellow-ochre houses are built so close together that the roof of one house is the yard and the street of the house above, and cars stay outside. On the way up, the town of Fuman is famous for its کلوچه (*koluche*, ‘kolucheh’) cookies.

Above the coast stretch the جنگل‌های هیرکانی (*jangal-hā-ye Hirkāni*, ‘Hyrcanian forests’), ancient broadleaf forests inscribed by UNESCO in 2019, whose oaks, beeches and Persian ironwoods turn red and gold in October and November. In the east, پارک ملی گلستان (*pārk-e melli-ye Golestān*, ‘Golestan National Park’) is Iran's oldest national park.

### Garlic, herbs and sour flavours

![Mirza ghasemi, smoky aubergine with egg and garlic](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/43/%D0%9C%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%B7%D0%B0_%D0%93%D0%B0%D1%81%D1%81%D0%B5%D0%BC%D0%B8.jpg/960px-%D0%9C%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%B7%D0%B0_%D0%93%D0%B0%D1%81%D1%81%D0%B5%D0%BC%D0%B8.jpg)
<sub>Mirza ghasemi, smoky aubergine with egg and garlic. Photo: [Anastasiya Lvova](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%D0%9C%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%B7%D0%B0_%D0%93%D0%B0%D1%81%D1%81%D0%B5%D0%BC%D0%B8.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

Northern cooking has its own character: plenty of garlic, herbs and sour flavours, and rice with almost everything. Try میرزاقاسمی (*mirzā-ghāsemi*, ‘Mirza Ghasemi’), smoky grilled aubergine mashed with garlic, tomato and egg; باقلاقاتق (*bāghelā-ghātogh*, ‘baghala ghatogh’), broad beans with dill and egg; and کباب ترش (*kabāb-e torsh*, ‘sour kebab’), meat marinated with pomegranate paste and walnuts. On the coast, ماهی سفید (*māhi-ye sefid*, ‘Caspian white fish’) is fried or smoked.

Northern rice such as هاشمی (*hāshemi*, ‘Hashemi’) is the most prized in Iran, and tea from the gardens of لاهیجان (*Lāhijān*, ‘Lahijan’), first planted around 1900, is drunk all over the country.

### Gilaki and Mazandarani

In the villages and markets you will hear گیلکی (*Gilaki*, ‘Gilaki’) and مازندرانی (*Māzandarāni*, ‘Mazandarani’), two Iranian languages related to Persian but different enough that a Tehrani cannot follow them. Everyone also speaks Persian, often with a lilting northern accent.

### Getting there and when to go

![The Veresk Bridge on the Trans-Iranian Railway](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/VERESK_Bridge_-_panoramio.jpg/960px-VERESK_Bridge_-_panoramio.jpg)
<sub>The Veresk Bridge on the Trans-Iranian Railway. Photo: [Alireza Shakernia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:VERESK_Bridge_-_panoramio.jpg), CC BY-SA 3.0.</sub>

Three roads cross the Alborz from Tehran: the spectacular جاده‌ی چالوس (*jādde-ye Chālus*, ‘Chalus road’), the جاده‌ی هراز (*jādde-ye Harāz*, ‘Haraz road’) past Mount Damavand, and the motorway via Qazvin to Rasht. On holiday weekends all of them are jammed. You can also take the train on the راه‌آهن سراسری (*rāh-āhan-e sarāsari*, ‘Trans-Iranian Railway’) from Tehran to Sari, through tunnels and over the پل ورسک (*Pol-e Veresk*, ‘Veresk Bridge’) of 1936; the railway became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021.

The best seasons are late spring and early autumn. Summer is hot, humid and crowded, and autumn brings the colours of the forest.

### Facts

- The Caspian is the largest enclosed body of water on Earth; its surface lies about 28 metres below sea level.
- The Hyrcanian forests were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 2019.
- Rasht became a UNESCO Creative City of Gastronomy in 2015.
- Tea growing in Iran began around Lahijan at the start of the 20th century, after Kashef al-Saltaneh brought tea plants from India.
- The Trans-Iranian Railway, built between 1927 and 1938, became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021.
- Golestan National Park, protected since 1957, is Iran's oldest national park.
- Gilaki and Mazandarani are Iranian languages in their own right, related to Persian.

### Say it in Persian

- آخر هفته به شمال می‌رویم.  
  *ākhar-e hafte be shomāl mi-ravim.* — We're going up north at the weekend.  
  Spoken: آخر هفته می‌ریم شمال. *ākhar-e hafte mi-rim shomāl.*
- در رشت زیاد باران می‌بارد؛ چترت را فراموش نکن!  
  *dar Rasht ziyād bārān mi-bārad; chatr-at rā farāmush na-kon!* — It rains a lot in Rasht – don't forget your umbrella!
- بام خانه‌ی پایینی حیاط خانه‌ی بالایی است.  
  *bām-e khāne-ye pāyini hayāt-e khāne-ye bālāyi ast.* — The roof of the lower house is the yard of the house above.
- ماشین‌ها نمی‌توانند وارد ماسوله شوند.  
  *māshin-hā nemi-tavānand vāred-e Māsule shavand.* — Cars cannot enter Masuleh.
- قایق‌ها از اسکله‌ی انزلی به تالاب می‌روند.  
  *ghāyegh-hā az eskele-ye Anzali be tālāb mi-ravand.* — The boats go from the Anzali jetty out to the lagoon.
- یک میرزاقاسمی و یک باقلاقاتق، لطفاً.  
  *yek mirzā-ghāsemi va yek bāghelā-ghātogh, lotfan.* — One Mirza Ghasemi and one baghala ghatogh, please.
- این ماهی دودی است یا تازه؟  
  *in māhi dudi ast yā tāze?* — Is this fish smoked or fresh?
- برنج هاشمی بهترین برنج گیلان است.  
  *berenj-e hāshemi beh-tarin berenj-e Gilān ast.* — Hashemi rice is the best rice in Gilan.
- چای لاهیجان را امتحان کنید.  
  *chāy-e Lāhijān rā emtehān konid.* — Try the Lahijan tea.
- در پاییز جنگل‌های شمال قرمز و زرد می‌شوند.  
  *dar pāyiz jangal-hā-ye shomāl ghermez o zard mi-shavand.* — In autumn the northern forests turn red and yellow.
- جاده‌ی چالوس آخر هفته‌ها خیلی شلوغ است.  
  *jādde-ye Chālus ākhar-e hafte-hā kheyli sholugh ast.* — The Chalus road is very busy at weekends.
- قطار از روی پل ورسک می‌گذرد.  
  *ghatār az ru-ye Pol-e Veresk mi-gozarad.* — The train crosses the Veresk Bridge.
