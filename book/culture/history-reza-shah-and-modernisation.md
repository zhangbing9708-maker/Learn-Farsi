# Reza Shah and modernisation
## رضاشاه و نوسازی ایران
*Rezā-shāh o nowsāzi-ye Irān* — [read on the website with audio](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/culture/history-reza-shah-and-modernisation.html)

_Between 1925 and 1941 a former Cossack officer rebuilt the Iranian state from the top down: a railway across the country, a modern university, a national army, a new name abroad and a forced change in how people dressed. His reign is still argued over – as the birth of modern Iran and as a harsh dictatorship._

### From soldier to shah

رضاخان (*Rezā-khān*, ‘Reza Khan’) was born in 1878 in Savadkuh, in the mountains of Mazandaran, and made his career in the Persian Cossack Brigade, the best-trained unit of the weak Qajar army. In February 1921, with the journalist Sayyed Zia al-Din Tabatabai, he marched on Tehran and seized power in a کودتا (*kudetā*, ‘coup’).

He became minister of war and then prime minister, and built a single national army out of the country's scattered forces. In 1925 the parliament deposed the last Qajar ruler, Ahmad Shah, and an assembly made Reza Khan shah. He took the family name پهلوی (*Pahlavi*, ‘Pahlavi’), the name of the Middle Persian language of the Sasanians – a deliberate link to Iran's pre-Islamic past.

### Railway, roads and a university

![The Veresk Bridge on the Trans-Iranian Railway](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/VERESK_Bridge_-_panoramio.jpg/960px-VERESK_Bridge_-_panoramio.jpg)
<sub>The Veresk Bridge on the Trans-Iranian Railway. Photo: [Alireza Shakernia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:VERESK_Bridge_-_panoramio.jpg), CC BY-SA 3.0.</sub>

Reza Shah's great project was the راه‌آهن سراسری (*rāh-āhan-e sarāsari*, ‘Trans-Iranian Railway’), built between 1927 and 1938 from the Caspian Sea to the Persian Gulf, about 1,400 kilometres through two mountain ranges. Engineers from many countries worked on it, but it was paid for without foreign loans, largely through taxes on sugar and tea. Its most famous structure, the پل ورسک (*Pol-e Veresk*, ‘Veresk Bridge’) in Mazandaran, opened in 1936; the railway became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021.

In 1934 the دانشگاه تهران (*Dāneshgāh-e Tehrān*, ‘University of Tehran’) was founded, the country's first modern university. The same years saw a civil code, a national bank, state schools, compulsory military service, family names for every citizen and the official adoption of the Iranian solar calendar.

### 'Iran', not 'Persia'

![The National Museum of Iran, Tehran](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/N%C3%A1rodn%C3%AD_muzeum_%C3%8Dr%C3%A1nu.jpg/960px-N%C3%A1rodn%C3%AD_muzeum_%C3%8Dr%C3%A1nu.jpg)
<sub>The National Museum of Iran, Tehran. Photo: [Ondřej Žváček](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:N%C3%A1rodn%C3%AD_muzeum_%C3%8Dr%C3%A1nu.jpg), CC BY 2.5.</sub>

Iranians had always called their country ایران (*Irān*, ‘Iran’); 'Persia' was a foreign name, taken from the province of Fars. In 1935 the government asked foreign states to use 'Iran' in official correspondence, and the change took effect at Nowruz that year. Some scholars objected that 'Persia' carried a famous cultural heritage, and in 1959 the government said that both names could be used.

The same spirit of national revival led to a new فرهنگستان (*farhangestān*, ‘language academy’), founded in 1935, which approved Persian words to replace Arabic and European borrowings – words such as هواپیما (*havāpeymā*, ‘aeroplane’) – and to a new interest in ancient sites and monuments: the National Museum of Iran in Tehran, designed by the French architect André Godard, opened in 1937.

### Dress and unveiling

Reza Shah also wanted Iranians to look modern. In 1928 men were ordered to wear Western-style clothes and a peaked 'Pahlavi hat', and in 1935 a European brimmed hat. In July 1935 a protest against these rules at the Goharshad Mosque in Mashhad was crushed by troops, and many people were killed.

In January 1936 came کشف حجاب (*kashf-e hejāb*, ‘the unveiling’): women were forbidden to wear the چادر (*chādor*, ‘chador’) or face veil in public, and police sometimes pulled veils off in the street. For some women, especially in the educated urban middle class, it opened doors to schools, offices and public life. For many others it was a humiliation, and some stopped leaving their homes. After 1941 the ban was no longer enforced.

### An iron hand

The reforms came with استبداد (*estebdād*, ‘authoritarian rule’). Newspapers were censored, parliament became a rubber stamp, and critics and even former allies – such as the court minister Teymourtash and the cleric Hassan Modarres – died in prison or in custody. Nomadic tribes were forced to settle, often with great suffering, and the shah himself acquired huge estates. Admirers see a builder who turned a divided, bankrupt country into a modern state; critics see modernisation imposed by force from above. Both pictures contain truth.

### Abdication

![The Trans-Iranian Railway in the mountains of Mazandaran](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c5/Mazandaran_Sevatcow_Dowab.jpg)
<sub>The Trans-Iranian Railway in the mountains of Mazandaran. Photo: [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mazandaran_Sevatcow_Dowab.jpg), CC BY-SA 3.0.</sub>

In the Second World War Iran declared itself neutral, but Britain and the Soviet Union wanted the Trans-Iranian Railway to send supplies to the Soviets, and they distrusted the many Germans in the country. On 25 August 1941 they invaded. Within weeks, on 16 September, Reza Shah کناره‌گیری (*kenāre-giri*, ‘abdicated’) in favour of his son, Mohammad Reza. He was taken into تبعید (*tab'id*, ‘exile’), first to Mauritius and then to Johannesburg, where he died in 1944.

### Facts

- Reza Khan's coup took place on 21 February 1921 (3 Esfand 1299).
- The Qajar dynasty was deposed by parliament in October 1925; Reza Shah was crowned in April 1926.
- The Trans-Iranian Railway, built 1927–1938, runs about 1,400 km from the Caspian Sea to the Persian Gulf and became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021.
- The University of Tehran was founded in 1934.
- From Nowruz 1935 the Iranian government asked foreign states to use the name 'Iran' instead of 'Persia'.
- The unveiling decree (kashf-e hejāb) was announced in January 1936 (Dey 1314).
- Reza Shah abdicated on 16 September 1941 and died in exile in Johannesburg in 1944.

### Say it in Persian

- رضاخان در سال ۱۲۹۹ با یک کودتا به قدرت رسید.  
  *Rezā-khān dar sāl-e hezār-o-devist-o-navad-o-noh bā yek kudetā be ghodrat resid.* — Reza Khan came to power in a coup in 1921 (1299 in the Iranian calendar).
- او در سال ۱۳۰۴ شاه ایران شد.  
  *u dar sāl-e hezār-o-sisad-o-chahār shāh-e Irān shod.* — He became shah of Iran in 1925 (1304).
- راه‌آهن سراسری دریای خزر را به خلیج فارس وصل کرد.  
  *rāh-āhan-e sarāsari daryā-ye Khazar rā be Khalij-e Fārs vasl kard.* — The Trans-Iranian Railway connected the Caspian Sea to the Persian Gulf.
- هزینه‌ی راه‌آهن از مالیات قند و چای پرداخت شد.  
  *hazine-ye rāh-āhan az māliyāt-e ghand o chāy pardākht shod.* — The railway was paid for by a tax on sugar and tea.
- دانشگاه تهران در سال ۱۳۱۳ تأسیس شد.  
  *Dāneshgāh-e Tehrān dar sāl-e hezār-o-sisad-o-sizdah ta'sis shod.* — The University of Tehran was founded in 1934 (1313).
- دولت ایران از کشورهای دیگر خواست که به جای «پرشیا» بگویند «ایران».  
  *dowlat-e Irān az keshvar-hā-ye digar khāst ke be jā-ye «Pershiyā» beguyand «Irān».* — The Iranian government asked other countries to say 'Iran' instead of 'Persia'.
- در سال ۱۳۱۴ چادر در جاهای عمومی ممنوع شد.  
  *dar sāl-e hezār-o-sisad-o-chahārdah chādor dar jā-hā-ye 'omumi mamnu' shod.* — In 1314 (1936) the chador was banned in public places.
- بعضی از زنان از این قانون خوشحال بودند، اما بسیاری دیگر از خانه بیرون نمی‌رفتند.  
  *ba'zi az zanān az in ghānun khoshhāl budand, ammā besyāri digar az khāne birun nemi-raftand.* — Some women were happy with this law, but many others stopped going out of the house.
- رضاشاه کشور را با دست آهنین اداره می‌کرد.  
  *Rezā-shāh keshvar rā bā dast-e āhanin edāre mi-kard.* — Reza Shah ran the country with an iron hand.
- روزنامه‌ها آزاد نبودند و مخالفان زندانی می‌شدند.  
  *ruznāme-hā āzād nabudand va mokhālefān zendāni mi-shodand.* — Newspapers were not free and opponents were imprisoned.
- در شهریور ۱۳۲۰ ارتش‌های بریتانیا و شوروی وارد ایران شدند.  
  *dar Shahrivar-e hezār-o-sisad-o-bist artesh-hā-ye Beritāniyā va Showravi vāred-e Irān shodand.* — In August–September 1941 (Shahrivar 1320) British and Soviet armies entered Iran.
- رضاشاه به نفع پسرش از سلطنت کناره گرفت.  
  *Rezā-shāh be naf'-e pesar-ash az saltanat kenāre gereft.* — Reza Shah gave up the throne in favour of his son.
