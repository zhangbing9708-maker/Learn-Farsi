# Tabriz
## تبریز

_The great city of Iran's north-west: a Mongol and Safavid capital, home of one of the largest covered bazaars in the world, and a place where people switch between Azerbaijani Turkish and Persian in mid-sentence._

### The capital of Azerbaijan

تبریز (*Tabriz*, ‘Tabriz’), the largest city of Iran's north-west and capital of East Azerbaijan province, lies on a high plain at about 1,350 metres below the volcanic peak of سهند (*Sahand*, ‘Sahand’). Its people speak ترکی آذربایجانی (*torki-ye āzarbāyjāni*, ‘Azerbaijani Turkish’) at home and in the street and Persian at school and work, and most Tabrizis move between the two without a thought.

Tabriz has been a capital several times: of the Mongol Ilkhans in the 13th and 14th centuries, of the Turkmen dynasties of the 15th, and of the first Safavid shah in 1501. Under the Qajars the crown prince lived here, and Tabrizis were at the heart of the Constitutional Revolution of 1906–11. They proudly call their home شهر اولین‌ها (*shahr-e avvalin-hā*, ‘the city of firsts’).

### The bazaar

![The Mozaffariyeh hall of the Tabriz bazaar](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/Mozaffariyeh,_Grand_Bazzar_of_Tabriz,_IRAN.jpg/960px-Mozaffariyeh,_Grand_Bazzar_of_Tabriz,_IRAN.jpg)
<sub>The Mozaffariyeh hall of the Tabriz bazaar. Photo: [Navid Alizadeh Sadighi](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mozaffariyeh,_Grand_Bazzar_of_Tabriz,_IRAN.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

The بازار تبریز (*bāzār-e Tabriz*, ‘Tabriz bazaar’) is often called the largest covered bazaar in the world, and since 2010 it has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Under its brick vaults run dozens of lanes and halls, each with its own trade. A تیمچه (*timche*, ‘timcheh’) is a covered courtyard inside the bazaar, often domed, with shops on two floors. The most beautiful, the تیمچه‌ی مظفریه (*timche-ye Mozaffariye*, ‘Mozaffariyeh hall’), belongs to the carpet dealers – Tabriz is one of Iran's great carpet cities – while the Amir bazaar glitters with gold.

### The Blue Mosque, the Arg and El Goli

![The Blue Mosque, Tabriz](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fc/Blue_Mosque_(Tabriz)_General_view.jpg)
<sub>The Blue Mosque, Tabriz. Photo: [Saeedeh Joshaghani et al.](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Blue_Mosque_(Tabriz)_General_view.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

The مسجد کبود (*Masjed-e Kabud*, ‘Blue Mosque’) was completed in 1465 for the wife of Jahan Shah, ruler of the Qara Qoyunlu, and was once covered inside and out with deep blue tiles. The earthquake of 1780 left only its great arch standing; restoration began in 1973, and the surviving tilework is still among the finest in Iran.

Nearby rise the massive brick walls of the ارگ علیشاه (*Arg-e Alishāh*, ‘Arg of Alishah’), from the 14th century. On summer evenings families stroll around the lake of ائل‌گلی (*El-goli*, ‘El Goli’) park, with its pavilion in the middle of the water.

### Kufteh, sweets and cheese

Tabriz is famous across Iran for کوفته‌ی تبریزی (*kufte-ye tabrizi*, ‘Tabrizi kufteh’), a giant meatball of minced meat, rice and split peas that hides a boiled egg, walnuts and dried fruit inside and is served in its broth. The pastry shops sell قرابیه (*ghorābiye*, ‘almond biscuits’) that visitors take home by the box, and from the village of لیقوان (*Liqvān*, ‘Liqvan’) nearby comes a white sheep's-milk cheese that is the pride of the region.

### A few words of Turkish

![A shopkeeper in the cave village of Kandovan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/Village_Shopkeeper,_Kandovan,_Iran_(51178232307).jpg/960px-Village_Shopkeeper,_Kandovan,_Iran_(51178232307).jpg)
<sub>A shopkeeper in the cave village of Kandovan. Photo: [Julia Maudlin](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Village_Shopkeeper,_Kandovan,_Iran_(51178232307).jpg), CC BY 2.0.</sub>

You can manage in Tabriz with Persian alone, but a word of Azerbaijani wins hearts: *salam* for hello and ساغ اول (*sāgh ol*, ‘thank you’) (*sağ ol*) for thanks. Turkish words have also found their way into everyday Persian all over Iran, among them قاشق (*ghāshogh*, ‘spoon’) and اتاق (*otāgh*, ‘room’).

An hour south of the city, on the slopes of Sahand, the village of کندوان (*Kandovān*, ‘Kandovan’) is carved into cones of volcanic rock, and people still live in its cave houses. To the west, دریاچه‌ی ارومیه (*daryāche-ye Orumiye*, ‘Lake Urmia’), once the largest lake in western Asia, has shrunk dramatically since the late 1990s because of drought, dams and heavy use of water for farming.

### Getting there and when to go

![El Goli park, Tabriz](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/El-Goli_Park.jpg/960px-El-Goli_Park.jpg)
<sub>El Goli park, Tabriz. Photo: [Talahemmati](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El-Goli_Park.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

Tabriz has an airport with flights from Tehran and other cities, and overnight trains and buses come from the capital. The city is high and cold: winter nights often fall well below zero and snow is common. The best time to visit is from late spring to early autumn, when the rest of Iran is hot and Tabriz is pleasantly mild.

### Facts

- The Tabriz Historic Bazaar Complex became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2010.
- Tabriz was the first Safavid capital: Shah Ismail was crowned here in 1501, and the capital moved to Qazvin in 1555.
- The Blue Mosque was completed in 1465 and badly damaged by the earthquake of 1780.
- Under the Qajars (1789–1925) the crown prince traditionally lived in Tabriz as governor of Azerbaijan.
- Shams of Tabriz, the wandering mystic who changed the life of Rumi, came from this city.
- The 2016 census counted about 1.56 million people in Tabriz.
- The everyday Persian words ghāshogh (spoon) and otāgh (room) were borrowed from Turkic languages.

### Say it in Persian

- بازار تبریز یکی از بزرگ‌ترین بازارهای سرپوشیده‌ی جهان است.  
  *bāzār-e Tabriz yek-i az bozorg-tarin bāzār-hā-ye sarpushide-ye jahān ast.* — The Tabriz bazaar is one of the largest covered bazaars in the world.
- فرش‌فروش‌ها در تیمچه‌ی مظفریه هستند.  
  *farsh-forush-hā dar timche-ye Mozaffariye hastand.* — The carpet sellers are in the Mozaffariyeh hall.
- این فرش دست‌باف تبریز است.  
  *in farsh dast-bāf-e Tabriz ast.* — This carpet was hand-woven in Tabriz.
- لطفاً یک کوفته‌ی تبریزی بیاورید.  
  *lotfan yek kufte-ye tabrizi bi-yāvarid.* — Please bring one Tabrizi kufteh.
- قرابیه سوغاتی معروف تبریز است.  
  *ghorābiye soghāti-ye ma'ruf-e Tabriz ast.* — Ghorabiye biscuits are Tabriz's famous souvenir.
- در تبریز مردم به ترکی و فارسی حرف می‌زنند.  
  *dar Tabriz mardom be torki va fārsi harf mi-zanand.* — In Tabriz people speak Turkish and Persian.
- «ساغ اول» یعنی «متشکرم».  
  *«sāgh ol» ya'ni «moteshakker-am».* — 'Sağ ol' means 'thank you'.
- مسجد کبود در زلزله خراب شد.  
  *Masjed-e Kabud dar zelzele kharāb shod.* — The Blue Mosque was destroyed in an earthquake.
- عصرها خانواده‌ها دور دریاچه‌ی ائل‌گلی قدم می‌زنند.  
  *asr-hā khānevāde-hā dowr-e daryāche-ye El-goli ghadam mi-zanand.* — In the evenings families walk around the lake at El Goli.
- در کندوان مردم هنوز در خانه‌های سنگی زندگی می‌کنند.  
  *dar Kandovān mardom hanuz dar khāne-hā-ye sangi zendegi mi-konand.* — In Kandovan people still live in houses of rock.
- دریاچه‌ی ارومیه در سال‌های اخیر خیلی کوچک شده است.  
  *daryāche-ye Orumiye dar sāl-hā-ye akhir kheyli kuchak shode ast.* — Lake Urmia has become much smaller in recent years.
- زمستان تبریز خیلی سرد است و برف زیاد می‌بارد.  
  *zemestān-e Tabriz kheyli sard ast va barf ziyād mi-bārad.* — Winter in Tabriz is very cold and it snows a lot.
