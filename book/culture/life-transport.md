# Getting around

### Shared taxis

The classic way to get around an Iranian city is the shared تاکسی (*tāksi*, ‘taxi’). It runs along a fixed route between two squares and picks up passengers on the way, usually four at a time. To stop one, stand at the kerb and call out your destination – the name of a square, or simply مستقیم (*mostaghim*, ‘straight on’) – as the car slows down; if the driver is going your way, he stops. Pay the fixed fare, the کرایه (*kerāye*, ‘fare’), when you get out, and tell the driver where with the everyday همین‌جا پیاده می‌شم (*hamin-jā piyāde mi-sham*, ‘I'll get out right here’). To have the car to yourself, ask for a دربست (*darbast*, ‘private hire’): the whole car, at a higher price.

### Ride apps

In the big cities most people now book rides through Iranian ride-hailing apps, known simply as تاکسی اینترنتی (*tāksi-ye interneti*, ‘'internet taxis'’). You type in your destination, see the price before you set off, and pay in cash or through the app. Similar apps deliver food and parcels with a motorbike courier, the پیک (*peyk*, ‘courier’), and motorbike taxis carry single passengers through the traffic.

### Buses and the metro

City buses are cheap and stop at every ایستگاه (*istgāh*, ‘stop’); in Tehran the faster BRT buses have their own lanes. You pay with a rechargeable card or a phone, and on most buses men sit at the front and women at the back. Tehran's مترو (*metro*, ‘metro’) is the largest in the country, and Mashhad, Isfahan, Shiraz and Tabriz have metro lines of their own. Station signs are in Persian and English, and three words go a long way: خط (*khatt*, ‘line’), ورودی (*vorudi*, ‘entrance’), خروجی (*khoruji*, ‘exit’).

### Trains

![The Veresk Bridge on the Trans-Iranian Railway](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/VERESK_Bridge_-_panoramio.jpg/960px-VERESK_Bridge_-_panoramio.jpg)
<sub>The Veresk Bridge on the Trans-Iranian Railway. Photo: [Alireza Shakernia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:VERESK_Bridge_-_panoramio.jpg), CC BY-SA 3.0.</sub>

Iran's railways fan out from the great station in south Tehran, known simply as راه‌آهن (*rāh-āhan*, ‘the railway’). The Trans-Iranian Railway, built in the 1930s, climbs through the Alborz from the Caspian Sea and crosses the plateau to the Persian Gulf, over bridges such as the one at Veresk; it became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021. Long journeys are often made overnight: a sleeper قطار (*ghatār*, ‘train’) is divided into compartments, each a کوپه (*kupe*, ‘compartment’) with four or six berths, and you wake up in Mashhad, Yazd or Tabriz. Buy your بلیت (*belit*, ‘ticket’) early, especially around the holidays.

### Terminals, coaches and planes

![An Iran Air Airbus A330](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/Airbus_A330-243_%E2%80%98EP-IJA%E2%80%99_Iran_Air_(50401905668).jpg/960px-Airbus_A330-243_%E2%80%98EP-IJA%E2%80%99_Iran_Air_(50401905668).jpg)
<sub>An Iran Air Airbus A330. Photo: [Alan Wilson](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Airbus_A330-243_%E2%80%98EP-IJA%E2%80%99_Iran_Air_(50401905668).jpg), CC BY-SA 2.0.</sub>

For most journeys between cities Iranians take the coach. Tehran has four main bus stations, each called a ترمینال (*termināl*, ‘bus terminal’) – South, West, East and Beyhaghi – and from them buses leave day and night for every corner of the country. The comfortable VIP coaches have only three wide seats in each row, and passengers usually receive a little box of cake and juice. For the longest distances there are domestic flights from Tehran's Mehrabad Airport, a فرودگاه (*forudgāh*, ‘airport’) close to the city centre, to every major city.

### Driving

![The Alborz range and Damavand from the air](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Aerial_View_of_Damavand_26.11.2008_04-23-59.JPG/960px-Aerial_View_of_Damavand_26.11.2008_04-23-59.JPG)
<sub>The Alborz range and Damavand from the air. Photo: [Hansueli Krapf](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Aerial_View_of_Damavand_26.11.2008_04-23-59.JPG), CC BY-SA 3.0.</sub>

Driving in Iran calls for quick reflexes. Lane markings are treated as suggestions, the بوق (*bugh*, ‘horn’) is a means of conversation, headlights flash to claim the right of way, and every میدان (*meydān*, ‘square, roundabout’) is a test of nerve. Pedestrians cross through moving traffic, so the safest way to cross is next to a local. Out of town, the most famous road is the جاده‌ی چالوس (*jādde-ye Chālus*, ‘Chalus road’), which winds through the Alborz from Karaj down to the Caspian; on holiday weekends it can become one long traffic jam.

### Facts

- Say darbast! to a taxi driver to hire the whole car instead of sharing it.
- The Trans-Iranian Railway, built between 1927 and 1938 from the Caspian Sea to the Persian Gulf, became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021.
- Tehran has four main intercity bus terminals: South, West, East and Beyhaghi.
- VIP coaches have three seats in each row, so every passenger gets a wide reclining seat.
- Kupe comes from French coupé, and otobus from French autobus.
- The Chalus road crosses the Alborz from Karaj to the Caspian coast and is one of the most scenic drives in Iran.
- In Tehran, domestic flights use Mehrabad Airport in the city, while international flights use Imam Khomeini Airport to the south.

### Say it in Persian

- آقا، مستقیم؟  
  *āghā, mostaghim?* — Are you going straight on? (to a passing shared taxi)
- دربست تا فرودگاه چند می‌گیرید؟  
  *darbast tā forudgāh chand mi-girid?* — How much do you charge for a private ride to the airport?  
  Spoken: دربست تا فرودگاه چند می‌گیرین؟ *darbast tā forudgāh chand mi-girin?*
- لطفاً همین‌جا نگه دارید.  
  *lotfan hamin-jā negah dārid.* — Please stop right here.  
  Spoken: لطفاً همین‌جا نگه دارین. *lotfan hamin-jā negah dārin.*
- کرایه چقدر می‌شود؟  
  *kerāye cheghadr mi-shavad?* — How much is the fare?  
  Spoken: کرایه چقدر می‌شه؟ *kerāye cheghadr mi-she?*
- ببخشید، این اتوبوس به میدان آزادی می‌رود؟  
  *be-bakhshid, in otobus be meydān-e Āzādi mi-ravad?* — Excuse me, does this bus go to Azadi Square?  
  Spoken: ببخشید، این اتوبوس میره میدون آزادی؟ *be-bakhshid, in otobus mi-re meydun-e Āzādi?*
- برای رفتن به راه‌آهن کجا باید خط را عوض کنم؟  
  *barā-ye raftan be rāh-āhan kojā bāyad khatt rā avaz konam?* — Where do I have to change lines to get to the railway station?  
  Spoken: واسه رفتن به راه‌آهن کجا باید خط عوض کنم؟ *vāse raftan be rāh-āhan kojā bāyad khatt avaz konam?*
- یک بلیت قطار برای مشهد می‌خواهم.  
  *yek belit-e ghatār barā-ye Mashhad mi-khāham.* — I'd like a train ticket to Mashhad.  
  Spoken: یه بلیت قطار واسه مشهد می‌خوام. *ye belit-e ghatār vāse Mashhad mi-khām.*
- قطار ما ساعت ده شب حرکت می‌کند.  
  *ghatār-e mā sā'at-e dah-e shab harekat mi-konad.* — Our train leaves at ten at night.  
  Spoken: قطارمون ساعت ده شب راه می‌افته. *ghatār-emun sā'at-e dah-e shab rāh mi-ofte.*
- اتوبوس‌های وی‌آی‌پی صندلی‌های راحتی دارند.  
  *otobus-hā-ye vi-āy-pi sandali-hā-ye rāhat-i dārand.* — VIP coaches have comfortable seats.  
  Spoken: اتوبوسای وی‌آی‌پی صندلیای راحتی دارن. *otobus-ā-ye vi-āy-pi sandali-yā-ye rāhat-i dāran.*
- از ترمینال جنوب به اصفهان اتوبوس زیاد است.  
  *az termināl-e jonub be Esfahān otobus ziyād ast.* — There are plenty of buses to Isfahan from the South Terminal.  
  Spoken: از ترمینال جنوب واسه اصفهان اتوبوس زیاده. *az termināl-e jonub vāse Esfahān otobus ziyād-e.*
- در تهران رانندگی کار آسانی نیست.  
  *dar Tehrān rānandegi kār-e āsān-i nist.* — Driving in Tehran is no easy job.  
  Spoken: تو تهران رانندگی کار آسونی نیست. *tu Tehrān rānandegi kār-e āsun-i nist.*
- موقع رد شدن از خیابان مواظب موتورها باش!  
  *mowghe'-e rad shodan az khiyābān movāzeb-e motor-hā bāsh!* — Watch out for motorbikes when you cross the street!  
  Spoken: موقع رد شدن از خیابون مواظب موتورا باش! *mowghe'-e rad shodan az khiyābun movāzeb-e motor-ā bāsh!*
