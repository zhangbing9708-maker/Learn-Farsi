# Mohammad Mosaddegh
## محمد مصدق
*Mohammad Mosaddegh* — [read on the website with audio](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/culture/people-mosaddegh.html)

_The aristocratic lawyer who, as prime minister, nationalised Iran's oil industry in 1951 and was overthrown in the coup of August 1953. The facts of his career, the oil crisis and the events Iranians call '28 Mordad'._

### An aristocrat and a lawyer

Mohammad Mosaddegh was born in Tehran in 1882. His father had been a finance minister under Naser al-Din Shah, and his mother was a Qajar princess. As a young man he received the court title Mosaddegh al-Saltaneh, and the first part became his name. He studied in Paris and then in Switzerland, where he took a doctorate in law at the University of Neuchâtel in 1914. Back in Iran he served as a provincial governor and as minister of finance and of foreign affairs.

### Against Reza Shah

![Reza Shah](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Reza_shah_uniform.jpg/960px-Reza_shah_uniform.jpg)
<sub>Reza Shah. Photo: [Unknown author](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Reza_shah_uniform.jpg), Public domain.</sub>

As a member of the مجلس (*majles*, ‘parliament’), Mosaddegh spoke against the 1925 vote that deposed the Qajars and made Reza Khan shah. Under Reza Shah he withdrew from public life; in 1940 he was arrested and sent into internal exile for some months. After Reza Shah's abdication in 1941 he returned to parliament, and in 1949 he helped found the جبهه‌ی ملی (*Jebhe-ye Melli*, ‘National Front’), a coalition that demanded fair elections and control of Iran's oil.

### Nationalising oil

![Petrochemical plants at Asaluyeh](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c8/Petrochemical_Complexes_in_Asaluyeh_(8).jpg)
<sub>Petrochemical plants at Asaluyeh. Photo: [Majmood Hosseini](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Petrochemical_Complexes_in_Asaluyeh_(8).jpg), CC BY 4.0.</sub>

Since the early 20th century Iran's oil had been produced by a British company, the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company (today's BP), which ran the great refinery at Abadan and paid Iran a share that many Iranians considered unfair. In March 1951 the Majlis voted for the ملی شدن صنعت نفت (*melli shodan-e san'at-e naft*, ‘nationalisation of the oil industry’), and at the end of April it chose Mosaddegh as نخست‌وزیر (*nakhost-vazir*, ‘prime minister’).

Britain withdrew its technicians, blocked Iranian oil sales and took the case to the United Nations and to the International Court of Justice, which in 1952 ruled that it had no jurisdiction. That year *Time* magazine named Mosaddegh its Man of the Year.

### Crisis

Without oil income the economy suffered. In July 1952 Mosaddegh resigned after a dispute with the shah over control of the army, but mass protests on 30 Tir (21 July) brought him back days later. His coalition then began to split: some former allies, including the cleric Ayatollah Kashani, turned against him, while the communist Tudeh party grew more active. In August 1953 he dissolved parliament after a referendum – a step his critics called unconstitutional.

### 28 Mordad

On 19 August 1953 – ۲۸ مرداد (*bist-o-hasht-e Mordād*, ‘28 Mordad’) in the Iranian calendar – army units and crowds loyal to the shah, led by General Fazlollah Zahedi, overthrew the government. The کودتا (*kudetā*, ‘coup’) was organised with the American CIA and British intelligence, after a first attempt a few days earlier had failed and the shah had briefly left the country. How large a part was played by Iranian officers, clergy and crowds is still debated, but the American and British roles are documented, and the CIA released papers acknowledging its part in 2013.

### Trial and memory

Mosaddegh was tried by a military court and spent three years in prison, then lived under house arrest on his estate at احمدآباد (*Ahmad-ābād*, ‘Ahmadabad’), west of Tehran, until his death on 5 March 1967. He was buried in his own house there. In 1954 an international group of oil companies took over the running of Iran's oil, though the industry remained nationalised in name. For many Iranians Mosaddegh remains a symbol of national independence; others judge his handling of the crisis more critically.

### Facts

- Mosaddegh was born in Tehran in 1882 and died at Ahmadabad on 5 March 1967.
- He earned a doctorate in law from the University of Neuchâtel in Switzerland in 1914.
- Parliament voted to nationalise the oil industry in March 1951; 29 Esfand (around 20 March) is still a public holiday marking the event.
- Time magazine named Mosaddegh its Man of the Year for 1951.
- In 1952 the International Court of Justice ruled that it had no jurisdiction in Britain's case against Iran over oil.
- The coup of 19 August 1953 is known in Iran simply as '28 Mordad'.
- In 2013 the CIA released documents acknowledging its role in the 1953 coup.

  *Mohammad Mosaddegh dar sāl-e hezār-o-hashtsad-o-hashtād-o-do dar Tehrān be donyā āmad.* — Mohammad Mosaddegh was born in Tehran in 1882.
- مادرش از شاهزادگان قاجار بود.  
  *mādar-ash az shāhzādegān-e Ghājār bud.* — His mother was a Qajar princess.
- او در فرانسه و سوئیس حقوق خواند.  
  *u dar Farānse va Su'is hoghugh khānd.* — He studied law in France and Switzerland.
- در آن زمان نفت ایران در دست یک شرکت انگلیسی بود.  
  *dar ān zamān naft-e Irān dar dast-e yek sherkat-e engelisi bud.* — At that time Iran's oil was in the hands of a British company.
- مجلس در اسفند ۱۳۲۹ صنعت نفت را ملی کرد.  
  *majles dar Esfand-e hezār-o-sisad-o-bist-o-noh san'at-e naft rā melli kard.* — In Esfand 1329 (March 1951) parliament nationalised the oil industry.
- مصدق در سال ۱۹۵۱ نخست‌وزیر شد.  
  *Mosaddegh dar sāl-e hezār-o-nohsad-o-panjāh-o-yek nakhost-vazir shod.* — Mosaddegh became prime minister in 1951.
- بریتانیا با این تصمیم مخالف بود.  
  *Beritāniyā bā in tasmim mokhālef bud.* — Britain opposed this decision.
- اقتصاد ایران در آن سال‌ها مشکلات زیادی داشت.  
  *eghtesād-e Irān dar ān sāl-hā moshkelāt-e ziyād-i dāsht.* — Iran's economy had many problems in those years.
- در ۲۸ مرداد ۱۳۳۲ یک کودتا دولت مصدق را سرنگون کرد.  
  *dar bist-o-hasht-e Mordād-e hezār-o-sisad-o-si-o-do yek kudetā dowlat-e Mosaddegh rā sarnegun kard.* — On 28 Mordad 1332 (19 August 1953) a coup overthrew Mosaddegh's government.
- آمریکا و بریتانیا در این کودتا نقش داشتند.  
  *Āmrikā va Beritāniyā dar in kudetā naghsh dāshtand.* — America and Britain played a part in this coup.
- مصدق سال‌های آخر عمرش را در احمدآباد گذراند.  
  *Mosaddegh sāl-hā-ye ākhar-e 'omr-ash rā dar Ahmad-ābād gozarānd.* — Mosaddegh spent the last years of his life in Ahmadabad.
- روز ۲۹ اسفند در ایران روز ملی شدن صنعت نفت است.  
  *ruz-e bist-o-noh-e Esfand dar Irān ruz-e melli shodan-e san'at-e naft ast.* — In Iran, 29 Esfand is the day of the nationalisation of the oil industry.
