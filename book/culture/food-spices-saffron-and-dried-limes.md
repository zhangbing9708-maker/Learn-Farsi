# Saffron, dried limes and spices
## زعفران، لیمو عمانی و ادویه
*za'farān, limu omāni va adviye* — [read on the website with audio](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/culture/food-spices-saffron-and-dried-limes.html)

_Iranian food is fragrant rather than fiery. Its spice box holds saffron, the 'red gold' of Khorasan, sour dried limes, turmeric for every stew and the rose-scented mixture called advieh._

### Fragrant, not fiery

Visitors who expect fiery curries are often surprised: Iranian cooks use spices gently, to perfume a dish rather than burn it. The basic kit is short – زردچوبه (*zardchube*, ‘turmeric’), black pepper, salt, زعفران (*za'farān*, ‘saffron’), دارچین (*dārchin*, ‘cinnamon’), dried limes and dried herbs – and the neighbourhood herbalist, the عطاری (*attāri*, ‘herb and spice shop’), supplies the rest. Turmeric is the quiet workhorse: almost every khoresh begins with onions fried golden with a spoonful of it.

### Saffron, the red gold

زعفران (*za'farān*, ‘saffron’) is the dried red stigma of a purple autumn crocus. Each flower has only three, and they are picked by hand in October and November, early in the morning; it takes well over a hundred thousand flowers to make a single kilo. No wonder Iranians call it طلای سرخ (*talā-ye sorkh*, ‘red gold’). Iran grows the great majority of the world's saffron, above all in Khorasan around تربت حیدریه (*Torbat-e Heydariye*, ‘Torbat-e Heydarieh’), Gonabad and قائنات (*Ghā'enāt*, ‘Qaenat’), and sells it by the مثقال (*mesghāl*, ‘mesghal, about 4.6 g’).

To use it, grind a pinch of threads in a small mortar (هاون (*hāvan*, ‘mortar’)) with a lump of sugar, pour on a few spoonfuls of hot water – or put an ice cube on top – and let it brew (دم کردن (*dam kardan*, ‘to brew’)) for ten minutes or more. A few drops of this golden liquid colour a whole pot of rice.

### Dried limes and the love of sour

![Dried limes at a market](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/71/Loomi_at_market_in_Bahrain.JPG/960px-Loomi_at_market_in_Bahrain.JPG)
<sub>Dried limes at a market. Photo: [Theinfo](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Loomi_at_market_in_Bahrain.JPG), CC BY 3.0.</sub>

لیمو عمانی (*limu omāni*, ‘dried lime’) – 'Omani lime', a name that recalls the old Gulf trade with Oman – is a small lime boiled in brine and dried in the sun until it is hard, light and brown or black. Pierced and dropped into ghormeh sabzi or gheymeh, it gives a sour, slightly bitter, almost smoky taste; ground, it seasons rice and soups.

Iranians love ترش (*torsh*, ‘sour’) flavours, and dried lime is only one way to get them. Others are آبغوره (*ābghure*, ‘verjuice’), the juice of unripe grapes; رب انار (*rob-e anār*, ‘pomegranate molasses’); bitter oranges in the north; and tamarind (تمر هندی (*tamr-e hendi*, ‘tamarind’)) on the Gulf coast.

### Advieh, sumac and golpar

![Saffron rice with barberries](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Rice_flavoed_with_saffron_and_Zereshk.jpg/960px-Rice_flavoed_with_saffron_and_Zereshk.jpg)
<sub>Saffron rice with barberries. Photo: [Julia Maudlin](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rice_flavoed_with_saffron_and_Zereshk.jpg), CC BY 2.0.</sub>

ادویه (*adviye*, ‘spice mix’) simply means 'spices', but every family has its own blend. The mix for rice usually contains dried rose petals (گل محمدی (*gol-e mohammadi*, ‘Damask rose’)), cinnamon, cardamom (هل (*hel*, ‘cardamom’)) and cumin (زیره (*zire*, ‘cumin’)); the mix for stews may add turmeric, black pepper, ginger and nutmeg. Sour red barberries (زرشک (*zereshk*, ‘barberries’)), fried in butter, jewel the rice; سماق (*somāgh*, ‘sumac’) goes on kebab; and گلپر (*golpar*, ‘golpar’), the ground seed of the Persian hogweed, is sprinkled on pomegranate seeds, boiled broad beans and potatoes. Kerman's cumin is so famous that زیره به کرمان بردن (*zire be Kermān bordan*, ‘taking cumin to Kerman’) means bringing something to a place that already has plenty.

### Rosewater and the herbalist

![The Grand Bazaar of Tehran](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/Grand_Bazaar,_Tehran_bb.jpg/960px-Grand_Bazaar,_Tehran_bb.jpg)
<sub>The Grand Bazaar of Tehran. Photo: [Brunobuisson](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Grand_Bazaar,_Tehran_bb.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

Kashan and the nearby village of قمصر (*Ghamsar*, ‘Qamsar’) are famous for گلاب (*golāb*, ‘rosewater’), distilled from pink Damask roses every May in the season of گلاب‌گیری (*golāb-giri*, ‘rosewater making’). It flavours sweets, puddings and drinks.

To buy any of these, go to an عطار (*attār*, ‘herbalist’) in the bazaar: a narrow shop lined with jars and sacks of dried flowers, seeds and roots, where the owner will happily recommend a herbal tea for your cough as well. The medieval poet Attar of Nishapur took his name from this very trade.

### Facts

- Saffron is the stigma of the saffron crocus (Crocus sativus); each flower has only three, picked by hand.
- Iran produces the great majority of the world's saffron, mostly in Razavi and South Khorasan.
- In Iran saffron is sold by the mesghal, a traditional unit of about 4.6 grams.
- Limu omani, 'Omani lime', is named after the old Persian Gulf trade with Oman.
- Qamsar near Kashan distils rosewater from Damask roses every May.
- The poet Attar of Nishapur took his name from his trade: an attar is a perfumer and herbalist.

### Say it in Persian

- زعفران را با کمی قند در هاون بساب.  
  *za'farān rā bā kami ghand dar hāvan be-sāb.* — Grind the saffron with a little sugar in a mortar.
- زعفران ساییده را با آب جوش دم کن.  
  *za'farān-e sāyide rā bā āb-e jush dam kon.* — Brew the ground saffron with boiling water.
- بیشتر زعفران ایران در خراسان کاشته می‌شود.  
  *bishtar-e za'farān-e Irān dar Khorāsān kāshte mi-shavad.* — Most of Iran's saffron is grown in Khorasan.
- یک مثقال زعفران خوب چند است؟  
  *yek mesghāl za'farān-e khub chand ast?* — How much is a mesghal of good saffron?  
  Spoken: یه مثقال زعفرون خوب چنده؟ *ye mesghāl za'farun-e khub chand-e?*
- لیمو عمانی را قبل از ریختن در خورش سوراخ کن.  
  *limu omāni rā ghabl az rikhtan dar khoresh surākh kon.* — Pierce the dried lime before you put it in the stew.
- این خورش به خاطر لیمو عمانی کمی ترش است.  
  *in khoresh be khāter-e limu omāni kami torsh ast.* — This stew is a little sour because of the dried lime.
- پیاز را با یک قاشق زردچوبه سرخ کن.  
  *piyāz rā bā yek ghāshogh zardchube sorkh kon.* — Fry the onion with a spoonful of turmeric.
- روی کباب سماق بپاشید.  
  *ru-ye kabāb somāgh be-pāshid.* — Sprinkle sumac on the kebab.
- گلاب قمصر کاشان خیلی معروف است.  
  *golāb-e Ghamsar-e Kāshān kheyli ma'ruf ast.* — The rosewater of Qamsar, near Kashan, is very famous.
- از عطاری سر کوچه کمی دارچین و هل بخر.  
  *az attāri-ye sar-e kuche kami dārchin o hel be-khar.* — Buy a little cinnamon and cardamom at the herb shop on the corner.
- زیره به کرمان می‌بری؟  
  *zire be Kermān mi-bari?* — Are you taking cumin to Kerman?  
  _Said when someone brings something to a place that already has plenty – like 'coals to Newcastle'._
