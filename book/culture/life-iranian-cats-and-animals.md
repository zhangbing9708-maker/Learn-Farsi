# Persian cats and other animals

### The Persian cat

The long-haired گربه‌ی ایرانی (*gorbe-ye irāni*, ‘Persian cat’) is probably the most famous animal to carry Iran's name. Long-haired cats from Persia reached Europe in the seventeenth century – the Italian traveller Pietro della Valle is said to have brought some home around 1620 – and the flat-faced show cat of today was bred much later, in Britain and America. In Iran itself the everyday گربه (*gorbe*, ‘cat’) is a lean, clever street cat. Every neighbourhood has its own, living on scraps from kind neighbours and restaurant kitchens, and keeping a cat as a حیوان خانگی (*heyvān-e khānegi*, ‘pet’) has become popular in the cities.

### The last Asiatic cheetahs

The یوزپلنگ ایرانی (*yuzpalang-e irāni*, ‘Asiatic cheetah’), or simply یوز (*yuz*, ‘cheetah’), once roamed from Arabia to India. Today it survives only in Iran, in the deserts and dry hills of the plateau, such as the Touran reserve in Semnan province and Miandasht in North Khorasan, and it is critically endangered: only a small number remain, and every new sighting makes the news. Rangers, scientists and villagers work to protect it, and at the 2014 World Cup Iran's footballers wore the cheetah on their shirts to make people care.

### Leopards, lions and tigers

![Forest in Golestan National Park](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/7e/Golestan_jungle.jpg)
<sub>Forest in Golestan National Park. Photo: [Nima Farid](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Golestan_jungle.jpg), Public domain.</sub>

The پلنگ ایرانی (*palang-e irāni*, ‘Persian leopard’) is luckier. One of the largest leopards in the world, it has its stronghold in Iran: in the Caspian forests and Golestan National Park, across the Alborz and the Zagros, and even in the protected hills just east of Tehran. Two other big cats are gone. The Asiatic lion, شیر (*shir*, ‘lion’), and the Caspian tiger, ببر (*babr*, ‘tiger’), both disappeared from Iran in the twentieth century. Shir is a word to watch: it also means 'milk' and 'tap'.

### The onager

On the dry plains lives the گورخر ایرانی (*gur-khar-e irāni*, ‘Persian onager’), a pale, fast wild ass, protected today in the Touran reserve and the Bahram-e Gur reserve in Fars. In modern Persian گورخر (*gur-khar*, ‘zebra’) on its own usually means 'zebra', but the old word گور (*gur*, ‘onager’) is famous from history: the Sasanian king Bahram V loved hunting onagers so much that he was called Bahram-e Gur. A quatrain attributed to Khayyam plays on the fact that gur also means 'grave': دیدی که چگونه گور بهرام گرفت (*didi ke chegune gur Bahrām gereft*, ‘you saw how the grave caught Bahram’).

### Birds of the wetlands and of poetry

![The Anzali lagoon, Gilan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/95/Anzali_Lagoon.jpg/960px-Anzali_Lagoon.jpg)
<sub>The Anzali lagoon, Gilan. Photo: [Hamid Hajihusseini](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Anzali_Lagoon.jpg), CC BY 3.0.</sub>

Iran lies on the great migration routes between Siberia and Africa, and every winter wetlands such as the تالاب انزلی (*tālāb-e Anzali*, ‘Anzali Lagoon’) on the Caspian fill with migrating ducks, geese and waders. For well over a decade a lone Siberian crane nicknamed Omid, 'Hope' – the last of his western flock – flew back each winter to the wetlands of Fereydunkenar. Poetry has its own birds: the بلبل (*bolbol*, ‘nightingale’), forever in love with the گل (*gol*, ‘rose’); the هدهد (*hodhod*, ‘hoopoe’), who leads the birds in Attar's *Conference of the Birds*; and the سیمرغ (*simorgh*, ‘Simorgh’), the great mythical bird of the Shahnameh.

### Animals in proverbs and stories

![A page from Kalila and Dimna](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fe/Kalila-wa-Dimna_1.jpg/960px-Kalila-wa-Dimna_1.jpg)
<sub>A page from Kalila and Dimna. Photo: [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kalila-wa-Dimna_1.jpg), Public domain.</sub>

Animals fill Persian stories, from the two jackals of کلیله و دمنه (*Kalile va Demne*, ‘Kalila and Dimna’) to the fables in Rumi's *Masnavi* and the comic poem *Mouse and Cat* attributed to Obeyd Zakani. They live on in everyday proverbs:

- شتر دیدی، ندیدی (*shotor didi, na-didi*, ‘you saw a camel? you didn't’) – keep it to yourself
- جوجه را آخر پاییز می‌شمارند (*juje rā ākhar-e pāyiz mi-shomārand*, ‘chicks are counted at the end of autumn’) – don't count your chickens
- گربه دستش به گوشت نمی‌رسد، می‌گوید بو می‌دهد (*gorbe dast-ash be gusht nemi-rasad, mi-guyad bu mi-dahad*, ‘the cat can't reach the meat, so it says the meat smells’) – sour grapes
- مارگزیده از ریسمان سیاه و سفید می‌ترسد (*mār-gazide az rismān-e siyāh o sefid mi-tarsad*, ‘someone bitten by a snake fears a black-and-white rope’) – once bitten, twice shy

### Facts

- The Asiatic cheetah now survives in the wild only in Iran, and it is critically endangered.
- The Italian traveller Pietro della Valle is said to have brought long-haired cats from Persia to Italy around 1620.
- At the 2014 World Cup Iran's footballers wore the Asiatic cheetah on their shirts to draw attention to its plight.
- Lions and Caspian tigers once lived in Iran; both had vanished from the country by the second half of the 20th century.
- The Sasanian king Bahram V was nicknamed Bahrām-e Gur for his love of hunting onagers (gur).
- In Attar's Conference of the Birds, the hoopoe leads the birds on their journey in search of the Simorgh.
- Isfahan's old pigeon towers were built to collect pigeon droppings as fertiliser for the fields.
- Shir means 'lion', 'milk' and 'tap' – only the context tells you which.

### Say it in Persian

- گربه‌ی ایرانی موی بلند و نرمی دارد.  
  *gorbe-ye irāni mu-ye boland va narm-i dārad.* — The Persian cat has long, soft fur.  
  Spoken: گربه‌ی ایرانی موی بلند و نرمی داره. *gorbe-ye irāni mu-ye boland o narm-i dāre.*
- ما در خانه یک گربه داریم.  
  *mā dar khāne yek gorbe dārim.* — We have a cat at home.  
  Spoken: ما تو خونه یه گربه داریم. *mā tu khune ye gorbe dārim.*
- یوزپلنگ ایرانی فقط در ایران زندگی می‌کند.  
  *yuzpalang-e irāni faghat dar Irān zendegi mi-konad.* — The Asiatic cheetah lives only in Iran.  
  Spoken: یوزپلنگ ایرانی فقط تو ایران زندگی می‌کنه. *yuzpalang-e irāni faghat tu Irān zendegi mi-kone.*
- یوزپلنگ سریع‌ترین حیوان خشکی است.  
  *yuzpalang sari'-tarin heyvān-e khoshki ast.* — The cheetah is the fastest land animal.  
  Spoken: یوزپلنگ سریع‌ترین حیوون خشکیه. *yuzpalang sari'-tarin heyvun-e khoshki-ye.*
- پلنگ ایرانی در کوه‌های البرز و زاگرس زندگی می‌کند.  
  *palang-e irāni dar kuh-hā-ye Alborz va Zāgros zendegi mi-konad.* — The Persian leopard lives in the Alborz and Zagros mountains.
- شیر هم حیوان است، هم نوشیدنی!  
  *shir ham heyvān ast, ham nushidani!* — Shir is both an animal (a lion) and a drink (milk)!  
  Spoken: شیر هم حیوونه، هم نوشیدنی! *shir ham heyvun-e, ham nushidani!*
- گورخر ایرانی در دشت‌های توران زندگی می‌کند.  
  *gur-khar-e irāni dar dasht-hā-ye Turān zendegi mi-konad.* — The Persian onager lives on the plains of Touran.
- بلبل در شعر فارسی عاشق گل است.  
  *bolbol dar she'r-e fārsi āshegh-e gol ast.* — In Persian poetry the nightingale is in love with the rose.
- در کلیله و دمنه حیوانات مثل آدم‌ها حرف می‌زنند.  
  *dar Kalile va Demne heyvānāt mesl-e ādam-hā harf mi-zanand.* — In Kalila and Dimna the animals talk like people.  
  Spoken: تو کلیله و دمنه حیوونا مثل آدما حرف می‌زنن. *tu Kalile o Demne heyvun-ā mesl-e ādam-ā harf mi-zanan.*
- جوجه را آخر پاییز می‌شمارند.  
  *juje rā ākhar-e pāyiz mi-shomārand.* — Don't count your chickens before they hatch.  
  Spoken: جوجه رو آخر پاییز می‌شمرن. *juje ro ākhar-e pāyiz mi-shomoran.*  
  Literally: chicks are counted at the end of autumn
- گربه دستش به گوشت نمی‌رسد، می‌گوید بو می‌دهد.  
  *gorbe dast-ash be gusht nemi-rasad, mi-guyad bu mi-dahad.* — Sour grapes.  
  Spoken: گربه دستش به گوشت نمی‌رسه، می‌گه بو می‌ده. *gorbe dast-esh be gusht nemi-rese, mi-ge bu mi-de.*  
  Literally: the cat can't reach the meat, so it says the meat smells
- شتر دیدی؟ ندیدی!  
  *shotor didi? na-didi!* — You saw nothing – keep it to yourself!  
  Literally: did you see a camel? you didn't see it!
