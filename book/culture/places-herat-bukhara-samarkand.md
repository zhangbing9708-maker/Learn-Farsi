# Herat, Bukhara and Samarkand
## هرات، بخارا و سمرقند

_Persian was born as a literary language in the east, in the cities of Khorasan and Transoxiana. A journey to Herat, Bukhara and Samarkand – and how Dari and Tajik differ from the Persian of Iran._

### The Persian world beyond Iran

Persian is not only the language of Iran. In Afghanistan it is called دری (*Dari*, ‘Dari’) and is one of the two main official languages; in Tajikistan it is تاجیکی (*tājiki*, ‘Tajik’), written in the Cyrillic alphabet since 1940; and in Uzbekistan the old cities of Bukhara and Samarkand still have many Persian-speaking inhabitants.

For a thousand years these lands were the eastern half of the Persian world: خراسان (*Khorāsān*, ‘Khorasan’), 'where the sun rises', and the lands beyond the Oxus river, فرارود (*Farārud*, ‘Transoxiana’). It was here, not in western Iran, that New Persian poetry was born.

### Herat

![The citadel of Herat](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/View_of_Herat_Citadel_from_atop_the_premises.jpg/960px-View_of_Herat_Citadel_from_atop_the_premises.jpg)
<sub>The citadel of Herat. Photo: [Todd Huffman](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:View_of_Herat_Citadel_from_atop_the_premises.jpg), CC BY 2.0.</sub>

هرات (*Herāt*, ‘Herat’), in western Afghanistan near the Iranian border, was in the 15th century the capital of the Timurid rulers Shahrukh and Sultan Husayn Bayqara and one of the most brilliant cities in the world. Here the poet جامی (*Jāmi*, ‘Jami’) wrote and the painter بهزاد (*Behzād*, ‘Behzad’) brought the Persian miniature to perfection.

The citadel of قلعه‌ی اختیارالدین (*Ghal'e-ye Ekhtiyār-od-din*, ‘Ikhtiyaruddin’) rises over the old city, and the tiles of the great Friday Mosque are still made in its own workshop. Of the Musalla complex built by Queen Goharshad – the same queen who built the great mosque in Mashhad – five tall minarets survive.

### Bukhara

![The Kalon mosque and minaret, Bukhara](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Kalon-Ensemble_Buchara.jpg/960px-Kalon-Ensemble_Buchara.jpg)
<sub>The Kalon mosque and minaret, Bukhara. Photo: [Euyasik](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kalon-Ensemble_Buchara.jpg), CC BY-SA 3.0.</sub>

In the 9th and 10th centuries بخارا (*Bokhārā*, ‘Bukhara’), now in Uzbekistan, was the capital of the Samanids, an Iranian dynasty whose court welcomed poets writing in Persian rather than Arabic. The greatest of them was رودکی (*Rudaki*, ‘Rudaki’), often called the father of Persian poetry. The Samanid mausoleum, a small cube of patterned brick from the 10th century, survived the Mongols because it lay buried under sand and mud.

The منار کلان (*Menār-e Kalān*, ‘Kalon Minaret’), built in 1127 and about 46 metres high, was so admired, the story goes, that Genghis Khan ordered it spared. In the evening people gather at لب حوض (*Lab-e Howz*, ‘Lyabi Hauz’), 'by the pool', a square around a pool shaded by old mulberry trees.

### Samarkand

![Girih tilework, Shah-i Zinda, Samarkand](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Samarkand_Shah-i_Zinda_Tuman_Aqa_complex_cropped2.jpg/960px-Samarkand_Shah-i_Zinda_Tuman_Aqa_complex_cropped2.jpg)
<sub>Girih tilework, Shah-i Zinda, Samarkand. Photo: [Patrickringgenberg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Samarkand_Shah-i_Zinda_Tuman_Aqa_complex_cropped2.jpg), CC BY 3.0.</sub>

سمرقند (*Samarghand*, ‘Samarkand’) was Timur's capital at the end of the 14th century, and he filled it with craftsmen taken from conquered cities, many of them in Iran. Its centre is the ریگستان (*Rigestān*, ‘Registan’), 'the sandy place', a square framed by three tiled madrasas: Ulugh Beg (1417–1420), Sher-Dor (1619–1636) and Tilya-Kori (1646–1660). Nearby are Timur's tomb, the گور امیر (*Gur-e Amir*, ‘Gur-e Amir’), and the street of blue-tiled tombs called Shah-i Zinda. Timur's grandson Ulugh Beg built an observatory here in the 1420s, and his star tables were among the most accurate of their time.

### Hafez, Dari and Tajik

![Dushanbe, capital of Tajikistan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/Panorama_with_buildings,_Dushanbe.jpg/960px-Panorama_with_buildings,_Dushanbe.jpg)
<sub>Dushanbe, capital of Tajikistan. Photo: [Adam Harangozó](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Panorama_with_buildings,_Dushanbe.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

Hafez made the two cities famous in a single couplet. If the beautiful 'Turk of Shiraz' would take his heart, he says, به خال هندویش بخشم سمرقند و بخارا را (*be khāl-e hendu-yash bakhsham Samarghand o Bokhārā rā*, ‘for her dark mole I would give Samarkand and Bukhara’).

A Persian speaker from Iran understands Dari and Tajik but will notice differences. Dari keeps two old vowels that Iranian Persian has lost, so 'lion', *shēr*, and 'milk', *shir*, sound different. Everyday words vary too: 'thank you' is تشکر (*tashakkor*, ‘thanks’) in Kabul, *rahmat* in Dushanbe and *mersi* or *mamnun* in Tehran; a potato is *sib-zamini* in Iran but کچالو (*kachālu*, ‘potato’) in Afghanistan; and 'how are you?' in Kabul is چطور استی؟ (*chetor asti?*, ‘how are you?’)

### Getting there and when to go

Samarkand and Bukhara are linked to Tashkent by fast trains and are easy to visit. The best seasons are April–May and September–October; summers are very hot and winters cold. In Dushanbe, the capital of Tajikistan, a statue of Rudaki stands on the main avenue, which also bears his name. Before planning a trip to Herat, check your government's current travel advice for Afghanistan.

### Facts

- Tajik has been written in the Cyrillic alphabet since 1940; before that it used a Latin alphabet (1928–1940) and, earlier, the Perso-Arabic script.
- The historic centre of Bukhara was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 1993, and Samarkand in 2001.
- The Kalon Minaret in Bukhara was built in 1127 and is about 46 metres tall.
- The Samanid mausoleum in Bukhara survived the Mongol conquest because it lay buried under flood silt and sand.
- Rudaki (died c. 941), court poet of the Samanids at Bukhara, is often called the father of Persian poetry.
- Queen Goharshad built both the Musalla complex in Herat and the Goharshad Mosque in Mashhad.
- Ulugh Beg's observatory in Samarkand was built in the 1420s.

### Say it in Persian

- در افغانستان به فارسی «دری» می‌گویند.  
  *dar Afghānestān be fārsi «Dari» mi-guyand.* — In Afghanistan Persian is called 'Dari'.
- تاجیکی را با خط سیریلیک می‌نویسند.  
  *tājiki rā bā khatt-e sirilik mi-nevisand.* — Tajik is written in the Cyrillic alphabet.
- یک فارسی‌زبان در بخارا و سمرقند می‌تواند با مردم حرف بزند.  
  *yek fārsi-zabān dar Bokhārā va Samarghand mi-tavānad bā mardom harf be-zanad.* — A Persian speaker can talk with people in Bukhara and Samarkand.
- رودکی پدر شعر فارسی است.  
  *Rudaki pedar-e she'r-e fārsi ast.* — Rudaki is the father of Persian poetry.
- جامی و بهزاد در هرات زندگی می‌کردند.  
  *Jāmi va Behzād dar Herāt zendegi mi-kardand.* — Jami and Behzad lived in Herat.
- منار کلان را در سال ۱۱۲۷ ساختند.  
  *Menār-e Kalān rā dar sāl-e hezār-o-sad-o-bist-o-haft sākhtand.* — The Kalon Minaret was built in 1127.
- سمرقند پایتخت تیمور بود.  
  *Samarghand pāytakht-e Teymur bud.* — Samarkand was Timur's capital.
- ریگستان یعنی «جای ریگ».  
  *Rigestān ya'ni «jā-ye rig».* — Registan means 'the sandy place'.
- در کابل می‌گویند «چطور استی؟».  
  *dar Kābol mi-guyand «chetor asti?».* — In Kabul they say 'chetor asti?' (how are you?).
- در دوشنبه به جای «مرسی» می‌گویند «رحمت».  
  *dar Dushanbe be jā-ye «mersi» mi-guyand «rahmat».* — In Dushanbe they say 'rahmat' instead of 'mersi'.
- در افغانستان به سیب‌زمینی «کچالو» می‌گویند.  
  *dar Afghānestān be sib-zamini «kachālu» mi-guyand.* — In Afghanistan they call a potato 'kachalu'.
- بهترین فصل سفر به سمرقند بهار و پاییز است.  
  *beh-tarin fasl-e safar be Samarghand bahār o pāyiz ast.* — The best seasons for a trip to Samarkand are spring and autumn.
