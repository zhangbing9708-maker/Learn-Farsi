# Karim Khan Zand and Shiraz
## کریم‌خان زند و شیراز
*Karim-khān-e Zand o Shirāz* — [read on the website with audio](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/culture/history-karim-khan-zand-and-shiraz.html)

_After decades of war, a tribal chief from the Zagros gave Iran almost thirty years of relative peace. Karim Khan Zand refused to call himself shah, ruled as the 'vakil', and filled his capital Shiraz with a citadel, a bazaar, a mosque and a bath that still stand._

### The scramble after Nader

When Nader Shah was murdered in 1747, his empire broke apart. For several years rival warlords fought over Iran: an Afghan general, the chief of the Qajar tribe, a Bakhtiari leader – and کریم‌خان (*Karim-khān*, ‘Karim Khan’), head of the small ایل زند (*il-e Zand*, ‘Zand tribe’) from the Zagros mountains near Malayer.

Karim Khan was not the strongest of them at first, but he was patient and well liked by his men. By the early 1760s he had defeated or outlasted his rivals and ruled all of Iran except Khorasan, which he left to Nader's blind grandson Shahrokh.

### The 'vakil' who would not be shah

At first Karim Khan ruled in the name of a young Safavid prince, Ismail III, whom he kept in comfortable confinement. Even when there was no longer any reason for this, he refused the title of شاه (*shāh*, ‘shah’). He called himself وکیل (*vakil*, ‘vakil, 'deputy, representative'’) – first the deputy of the Safavid state and later وکیل‌الرعایا (*vakil-or-ro'āyā*, ‘the representative of the people’).

Today the word وکیل (*vakil*) usually means 'lawyer', and Iranians enjoy the idea of a ruler who saw himself as the people's advocate. Stories about him stress his simplicity, his humour and his care for ordinary people. Historians add that he could be harsh with rebels, but compared with the years before and after, his reign (from 1751 until his death in 1779) was a time of آرامش (*ārāmesh*, ‘calm’) and recovery.

### Shiraz, the Zand capital

![The Vakil Bazaar, Shiraz](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e4/Bazaar_de_Vakil,_Shiraz,_Ir%C3%A1n,_2016-09-24,_DD_48.jpg/960px-Bazaar_de_Vakil,_Shiraz,_Ir%C3%A1n,_2016-09-24,_DD_48.jpg)
<sub>The Vakil Bazaar, Shiraz. Photo: [Diego Delso](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bazaar_de_Vakil,_Shiraz,_Ir%C3%A1n,_2016-09-24,_DD_48.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

Karim Khan made شیراز (*Shirāz*, ‘Shiraz’) his capital in the 1760s and rebuilt it. In the centre he raised the ارگ کریم‌خان (*Arg-e Karim-khān*, ‘Citadel of Karim Khan’), a square fortress with round corner towers of brick; one of them visibly leans. Around it he laid out squares and gardens and a new city wall.

The بازار وکیل (*Bāzār-e Vakil*, ‘Vakil Bazaar’) was designed as one long, high-vaulted street, wide enough for light and air, with caravanserais and courtyards opening off it. It is still a working market where you can buy carpets, spices and copperware.

### The Vakil mosque and the Vakil bath

Beside the bazaar stands the مسجد وکیل (*Masjed-e Vakil*, ‘Vakil Mosque’). Its tiles, with their roses and pink and yellow flowers, are typical of Shiraz, but its most famous part is the شبستان (*shabestān*, ‘prayer hall’): a forest of forty-eight stone ستون (*sotun*, ‘columns’), each carved with a spiral and each cut from a single block.

Nearby is the حمام وکیل (*Hammām-e Vakil*, ‘Vakil Bath’), a public bathhouse with a domed dressing hall, a warm hall and a hot hall, decorated with stone carvings and paintings. It is now a museum, where life-size figures show how people once washed, chatted and had their hair cut at the حمام (*hammām*, ‘bathhouse’).

### Poets, merchants and an end

![The tomb of Hafez, Shiraz](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0d/Hafez_880714_095.jpg)
<sub>The tomb of Hafez, Shiraz. Photo: [Amirskip4life](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hafez_880714_095.jpg), CC BY-SA 3.0.</sub>

Karim Khan also restored the tombs of the two great poets of Shiraz, حافظ (*Hāfez*, ‘Hafez’) and سعدی (*Sa'di*, ‘Saadi’), and allowed the British East India Company to open a trading post at Bushehr. Under his rule the trade and farming of the south slowly recovered.

After his death in 1779 his relatives fought over the succession. The last Zand ruler, the brave young Lotf Ali Khan, was defeated at Kerman in 1794 by Agha Mohammad Khan Qajar – who, as a youth, had spent years as a hostage at Karim Khan's court in Shiraz. The Qajars moved the capital to Tehran, but Shiraz kept the monuments of its vakil.

### Facts

- Karim Khan Zand ruled from 1751 until his death in 1779 and never took the title of shah.
- His title vakil-or-ro'āyā means 'representative of the people'; in modern Persian vakil usually means 'lawyer'.
- The prayer hall of the Vakil Mosque in Shiraz has 48 monolithic stone columns carved with spirals.
- One of the corner towers of the Citadel of Karim Khan leans noticeably.
- The Vakil Bath in Shiraz is now a museum of bathhouse life.
- Agha Mohammad Khan, founder of the Qajar dynasty, spent years of his youth as a hostage at Karim Khan's court in Shiraz.
- The last Zand ruler, Lotf Ali Khan, was defeated at Kerman in 1794.

### Say it in Persian

- کریم‌خان زند از سال ۱۷۵۱ تا ۱۷۷۹ بر بیشتر ایران حکومت کرد.  
  *Karim-khān-e Zand az sāl-e hezār-o-haftsad-o-panjāh-o-yek tā hezār-o-haftsad-o-haftād-o-noh bar bishtar-e Irān hokumat kard.* — Karim Khan Zand ruled most of Iran from 1751 to 1779.
- او هیچ‌وقت خودش را شاه ننامید.  
  *u hich-vaght khod-ash rā shāh nanāmid.* — He never called himself shah.
- کریم‌خان لقب وکیل را برای خودش انتخاب کرد.  
  *Karim-khān laghab-e vakil rā barā-ye khod-ash entekhāb kard.* — Karim Khan chose the title 'vakil' for himself.
- در این‌جا «وکیل» یعنی نماینده‌ی مردم.  
  *dar in-jā «vakil» ya'ni namāyande-ye mardom.* — Here 'vakil' means the representative of the people.
- دوران او برای ایران دوره‌ی آرامش بود.  
  *dowrān-e u barā-ye Irān dowre-ye ārāmesh bud.* — His reign was a time of calm for Iran.
- او شیراز را پایتخت خود کرد.  
  *u Shirāz rā pāytakht-e khod kard.* — He made Shiraz his capital.
- ارگ کریم‌خان در مرکز شیراز است.  
  *Arg-e Karim-khān dar markaz-e Shirāz ast.* — The Citadel of Karim Khan is in the centre of Shiraz.
- بازار وکیل هنوز یکی از زیباترین بازارهای ایران است.  
  *Bāzār-e Vakil hanuz yek-i az zibā-tarin bāzār-hā-ye Irān ast.* — The Vakil Bazaar is still one of the most beautiful bazaars in Iran.
- شبستان مسجد وکیل ستون‌های سنگی زیبایی دارد.  
  *shabestān-e Masjed-e Vakil sotun-hā-ye sangi-ye zibā-yi dārad.* — The prayer hall of the Vakil Mosque has beautiful stone columns.
- حمام وکیل امروز موزه است.  
  *Hammām-e Vakil emruz muze ast.* — The Vakil Bath is a museum today.
- کریم‌خان آرامگاه حافظ را تعمیر کرد.  
  *Karim-khān ārāmgāh-e Hāfez rā ta'mir kard.* — Karim Khan repaired the tomb of Hafez.
- شیرازی‌ها کریم‌خان را به نیکی یاد می‌کنند.  
  *shirāzi-hā Karim-khān rā be niki yād mi-konand.* — The people of Shiraz remember Karim Khan fondly.
