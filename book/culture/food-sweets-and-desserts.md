# Sweets and desserts
## شیرینی‌های ایرانی
*shirini-hā-ye irāni* — [read on the website with audio](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/culture/food-sweets-and-desserts.html)

_Almost every Iranian city has a sweet it is famous for, from Isfahan's gaz to Qom's sohan. Add saffron rice pudding, rosewater sorbet and saffron ice cream, and you have a sweet tooth's map of Iran._

### A land of sweets

Iranians have a famously sweet tooth. Good news is celebrated with a box of شیرینی (*shirini*, ‘pastries, sweets’): pass an exam, get engaged or buy a car, and your friends will ask where the sweets are. Every Nowruz visit ends with a plate of them, and almost every city has a speciality that travellers carry home as سوغاتی (*soghāti*, ‘a souvenir from a trip’). The classic flavours are گلاب (*golāb*, ‘rosewater’), saffron, cardamom and pistachio.

### Gaz from Isfahan

![Gaz, the nougat of Isfahan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/Gaz_Candy_From_Iran.jpg/960px-Gaz_Candy_From_Iran.jpg)
<sub>Gaz, the nougat of Isfahan. Photo: [Sidster](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gaz_Candy_From_Iran.jpg), CC BY-SA 2.5.</sub>

گز (*gaz*, ‘gaz’), Isfahan's famous nougat, is made from egg whites, sugar, rosewater and pistachios or almonds – and, in the traditional recipe, گزانگبین (*gaz-angabin*, ‘gaz manna’), a sweet white sap gathered from a thorny shrub that grows wild in the Zagros mountains. It is sold in round pieces dusted with flour and packed in decorated tins, and few visitors leave Isfahan without a box.

### Sohan and other city sweets

![Sohan, the brittle of Qom](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/%D8%B3%D9%88%D9%87%D8%A7%D9%86_%D8%B3%D9%88%D8%BA%D8%A7%D8%AA_%D8%B4%D9%87%D8%B1_%D9%82%D9%85_04.jpg/960px-%D8%B3%D9%88%D9%87%D8%A7%D9%86_%D8%B3%D9%88%D8%BA%D8%A7%D8%AA_%D8%B4%D9%87%D8%B1_%D9%82%D9%85_04.jpg)
<sub>Sohan, the brittle of Qom. Photo: [Mostafameraji](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%D8%B3%D9%88%D9%87%D8%A7%D9%86_%D8%B3%D9%88%D8%BA%D8%A7%D8%AA_%D8%B4%D9%87%D8%B1_%D9%82%D9%85_04.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

Qom is famous for سوهان (*sohān*, ‘sohan’), a brittle, buttery toffee made with sprouted-wheat flour, sugar, saffron and pistachios. Other cities have their own: Yazd its باقلوا (*bāghlavā*, ‘baklava’) and cakes, Kerman its date-filled کلمپه (*kolompe*, ‘kolompeh’), Tabriz its almond قرابیه (*ghorābiye*, ‘qurabiyeh’), Kermanshah its crumbly نان برنجی (*nān-e berenji*, ‘rice-flour cookies’), and the towns of Gilan their soft کلوچه (*koluche*, ‘koloocheh’).

### Zoolbia, bamieh and halva

In the month of Ramadan, pastry shops fill with زولبیا (*zulbiyā*, ‘zoolbia’) and بامیه (*bāmiye*, ‘bamieh’) – fried dough soaked in saffron and rosewater syrup. Zoolbia is a lacy spiral; bamieh is a ridged little finger named after the okra it resembles. Both are favourites at افطار (*eftār*, ‘the meal that breaks the fast’).

حلوا (*halvā*, ‘halva’) in Iran is a dense paste of flour toasted in butter or oil and mixed with saffron and rosewater syrup, smoothed flat and decorated with nuts. It belongs to remembrance: it is served at funerals and memorials, and on Thursday evenings families share it in memory of the dead.

### Sholeh zard

![Sholeh zard, saffron rice pudding](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/10/Shole-zrad-2014.JPG/960px-Shole-zrad-2014.JPG)
<sub>Sholeh zard, saffron rice pudding. Photo: [Rezarad](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shole-zrad-2014.JPG), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

شله‌زرد (*shole-zard*, ‘sholeh zard’) – 'yellow sholeh', a sholeh being a thick, slow-cooked dish – is a saffron rice pudding scented with rosewater and cardamom. It is above all a نذری (*nazri*, ‘votive food’), made in big pots and shared after a vow, and each bowl is decorated with cinnamon (دارچین (*dārchin*, ‘cinnamon’)) and slivered almonds and pistachios, sometimes in the shape of a name or a prayer.

1. Wash the rice and cook it in plenty of water until very soft.
2. Add sugar and keep cooking, stirring, until the pudding thickens.
3. Stir in saffron dissolved in rosewater, a little butter or oil, and ground cardamom.
4. Add slivered almonds and pour into bowls.
5. When it has cooled, draw patterns on top with cinnamon and pistachios.

### Faloodeh and bastani

![Faloodeh, frozen rose-water noodles](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Faloodeh1.jpg/960px-Faloodeh1.jpg)
<sub>Faloodeh, frozen rose-water noodles. Photo: [Rka11111](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Faloodeh1.jpg), CC BY-SA 3.0.</sub>

In summer, Shiraz offers فالوده (*fālude*, ‘faloodeh’): thin noodles of starch, half-frozen in a syrup of sugar and rosewater, eaten with a squeeze of lime juice or a spoonful of sour cherry syrup. It is often called one of the world's oldest frozen desserts, from a land that kept winter ice through the desert summer in great domed ice houses (یخچال (*yakhchāl*, ‘ice house; today 'fridge'’)).

Iran's بستنی سنتی (*bastani-ye sonnati*, ‘traditional ice cream’) is yellow with saffron, scented with rosewater and full of pistachios and frozen flakes of cream. Pressed between two wafers it becomes بستنی نونی (*bastani nuni*, ‘an ice-cream sandwich’) – the taste of a Tehran summer.

### Facts

- Gaz takes its name from gaz-angabin, a sweet sap gathered from a wild shrub in the Zagros mountains.
- Sohan is Qom's speciality, made with sprouted-wheat flour, saffron and pistachios.
- Bamieh is named after okra (bāmiye), whose shape it resembles.
- Zoolbia and bamieh are the classic sweets of Ramadan iftars.
- Halva is served at funerals and memorials and shared on Thursday evenings in memory of the dead.
- Yakhchāl, 'ice pit', once meant the domed ice houses of the desert; today it is the word for a fridge.

### Say it in Persian

- شیرینی قبولی‌ات کجاست؟  
  *shirini-ye ghabuli-at kojā-st?* — Where are the sweets for passing your exam?  
  Spoken: شیرینی قبولیت کو؟ *shirini-ye ghabuli-t ku?*
- گز سوغاتی معروف اصفهان است.  
  *gaz soghāti-ye ma'ruf-e Esfahān ast.* — Gaz is Isfahan's famous souvenir.
- از قم برایمان سوهان بیاور.  
  *az Ghom barā-yemān sohān bi-yāvar.* — Bring us some sohan from Qom.
- در ماه رمضان زولبیا و بامیه خیلی طرفدار دارد.  
  *dar māh-e ramezān zulbiyā va bāmiye kheyli tarafdār dārad.* — In Ramadan zoolbia and bamieh are hugely popular.
- شله‌زرد را با دارچین و خلال بادام تزیین می‌کنند.  
  *shole-zard rā bā dārchin va khalāl-e bādām tazyin mi-konand.* — Sholeh zard is decorated with cinnamon and slivered almonds.
- برنج شله‌زرد باید خیلی آرام بپزد.  
  *berenj-e shole-zard bāyad kheyli ārām be-pazad.* — The rice for sholeh zard has to cook very slowly.
- در شیراز فالوده را با آب‌لیمو می‌خورند.  
  *dar Shirāz fālude rā bā āb-limu mi-khorand.* — In Shiraz they eat faloodeh with lime juice.
- یک بستنی سنتی با فالوده، لطفاً.  
  *yek bastani-ye sonnati bā fālude, lotfan.* — A traditional ice cream with faloodeh, please.  
  Spoken: یه بستنی سنتی با فالوده، لطفاً. *ye bastani-ye sonnati bā fālude, lotfan.*
- این شیرینی‌ها را خودتان پخته‌اید؟  
  *in shirini-hā rā khod-etān pokhte-id?* — Did you bake these pastries yourself?  
  Spoken: این شیرینی‌ها رو خودتون پختین؟ *in shirini-hā ro khod-etun pokhtin?*
- حلوا را برای مراسم ختم درست می‌کنند.  
  *halvā rā barā-ye marāsem-e khatm dorost mi-konand.* — Halva is made for memorial services.
- دستتان درد نکند، شیرینی‌ها فوق‌العاده بودند.  
  *dast-etān dard na-konad, shirini-hā fowgh-ol-āde budand.* — Thank you – the pastries were wonderful.
