# Omar Khayyam
## عمر خیام
*Omar Khayyām* — [read on the website with audio](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/culture/people-khayyam.html)

_In his own day Omar Khayyam was a mathematician and astronomer who helped create Iran's calendar. Today the world knows him for a few dozen short, sceptical quatrains – some of which he may not even have written._

### The tent-maker's son from Nishapur

![Countryside near Nishapur](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e6/Kamar_Zard_Buzhan_-_Nishapur_1.jpg)
<sub>Countryside near Nishapur. Photo: [Sonia Sevilla](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kamar_Zard_Buzhan_-_Nishapur_1.jpg), CC0.</sub>

Omar Khayyam was born in 1048 in نیشابور (*Neyshābur*, ‘Nishapur’), a rich city of Khorasan on the Silk Road. His name, خیام (*khayyām*, ‘tent-maker’), suggests that his father or grandfather made tents, but nothing certain is known about his family. He grew up under the Seljuks, Turkish sultans whose empire stretched from Central Asia to Anatolia and whose court used Persian.

### A mathematician first

In his own time Khayyam was famous not as a poet but as a scientist. His *Treatise on Algebra*, written in Arabic, classified cubic equations – those with x³ – and showed how to solve them geometrically, by finding where two curves such as a circle and a parabola cross. It was one of the great achievements of medieval جبر (*jabr*, ‘algebra’). He also wrote on Euclid's هندسه (*hendese*, ‘geometry’), questioning the famous 'parallel postulate' centuries before European mathematicians took up the same problem.

### The Jalali calendar

![A calendar page for Ordibehesht](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f3/Ordibehesht_1383_calendar.jpg)
<sub>A calendar page for Ordibehesht. Photo: [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ordibehesht_1383_calendar.jpg), Public domain.</sub>

In 1074 the Seljuk sultan Malik-Shah and his minister Nizam al-Mulk invited Khayyam to Isfahan to lead a team of astronomers at a new رصدخانه (*rasadkhāne*, ‘observatory’). Their task was to reform the calendar. The result, the تقویم جلالی (*taghvim-e Jalāli*, ‘Jalali calendar’), introduced in 1079, is a solar calendar in which the year begins at the spring equinox – the moment of نوروز (*Nowruz*). Its leap years follow the sun so closely that it is often said to be more accurate than the Gregorian calendar adopted in Europe five centuries later. Iran's official calendar today, adopted in 1925, is based on it.

### The quatrains

Khayyam's fame as a poet rests on the رباعی (*robā'i*, ‘quatrain’), a four-line poem in which the first, second and fourth lines rhyme. The quatrains linked to his name are short and sceptical: life is brief, the dead do not come back, the learned have no answers – so drink شراب (*sharāb*, ‘wine’) with friends and live in the moment. One of the best known begins این قافله‌ی عمر عجب می‌گذرد (*in ghāfele-ye 'omr 'ajab mi-gozarad*, ‘how strangely this caravan of life passes by’).

Which of them he really wrote is one of the great puzzles of Persian literature. Hundreds of quatrains have been attributed to him, mostly in manuscripts copied long after his death, and scholars think only a small number may be his.

### FitzGerald and the world

In 1859 the English poet Edward FitzGerald published *The Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam*, a free and musical adaptation rather than a translation. It sold almost nothing at first, then became one of the most popular poems in English. Omar Khayyam clubs, illustrated editions and translations into many languages followed, and a crater on the Moon now bears his name.

### A tomb among the blossoms

![The mausoleum of Omar Khayyam, Nishapur](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/27/Omar_Khayyam_Mausoleum_254.jpg/960px-Omar_Khayyam_Mausoleum_254.jpg)
<sub>The mausoleum of Omar Khayyam, Nishapur. Photo: [dynamosquito](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Omar_Khayyam_Mausoleum_254.jpg), CC BY-SA 2.0.</sub>

Khayyam died in Nishapur around 1131. The writer Nezami Aruzi recalls hearing him say that his grave would lie where the north wind scattered blossoms on it; visiting Nishapur years later, he found the tomb beside a garden wall, covered in fallen petals. Today a modern monument of crossing tiled arches, completed in 1963 to a design by the architect Houshang Seyhoun, stands in a garden in Nishapur, decorated with his quatrains.

### Facts

- Khayyam was born in Nishapur in 1048 and died there around 1131.
- Khayyām means 'tent-maker'.
- The Jalali calendar was introduced in 1079 and takes its name from Sultan Malik-Shah, whose title was Jalāl al-Dowle.
- Iran's official calendar, adopted by parliament in 1925, is based on the Jalali calendar.
- FitzGerald's Rubaiyat was first published, anonymously, in 1859.
- A crater on the far side of the Moon is named after Omar Khayyam.
- His mausoleum in Nishapur, designed by Houshang Seyhoun, was completed in 1963.

### Say it in Persian

- خیام در نیشابور به دنیا آمد.  
  *Khayyām dar Neyshābur be donyā āmad.* — Khayyam was born in Nishapur.
- خیام ریاضی‌دان، ستاره‌شناس و شاعر بود.  
  *Khayyām riyāzi-dān, setāre-shenās va shā'er bud.* — Khayyam was a mathematician, an astronomer and a poet.
- او معادله‌های درجه‌ی سوم را با هندسه حل کرد.  
  *u mo'ādele-hā-ye daraje-ye sevvom rā bā hendese hall kard.* — He solved cubic equations with geometry.
- سلطان ملکشاه او را به اصفهان دعوت کرد.  
  *Soltān Malekshāh u rā be Esfahān da'vat kard.* — Sultan Malik-Shah invited him to Isfahan.
- خیام و همکارانش تقویم جلالی را ساختند.  
  *Khayyām va ham-kārān-ash taghvim-e Jalāli rā sākhtand.* — Khayyam and his colleagues created the Jalali calendar.
- سال در این تقویم با نوروز شروع می‌شود.  
  *sāl dar in taghvim bā Nowruz shoru' mi-shavad.* — In this calendar the year begins with Nowruz.
- تقویم امروز ایران بر پایه‌ی تقویم جلالی است.  
  *taghvim-e emruz-e Irān bar pāye-ye taghvim-e Jalāli ast.* — Iran's calendar today is based on the Jalali calendar.
- رباعی شعری با چهار مصراع است.  
  *robā'i she'r-i bā chahār mesrā' ast.* — A quatrain is a poem with four lines.
- رباعیات خیام به زبان‌های زیادی ترجمه شده است.  
  *robā'iyyāt-e Khayyām be zabān-hā-ye ziyād-i tarjome shode ast.* — Khayyam's quatrains have been translated into many languages.
- فیتزجرالد رباعیات را به انگلیسی برگرداند.  
  *Fitzjerāld robā'iyyāt rā be engelisi bargardānd.* — FitzGerald put the quatrains into English.
- بسیاری از رباعیات خیام درباره‌ی کوتاهی زندگی است.  
  *besyāri az robā'iyyāt-e Khayyām darbāre-ye kutāhi-ye zendegi ast.* — Many of Khayyam's quatrains are about the shortness of life.
- آرامگاه خیام در باغی در نیشابور است.  
  *ārāmgāh-e Khayyām dar bāgh-i dar Neyshābur ast.* — Khayyam's tomb is in a garden in Nishapur.
