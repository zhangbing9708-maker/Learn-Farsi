# Alexander, the Parthians and the Sasanians
## اسکندر، اشکانیان و ساسانیان
*Eskandar, Ashkāniyān o Sāsāniyān* — [read on the website with audio](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/learn-farsi/culture/history-alexander-parthians-sasanians.html)

_After Alexander, Iran was ruled first by Greek kings, then for almost five centuries by the Parthian horse-archers, and then for four more by the Sasanians – the last great Iranian empire before Islam and Rome's most dangerous rival._

### Alexander and the Seleucids

Alexander of Macedon tried to take over the Persian Empire rather than destroy it. He married a Bactrian princess, Roxana, adopted Persian court customs, and in 324 BC held a mass wedding at Susa at which dozens of his officers married Persian noblewomen. But he died in Babylon the next year, aged 32, and his generals divided his conquests. Iran fell to Seleucus, whose dynasty, the سلوکیان (*Salukiyān*, ‘Seleucids’), founded Greek cities across the east.

Iranians remember Alexander in two ways. Zoroastrian books call him گجسته (*gojaste*, ‘accursed’), the man who burned Persepolis and the holy texts. In Ferdowsi's Shahnameh, though, اسکندر (*Eskandar*) is the son of an Iranian king – a conqueror turned into one of the family.

### The Parthians

![The Parthian Empire under Mithridates II](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/Map_of_the_Parthian_Empire_under_Mithridates_II.svg/800px-Map_of_the_Parthian_Empire_under_Mithridates_II.svg.png)
<sub>The Parthian Empire under Mithridates II. Photo: [Ro4444](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Map_of_the_Parthian_Empire_under_Mithridates_II.svg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

Around 247 BC a chief of the Parni nomads, اشک (*Arshak*, ‘Arsaces’), seized the province of Parthia, in today's Khorasan. His descendants, the اشکانیان (*Ashkāniyān*, ‘Arsacids’) or Parthians, drove out the Seleucids, took Mesopotamia and ruled from Ctesiphon on the Tigris, near modern Baghdad. Their kingdom was a loose union of great noble families – Muslim historians later called the period ملوک‌الطوایف (*moluk-ot-tavāyef*, ‘'the tribal kings'’).

Their great weapon was the mounted archer. At Carrhae in 53 BC the Parthian general Surena destroyed a Roman army and killed its commander, Crassus. Their trick of turning in the saddle to shoot while riding away, the 'Parthian shot', probably gave English the phrase 'a parting shot'. The dynasty lasted nearly five centuries, longer than any other in Iran's history.

### Ardashir and the Sasanians

![The royal tombs of Naqsh-e Rostam near Persepolis](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Naqsh-e_Rustam_necropolis_in_Iran.jpg/800px-Naqsh-e_Rustam_necropolis_in_Iran.jpg)
<sub>The royal tombs of Naqsh-e Rostam near Persepolis. Photo: [Maasaak](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Naqsh-e_Rustam_necropolis_in_Iran.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

In AD 224 a Persian vassal king from Fars, اردشیر بابکان (*Ardeshir-e Bābakān*, ‘Ardashir son of Babak’), defeated and killed the last Parthian king. His dynasty, the ساسانیان (*Sāsāniyān*, ‘Sasanians’), named after an ancestor called Sasan, ruled for more than four hundred years. They called their realm ایرانشهر (*Irānshahr*, ‘the land of the Iranians’), and Zoroastrianism became the religion of the state.

Ardashir's son Shapur I (ruled 240–270) fought three Roman emperors: Gordian III died on campaign against him, Philip the Arab paid for peace, and in 260 Valerian was taken prisoner – the first Roman emperor ever captured by a foreign enemy. Shapur had his triumph carved into the cliff at نقش رستم (*Naghsh-e Rostam*), below the tombs of the Achaemenid kings.

### Khosrow and the golden age

![Sasanian rock reliefs at Taq-e Bostan, Kermanshah](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/%D8%AA%D8%A7%D9%82%E2%80%8C%D8%A8%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%A7%D9%86.jpg/800px-%D8%AA%D8%A7%D9%82%E2%80%8C%D8%A8%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%A7%D9%86.jpg)
<sub>Sasanian rock reliefs at Taq-e Bostan, Kermanshah. Photo: [Ali Heidari - علی حیدری](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%D8%AA%D8%A7%D9%82%E2%80%8C%D8%A8%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%A7%D9%86.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

The Sasanian golden age came under خسرو انوشیروان (*Khosrow Anushirvān*, ‘Khosrow I, 'of the immortal soul'’) (ruled 531–579), who reformed taxes and the army and became, in Persian literature, the model of the just king. His court welcomed Greek philosophers when their schools in Athens were closed, and his physician Borzuy brought back from India the animal fables that became کلیله و دمنه (*Kalile o Demne*, ‘Kalila and Dimna’). Chess, شطرنج (*shatranj*), also arrived from India in his reign; in the Shahnameh's version the Indian king sent it as a riddle, and Khosrow's wise vizier بزرگمهر (*Bozorgmehr*) not only worked out the rules but invented a game in reply – نرد (*nard*, ‘backgammon’).

The great brick arch of the palace at Ctesiphon, طاق کسری (*Tāgh-e Kasrā*, ‘the Arch of Khosrow’), still stands in Iraq, and the reliefs of طاق بستان (*Tāgh-e Bostān*) near Kermanshah show later Sasanian kings hunting and riding in armour.

### The fall

Under Khosrow II (590–628) the Sasanians took Jerusalem and Egypt and besieged Constantinople, but the Byzantine emperor Heraclius struck back deep into Iran, and Khosrow was overthrown and killed by his own nobles. Weakened by war, plague and civil war – around ten rulers in four years – the empire could not stop the Arab Muslim armies of the 630s. After defeats at Qadisiyya (636) and Nahavand (642), the last king, یزدگرد سوم (*Yazdegerd-e sevvom*, ‘Yazdegerd III’), was killed near Merv in 651.

Yet Sasanian Iran lived on – in the court ceremony of the caliphs, in art admired from China to Europe, and above all in its language, Middle Persian, the parent of the Persian spoken today.

### Facts

- The Parthian dynasty lasted about 470 years (c. 247 BC – AD 224), the longest of any Iranian dynasty.
- At Carrhae in 53 BC the Parthians destroyed the Roman army of Crassus, one of Rome's worst defeats.
- In AD 260 Shapur I captured the emperor Valerian, the first Roman emperor taken prisoner by a foreign enemy.
- The Taq Kasra at Ctesiphon is often called the largest single-span brick vault ever built.
- The Sasanians' name for their realm, Ērānšahr ('land of the Iranians'), lies behind the modern name of the country, Iran.
- Chess reached Iran from India in the 6th century; English 'checkmate' comes from Persian shāh māt and 'rook' from rokh.
- Arab writers called the Battle of Nahavand (642) 'the victory of victories'.

### Say it in Persian

- اسکندر مقدونی شاهنشاهی هخامنشی را شکست داد.  
  *Eskandar-e Maghduni shāhanshāhi-ye Hakhāmaneshi rā shekast dād.* — Alexander of Macedon defeated the Achaemenid Empire.
- در شاهنامه، اسکندر یک شاهزاده‌ی ایرانی است.  
  *dar Shāhnāme, Eskandar yek shāhzāde-ye irāni ast.* — In the Shahnameh, Alexander is an Iranian prince.
- اشکانیان نزدیک به پانصد سال بر ایران حکومت کردند.  
  *Ashkāniyān nazdik be pānsad sāl bar Irān hokumat kardand.* — The Parthians ruled Iran for nearly five hundred years.
- سواران اشکانی تیراندازان بسیار ماهری بودند.  
  *savārān-e Ashkāni tir-andāzān-e besyār māher-i budand.* — The Parthian horsemen were very skilful archers.
- اردشیر بابکان در سال ۲۲۴ میلادی شاهنشاهی ساسانی را بنیان گذاشت.  
  *Ardeshir-e Bābakān dar sāl-e devist-o-bist-o-chahār-e milādi shāhanshāhi-ye Sāsāni rā bonyān gozāsht.* — Ardashir son of Babak founded the Sasanian Empire in AD 224.
- ساسانیان کشورشان را «ایرانشهر» می‌نامیدند.  
  *Sāsāniyān keshvar-eshān rā «Irānshahr» mi-nāmidand.* — The Sasanians called their country Iranshahr.
- شاپور یکم امپراتور روم را اسیر کرد.  
  *Shāpur-e yekom emperātur-e Rum rā asir kard.* — Shapur I took the Roman emperor prisoner.
- انوشیروان در ادبیات فارسی نماد عدالت است.  
  *Anushirvān dar adabiyāt-e fārsi namād-e edālat ast.* — In Persian literature, Anushirvan is the symbol of justice.
- شطرنج در زمان ساسانیان از هند به ایران آمد.  
  *shatranj dar zamān-e Sāsāniyān az Hend be Irān āmad.* — Chess came to Iran from India in Sasanian times.
- طاق کسری هنوز در نزدیکی بغداد ایستاده است.  
  *Tāgh-e Kasrā hanuz dar nazdiki-ye Baghdād istāde ast.* — The Arch of Khosrow still stands near Baghdad.
- آخرین شاه ساسانی یزدگرد سوم بود.  
  *ākharin shāh-e Sāsāni Yazdegerd-e sevvom bud.* — The last Sasanian king was Yazdegerd III.
- فارسی امروز از زبان پهلوی ساسانیان آمده است.  
  *fārsi-ye emruz az zabān-e pahlavi-ye Sāsāniyān āmade ast.* — Today's Persian comes from the Pahlavi language of the Sasanians.
