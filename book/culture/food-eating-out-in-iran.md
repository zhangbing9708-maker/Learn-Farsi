# Eating out in Iran
## بیرون غذا خوردن
*birun ghazā khordan* — [read on the website with audio](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/culture/food-eating-out-in-iran.html)

_Kebab houses and carpeted teahouses, busy cafés and street carts selling beetroot and broad beans – plus the art of ta'arof at the table and the polite battle over who pays._

### Where to eat

![Darband, at the foot of the mountains in north Tehran](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/Darband,_T%C3%A9h%C3%A9ran_(3).jpg/960px-Darband,_T%C3%A9h%C3%A9ran_(3).jpg)
<sub>Darband, at the foot of the mountains in north Tehran. Photo: [ZarlokX](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Darband,_T%C3%A9h%C3%A9ran_(3).jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

For Iranians home cooking is the gold standard, and many of the best stews and polos are hard to find on menus. But there is plenty to eat out:

- the کبابی (*kabābi*, ‘kebab house’) or چلوکبابی (*chelow-kabābi*, ‘chelow kabab restaurant’): kebab, rice and doogh, served fast;
- the سفره‌خانه (*sofre-khāne*, ‘traditional restaurant’): carpets, cushions and wooden day-beds (تخت (*takht*, ‘day-bed’)) where you sit cross-legged to eat dizi, drink tea and smoke a water pipe (قلیان (*ghalyān*, ‘water pipe’));
- the رستوران (*restorān*, ‘restaurant’), from simple to grand, with Iranian classics and, in the big cities, pizza, burgers and foreign cuisines;
- the کافه (*kāfe*, ‘café’), hugely popular with young people in Tehran and other cities.

On summer evenings Tehranis head up to Darband and Darakeh at the foot of the mountains, where restaurants set their day-beds beside the stream.

### Street food

![Kalleh pacheh](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/Khash_(dish)_05.jpg/960px-Khash_(dish)_05.jpg)
<sub>Kalleh pacheh. Photo: [Shams948](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Khash_(dish)_05.jpg), CC0.</sub>

Street food follows the seasons. In winter, carts sell steaming لبو (*labu*, ‘boiled beetroot’) and broad beans (باقالی (*bāghāli*, ‘broad beans’)) boiled with golpar and vinegar; in summer, corn on the cob (بلال (*balāl*, ‘corn on the cob’)) is grilled over charcoal and dipped in salt water. جگرکی (*jegaraki*, ‘liver-kebab stall’)s grill lamb's liver, heart and kidneys late into the night. Khuzestan is famous for spicy فلافل (*falāfel*, ‘falafel’), and every city has ساندویچی (*sāndevichi*, ‘sandwich shop’)s selling sausage and الویه (*olviye*, ‘chicken and potato salad’) sandwiches. And before dawn the کله‌پزی (*kalle-pazi*, ‘sheep's-head restaurant’) serves کله‌پاچه (*kalle-pāche*, ‘sheep's head and trotters’) – a winter breakfast for the brave.

### Ordering and paying

![A 10,000-rial banknote](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/16/10000_IRR_1981_Typ_2_Back.jpg)
<sub>A 10,000-rial banknote. Photo: [National Bank of Iran](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:10000_IRR_1981_Typ_2_Back.jpg), Public domain.</sub>

In simple places you often order and pay at the counter (صندوق (*sandugh*, ‘cash desk’)) first; in others you ask for the bill (صورت‌حساب (*surat-hesāb*, ‘bill’)) at the end and pay the cashier on the way out. Menus often give prices in تومان (*tumān*, ‘toman’) – one toman is ten rials – and people almost always talk in tomans. Some restaurants add a service charge; otherwise a small tip (انعام (*an'ām*, ‘tip’)) is welcome but not expected.

### Ta'arof at the table

تعارف (*ta'ārof*, ‘ritual politeness’) shapes every meal with guests. The host says بفرمایید (*befarmāyid*, ‘please, go ahead’) and keeps filling your plate; a guest is expected to protest a little, and a clean plate is quickly refilled – so if you are truly full, leave a little and say سیر شدم (*sir shodam*, ‘I'm full’). After the meal, thank the cook with دستتان درد نکند (*dast-etān dard na-konad*, ‘thank you; lit. 'may your hand not hurt'’); the answer is نوش جان (*nush-e jān*, ‘enjoy it; lit. 'sweet to your soul'’).

### Who pays?

When friends eat out, the bill starts a polite battle. Everyone reaches for a wallet, insisting مهمان من باشید (*mehmān-e man bāshid*, ‘be my guest’), and an experienced payer slips away to the cash desk before dessert. Splitting the bill – دنگی (*dongi*, ‘going Dutch’) – is now common among students and young colleagues, but with older relatives or a visitor from abroad, someone will insist on paying. And if a taxi driver or shopkeeper says قابل ندارد (*ghābel na-dārad*, ‘it's not worthy of you’) when you ask the price, that too is ta'arof: thank them and insist on paying.

### Facts

- In many Iranian restaurants you pay at the cash desk (sandugh), not at the table.
- Iranians usually speak of prices in tomans; one toman is ten rials.
- Ghābel nadārad, 'it is not worthy (of you)', is ta'arof: the customer is expected to insist on paying.
- Abadan and Ahvaz in Khuzestan are famous for spicy falafel.
- Boiled beetroot (labu) and boiled broad beans (baghali) are classic winter street foods.
- Traditional sofrekhanehs seat diners on carpeted wooden day-beds called takht.

### Say it in Persian

- یک میز برای چهار نفر دارید؟  
  *yek miz barā-ye chahār nafar dārid?* — Do you have a table for four?
- منو را می‌آورید، لطفاً؟  
  *menu rā mi-āvarid, lotfan?* — Could you bring the menu, please?  
  Spoken: منو رو می‌آرین، لطفاً؟ *menu ro mi-ārin, lotfan?*
- غذای امروز چیست؟  
  *ghazā-ye emruz chist?* — What's today's dish?  
  Spoken: غذای امروز چیه؟ *ghazā-ye emruz chiye?*
- برای من یک پرس جوجه‌کباب با دوغ بیاورید.  
  *barā-ye man yek pors juje-kabāb bā dugh bi-yāvarid.* — Bring me a portion of chicken kebab with doogh.  
  Spoken: برای من یه پرس جوجه‌کباب با دوغ بیارین. *barā-ye man ye pors juje-kabāb bā dugh bi-yārin.*
- سیر شدم، دستتان درد نکند.  
  *sir shodam, dast-etān dard na-konad.* — I'm full – thank you.  
  Spoken: سیر شدم، دستتون درد نکنه. *sir shodam, dast-etun dard na-kone.*
- نوش جان!  
  *nush-e jān!* — Enjoy! (said to someone who is eating or has eaten)
- صورت‌حساب را بیاورید، لطفاً.  
  *surat-hesāb rā bi-yāvarid, lotfan.* — The bill, please.  
  Spoken: صورت‌حساب رو بیارین، لطفاً. *surat-hesāb ro bi-yārin, lotfan.*
- این دفعه مهمان من باشید.  
  *in daf'e mehmān-e man bāshid.* — This time, be my guest.  
  Spoken: این دفعه مهمون من باشین. *in daf'e mehmun-e man bāshin.*
- نه، اصلاً؛ حساب را من می‌دهم.  
  *na, aslan; hesāb rā man mi-daham.* — No, absolutely not – I'm paying.
- حساب را دنگی حساب کنیم؟  
  *hesāb rā dongi hesāb konim?* — Shall we split the bill?
- قابل ندارد.  
  *ghābel na-dārad.* — It's nothing – no need to pay.  
  Spoken: قابل نداره. *ghābel na-dāre.*  
  Literally: it is not worthy (of you)  
  _Ta'arof: thank them and insist on paying._
- شب‌ها جگرکی‌ها تا دیروقت باز هستند.  
  *shab-hā jegaraki-hā tā dir-vaght bāz hastand.* — At night the liver-kebab stalls stay open late.
