# The bazaar
## بازار
*bāzār* — [read on the website with audio](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/culture/life-the-bazaar.html)

_Rows of shops grouped by trade, hidden caravanserais, domed halls and merchants who seal deals over a glass of tea: how the traditional Iranian bazaar works – with the phrases you need to bargain in it._

### A city under one roof

In the old cities of Iran the بازار (*bāzār*, ‘bazaar’) is not just a market but the spine of the town: a covered street of brick vaults, sometimes several kilometres long, often linking a city gate with the مسجد جامع (*masjed-e jāme'*, ‘Friday mosque’). Light falls through openings in the domes, the air stays cool in summer and dry in winter, and inside there are not only shops but mosques, schools, bathhouses, tea houses and workshops. English borrowed the word itself from Persian.

### Rows by trade

![The bazaar of Zanjan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/04/BAzar_zanjAn_01.jpg/960px-BAzar_zanjAn_01.jpg)
<sub>The bazaar of Zanjan. Photo: [Alireza Tajfar](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:BAzar_zanjAn_01.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

A traditional bazaar is organised by trade. Each راسته (*rāste*, ‘row’) belongs to one craft or one kind of goods and often carries its name: the row of the مسگر (*mesgar*, ‘coppersmith’)s, ringing with hammers; the goldsmiths' row of the زرگر (*zargar*, ‘goldsmith’)s; the carpet sellers; the shoemakers; the عطاری (*attāri*, ‘herbalist's shop’)s with their sacks of dried flowers, roots and spices. Customers can compare prices from shop to shop, and the members of each صنف (*senf*, ‘guild’) keep an eye on one another. Where two main rows cross, a great dome rises over the چهارسو (*chahār-su*, ‘crossing’).

### Caravanserais and timchehs

![The caravanserai of Izadkhast](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/Izadkhvast_Caravanserai_01.jpg/960px-Izadkhvast_Caravanserai_01.jpg)
<sub>The caravanserai of Izadkhast. Photo: [Bernard Gagnon](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Izadkhvast_Caravanserai_01.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

Behind the shopfronts open quieter courtyards. A کاروانسرا (*kārvānsarā*, ‘caravanserai’), or simply سرا (*sarā*, ‘sara’), inside the bazaar is where caravans once unloaded: a courtyard with a pool, surrounded by two storeys of rooms used as storerooms and merchants' offices. A تیمچه (*timche*, ‘timcheh’) is a smaller, roofed version, often crowned by a magnificent dome and given over to a single trade such as carpets or fine cloth. Each little shop or office is a حجره (*hojre*, ‘hojreh’), and many have been in the same family for generations.

### The bazaari

The بازاری (*bāzāri*, ‘bazaar merchant’) is a figure of his own in Iranian life. Many are wholesalers whose business rests on family ties, trust and a promise kept: large deals can be agreed over a glass of tea and paid by cheque months later. Bazaar merchants have long paid for mosques, schools and charities, and in the month of Muharram the guilds organise mourning processions and cook food to give away. Apprentices, each one a شاگرد (*shāgerd*, ‘apprentice; shop boy’), start young and learn by watching, while the باربر (*bārbar*, ‘porter’)s push heavy carts through the crowd, calling for people to make way.

### Four great bazaars

![The Mozaffariyeh hall of the Tabriz bazaar](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/Mozaffariyeh,_Grand_Bazzar_of_Tabriz,_IRAN.jpg/960px-Mozaffariyeh,_Grand_Bazzar_of_Tabriz,_IRAN.jpg)
<sub>The Mozaffariyeh hall of the Tabriz bazaar. Photo: [Navid Alizadeh Sadighi](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mozaffariyeh,_Grand_Bazzar_of_Tabriz,_IRAN.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

- **Tehran**: the بازار بزرگ (*bāzār-e bozorg*, ‘Grand Bazaar’), just south of the Golestan Palace, is a working market where wholesalers still trade in everything from fabric to kitchenware.
- **Tabriz**: one of the largest covered bazaars in the world and a great stop on the Silk Road, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2010. Its Mozaffariyeh hall is the place for carpets.
- **Isfahan**: the bazaar begins at the Qeysariyeh gate on Naqsh-e Jahan Square and winds north-east to the old Friday Mosque.
- **Kashan**: smaller but beautiful, with the Qajar-era تیمچه‌ی امین‌الدوله (*timche-ye Amin-od-dowle*, ‘Amin od-Dowleh timcheh’), whose dome of interlocking ribs and skylights is a favourite of photographers.

### Bargaining

In the bazaar, prices for carpets, crafts and antiques are the start of a conversation; for food, spices and everyday goods they are usually fixed. Useful phrases:

- چند است؟ (*chand ast?*, ‘how much is it?’) – in speech, چنده؟ (*chand-e?*, ‘how much?’)
- خیلی گران است (*kheyli gerān ast*, ‘it's very expensive’) – in speech, خیلی گرونه (*kheyli gerun-e*)
- تخفیف بدهید (*takhfif be-dahid*, ‘give me a discount’)
- آخرش چند؟ (*ākhar-esh chand?*, ‘what's your last price?’)
- بیشتر از این نمی‌دهم (*bishtar az in nemi-daham*, ‘I won't pay more than this’)

If the seller says قابل ندارد (*ghābel na-dārad*, ‘it's not worthy of you’), he is not giving the thing away: this is تعارف (*ta'ārof*, ‘ritual politeness’). Thank him, insist, and ask the price again.

### Facts

- The Historic Bazaar Complex of Tabriz was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2010.
- English 'bazaar' and 'caravanserai' both come from Persian: bāzār, and kārvānsarā, 'caravan house'.
- In a traditional bazaar each trade has its own row (rāste), so coppersmiths, goldsmiths or carpet sellers work side by side.
- Isfahan's bazaar begins at the Qeysariyeh gate on the north side of Naqsh-e Jahan Square.
- The Timche-ye Amin od-Dowleh in Kashan's bazaar dates from the Qajar period and is famous for its spectacular dome.
- Chāne zadan, 'to bargain', literally means 'to beat the chin'.
- Most shops in Iran's traditional bazaars close on Fridays.

### Say it in Persian

- بازار بزرگ تهران نزدیک کاخ گلستان است.  
  *bāzār-e bozorg-e Tehrān nazdik-e kākh-e Golestān ast.* — Tehran's Grand Bazaar is near the Golestan Palace.  
  Spoken: بازار بزرگ تهران نزدیک کاخ گلستانه. *bāzār-e bozorg-e Tehrān nazdik-e kākh-e Golestān-e.*
- در هر راسته‌ی بازار یک نوع جنس می‌فروشند.  
  *dar har rāste-ye bāzār yek now' jens mi-forushand.* — In each row of the bazaar they sell one kind of goods.  
  Spoken: تو هر راسته‌ی بازار یه جور جنس می‌فروشن. *tu har rāste-ye bāzār ye jur jens mi-forushan.*
- ببخشید، راسته‌ی فرش‌فروش‌ها کجاست؟  
  *be-bakhshid, rāste-ye farsh-forush-hā kojā-st?* — Excuse me, where is the carpet sellers' row?
- این قالیچه چند است؟  
  *in ghāliche chand ast?* — How much is this rug?  
  Spoken: این قالیچه چنده؟ *in ghāliche chand-e?*
- خیلی گران است؛ تخفیف نمی‌دهید؟  
  *kheyli gerān ast; takhfif nemi-dahid?* — That's very expensive – won't you give me a discount?  
  Spoken: خیلی گرونه؛ تخفیف نمی‌دین؟ *kheyli gerun-e; takhfif nemi-din?*
- آخرش چند می‌دهید؟  
  *ākhar-ash chand mi-dahid?* — What's your final price?  
  Spoken: آخرش چند می‌دین؟ *ākhar-esh chand mi-din?*  
  Literally: in the end, for how much will you give (it)?
- قابل شما را ندارد.  
  *ghābel-e shomā rā na-dārad.* — It's not worthy of you – please, take it.  
  Spoken: قابل شما رو نداره. *ghābel-e shomā ro na-dāre.*  
  _Ta'arof: the seller does not mean it is free. Thank him and ask the price again._
- پدربزرگم سی سال در این حجره کار کرد.  
  *pedar-bozorg-am si sāl dar in hojre kār kard.* — My grandfather worked in this shop for thirty years.  
  Spoken: بابابزرگم سی سال تو این حجره کار کرد. *bābā-bozorg-am si sāl tu in hojre kār kard.*
- کاروان‌ها بارشان را در کاروانسرا خالی می‌کردند.  
  *kārvān-hā bār-eshān rā dar kārvānsarā khāli mi-kardand.* — The caravans used to unload their goods in the caravanserai.
- بازار جمعه‌ها تعطیل است.  
  *bāzār jom'e-hā ta'til ast.* — The bazaar is closed on Fridays.  
  Spoken: بازار جمعه‌ها تعطیله. *bāzār jom'e-hā ta'til-e.*
- بازار تبریز یکی از بزرگ‌ترین بازارهای سرپوشیده‌ی جهان است.  
  *bāzār-e Tabriz yek-i az bozorg-tarin bāzār-hā-ye sarpushide-ye jahān ast.* — The Tabriz bazaar is one of the largest covered bazaars in the world.
- فروشنده برای ما چای آورد.  
  *forushande barā-ye mā chāy āvard.* — The shopkeeper brought us some tea.  
  Spoken: فروشنده واسه‌مون چایی آورد. *forushande vāse-mun chāyi āvord.*
