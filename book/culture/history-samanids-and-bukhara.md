# The Samanids and Bukhara
## سامانیان و بخارا
*Sāmāniyān o Bokhārā* — [read on the website with audio](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/culture/history-samanids-and-bukhara.html)

_In the ninth and tenth centuries a Persian dynasty in Bukhara made the language of the people a language of poetry and scholarship again. Under the Samanids, Rudaki sang the first great Persian verse and the young Avicenna read his way through a royal library._

### After the conquest

After the Arab conquest of the seventh century, Arabic became the language of religion, government and learning across the old Sasanian lands. Persian did not die – people went on speaking it at home and in the market – but for about two hundred years very little was written in it. Iranians sometimes call this period دو قرن سکوت (*do gharn sokut*, ‘two centuries of silence’), after the title of a well-known history book.

In the ninth century a new written form of the language appeared in the east, in Khorasan and Central Asia: فارسی دری (*fārsi-ye dari*, ‘Dari Persian’), written in the Arabic alphabet with four extra letters. This is the direct ancestor of the Persian you are learning. The dynasty that made it a language of high culture was the سامانیان (*Sāmāniyān*, ‘Samanids’).

### A Persian dynasty in Central Asia

![The Samanid realm in 943](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6c/Map_of_the_Samanid_amirate_at_the_death_of_Nasr_II,_943.svg/960px-Map_of_the_Samanid_amirate_at_the_death_of_Nasr_II,_943.svg.png)
<sub>The Samanid realm in 943. Photo: [Ro4444](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Map_of_the_Samanid_amirate_at_the_death_of_Nasr_II,_943.svg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

The family descended from Saman, a landowner from near Balkh who became a Muslim. Around 819 his grandsons were made governors of cities such as Samarkand and Herat by the Abbasid caliph. Officially they remained the caliph's governors; in practice they ruled for themselves.

The greatest of them, اسماعیل سامانی (*Esmā'il-e Sāmāni*, ‘Ismail Samani’) (ruled 892–907), made بخارا (*Bokhārā*, ‘Bukhara’) his پایتخت (*pāytakht*, ‘capital’) and in 900 defeated the rival Saffarid ruler, bringing Khorasan under his control. His brick mausoleum in Bukhara, built in the early tenth century, is one of the oldest surviving monuments of Islamic Central Asia. In Tajikistan he is a national hero, and the country's currency is called the somoni.

### Rudaki, the father of Persian poetry

![Rudaki, by Hossein Behzad](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Miniature_of_Rudaki_by_Hossein_Behzad.jpg/960px-Miniature_of_Rudaki_by_Hossein_Behzad.jpg)
<sub>Rudaki, by Hossein Behzad. Photo: [Hossein Behzad](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Miniature_of_Rudaki_by_Hossein_Behzad.jpg), Public domain.</sub>

At the court of Nasr II (ruled 914–943) lived رودکی (*Rudaki*, ‘Rudaki’), born in the village of Panjrud near Samarkand. Iranians call him پدر شعر فارسی (*pedar-e she'r-e fārsi*, ‘the father of Persian poetry’). Tradition says he was blind; he was certainly a musician as well as a شاعر (*shā'er*, ‘poet’). Only a small part of his work survives.

His most famous poem comes with a story, told about two centuries later by the writer Nezami Aruzi. The amir stayed so long in Herat that his homesick courtiers asked Rudaki for help. He took his harp and sang of Bukhara: بوی جوی مولیان آید همی (*bu-ye ju-ye Muliyān āyad hami*, ‘the scent of the Muliyan stream is coming’), یاد یار مهربان آید همی (*yād-e yār-e mehrabān āyad hami*, ‘the memory of a kind friend is coming’). The amir, the story goes, jumped on his horse without even putting on his boots and rode home.

### Bukhara, a city of books

![A Samanid bowl with Kufic writing, 10th century](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/Bowl_with_Kufic_Inscription,_10th_century.jpg/960px-Bowl_with_Kufic_Inscription,_10th_century.jpg)
<sub>A Samanid bowl with Kufic writing, 10th century. Photo: [Samanid Period Ceramics](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bowl_with_Kufic_Inscription,_10th_century.jpg), No restrictions.</sub>

Under the Samanids Bukhara and Samarkand were among the great centres of learning in the Islamic world. Samarkand made paper, a technique that had arrived from China, and Samanid potters decorated their bowls with elegant Arabic writing.

The young ابن سینا (*Ebn-e Sinā*, ‘Avicenna’), born near Bukhara in 980, treated the Samanid ruler Nuh II when he was still a teenager, and as a reward was allowed into the royal کتابخانه (*ketāb-khāne*, ‘library’). He later described its many rooms, each given to one subject, with chests full of books – some of which, he said, he never saw anywhere again.

The Samanid court did more than enjoy poetry. Under Mansur I (ruled 961–976) the vizier Bal'ami made a Persian version of al-Tabari's great Arabic history, and scholars produced a Persian ترجمه (*tarjome*, ‘translation’) of a commentary on the Qur'an – among the oldest books of Persian prose that we have. The poet Daqiqi began to put the old Iranian kings' stories into verse, and Ferdowsi took up the work of the شاهنامه (*Shāhnāme*, ‘Shahnameh’) in the last years of Samanid rule.

The dynasty fell in 999, when Turkish Qarakhanids took Bukhara and the Ghaznavids took Khorasan. But the new Turkish rulers adopted the Samanids' Persian court culture, and that is why Persian became the language of literature and government from Anatolia to India for the next eight hundred years.

### Facts

- The Samanids governed from about 819 and lost Bukhara to the Qarakhanids in 999.
- Ismail Samani (ruled 892–907) made Bukhara the capital; his mausoleum there dates from the early tenth century.
- Tajikistan's currency, the somoni, is named after Ismail Samani.
- Rudaki (died c. 941) was born in Panjrud, a village in today's Tajikistan.
- The story of Rudaki's poem 'Bu-ye ju-ye Muliyan' is told in Nezami Aruzi's Chahar Maqala, written in the 12th century.
- Avicenna was born near Bukhara in 980 and studied in the Samanid royal library.
- The Persian version of al-Tabari's history, made by the vizier Bal'ami in the 960s, is one of the oldest surviving works of Persian prose.

### Say it in Persian

- سامانیان بیش از صد سال در خراسان و آسیای میانه حکومت کردند.  
  *Sāmāniyān bish az sad sāl dar Khorāsān va Āsiyā-ye miyāne hokumat kardand.* — The Samanids ruled Khorasan and Central Asia for more than a hundred years.
- پایتخت سامانیان شهر بخارا بود.  
  *pāytakht-e Sāmāniyān shahr-e Bokhārā bud.* — The capital of the Samanids was the city of Bukhara.
- در زمان سامانیان، فارسی دوباره زبان ادبیات شد.  
  *dar zamān-e Sāmāniyān, fārsi dobāre zabān-e adabiyyāt shod.* — In the time of the Samanids, Persian became a language of literature again.
- در دربار سامانیان، شاعران به فارسی شعر می‌گفتند.  
  *dar darbār-e Sāmāniyān, shā'erān be fārsi she'r mi-goftand.* — At the Samanid court, poets wrote poetry in Persian.
- رودکی را پدر شعر فارسی می‌نامند.  
  *Rudaki rā pedar-e she'r-e fārsi mi-nāmand.* — Rudaki is called the father of Persian poetry.
- رودکی در روستایی نزدیک سمرقند به دنیا آمد.  
  *Rudaki dar rustā-yi nazdik-e Samarghand be donyā āmad.* — Rudaki was born in a village near Samarkand.
- بیشتر شعرهای رودکی از بین رفته است.  
  *bishtar-e she'r-hā-ye Rudaki az beyn rafte ast.* — Most of Rudaki's poems have been lost.
- در زمان سامانیان، کتاب‌های مهمی از عربی به فارسی ترجمه شد.  
  *dar zamān-e Sāmāniyān, ketāb-hā-ye mohemm-i az 'arabi be fārsi tarjome shod.* — In the time of the Samanids, important books were translated from Arabic into Persian.
- بخارا یکی از مراکز بزرگ علم و دانش بود.  
  *Bokhārā yek-i az marākez-e bozorg-e 'elm va dānesh bud.* — Bukhara was one of the great centres of science and learning.
- ابن سینا در کتابخانه‌ی بزرگ بخارا کتاب می‌خواند.  
  *Ebn-e Sinā dar ketāb-khāne-ye bozorg-e Bokhārā ketāb mi-khānd.* — Avicenna used to read books in the great library of Bukhara.
- آرامگاه اسماعیل سامانی یکی از قدیمی‌ترین بناهای آجری آسیای میانه است.  
  *ārāmgāh-e Esmā'il-e Sāmāni yek-i az ghadimi-tarin banā-hā-ye ājori-ye Āsiyā-ye miyāne ast.* — The mausoleum of Ismail Samani is one of the oldest brick buildings in Central Asia.
- امروز تاجیک‌ها اسماعیل سامانی را قهرمان ملی می‌دانند.  
  *emruz tājik-hā Esmā'il-e Sāmāni rā ghahremān-e melli mi-dānand.* — Today Tajiks regard Ismail Samani as a national hero.
