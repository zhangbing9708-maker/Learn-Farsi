# Bread
## نان
*nān* — [read on the website with audio](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/culture/food-bread.html)

_Sangak baked on hot pebbles, ridged golden barbari, paper-thin lavash and soft taftun: bread is Iran's oldest staple, bought fresh every day from the neighbourhood bakery._

### Bread first

Long before rice, Iran lived on نان (*nān*, ‘bread’). The foothills of the Zagros mountains are among the places where wheat and barley were first farmed, more than ten thousand years ago, and bread is still eaten with almost every meal: with cheese and walnuts at breakfast, with kebab and dizi at lunch, wrapped round herbs at any time.

Bread is treated with respect. Iranians do not throw it away carelessly, and many people who see a piece on the ground pick it up and put it somewhere it will not be stepped on. To have shared نان و نمک (*nān o namak*, ‘bread and salt’) with someone is to be bound to them, and an ungrateful person is نمک‌نشناس (*namak-nashenās*, ‘'one who does not know the salt'’).

### Sangak

سنگک (*sangak*, ‘sangak’) – 'little stone' – is a long, chewy wholewheat flatbread baked on a bed of hot pebbles in a sloping oven. The baker stretches the dough on a long paddle, lays it on the stones and pulls it out a few minutes later, dimpled and blistered. Customers shake off any pebbles still stuck to it before they fold it, and a version sprinkled with sesame (کنجد (*konjed*, ‘sesame’)) costs a little more. A popular story credits the Safavid scholar Sheikh Baha'i with inventing sangak as bread for the army, though nobody can prove it.

### Barbari

![Barbari bread](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Nun_Barbar%C3%AD_fatto_con_farina_di_grano_del_Senatore_Cappelli.jpg/960px-Nun_Barbar%C3%AD_fatto_con_farina_di_grano_del_Senatore_Cappelli.jpg)
<sub>Barbari bread. Photo: [Marcuscalabresus](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nun_Barbar%C3%AD_fatto_con_farina_di_grano_del_Senatore_Cappelli.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

بربری (*barbari*, ‘barbari’) is thick, oval and golden, with long ridges pressed in by the baker's fingers and a glaze of flour paste that makes the crust shine; it is sprinkled with sesame or nigella seeds. It is named after the Barbari people – Hazaras from eastern Khorasan and Afghanistan – who are said to have brought it to Tehran in the Qajar era. Hot barbari with white cheese (پنیر (*panir*, ‘cheese’)), walnuts and sweet tea is the classic Tehran breakfast.

### Lavash

![Lavash bread](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/98/Lavash_Bread.jpg/960px-Lavash_Bread.jpg)
<sub>Lavash bread. Photo: [Shams948](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lavash_Bread.jpg), CC BY-SA 4.0.</sub>

لواش (*lavāsh*, ‘lavash’) is thin, soft and flat, baked in seconds on the wall of a clay oven (تنور (*tanur*, ‘clay oven’)) or on a hot metal plate. It dries out fast but keeps for weeks – sprinkle dry lavash with water and it softens again – which is why village families bake large batches at a time. Lavash is the bread for wrapping: kebab, or cheese, herbs and walnuts rolled into a لقمه (*loghme*, ‘mouthful; a little wrap’). In 2016 UNESCO added the flatbread culture of lavash to its intangible heritage list, in a joint file from Iran, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Turkey.

### Taftun and the bakery queue

![Taftan bread](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/ed/Taftan_nan_01.jpg)
<sub>Taftan bread. Photo: [theedinburghblog.co.uk](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Taftan_nan_01.jpg), CC BY 2.0.</sub>

تافتون (*tāftun*, ‘taftun’) is a round, soft bread, thicker than lavash and thinner than barbari, also slapped onto the wall of a clay oven.

Whatever the bread, most Iranians buy it fresh from the neighbourhood نانوایی (*nānvāyi*, ‘bakery’), and each bakery usually bakes just one kind – so a street may have a sangak bakery, a barbari bakery and a lavash bakery. Before breakfast, lunch and dinner a queue (صف (*saff*, ‘queue’)) forms outside. People ask for 'two sangaks' or 'five lavash', chat, watch the نانوا (*nānvā*, ‘baker’) at work and make sure no one jumps the line. Bread has long been subsidised in Iran and is cheap, and few people walk home without tearing off a corner of the hot loaf.

### Facts

- A traditional Iranian bakery usually bakes only one kind of bread, so neighbourhoods have separate sangak, barbari and lavash bakeries.
- In 2016 UNESCO inscribed 'Flatbread making and sharing culture: Lavash, Katyrma, Jupka, Yufka', a joint file of Azerbaijan, Iran, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Turkey.
- Sangak means 'little stone': the bread is baked on a bed of hot pebbles.
- Barbari is named after the Barbari (Hazara) people, who are said to have brought it to Tehran in the Qajar era.
- Namak-nashenās, 'not knowing the salt', is the Persian word for an ungrateful person.
- The Zagros foothills of western Iran are among the places where wheat and barley were first cultivated.

### Say it in Persian

- دو تا سنگک، لطفاً.  
  *do tā sangak, lotfan.* — Two sangaks, please.
- نان بربری را با پنیر و گردو می‌خوریم.  
  *nān-e barbari rā bā panir o gerdu mi-khorim.* — We eat barbari bread with cheese and walnuts.
- صف نانوایی امروز خیلی طولانی است.  
  *saff-e nānvāyi emruz kheyli tulāni ast.* — The bakery queue is very long today.
- ببخشید، آخر صف کجاست؟  
  *be-bakhshid, ākhar-e saff kojā-st?* — Excuse me, where's the end of the queue?
- سنگک را روی سنگ‌ریزه‌های داغ می‌پزند.  
  *sangak rā ru-ye sang-rize-hā-ye dāgh mi-pazand.* — Sangak is baked on hot pebbles.
- نان داغ را تا نکن، خمیر می‌شود.  
  *nān-e dāgh rā tā na-kon, khamir mi-shavad.* — Don't fold hot bread – it turns doughy.
- لواش خشک را با کمی آب نرم کن.  
  *lavāsh-e khoshk rā bā kami āb narm kon.* — Soften dry lavash with a little water.
- نانوایی سر کوچه‌ی ما بربری خیلی خوبی دارد.  
  *nānvāyi-ye sar-e kuche-ye mā barbari-ye kheyli khub-i dārad.* — The bakery at the end of our street has really good barbari.
- ما با هم نان و نمک خورده‌ایم.  
  *mā bā ham nān o namak khorde-im.* — We have shared bread and salt.  
  _That is: we are bound by friendship and loyalty._
- نان را دور نریز؛ نان برکت است.  
  *nān rā dur na-riz; nān barakat ast.* — Don't throw bread away – bread is a blessing.
- هر روز صبح نان تازه می‌خرم.  
  *har ruz sobh nān-e tāze mi-kharam.* — Every morning I buy fresh bread.
