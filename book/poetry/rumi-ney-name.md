# The Song of the Reed
## نی‌نامه
Rumi (مولوی), Masnavi, 13th century. [Listen on the website](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/poetry/rumi-ney-name.html)

Jalal al-Din Rumi (1207–1273), born in Balkh and settled in Konya in Anatolia, spent the last fifteen years of his life dictating the *Masnavi-ye Ma'navi* ('Spiritual Couplets') to his disciple Hosam al-Din Chalabi: six books and some 26,000 couplets of stories, teaching and jokes. Iranians call it simply the *Masnavi*, and its first eighteen couplets, the *Ney-nāme* or 'Song of the Reed', are the most famous lines in it. An old story says Rumi already had them written on a slip of paper in his turban when Hosam al-Din asked him for a book. The reed flute, the *ney*, was cut from its reed bed, and every note it plays is a lament for home; readers have always heard in it the soul, cut off from its source and longing to go back.

### The poem

> بشنو این نی چون شکایت می‌کند &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; از جدایی‌ها حکایت می‌کند  
> کز نیستان تا مرا ببریده‌اند &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; در نفیرم مرد و زن نالیده‌اند  
> سینه خواهم شرحه شرحه از فراق &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; تا بگویم شرح درد اشتیاق  
> هر کسی کو دور ماند از اصل خویش &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; باز جوید روزگار وصل خویش  
> من به هر جمعیتی نالان شدم &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; جفت بدحالان و خوش‌حالان شدم  
> هر کسی از ظن خود شد یار من &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; از درون من نجست اسرار من  
> سر من از ناله‌ی من دور نیست &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; لیک چشم و گوش را آن نور نیست  
> تن ز جان و جان ز تن مستور نیست &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; لیک کس را دید جان دستور نیست  
> آتش است این بانگ نای و نیست باد &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; هر که این آتش ندارد نیست باد  
> آتش عشق است کاندر نی فتاد &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; جوشش عشق است کاندر می فتاد  
> نی حریف هر که از یاری برید &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; پرده‌هایش پرده‌های ما درید  
> همچو نی زهری و تریاقی که دید &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; همچو نی دمساز و مشتاقی که دید  
> نی حدیث راه پر خون می‌کند &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; قصه‌های عشق مجنون می‌کند  
> محرم این هوش جز بی‌هوش نیست &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; مر زبان را مشتری جز گوش نیست  
> در غم ما روزها بیگاه شد &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; روزها با سوزها همراه شد  
> روزها گر رفت گو رو باک نیست &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; تو بمان ای آن که چون تو پاک نیست  
> هر که جز ماهی ز آبش سیر شد &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; هر که بی‌روزیست روزش دیر شد  
> در نیابد حال پخته هیچ خام &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; پس سخن کوتاه باید والسلام  

### Line by line

- بشنو این نی چون شکایت می‌کند / از جدایی‌ها حکایت می‌کند  
  *beshnow in ney chon shekāyat mi-konad / az jodāyi-hā hekāyat mi-konad* — Listen to this reed, how it complains, / how it tells the story of separations.  
  _The best-known form of this line, quoted everywhere and printed in many popular editions, is «بشنو از نی چون حکایت می‌کند / از جدایی‌ها شکایت می‌کند» *beshnow az ney chon hekāyat mi-konad / az jodāyi-hā shekāyat mi-konad* – 'listen to the reed, how it tells its tale, / how it complains of separations'. Nicholson's edition, following the oldest manuscripts, has «بشنو این نی چون شکایت می‌کند», with 'complaint' and 'tale' the other way round._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  بشنو *beshnow* listen! – imperative of شنیدن (today usually said beshno) · نی *ney* reed; the reed flute · چون *chon* how (here); also 'when, like, because' · شکایت کردن *shekāyat kardan* to complain, to lament · جدایی‌ها *jodāyi-hā* separations, partings · حکایت کردن *hekāyat kardan* to tell (a story)

  </details>
- کز نیستان تا مرا ببریده‌اند / در نفیرم مرد و زن نالیده‌اند  
  *kaz neyestān tā marā bobride-and / dar nafir-am mard o zan nālide-and* — 'Ever since they cut me from the reed bed, / men and women have wept at my cry.'  
  _Some editions read «از نفیرم» *az nafir-am* 'at my cry'. From this couplet on, the reed itself is speaking._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  کز *kaz* that from – که + از; here 'since' · نیستان *neyestān* reed bed (نی + ـستان 'place of') · تا *tā* ever since (here) · ببریده‌اند *bobride-and* they have cut (old ب- prefix + بریدن) · نفیر *nafir* cry, wail; the blast of a horn · نالیدن *nālidan* to moan, to lament

  </details>
- سینه خواهم شرحه شرحه از فراق / تا بگویم شرح درد اشتیاق  
  *sine khāham sharhe sharhe az ferāgh / tā beguyam sharh-e dard-e eshtiyāgh* — 'I want a breast torn to shreds by parting, / so that I can tell the pain of longing in full.'  
  Literally: a breast I want, strip by strip from separation, so that I tell the explanation of the pain of longing  
  _A play on two words that look alike: شرحه *sharhe* 'a strip of flesh' and شرح *sharh* 'a full account'._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  سینه *sine* breast, chest · خواهم *khāham* I want – no می‌ in the classical present · شرحه شرحه *sharhe sharhe* torn in strips, in shreds · فراق *ferāgh* separation (from the beloved) · شرح *sharh* explanation, a full account · اشتیاق *eshtiyāgh* longing, yearning

  </details>
- هر کسی کو دور ماند از اصل خویش / باز جوید روزگار وصل خویش  
  *har kasi ku dur mānd az asl-e khish / bāz juyad ruzgār-e vasl-e khish* — 'Whoever is left far from their origin / longs to find again the days of union.'
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  کو *ku* who – که + او · ماند *mānd* remained, was left (past of ماندن) · اصل *asl* root, origin · خویش *khish* one's own – the same as خود · باز جستن *bāz jostan* to seek again · روزگار وصل *ruzgār-e vasl* the time of union

  </details>
- من به هر جمعیتی نالان شدم / جفت بدحالان و خوش‌حالان شدم  
  *man be har jam'iyyati nālān shodam / joft-e bad-hālān o khosh-hālān shodam* — 'I have wept in every company; / I have kept company with the unhappy and the happy.'
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  جمعیت *jam'iyyat* gathering, a company of people (today 'population') · نالان *nālān* moaning, lamenting · جفت *joft* pair, companion · بدحال *bad-hāl* in a bad way, unhappy · خوش‌حال *khosh-hāl* happy

  </details>
- هر کسی از ظن خود شد یار من / از درون من نجست اسرار من  
  *har kasi az zann-e khod shod yār-e man / az darun-e man najost asrār-e man* — 'Everyone became my friend through their own guesswork; / no one looked for the secrets within me.'
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  ظن *zann* guess, opinion (Arabic) · یار *yār* friend, companion · درون *darun* inside, the inner self · نجست *najost* did not seek (past of جستن) · اسرار *asrār* secrets (Arabic plural of سر)

  </details>
- سر من از ناله‌ی من دور نیست / لیک چشم و گوش را آن نور نیست  
  *serr-e man az nāle-ye man dur nist / lik cheshm o gush rā ān nur nist* — 'My secret is not far from my lament, / but eye and ear do not have the light to find it.'
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  سر *serr* secret – not سر sar 'head' · ناله *nāle* moan, lament · لیک *lik* but – short for لیکن · چشم و گوش *cheshm o gush* eye and ear · نور *nur* light

  </details>
- تن ز جان و جان ز تن مستور نیست / لیک کس را دید جان دستور نیست  
  *tan ze jān o jān ze tan mastur nist / lik kas rā did-e jān dastur nist* — 'Body is not hidden from soul, nor soul from body, / yet no one is allowed to see the soul.'
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  تن *tan* body · جان *jān* soul, life · ز *ze* from – the poetic short form of از · مستور *mastur* hidden, veiled · دید *did* seeing, sight – the past stem used as a noun · دستور *dastur* permission (the old meaning; today 'instruction, grammar')

  </details>
- آتش است این بانگ نای و نیست باد / هر که این آتش ندارد نیست باد  
  *ātash ast in bāng-e nāy o nist bād / har ke in ātash nadārad nist bād* — 'This cry of the reed is fire, not wind; / whoever does not have this fire – may he be nothing!'  
  _Both halves end in the same two words, نیست باد, with two meanings: 'is not wind' and 'may he not be'. A rhyme on one word with two meanings is called جناس *jenās*._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  بانگ *bāng* cry, call, sound · نای *nāy* reed flute – another form of نی · باد *bād* wind, breath; also 'may it be' · نیست باد *nist bād* may he be nothing, may he perish

  </details>
- آتش عشق است کاندر نی فتاد / جوشش عشق است کاندر می فتاد  
  *ātash-e 'eshgh ast kandar ney fetād / jushesh-e 'eshgh ast kandar mey fetād* — 'It is the fire of love that has fallen into the reed; / it is the ferment of love that has fallen into the wine.'
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  کاندر *kandar* that into – که + اندر · اندر *andar* in, into – the old form of در · فتاد *fetād* fell – short for افتاد · جوشش *jushesh* boiling, fermenting (from جوشیدن) · می *mey* wine

  </details>
- نی حریف هر که از یاری برید / پرده‌هایش پرده‌های ما درید  
  *ney harif-e har ke az yāri borid / parde-hā-yash parde-hā-ye mā darid* — 'The reed is the companion of all who are cut off from a friend; / its melodies have torn away our veils.'  
  _پرده is both a curtain or veil and a note or mode in music: the reed's tunes tear away the veils we hide behind._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  حریف *harif* companion, fellow (today also 'rival, opponent') · یاری *yāri* a friend (یار + ـی) · بریدن از *boridan az* to be cut off from, to break with · پرده *parde* curtain, veil; a mode or note in music · دریدن *daridan* to tear

  </details>
- همچو نی زهری و تریاقی که دید / همچو نی دمساز و مشتاقی که دید  
  *hamcho ney zahri o taryāghi ke did / hamcho ney damsāz o moshtāghi ke did* — 'Who ever saw a poison and an antidote like the reed? / Who ever saw a friend and a lover like the reed?'
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  همچو *hamcho* like, just like · زهر *zahr* poison · تریاق *taryāgh* antidote (from Greek *thēriakē*) · که دید *ke did* who has seen? – a question expecting 'no one' · دمساز *damsāz* close friend, confidant – one who breathes in tune · مشتاق *moshtāgh* longing, a lover

  </details>
- نی حدیث راه پر خون می‌کند / قصه‌های عشق مجنون می‌کند  
  *ney hadis-e rāh-e por khun mi-konad / ghesse-hā-ye 'eshgh-e majnun mi-konad* — 'The reed tells of the road soaked in blood; / it tells the tales of Majnun's love.'  
  _Majnun, 'the possessed', is the madman of love whose story Nezami told in *Leyli and Majnun*._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  حدیث *hadis* talk, account, story · حدیث کردن *hadis kardan* to tell, to speak of · پر خون *por khun* full of blood · قصه *ghesse* story, tale · مجنون *majnun* mad, possessed – the lover of Leyli

  </details>
- محرم این هوش جز بی‌هوش نیست / مر زبان را مشتری جز گوش نیست  
  *mahram-e in hush joz bi-hush nist / mar zabān rā moshtari joz gush nist* — 'Only the senseless are let into this sense, / just as the tongue has no buyer but the ear.'  
  Literally: the confidant of this mind is none but the mindless; for the tongue there is no buyer but the ear
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  محرم *mahram* one allowed inside, a confidant · هوش *hush* mind, understanding, awareness · بی‌هوش *bi-hush* unconscious, senseless – here 'out of oneself' · جز *joz* except, but · مر … را *mar … rā* an old way of marking 'for, to' or the object; مر adds no meaning · مشتری *moshtari* buyer, customer

  </details>
- در غم ما روزها بیگاه شد / روزها با سوزها همراه شد  
  *dar gham-e mā ruz-hā bigāh shod / ruz-hā bā suz-hā hamrāh shod* — 'In our grief the days have grown late; / the days have gone hand in hand with burning.'  
  _The verbs are singular (شد) after the plural روزها: in classical Persian, things that are not people often take a singular verb._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  غم *gham* grief, sorrow · روزها *ruz-hā* days · بیگاه *bigāh* late, untimely (بی + گاه 'time') · سوز *suz* burning, heartache · همراه *hamrāh* together with, companion

  </details>
- روزها گر رفت گو رو باک نیست / تو بمان ای آن که چون تو پاک نیست  
  *ruz-hā gar raft gu row bāk nist / to bemān ey ān ke chon to pāk nist* — 'If the days have gone, say: let them go, it does not matter – / only you remain, O you, the purest of all.'  
  Literally: the days, if they went, say 'go', there is no fear; you stay, O that one like whom none is pure
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  گر *gar* if – short for اگر · گو *gu* say! – imperative of گفتن (today بگو) · رو *row* go! – imperative of رفتن (today برو) · باک *bāk* fear, worry; باک نیست 'no matter' · بمان *bemān* stay! remain! · پاک *pāk* pure, clean

  </details>
- هر که جز ماهی ز آبش سیر شد / هر که بی‌روزیست روزش دیر شد  
  *har ke joz māhi ze āb-ash sir shod / har ke bi-ruzist ruz-ash dir shod* — 'All but the fish have had their fill of water; / whoever lacks his daily bread finds the day long.'  
  _روز 'day' and روزی 'daily bread' are another play on words. The fish is the true lover: it never has enough of the water it lives in._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  ماهی *māhi* fish · ز آبش *ze āb-ash* of its water, of the water · سیر شدن *sir shodan* to be full, to have had enough · روزی *ruzi* daily bread, one's portion (from روز 'day') · بی‌روزیست *bi-ruzist* is without his daily bread – بی‌روزی است · دیر *dir* late, long

  </details>
- در نیابد حال پخته هیچ خام / پس سخن کوتاه باید والسلام  
  *dar nayābad hāl-e pokhte hich khām / pas sokhan kutāh bāyad vas-salām* — 'No one raw can grasp the state of the ripe, / so the talk must be short – and that is all.'
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  در یافتن *dar yāftan* to grasp, to understand – here split: در نیابد 'does not grasp' · پخته *pokhte* cooked, ripe – mature in experience · خام *khām* raw, unripe – inexperienced · هیچ *hich* any; with a negative verb, 'no one' · کوتاه *kutāh* short · والسلام *vas-salām* 'and peace' (Arabic) – and that's that, end of story

  </details>

### Reading the poem

**The image.** The *ney*, the end-blown reed flute of Persian music, is cut from a reed bed, hollowed out and pierced, and it only sings when someone breathes into it. Rumi makes it the voice of every soul cut off from where it came from: its music is homesickness, and only those who have lost something hear what it is really saying. Notice how the speaker changes – Rumi asks us to listen in the first couplet, and from the second on the reed talks itself. Notice too the paradoxes: the reed is poison and cure; its song is fire, not wind; its 'mind' is open only to those who are out of their minds. In the last couplet Rumi breaks off: the raw cannot understand the cooked, so the talk must be short.

**The rhythm.** The whole *Masnavi* is in *ramal*, the metre of Attar's *Conference of the Birds*: three beats to the half-line, *DUM-da-DUM-DUM DUM-da-DUM-DUM DUM-da-DUM*. Read the first half-line slowly: *besh-na-vin ney chon she-kā-yat mi-ko-nad*. In a masnavi each couplet has its own rhyme – شکایت (*shekāyat*) / حکایت (*hekāyat*), فراق (*ferāgh*) / اشتیاق (*eshtiyāgh*) – which is why the form can run on for 26,000 couplets. Many couplets also repeat a word after the rhyme, as می‌کند (*mi-konad*), شدم (*shodam*) and نیست (*nist*) do here.

**The grammar.** The language is easier than it looks. Watch for the run-together forms کز (*kaz*) (که + از), کو (*ku*) (که + او) and کاندر (*kandar*) (که + اندر), for فتاد (*fetād*) instead of افتاد (*oftād*) and لیک (*lik*) instead of لیکن (*liken*), and for imperatives without ب (*be-*): گو (*gu*) 'say', رو (*row*) 'go'. The old word مر (*mar*) in مر زبان را (*mar zabān rā*) only strengthens را (*rā*). Couplet 4 in modern prose: هر کسی که از اصل خودش دور بماند، دوباره دنبال روزگار وصل خودش می‌گردد (*har kasi ke az asl-e khod-ash dur bemānad, dobāre donbāl-e ruzgār-e vasl-e khod-ash mi-gardad*).

**Using it.** Recitals of the *Masnavi* begin with these lines, and singers and ney players have set them again and again. بشنو از نی (*beshnow az ney*), in the popular reading, is a phrase in its own right. People quote هر کسی از ظن خود شد یار من (*har kasi az zann-e khod shod yār-e man*) about friends who only see in us what they want to see, and پس سخن کوتاه باید والسلام (*pas sokhan kutāh bāyad vas-salām*) to cut a long argument short.
