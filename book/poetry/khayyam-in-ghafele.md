# This caravan of life
## این قافله‌ی عمر
Khayyam (خیام), Rubaiyat, 11th century. [Listen on the website](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/poetry/khayyam-in-ghafele.html)

Omar Khayyam (1048–1131) of Nishapur was famous in his lifetime as a mathematician and astronomer: he wrote a classic book on algebra and helped to reform the Persian calendar. The short four-line poems (*robā'i*) that made him world-famous were gathered only long after his death, and nobody knows how many of them are really his. Whoever wrote them, they speak with one voice: life is short, nobody knows what comes after it, so drink, love and do not waste today worrying about tomorrow. Here are six quatrains on time and the moment, beginning with life as a caravan passing in the night.

### The poem

> این قافله‌ی عمر عجب می‌گذرد &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; دریاب دمی که با طرب می‌گذرد  
> ساقی غم فردای حریفان چه خوری &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; پیش آر پیاله را که شب می‌گذرد  
> از دی که گذشت هیچ از او یاد مکن &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; فردا که نیامده‌ست فریاد مکن  
> بر نامده و گذشته بنیاد مکن &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; حالی خوش باش و عمر بر باد مکن  
> این یک دو سه روز نوبت عمر گذشت &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; چون آب به جویبار و چون باد به دشت  
> هرگز غم دو روز مرا یاد نگشت &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; روزی که نیامده‌ست و روزی که گذشت  
> چون عهده نمی‌شود کسی فردا را &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; حالی خوش دار این دل پرسودا را  
> می نوش به ماهتاب ای ماه که ماه &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; بسیار بتابد و نیابد ما را  
> ای دوست بیا تا غم فردا نخوریم &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; وین یک دم عمر را غنیمت شمریم  
> فردا که از این دیر فنا درگذریم &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; با هفت‌هزارسالگان سربه‌سریم  
> برخیز و مخور غم جهان گذران &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; بنشین و دمی به شادمانی گذران  
> در طبع جهان اگر وفایی بودی &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; نوبت به تو خود نیامدی از دگران  

### Line by line

- این قافله‌ی عمر عجب می‌گذرد / دریاب دمی که با طرب می‌گذرد  
  *in ghāfele-ye 'omr 'ajab mi-gozarad / daryāb dami ke bā tarab mi-gozarad* — How strangely fast this caravan of life goes by! / Catch the moment that passes in joy.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  قافله *ghāfele* caravan · عمر *'omr* life, lifetime · عجب *'ajab* strangely, amazingly – here an adverb · دریاب *daryāb* catch!, seize! – imperative of دریافتن · دم *dam* breath; moment · طرب *tarab* joy, merriment

  </details>
- ساقی غم فردای حریفان چه خوری / پیش آر پیاله را که شب می‌گذرد  
  *sāghi gham-e fardā-ye harifān che khori / pish ār piyāle rā ke shab mi-gozarad* — Cupbearer, why worry about your friends' tomorrow? / Bring the cup, for the night is passing.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  ساقی *sāghi* cupbearer, the one who pours the wine · غم خوردن *gham khordan* to worry, to grieve (lit. 'to eat sorrow') · فردا *fardā* tomorrow · حریفان *harifān* companions, drinking friends · چه خوری *che khori* why do you eat? – here 'why worry?' · پیش آر *pish ār* bring forward! – آر = بیاور · پیاله *piyāle* cup, wine bowl

  </details>
- از دی که گذشت هیچ از او یاد مکن / فردا که نیامده‌ست فریاد مکن  
  *az di ke gozasht hich az u yād makon / fardā ke nayāmade-st faryād makon* — Do not think at all of yesterday, which is gone; / do not cry out over tomorrow, which has not come.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  دی *di* yesterday – an old word (today دیروز) · گذشت *gozasht* passed, has gone · یاد کردن *yād kardan* to remember, to call to mind · مکن *makon* do not! – the old negative imperative (today نکن) · نیامده‌ست *nayāmade-st* has not come – نیامده است · فریاد کردن *faryād kardan* to cry out, to complain

  </details>
- بر نامده و گذشته بنیاد مکن / حالی خوش باش و عمر بر باد مکن  
  *bar nāmade vo gozashte bonyād makon / hāli khosh bāsh o 'omr bar bād makon* — Build nothing on what has not come or what is gone; / be happy now, and do not throw your life to the wind.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  نامده *nāmade* not yet come – نیامده · گذشته *gozashte* past, gone · بنیاد کردن بر *bonyād kardan bar* to build on, to rely on · حالی *hāli* now, for the moment · خوش باش *khosh bāsh* be happy, enjoy yourself · بر باد کردن *bar bād kardan* to throw to the wind, to waste

  </details>
- این یک دو سه روز نوبت عمر گذشت / چون آب به جویبار و چون باد به دشت  
  *in yek do se ruz nowbat-e 'omr gozasht / chon āb be juybār o chon bād be dasht* — Our turn at life, these two or three days, has gone by / like water down a stream, like wind across the plain.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  یک دو سه روز *yek do se ruz* a few days (lit. 'one, two, three days') · نوبت *nowbat* turn, spell · چون *chon* like · جویبار *juybār* stream, brook · دشت *dasht* plain, open country

  </details>
- هرگز غم دو روز مرا یاد نگشت / روزی که نیامده‌ست و روزی که گذشت  
  *hargez gham-e do ruz marā yād nagasht / ruzi ke nayāmade-st o ruzi ke gozasht* — Two days have never troubled me: / the day that has not come, and the day that has gone.  
  Literally: never did the worry of two days come to my mind: the day that has not come and the day that passed
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  هرگز *hargez* never · غم *gham* worry, grief · مرا *marā* to me, me – من را · یاد گشتن *yād gashtan* to come to mind (old; گشتن = شدن) · روزی که *ruzi ke* the day that

  </details>
- چون عهده نمی‌شود کسی فردا را / حالی خوش دار این دل پرسودا را  
  *chon 'ohde nemi-shavad kasi fardā rā / hāli khosh dār in del-e por-sowdā rā* — Since no one can vouch for tomorrow, / keep this anxious heart happy now.  
  _Another common reading of the second half is «حالی خوش کن تو این دل شیدا را» 'make this love-crazed heart happy now'._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  چون *chon* since, because · عهده شدن *'ohde shodan* to take on, to guarantee (old) · حالی *hāli* now, for the moment · خوش داشتن *khosh dāshtan* to keep happy · پرسودا *por-sowdā* full of passion, anxious (سودا 'melancholy; passion')

  </details>
- می نوش به ماهتاب ای ماه که ماه / بسیار بتابد و نیابد ما را  
  *mey nush be māhtāb ey māh ke māh / besyār betābad o nayābad mā rā* — Drink wine by moonlight, my moon, for the moon / will shine again and again and not find us here.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  می نوش *mey nush* drink wine! – نوشیدن 'to drink' · ماهتاب *māhtāb* moonlight · ای ماه *ey māh* O moon! – my beautiful one · بسیار *besyār* many times, a great deal · بتابد *betābad* will shine (تابیدن) · نیابد *nayābad* will not find (یافتن)

  </details>
- ای دوست بیا تا غم فردا نخوریم / وین یک دم عمر را غنیمت شمریم  
  *ey dust biyā tā gham-e fardā nakhorim / vin yek dam-e 'omr rā ghanimat shomarim* — Come, friend, let us not worry about tomorrow, / and count this one breath of life as a gift.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  بیا تا *biyā tā* come, let us… (lit. 'come so that') · نخوریم *nakhorim* let us not eat – غم نخوریم 'let us not worry' · وین *vin* and this – و + این · دم *dam* breath, moment · غنیمت شمردن *ghanimat shomordan* to count as a windfall, to make the most of

  </details>
- فردا که از این دیر فنا درگذریم / با هفت‌هزارسالگان سربه‌سریم  
  *fardā ke az in deyr-e fanā dar-gozarim / bā haft-hezār-sālegān sar-be-sar-im* — Tomorrow, when we leave this house of passing things, / we shall be equal with those dead these seven thousand years.  
  _Many people say «دیر کهن» *deyr-e kohan* 'this old house'. The 'seven-thousand-year-olds' are all who have died since the creation, which was thought to have been about seven thousand years ago._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  دیر *deyr* monastery, cloister – here the world · فنا *fanā* passing away, mortality · درگذشتن *dar-gozashtan* to pass away; to pass beyond · هفت‌هزارسالگان *haft-hezār-sālegān* the seven-thousand-year-olds – the long dead · سربه‌سر *sar-be-sar* equal, even, quits

  </details>
- برخیز و مخور غم جهان گذران / بنشین و دمی به شادمانی گذران  
  *barkhiz o makhor gham-e jahān-e gozarān / benshin o dami be shādmāni gozarān* — Get up, and do not grieve for this passing world; / sit down, and spend a moment in happiness.  
  _The rhyme word is the same, گذران *gozarān*, but it means 'passing' in the first half and 'spend!' in the second – a play on one word with two meanings._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  برخیز *barkhiz* get up! (برخاستن) · مخور *makhor* do not eat – غم مخور 'do not grieve' · گذران *gozarān* passing, fleeting (adjective) · بنشین *benshin* sit down! (نشستن) · شادمانی *shādmāni* happiness, joy · گذراندن *gozarāndan* to spend (time); گذران 'spend!'

  </details>
- در طبع جهان اگر وفایی بودی / نوبت به تو خود نیامدی از دگران  
  *dar tab'-e jahān agar vafāyi budi / nowbat be to khod nayāmadi az degarān* — If there were any faithfulness in the nature of the world, / your turn would never have come after the others'.  
  _بودی and نیامدی end in the old ـی of the unreal condition: 'if there were…, it would not have come'._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  طبع *tab'* nature, temperament · وفا *vafā* faithfulness, loyalty · بودی *budi* there would be – old unreal ـی · نوبت *nowbat* turn · خود *khod* at all, even – for emphasis · نیامدی *nayāmadi* would not have come · دگران *degarān* others – دیگران

  </details>

### Reading the poem

**The ideas.** Each quatrain is a tiny argument with the same conclusion. Yesterday is gone and tomorrow has not come; the world is faithful to no one – if it were, it would still belong to the people before us; so the only time we own is now, this دم (*dam*), this breath. The images are plain and physical: a caravan moving through the night, water running down a stream, wind over the plain, a moon that will rise again over our empty place. The wine, the cupbearer and the friends are the furniture of a good evening – and a challenge to the preachers who promise their rewards for tomorrow.

**The rhythm.** A *robā'i* has four half-lines. Lines 1, 2 and 4 rhyme and the third usually does not, so the last line lands like a punch line. Often the same word follows the rhyme each time – عجب (*'ajab*) / طرب (*tarab*) / شب (*shab*) + می‌گذرد (*mi-gozarad*) – which Persian calls a ردیف (*radif*). The metre has a heavy, swinging beat that poets compare to the Arabic phrase *lā howla va lā ghovvata ellā be-llāh*; listen to *in ghā-fe-le-ye 'om-r 'a-jab mi-go-za-rad*.

**The grammar.** The verbs show the poems' age. Negative commands begin with م (*ma-*), not ن (*na-*): مکن (*makon*) 'don't do', مخور (*makhor*) 'don't eat'. دی (*di*) is 'yesterday' and حالی (*hāli*) 'now'. In the last quatrain بودی (*budi*) and نیامدی (*nayāmadi*) are not 'you were' and 'you did not come' but the old forms of the unreal condition. Its last two lines in modern prose: اگر در طبیعت جهان وفایی بود، نوبت از دیگران به تو نمی‌رسید (*agar dar tabi'at-e jahān vafāyi bud, nowbat az digarān be to nemi-resid*). And غم خوردن (*gham khordan*), 'to eat sorrow', is still the everyday Persian for 'to worry': غم نخور! (*gham nakhor!*) 'don't worry!'

**Using it.** دریاب دمی که با طرب می‌گذرد (*daryāb dami ke bā tarab mi-gozarad*) and حالی خوش باش (*hāli khosh bāsh*) are Iran's *carpe diem*. People quote از دی که گذشت هیچ از او یاد مکن (*az di ke gozasht hich az u yād makon*) to someone stuck on a past mistake, and غنیمت شمردن (*ghanimat shomordan*) 'to count as a windfall' is the everyday way to say 'make the most of it': این فرصت را غنیمت بشمار (*in forsat rā ghanimat beshomār*).
