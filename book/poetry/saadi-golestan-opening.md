# Thanks be to God
## منت خدای را
Saadi (سعدی), Golestan, 13th century. [Listen on the website](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/poetry/saadi-golestan-opening.html)

Saadi of Shiraz (about 1210–1291) spent many years travelling before he came home and, in 1258, wrote the *Golestan* ('The Rose Garden'): eight chapters of short stories and sayings in prose, mixed with verse. Its Persian is so clear and polished that for centuries it was the book children learned to read and write from, from Iran to India and Anatolia, and Iranians still quote it more than any other prose. It opens, like most Persian books, with praise of God – but in Saadi's hands the praise becomes a poem in prose, built on rhyming pairs. Here are its first lines: the breath we draw, God's patience with sinners, spring dressing the trees, and the clouds, wind, moon, sun and sky all at work for us.

### The poem

> منت خدای را عز و جل که طاعتش موجب قربت است و به شکر اندرش مزید نعمت.  
> هر نفسی که فرو می‌رود ممد حیات است و چون برمی‌آید مفرح ذات.  
> پس در هر نفسی دو نعمت موجود است و بر هر نعمتی شکری واجب.  
> از دست و زبان که برآید &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; کز عهده‌ی شکرش به درآید  
> بنده همان به که ز تقصیر خویش &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; عذر به درگاه خدای آورد  
> ورنه سزاوار خداوندیش &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; کس نتواند که به جای آورد  
> باران رحمت بی‌حسابش همه را رسیده و خوان نعمت بی‌دریغش همه جا کشیده.  
> پرده‌ی ناموس بندگان به گناه فاحش ندرد و وظیفه‌ی روزی به خطای منکر نبرد.  
> ای کریمی که از خزانه‌ی غیب &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; گبر و ترسا وظیفه‌خور داری  
> دوستان را کجا کنی محروم &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; تو که با دشمن این نظر داری  
> فراش باد صبا را گفته تا فرش زمردین بگسترد و دایه‌ی ابر بهاری را فرموده تا بنات نبات در مهد زمین بپرورد.  
> درختان را به خلعت نوروزی قبای سبز ورق در بر گرفته و اطفال شاخ را به قدوم موسم ربیع کلاه شکوفه بر سر نهاده.  
> عصاره‌ی تاکی به قدرت او شهد فایق شده و تخم خرمایی به تربیتش نخل باسق گشته.  
> ابر و باد و مه و خورشید و فلک در کارند &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; تا تو نانی به کف آری و به غفلت نخوری  

### Line by line

- منت خدای را عز و جل که طاعتش موجب قربت است و به شکر اندرش مزید نعمت.  
  *mennat khodāy rā 'azza va jall ke tā'at-ash mujeb-e ghorbat ast va be shokr andar-ash mazid-e ne'mat.* — Thanks be to God, mighty and glorious: obeying him brings us near to him, and thanking him brings more blessings.  
  Literally: gratitude to God, mighty and majestic, whose obedience is the cause of nearness, and in thanks to whom is the increase of blessing  
  _Saadi's prose rhymes: قربت *ghorbat* with نعمت *ne'mat*. The second half has no verb – است is understood from the first._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  منت *mennat* gratitude, a debt of thanks (today also 'a favour held over you') · عز و جل *'azza va jall* mighty and glorious is He (Arabic) · طاعت *tā'at* obedience, worship · موجب *mujeb* cause, reason · قربت *ghorbat* nearness (to God) · به شکر اندرش *be shokr andar-ash* in thanking him – به … اندر is an old double preposition 'in' · مزید *mazid* increase

  </details>
- هر نفسی که فرو می‌رود ممد حیات است و چون برمی‌آید مفرح ذات.  
  *har nafasi ke foru mi-ravad momedd-e hayāt ast va chon bar-mi-āyad mofarreh-e zāt.* — Every breath that goes in sustains life, and when it comes out it gladdens the soul.  
  _Again the halves rhyme: حیات *hayāt* / ذات *zāt*._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  نفس *nafas* breath · فرو رفتن *foru raftan* to go down, to go in · ممد *momedd* helping, sustaining (Arabic) · حیات *hayāt* life · برآمدن *bar āmadan* to come out, to rise · مفرح *mofarreh* gladdening, refreshing (Arabic) · ذات *zāt* essence, self

  </details>
- پس در هر نفسی دو نعمت موجود است و بر هر نعمتی شکری واجب.  
  *pas dar har nafasi do ne'mat mowjud ast va bar har ne'mati shokri vājeb.* — So in every breath there are two blessings, and for every blessing a thanks is due.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  پس *pas* so, therefore · نعمت *ne'mat* blessing, gift · موجود *mowjud* present, existing · شکری *shokri* a thanks, an act of thanks · بر … واجب *bar … vājeb* due from, a duty for

  </details>
- از دست و زبان که برآید / کز عهده‌ی شکرش به درآید  
  *az dast o zabān ke bar-āyad / kaz 'ohde-ye shokr-ash be dar-āyad* — Whose hand or tongue is able / to give him all the thanks he is owed?  
  Literally: from whose hand and tongue does it come, that he should come out of the duty of thanking him?  
  _After this couplet Saadi quotes the Quran (34:13) in Arabic: «اعملوا آل داود شکراً و قلیل من عبادی الشکور» 'Give thanks, O family of David – and few of my servants are thankful.' It is left out here._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  که *ke* who? – here a question word, = چه کسی · از دست کسی برآمدن *az dast-e kasi bar āmadan* to be within someone's power · کز *kaz* that from – که + از · عهده *'ohde* duty, responsibility · از عهده‌ی … به درآمدن *az 'ohde-ye … be dar āmadan* to be equal to (a task), to manage

  </details>
- بنده همان به که ز تقصیر خویش / عذر به درگاه خدای آورد  
  *bande hamān beh ke ze taghsir-e khish / 'ozr be dargāh-e khodāy āvarad* — The best a servant can do is to bring / his apology for his failings to God's door;
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  بنده *bande* servant, slave – a human being before God · همان به *hamān beh* it is best that – به 'better' · تقصیر *taghsir* fault, shortcoming · خویش *khish* his own · عذر آوردن *'ozr āvardan* to apologise, to bring an excuse · درگاه *dargāh* threshold, court

  </details>
- ورنه سزاوار خداوندیش / کس نتواند که به جای آورد  
  *varna sezāvār-e khodāvandi-yash / kas natavānad ke be jāy āvarad* — otherwise, no one could ever give / the worship that his lordship deserves.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  ورنه *varna* or else, otherwise – و + اگر + نه · سزاوار *sezāvār* worthy of, fitting for · خداوندی *khodāvandi* lordship, divinity · نتواند *natavānad* cannot · به جای آوردن *be jāy āvardan* to perform, to carry out

  </details>
- باران رحمت بی‌حسابش همه را رسیده و خوان نعمت بی‌دریغش همه جا کشیده.  
  *bārān-e rahmat-e bi-hesāb-ash hame rā reside va khān-e ne'mat-e bi-darigh-ash hame jā keshide.* — The rain of his boundless mercy has reached everyone, and the table of his unstinting bounty is spread everywhere.  
  _Two matching halves: باران رحمت / خوان نعمت, بی‌حسابش / بی‌دریغش, همه را / همه جا, رسیده / کشیده. The است of both verbs is left out._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  رحمت *rahmat* mercy · بی‌حساب *bi-hesāb* beyond counting, boundless · رسیده *reside* has reached – رسیده است · خوان *khān* a laid table, a spread (the و is silent) · بی‌دریغ *bi-darigh* unstinting, generous · کشیده *keshide* spread out – کشیده است

  </details>
- پرده‌ی ناموس بندگان به گناه فاحش ندرد و وظیفه‌ی روزی به خطای منکر نبرد.  
  *parde-ye nāmus-e bandegān be gonāh-e fāhesh nadarad va vazife-ye ruzi be khatā-ye monkar naborad.* — He does not tear away the veil of his servants' honour for a shameful sin, nor cut off their daily bread for a wicked mistake.  
  _وظیفه meant a ration or allowance; its modern meaning, 'duty', grew out of that._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  پرده دریدن *parde daridan* to tear the veil – to expose, to shame · ناموس *nāmus* honour, reputation · فاحش *fāhesh* gross, shameful · وظیفه *vazife* allowance, ration (today 'duty') · روزی *ruzi* daily bread · منکر *monkar* wicked, disapproved · بریدن *boridan* to cut, to cut off

  </details>
- ای کریمی که از خزانه‌ی غیب / گبر و ترسا وظیفه‌خور داری  
  *ey karimi ke az khazāne-ye gheyb / gabr o tarsā vazife-khor dāri* — O generous one, who from the treasury of the unseen / feed even Zoroastrians and Christians,
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  کریم *karim* generous; the Generous, a name of God · خزانه *khazāne* treasury · غیب *gheyb* the unseen, the hidden world · گبر *gabr* Zoroastrian (an old, slighting word) · ترسا *tarsā* Christian (an old word) · وظیفه‌خور *vazife-khor* one who lives on an allowance, a pensioner

  </details>
- دوستان را کجا کنی محروم / تو که با دشمن این نظر داری  
  *dustān rā kojā koni mahrum / to ke bā doshman in nazar dāri* — how could you leave your friends with nothing, / you who show such kindness to your enemies?  
  _Saadi makes God's care for unbelievers the proof of his care for believers. These two couplets are among the most quoted in the book._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  دوستان *dustān* friends · کجا *kojā* where?; here 'how could…?' · محروم کردن *mahrum kardan* to deprive, to leave without · دشمن *doshman* enemy · نظر *nazar* a kindly look, favour

  </details>
- فراش باد صبا را گفته تا فرش زمردین بگسترد و دایه‌ی ابر بهاری را فرموده تا بنات نبات در مهد زمین بپرورد.  
  *farrāsh-e bād-e sabā rā gofte tā farsh-e zomorrodin begostarad va dāye-ye abr-e bahāri rā farmude tā banāt-e nabāt dar mahd-e zamin beparvarad.* — He has told the east wind, his carpet-layer, to spread an emerald carpet, and ordered the spring clouds, his nursemaids, to raise the daughters of the plants in the cradle of the earth.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  فراش *farrāsh* the servant who spreads the carpets (later 'school caretaker') · باد صبا *bād-e sabā* the east wind, the spring breeze · زمردین *zomorrodin* of emerald (زمرد 'emerald') · بگسترد *begostarad* should spread (گستردن) · دایه *dāye* nurse, nursemaid · بنات نبات *banāt-e nabāt* the daughters of plants – young shoots (Arabic بنات 'daughters') · مهد *mahd* cradle

  </details>
- درختان را به خلعت نوروزی قبای سبز ورق در بر گرفته و اطفال شاخ را به قدوم موسم ربیع کلاه شکوفه بر سر نهاده.  
  *derakhtān rā be khel'at-e nowruzi ghabā-ye sabz-e varagh dar bar gerefte va atfāl-e shākh rā be ghodum-e mowsem-e rabi' kolāh-e shekufe bar sar nahāde.* — He has dressed the trees in green robes of leaves, a New Year's gift of honour, and to welcome the spring he has set caps of blossom on the heads of the little branches.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  خلعت *khel'at* a robe of honour, given by a king · نوروزی *nowruzi* of Nowruz, the Persian New Year · قبا *ghabā* a long robe · ورق *varagh* leaf (today mostly 'sheet of paper') · اطفال *atfāl* children (Arabic plural of طفل) · قدوم موسم ربیع *ghodum-e mowsem-e rabi'* the arrival of the season of spring (Arabic words) · شکوفه *shekufe* blossom

  </details>
- عصاره‌ی تاکی به قدرت او شهد فایق شده و تخم خرمایی به تربیتش نخل باسق گشته.  
  *'osāre-ye tāki be ghodrat-e u shahd-e fāyegh shode va tokhm-e khormāyi be tarbiyat-ash nakhl-e bāsegh gashte.* — By his power the juice of a vine has become the sweetest honey, and by his care a date stone has grown into a towering palm.  
  _نخل باسق 'the towering palm' is a phrase from the Quran (50:10)._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  عصاره *'osāre* juice, extract · تاک *tāk* grapevine · شهد *shahd* honey · فایق *fāyegh* excellent, the finest · تخم *tokhm* seed; a date stone · تربیت *tarbiyat* upbringing, nurture · باسق *bāsegh* tall, towering (Arabic)

  </details>
- ابر و باد و مه و خورشید و فلک در کارند / تا تو نانی به کف آری و به غفلت نخوری  
  *abr o bād o mah o khorshid o falak dar kār-and / tā to nāni be kaf āri vo be ghaflat nakhori* — Cloud and wind, moon, sun and sky are all at work / so that you can earn your bread – and not eat it thoughtlessly.  
  _The poem goes on: «همه از بهر تو سرگشته و فرمانبردار / شرط انصاف نباشد که تو فرمان نبری» 'All of them toil and obey for your sake; it would not be fair if you did not obey.'_
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  ابر *abr* cloud · مه *mah* moon – short for ماه · فلک *falak* the sky, the heavens · در کارند *dar kār-and* are at work · به کف آوردن *be kaf āvardan* to get in hand, to earn (کف 'palm') · آری *āri* you bring – old form of آوری · غفلت *ghaflat* heedlessness, forgetfulness

  </details>

### Reading the poem

**The style.** This is *saj'*, rhymed prose. Each sentence is cut into two halves of about the same length, and the halves end in rhyming or matching words: قربت (*ghorbat*) / نعمت (*ne'mat*), حیات (*hayāt*) / ذات (*zāt*), رسیده (*reside*) / کشیده (*keshide*), ندرد (*nadarad*) / نبرد (*naborad*), بگسترد (*begostarad*) / بپرورد (*beparvarad*). Often the second half copies the shape of the first word for word: باران رحمت بی‌حسابش (*bārān-e rahmat-e bi-hesāb-ash*) / خوان نعمت بی‌دریغش (*khān-e ne'mat-e bi-darigh-ash*). Between the prose sentences Saadi sets short poems, so the page moves back and forth like a song between verse and chorus.

**The ideas.** The argument is simple and lovely. You cannot even breathe without two gifts – the breath in keeps you alive, the breath out refreshes you – so thanks are due twice with every breath, and no one can keep up. God does not shame us for our sins or stop our bread for our mistakes; he even feeds those who do not believe in him. Then the prose turns to spring: the wind lays a green carpet, the clouds nurse the seedlings, the trees get new robes for Nowruz. The whole universe is at work so that you can eat – and all that is asked of you is not to eat به غفلت (*be ghaflat*), without a thought.

**The rhythm.** The last couplet is in *ramal*, a long, flowing metre: *DUM-da-DUM-DUM da-da-DUM-DUM da-da-DUM-DUM DUM-DUM*, as in *ab-ro bā-do ma-ho khor-shi-do fa-lak dar kā-rand*. Five nouns tied together with و (*o*) fill the first half-line: the whole sky at work in one breath.

**The grammar.** The sentences are long but regular. Many verbs are past participles with است (*ast*) left out: رسیده (*reside*) is رسیده است (*reside ast*) and گفته (*gofte*) is گفته است (*gofte ast*). Two phrases are still everyday Persian: از عهده‌ی کاری برآمدن (*az 'ohde-ye kāri bar āmadan*) 'to manage something', and منت (*mennat*), which today usually means a favour held over you: منت بر سرم نگذار! (*mennat bar sar-am nagozār!*) 'don't make me feel I owe you!'. The second sentence in modern prose: هر نفسی که فرو می‌رود به زندگی کمک می‌کند و وقتی بیرون می‌آید جان را شاد می‌کند (*har nafasi ke foru mi-ravad be zendegi komak mi-konad va vaghti birun mi-āyad jān rā shād mi-konad*).

**Using it.** Every Iranian has met these sentences at school, and منت خدای را عز و جل (*mennat khodāy rā 'azza va jall*) is probably the best-known opening of any Persian book. ابر و باد و مه و خورشید و فلک در کارند (*abr o bād o mah o khorshid o falak dar kār-and*) is quoted to remind someone how much lies behind the bread on their table, and از دست و زبان که برآید (*az dast o zabān ke bar-āyad*) to say that no thanks could ever be enough.
