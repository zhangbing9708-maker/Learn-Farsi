# The eye and the heart
## ز دست دیده و دل
Baba Taher (باباطاهر), Dobeytis, 11th century. [Listen on the website](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/poetry/baba-taher-del.html)

Baba Taher, called *'Oryān* 'the Naked', was a wandering dervish of Hamadan in western Iran. The historian Ravandi tells that the Seljuk sultan Toghrul met him on a hill outside the city around 1055, got down from his horse, kissed his hand and asked for his blessing. His *dobeytis* – 'two-couplet' quatrains in a lighter, more singable metre than Khayyam's – were composed in the old dialect of the region, and shepherds, villagers and great singers have sung them for nine hundred years. They are printed here as they usually are today, in a Persian close to the standard language with a few dialect words. These six are among the best known: the eye and the heart, the beloved seen everywhere, and the pains and cures of love.

### The poem

> ز دست دیده و دل هر دو فریاد &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; که هر چه دیده بیند دل کند یاد  
> بسازم خنجری نیشش ز فولاد &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; زنم بر دیده تا دل گردد آزاد  
> به صحرا بنگرم صحرا ته وینم &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; به دریا بنگرم دریا ته وینم  
> به هر جا بنگرم کوه و در و دشت &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; نشان از قامت رعنا ته وینم  
> اگر دل دلبره دلبر کدامه &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; وگر دلبر دله دل را چه نامه  
> دل و دلبر به هم آمیته وینم &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; ندونم دل که و دلبر کدامه  
> دلی دیرم خریدار محبت &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; کز او گرم است بازار محبت  
> لباسی دوختم بر قامت دل &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; ز پود محنت و تار محبت  
> یکی درد و یکی درمان پسندد &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; یکی وصل و یکی هجران پسندد  
> من از درمان و درد و وصل و هجران &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; پسندم آنچه را جانان پسندد  
> دلا خوبان دل خونین پسندند &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; دلا خون شو که خوبان این پسندند  
> متاع کفر و دین بی‌مشتری نیست &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; گروهی آن گروهی این پسندند  

### Line by line

- ز دست دیده و دل هر دو فریاد / که هر چه دیده بیند دل کند یاد  
  *ze dast-e dide vo del har do faryād / ke har che dide binad del konad yād* — Help! I cry out against my eyes and my heart, / for whatever the eye sees, the heart remembers.  
  _«از دست کسی فریاد» 'help against someone!' is still how Persian complains: «از دست تو!» 'oh, you!'._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  ز دست … فریاد *ze dast-e … faryād* help against…!, I cry out against… (lit. 'from the hand of') · دیده *dide* eye (lit. 'the seeing one') · هر دو *har do* both · هر چه *har che* whatever · یاد کردن *yād kardan* to remember

  </details>
- بسازم خنجری نیشش ز فولاد / زنم بر دیده تا دل گردد آزاد  
  *besāzam khanjari nish-ash ze fulād / zanam bar dide tā del gardad āzād* — I'll make a dagger with a point of steel / and strike my eye, to set my heart free.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  بسازم *besāzam* I will make · خنجر *khanjar* dagger · نیش *nish* point, sting · فولاد *fulād* steel · زنم *zanam* I will strike – the old present used as a future · گردد *gardad* becomes – گشتن = شدن

  </details>
- به صحرا بنگرم صحرا ته وینم / به دریا بنگرم دریا ته وینم  
  *be sahrā bengaram sahrā ta vinam / be daryā bengaram daryā ta vinam* — When I look at the plain, I see you in the plain; / when I look at the sea, I see you in the sea.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  صحرا *sahrā* plain, open country, desert · بنگرم *bengaram* I look (نگریستن) · ته *ta* you (as object) – dialect for تو را · وینم *vinam* I see – dialect for بینم · دریا *daryā* sea

  </details>
- به هر جا بنگرم کوه و در و دشت / نشان از قامت رعنا ته وینم  
  *be har jā bengaram kuh o dar o dasht / neshān az ghāmat-e ra'nā ta vinam* — Wherever I look – mountain, valley, plain – / I see a sign of your tall and graceful figure.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  هر جا *har jā* everywhere, wherever · کوه و در و دشت *kuh o dar o dasht* mountain, valley and plain – everywhere · نشان *neshān* sign, trace · قامت *ghāmat* stature, figure · رعنا *ra'nā* graceful, beautiful

  </details>
- اگر دل دلبره دلبر کدامه / وگر دلبر دله دل را چه نامه  
  *agar del delbar-e delbar kodām-e / vagar delbar del-e del rā che nām-e* — If the heart is the beloved, then which is the beloved? / And if the beloved is the heart, what is the heart called?
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  دلبر *delbar* beloved (lit. 'heart-stealer') · دلبره *delbar-e* is the beloved – dialect ـه for است · کدامه *kodām-e* which is it? – کدام است · وگر *vagar* and if – و + اگر · دله *del-e* is the heart – دل است · چه نامه *che nām-e* what is the name? – چه نام است

  </details>
- دل و دلبر به هم آمیته وینم / ندونم دل که و دلبر کدامه  
  *del o delbar be ham āmite vinam / nadunam del ke vo delbar kodām-e* — I see the heart and the beloved mingled into one; / I cannot tell which is the heart and which the beloved.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  به هم *be ham* together, with each other · آمیته *āmite* mixed, mingled – dialect for آمیخته · وینم *vinam* I see – بینم · ندونم *nadunam* I don't know – ندانم (spoken Tehrani نمی‌دونم) · که *ke* who?, which? – here = کیست

  </details>
- دلی دیرم خریدار محبت / کز او گرم است بازار محبت  
  *deli diram kharidār-e mohabbat / kaz u garm ast bāzār-e mohabbat* — I have a heart that buys love, / and it keeps love's market busy.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  دیرم *diram* I have – dialect for دارم · خریدار *kharidār* buyer · محبت *mohabbat* love, affection · کز او *kaz u* by which – که از او · بازار … گرم است *bāzār … garm ast* the market is busy (lit. 'warm')

  </details>
- لباسی دوختم بر قامت دل / ز پود محنت و تار محبت  
  *lebāsi dukhtam bar ghāmat-e del / ze pud-e mehnat o tār-e mohabbat* — I have sewn a garment to fit my heart, / with a weft of suffering and a warp of love.  
  _تار و پود 'warp and weft' is the Persian for the very fabric of something: «تار و پود وجودم» 'every fibre of my being'._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  لباسی *lebāsi* a garment · دوختم *dukhtam* I sewed (دوختن) · قامت *ghāmat* figure, stature · پود *pud* weft – the cross threads · محنت *mehnat* hardship, suffering · تار *tār* warp – the long threads

  </details>
- یکی درد و یکی درمان پسندد / یکی وصل و یکی هجران پسندد  
  *yeki dard o yeki darmān pasandad / yeki vasl o yeki hejrān pasandad* — One wants the pain and one the cure; / one wants union and one separation.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  یکی … یکی *yeki … yeki* one … another · درد *dard* pain · درمان *darmān* cure, remedy · پسندیدن *pasandidan* to like, to choose · وصل *vasl* union (with the beloved) · هجران *hejrān* separation

  </details>
- من از درمان و درد و وصل و هجران / پسندم آنچه را جانان پسندد  
  *man az darmān o dard o vasl o hejrān / pasandam ānche rā jānān pasandad* — Of cure and pain, of union and parting, / I want whatever my beloved wants.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  پسندم *pasandam* I like, I choose · آنچه *ānche* what, whatever · آنچه را *ānche rā* whatever – as the object · جانان *jānān* the beloved

  </details>
- دلا خوبان دل خونین پسندند / دلا خون شو که خوبان این پسندند  
  *delā khubān del-e khunin pasandand / delā khun sho ke khubān in pasandand* — O heart, the beautiful love a bleeding heart; / so bleed, my heart, for that is what they love.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  دلا *delā* O heart! – ـا of address · خوبان *khubān* the beautiful ones, beauties · خونین *khunin* bloody, bleeding · خون شدن *khun shodan* to turn to blood – to suffer deeply · شو *sho* become! – imperative of شدن (today بشو)

  </details>
- متاع کفر و دین بی‌مشتری نیست / گروهی آن گروهی این پسندند  
  *matā'-e kofr o din bi-moshtari nist / goruhi ān goruhi in pasandand* — The goods of unbelief and of faith both find buyers: / some like that, and some like this.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  متاع *matā'* goods, merchandise · کفر *kofr* unbelief · دین *din* religion, faith · بی‌مشتری *bi-moshtari* without buyers · گروهی *goruhi* a group, some people

  </details>

### Reading the poem

**The images.** Baba Taher's world is small and concrete: the eye and the heart, the plain, the sea and the mountain, a dagger, a garment, a market. Out of these he makes the great themes of mystical love. The eye sees and the heart cannot forget, so the poet threatens to blind himself; the beloved is so present that he is seen in every landscape, and so close that lover and beloved can no longer be told apart. The heart is a buyer in love's market and wears a garment woven of love and suffering. The last two dobeytis are about surrender: the lover wants whatever the beloved wants, even pain – and if the beautiful like a bleeding heart, then bleed.

**The rhythm.** A *dobeyti* looks like a *robā'i* – four half-lines, rhyming in lines 1, 2 and 4 – but it has its own lighter metre, a short *hazaj*: *da-DUM-DUM-DUM da-DUM-DUM-DUM da-DUM-DUM*. Hear it in *be sah-rā ben-ga-ram sah-rā ta vi-nam*. It is the metre of folk song all over Iran, and Baba Taher's poems are more often sung than recited. Several repeat a word or two after the rhyme, a ردیف (*radif*): ته وینم (*ta vinam*), محبت (*mohabbat*), پسندد (*pasandad*), پسندند (*pasandand*).

**The grammar.** The dialect words are few and easy: دیرم (*diram*) for دارم (*dāram*), وینم (*vinam*) for بینم (*binam*), ته (*ta*) for تو را (*to rā*), آمیته (*āmite*) for آمیخته (*āmikhte*), ندونم (*nadunam*) for ندانم (*nadānam*). The ending ـه (*-e*) for است (*ast*) – دلبره (*delbar-e*) 'is the beloved', کدامه (*kodām-e*) 'which is it?' – is exactly what you hear in spoken Tehrani Persian today. The first dobeyti in modern prose: از دست چشم و دلم فریاد، چون دل هر چه را چشم می‌بیند به یاد می‌آورد. خنجری با نوک فولادی می‌سازم و به چشمم می‌زنم تا دلم آزاد شود (*az dast-e cheshm o del-am faryād, chon del har che rā cheshm mi-binad be yād mi-āvarad. khanjari bā nok-e fulādi mi-sāzam va be cheshm-am mi-zanam tā del-am āzād shavad*).

**Using it.** Baba Taher is the poet of ordinary people: his dobeytis are sung at weddings and in the mountains, and Iran's great classical singers have recorded them again and again. His tomb, a modern tower on a hill in Hamadan, is one of the city's landmarks. به صحرا بنگرم صحرا ته وینم (*be sahrā bengaram sahrā ta vinam*) is the line people quote about a love they see everywhere, and گروهی آن گروهی این پسندند (*goruhi ān goruhi in pasandand*) settles any argument about taste.
