# My King, I speak your praise
## ملکا ذکر تو گویم
Sanai (سنایی), Divan, 12th century. [Listen on the website](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/poetry/sanai-maleka-zekr-e-to.html)

Hakim Sanai of Ghazni (died about 1131), in today's Afghanistan, began as a court poet writing praise of the Ghaznavid sultans and ended as one of the first great Sufi poets. His *Hadiqat al-Haqiqa* ('The Garden of Truth') was the first long mystical poem in Persian, and Rumi wrote that he came 'following Sanai and Attar'. This short ghazal is a *monājāt*, a private prayer: it praises God as pure, beyond need, beyond description and beyond change, and ends with the poet's hope that a tongue that has spoken only of God's oneness will be spared the fire. Its simple words and dancing rhythm have made it a favourite of Iranian schoolbooks and singers.

### The poem

> ملکا ذکر تو گویم که تو پاکی و خدایی &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; نروم جز به همان ره که توام راه نمایی  
> همه درگاه تو جویم همه از فضل تو پویم &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; همه توحید تو گویم که به توحید سزایی  
> تو حکیمی تو عظیمی تو کریمی تو رحیمی &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; تو نماینده‌ی فضلی تو سزاوار ثنایی  
> بری از رنج و گدازی بری از درد و نیازی &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; بری از بیم و امیدی بری از چون و چرایی  
> بری از خوردن و خفتن بری از شرک و شبیهی &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; بری از صورت و رنگی بری از عیب و خطایی  
> نتوان وصف تو گفتن که تو در فهم نگنجی &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; نتوان شبه تو گفتن که تو در وهم نیایی  
> نبد این خلق و تو بودی نبود خلق و تو باشی &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; نه بجنبی نه بگردی نه بکاهی نه فزایی  
> همه عزی و جلالی همه علمی و یقینی &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; همه نوری و سروری همه جودی و جزایی  
> لب و دندان سنایی همه توحید تو گوید &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; مگر از آتش دوزخ بودش روی رهایی  

### Line by line

- ملکا ذکر تو گویم که تو پاکی و خدایی / نروم جز به همان ره که توام راه نمایی  
  *malekā zekr-e to guyam ke to pāki vo khodāyi / naravam joz be hamān rah ke to-am rāh namāyi* — My King, I speak your praise, for you are pure, you are God; / I will walk no road but the one you show me.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  ملکا *malekā* O King! – ملک 'king' + ـا of address · ذکر *zekr* mention, remembrance; praise of God · گویم *guyam* I say, I speak – no می · پاکی *pāki* you are pure – پاک + ی 'you are' · خدایی *khodāyi* you are God · ره *rah* road – short for راه · توام … نمایی *to-am … namāyi* you show me – the ـم 'me' is attached to تو

  </details>
- همه درگاه تو جویم همه از فضل تو پویم / همه توحید تو گویم که به توحید سزایی  
  *hame dargāh-e to juyam hame az fazl-e to puyam / hame towhid-e to guyam ke be towhid sezāyi* — I seek only your door, I run only by your grace, / I speak only of your oneness, for oneness is your due.  
  _Most printings add two couplets here, on God having no wife and no child; they are left out on this page._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  همه *hame* all, wholly – here 'only, always' · درگاه *dargāh* door, court · جویم *juyam* I seek (جستن) · فضل *fazl* grace, bounty · پویم *puyam* I run, I hurry (پوییدن) · توحید *towhid* declaring God one, the oneness of God · سزایی *sezāyi* you are worthy

  </details>
- تو حکیمی تو عظیمی تو کریمی تو رحیمی / تو نماینده‌ی فضلی تو سزاوار ثنایی  
  *to hakimi to 'azimi to karimi to rahimi / to namāyande-ye fazli to sezāvār-e sanāyi* — You are wise, you are great, you are generous, you are merciful; / you are the one who shows grace, you are worthy of praise.  
  _The ـی at the end of each word is 'you are': حکیمی = حکیم هستی._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  حکیم *hakim* wise · عظیم *'azim* great, mighty · کریم *karim* generous · رحیم *rahim* merciful · نماینده *namāyande* one who shows (today 'representative') · ثنا *sanā* praise

  </details>
- بری از رنج و گدازی بری از درد و نیازی / بری از بیم و امیدی بری از چون و چرایی  
  *bari az ranj o godāzi bari az dard o niyāzi / bari az bim o omidi bari az chun o cherāyi* — You are free of suffering and wasting, free of pain and need, / free of fear and hope, free of how and why.  
  _«بی‌چون و چرا» 'without how or why' is still everyday Persian for 'without question'._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  بری *bari* free of, innocent of (Arabic) · رنج *ranj* suffering, toil · گداز *godāz* melting, wasting away · نیاز *niyāz* need · بیم و امید *bim o omid* fear and hope · چون و چرا *chun o cherā* how and why; questioning

  </details>
- بری از خوردن و خفتن بری از شرک و شبیهی / بری از صورت و رنگی بری از عیب و خطایی  
  *bari az khordan o khoftan bari az sherk o shabihi / bari az surat o rangi bari az 'eyb o khatāyi* — Free of eating and sleeping, free of partners and likenesses, / free of form and colour, free of fault and error.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  خفتن *khoftan* to sleep (today خوابیدن) · شرک *sherk* giving God partners, polytheism · شبیه *shabih* likeness, something similar · صورت *surat* form, image (today 'face') · عیب *'eyb* fault, defect · خطا *khatā* error, mistake

  </details>
- نتوان وصف تو گفتن که تو در فهم نگنجی / نتوان شبه تو گفتن که تو در وهم نیایی  
  *natavān vasf-e to goftan ke to dar fahm nagonji / natavān shebh-e to goftan ke to dar vahm nayāyi* — No one can describe you, for you do not fit in the mind; / no one can name your like, for you do not come into the imagination.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  نتوان *natavān* one cannot · وصف *vasf* description · فهم *fahm* understanding · نگنجی *nagonji* you do not fit (گنجیدن) · شبه *shebh* likeness, equal · وهم *vahm* imagination, fancy

  </details>
- نبد این خلق و تو بودی نبود خلق و تو باشی / نه بجنبی نه بگردی نه بکاهی نه فزایی  
  *nabod in khalgh o to budi nabovad khalgh o to bāshi / na bejonbi na begardi na bekāhi na fazāyi* — This creation was not, and you were; creation will not be, and you will be; / you do not move, you do not change, you neither shrink nor grow.  
  _Look at the two forms: نبد *nabod* 'was not', and نبود read *nabovad* 'will not be' – the same letters as نبود *nabud* 'was not', read in the old way._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  نبد *nabod* was not – نبود · خلق *khalgh* creation; people · نبود *nabovad* will not be – the old present of بودن · بجنبی *bejonbi* you move (جنبیدن) · بگردی *begardi* you turn, you change · بکاهی *bekāhi* you decrease (کاستن) · فزایی *fazāyi* you increase – افزایی

  </details>
- همه عزی و جلالی همه علمی و یقینی / همه نوری و سروری همه جودی و جزایی  
  *hame 'ezzi vo jalāli hame 'elmi vo yaghini / hame nuri vo soruri hame judi vo jazāyi* — You are all glory and majesty, all knowledge and certainty, / all light and joy, all generosity and reward.  
  _Printings have one more couplet before the last; it is left out on this page._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  عز *'ezz* glory, might · جلال *jalāl* majesty · علم *'elm* knowledge · یقین *yaghin* certainty · سرور *sorur* joy, delight · جود *jud* generosity · جزا *jazā* reward, recompense

  </details>
- لب و دندان سنایی همه توحید تو گوید / مگر از آتش دوزخ بودش روی رهایی  
  *lab o dandān-e sanāyi hame towhid-e to guyad / magar az ātash-e duzakh bovad-ash ru-ye rahāyi* — Sanai's lips and teeth speak only of your oneness, / in the hope that he may find a way of escape from the fire of hell.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  لب و دندان *lab o dandān* lips and teeth – the whole mouth · گوید *guyad* says, speaks · مگر *magar* in the hope that, perhaps (an old meaning) · دوزخ *duzakh* hell · بودش *bovad-ash* he may have (lit. 'to him may be') · روی *ru* a way, a means (lit. 'face') · رهایی *rahāyi* escape, release

  </details>

### Reading the poem

**The ideas.** The poem is a list – and that is its power. Sanai piles up the qualities of God in short, balanced phrases: wise, great, generous, merciful; free of pain and need, of fear and hope, of eating and sleeping, of form and colour. It is a lesson in what theologians call the negative way: God is best described by what he is not, because nothing in our world is like him. He cannot be described or imagined, does not move or change; creation came after him and will pass before him. Only at the end does the poet appear, hoping that a mouth that has spoken of nothing but God's oneness will be saved.

**The rhythm.** The metre (*ramal*) breaks every half-line into four equal steps, *da-da-DUM-DUM* four times, and Sanai fills each step with a matching phrase: *ma-la-kā zek-re to gu-yam ke to pā-ki yo kho-dā-yi*. Most couplets also rhyme inside: جویم (*juyam*) / پویم (*puyam*) / گویم (*guyam*), حکیمی (*hakimi*) / عظیمی (*'azimi*) / کریمی (*karimi*) / رحیمی (*rahimi*). The end rhyme is the long ـایی (*-āyi*): خدایی (*khodāyi*), نمایی (*namāyi*), سزایی (*sezāyi*), چرایی (*cherāyi*). It is a rhythm children can clap to, which is one reason they learn it.

**The grammar.** Almost every phrase ends in ی (*-i*) 'you are': تو حکیمی (*to hakimi*) 'you are wise', بری از رنجی (*bari az ranji*) 'you are free of pain'. This is the short form of هستی (*hasti*) that modern Persian still uses after a noun or adjective: تو خسته‌ای (*to khaste-i*) 'you are tired'. The old verbs نبد (*nabod*) 'was not', نتوان (*natavān*) 'one cannot' and فزایی (*fazāyi*) 'you increase' are easy to decode. The sixth couplet in modern prose: نمی‌شود تو را وصف کرد، چون در فهم نمی‌گنجی؛ نمی‌شود برایت شبیهی گفت، چون به خیال نمی‌آیی (*nemi-shavad to rā vasf kard, chon dar fahm nemi-gonji; nemi-shavad barā-yat shabihi goft, chon be khiyāl nemi-āyi*).

**Using it.** ملکا ذکر تو گویم (*malekā zekr-e to guyam*) is one of the classical poems Iranian children meet in their school Persian books, and it is often sung and recited at gatherings. Its vocabulary of praise is still alive, too: بی‌چون و چرا (*bi chun o cherā*) 'without how or why' means 'without question', and ذکر (*zekr*) is the word for the repeated prayers of Sufis.
