# Your name, the best beginning
## ای نام تو بهترین سرآغاز
Nezami (نظامی), Leyli o Majnun, 12th century. [Listen on the website](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/poetry/nezami-leyli-opening.html)

Nezami of Ganja (about 1141–1209), in today's Azerbaijan, wrote five long verse romances, the *Khamse* ('Quintet'), which every later Persian storyteller tried to equal. *Leyli and Majnun* (1188) retells an Arab legend. Qeys falls in love with his schoolmate Leyli; the whole tribe talks of it, and her family keeps her from him. He loses his mind with love and wanders the desert among the wild animals, and people call him Majnun, 'the possessed'. Leyli is married against her will to another man but stays faithful to Majnun; they meet only rarely and never live together. When Leyli dies of grief, Majnun comes to her grave and dies there. Like all of Nezami's poems, it begins with a hymn to God; these are its first couplets.

### The poem

> ای نام تو بهترین سرآغاز &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; بی‌نام تو نامه کی کنم باز  
> ای یاد تو مونس روانم &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; جز نام تو نیست بر زبانم  
> ای کارگشای هر چه هستند &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; نام تو کلید هر چه بستند  
> ای هیچ خطی نگشته ز اول &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; بی‌حجت نام تو مسجل  
> ای هست کن اساس هستی &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; کوته ز درت دراز دستی  
> ای خطبه‌ی تو تبارک الله &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; فیض تو همیشه بارک الله  
> ای هفت عروس نه عماری &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; بر درگه تو به پرده‌داری  
> ای واهب عقل و باعث جان &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; با حکم تو هست و نیست یکسان  
> ای محرم عالم تحیر &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; عالم ز تو هم تهی و هم پر  

### Line by line

- ای نام تو بهترین سرآغاز / بی‌نام تو نامه کی کنم باز  
  *ey nām-e to behtarin sar-āghāz / bi-nām-e to nāme key konam bāz* — O you whose name is the best of beginnings, / how could I open a book without your name?
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  ای *ey* O (calling someone) · بهترین *behtarin* best · سرآغاز *sar-āghāz* beginning, opening · بی‌نام تو *bi-nām-e to* without your name · نامه *nāme* letter, book · کی *key* when?; here 'how could…?' · باز کردن *bāz kardan* to open

  </details>
- ای یاد تو مونس روانم / جز نام تو نیست بر زبانم  
  *ey yād-e to munes-e ravān-am / joz nām-e to nist bar zabān-am* — O you whose memory keeps my soul company, / there is nothing on my tongue but your name.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  یاد *yād* remembrance, memory · مونس *munes* companion, friend (Arabic مؤنس) · روان *ravān* soul, spirit · جز *joz* except, but · زبان *zabān* tongue; language

  </details>
- ای کارگشای هر چه هستند / نام تو کلید هر چه بستند  
  *ey kār-goshā-ye har che hastand / nām-e to kelid-e har che bastand* — O you who open the way for all that exists, / your name is the key to all that is locked.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  کارگشا *kār-goshā* one who opens the way, solves problems (lit. 'work-opener') · هر چه *har che* whatever, all that · هستند *hastand* are, exist · کلید *kelid* key · بستند *bastand* they closed – here 'is locked' (بستن)

  </details>
- ای هیچ خطی نگشته ز اول / بی‌حجت نام تو مسجل  
  *ey hich khatti nagashte ze avval / bi-hojjat-e nām-e to mosajjal* — O you without whose name as warrant / no document has been valid since the beginning.  
  _The image is a legal document: nothing is valid until it carries your name as its seal._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  هیچ خطی *hich khatti* no writing, no document · نگشته *nagashte* has not become – گشتن = شدن · ز اول *ze avval* from the beginning · حجت *hojjat* proof, warrant · مسجل *mosajjal* registered, sealed, made valid (Arabic)

  </details>
- ای هست کن اساس هستی / کوته ز درت دراز دستی  
  *ey hast kon-e asās-e hasti / kute ze dar-at derāz dasti* — O you who brought the foundation of being into being, / at your door the long arm of the mighty falls short.  
  _«دست‌درازی» 'long-handedness' is still the Persian for grabbing what is not yours, or for aggression._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  هست کن *hast kon* maker of being, the one who says 'Be!' · اساس *asās* foundation, basis · هستی *hasti* existence, being · کوته *kute* short – کوتاه · درت *dar-at* your door · دراز دستی *derāz dasti* long-handedness – reaching too far, aggression

  </details>
- ای خطبه‌ی تو تبارک الله / فیض تو همیشه بارک الله  
  *ey khotbe-ye to tabārak allāh / feyz-e to hamishe bārak allāh* — O you whose sermon is 'Blessed be God', / whose grace is forever 'God bless it'.  
  _بارک‌الله *bārakallāh* is still how Iranians say 'well done!', especially to children._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  خطبه *khotbe* sermon, solemn address · تبارک الله *tabārak allāh* blessed be God (Arabic) · فیض *feyz* grace, overflowing bounty · همیشه *hamishe* always · بارک الله *bārak allāh* God bless it!; well done! (Arabic)

  </details>
- ای هفت عروس نه عماری / بر درگه تو به پرده‌داری  
  *ey haft 'arus-e noh 'amāri / bar dargah-e to be parde-dāri* — O you at whose door the seven brides in their nine litters / stand as keepers of the curtain.  
  _In the old astronomy the seven planets ride through nine nested spheres; here they are brides in litters who serve as doorkeepers at God's court._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  هفت عروس *haft 'arus* the seven brides – the seven planets · نه *noh* nine (not نه na 'no') – the nine heavenly spheres · عماری *'amāri* a litter carried on a camel or an elephant · درگه *dargah* threshold, court – درگاه · پرده‌داری *parde-dāri* keeping the curtain – serving as a chamberlain

  </details>
- ای واهب عقل و باعث جان / با حکم تو هست و نیست یکسان  
  *ey vāheb-e 'aghl o bā'es-e jān / bā hokm-e to hast o nist yeksān* — O giver of reason and raiser of the soul, / before your command, being and not-being are the same.  
  _This couplet and the next come from a little further on in the hymn; a few couplets in between are left out here._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  واهب *vāheb* giver (Arabic) · عقل *'aghl* reason, intellect · باعث *bā'es* cause, the one who brings about (Arabic) · حکم *hokm* command, decree · هست و نیست *hast o nist* what is and what is not · یکسان *yeksān* the same, equal

  </details>
- ای محرم عالم تحیر / عالم ز تو هم تهی و هم پر  
  *ey mahram-e 'ālam-e tahayyor / 'ālam ze to ham tohi vo ham por* — O you who are at home in the world of bewilderment, / the world is both empty of you and full of you.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  محرم *mahram* intimate, one admitted inside · عالم *'ālam* world · تحیر *tahayyor* bewilderment, amazement · هم … هم *ham … ham* both … and · تهی *tohi* empty · پر *por* full

  </details>

### Reading the poem

**The images.** Every couplet begins with ای (*ey*) 'O you…', and each gives God a new title taken from ordinary life. He is the opening without which no book can begin, the companion of the soul, the key to every lock, the seal that makes a document valid, a door at which the mighty are powerless, a court where the planets wait as chamberlains. The last couplet turns to paradox, as mystical poetry loves to do: the world is empty of God, because he cannot be seen in it, and full of him, because everything comes from him. For a poem about a lover who loses himself completely in the beloved, it is a fitting beginning.

**The rhythm.** *Leyli and Majnun* is in a short, light metre (*hazaj*): *DUM-DUM-da da-DUM-da-DUM da-DUM-DUM*. Read *ey nā-me to beh-ta-rin sar-ā-ghāz*. Nezami made it the classic metre for the story: Jami and many later poets used it for their own versions of Leyli and Majnun. Each couplet rhymes on its own, sometimes with a repeated word after the rhyme: هستند (*hastand*) / بستند (*bastand*), تبارک الله (*tabārak allāh*) / بارک الله (*bārak allāh*).

**The grammar.** The hymn is built on one structure, ای (*ey*) + a description of God, and there are almost no verbs, because است (*ast*) 'is' is understood: ای یاد تو مونس روانم (*ey yād-e to munes-e ravān-am*) is 'O you (whose) memory (is) the companion of my soul'. ز (*ze*) is از (*az*), کوته (*kute*) is کوتاه (*kutāh*), درگه (*dargah*) is درگاه (*dargāh*), and نگشته (*nagashte*) is نشده (*nashode*). The first couplet in modern prose: ای خدایی که نامت بهترین آغاز است، بی‌نام تو چطور نامه‌ای را باز کنم؟ (*ey khodāyi ke nām-at behtarin āghāz ast, bi nām-e to chetowr nāme-i rā bāz konam?*)

**Using it.** ای نام تو بهترین سرآغاز (*ey nām-e to behtarin sar-āghāz*) is often written at the top of a first page – of a book, a speech or a school essay – and it is one of the first couplets of classical poetry many Iranians learn. کارگشا (*kār-goshā*) 'that gets things done' and دست‌درازی (*dast-derāzi*) 'aggression' are still everyday words, and بارک‌الله (*bārakallāh*) is what an Iranian teacher says to a pupil who gets it right.
