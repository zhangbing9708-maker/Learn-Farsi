# I was dead, I came alive
## مرده بدم زنده شدم
Rumi (مولوی), Divan-e Shams, 13th century. [Listen on the website](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/poetry/rumi-morde-bodam.html)

Rumi (1207–1273) was a respected preacher and teacher in Konya when, in 1244, he met the wandering dervish Shams of Tabriz. The meeting turned his life upside down: he left his sermons for music, dance and poetry, and after Shams disappeared a few years later, Rumi poured out more than three thousand ghazals of love and longing. He signed many of them not with his own name but with his friend's, and the collection is called the *Divan-e Shams*. This is one of its most joyful poems, a long ghazal (here are its first twelve couplets) in which Rumi tells of his own rebirth through love, in a running dialogue – 'He said… I went and…' – with a beloved who keeps pushing him further.

### The poem

> مرده بدم زنده شدم گریه بدم خنده شدم &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; دولت عشق آمد و من دولت پاینده شدم  
> دیده‌ی سیر است مرا جان دلیر است مرا &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; زهره‌ی شیر است مرا زهره‌ی تابنده شدم  
> گفت که دیوانه نه‌ای لایق این خانه نه‌ای &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; رفتم و دیوانه شدم سلسله‌بندنده شدم  
> گفت که سرمست نه‌ای رو که از این دست نه‌ای &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; رفتم و سرمست شدم وز طرب آکنده شدم  
> گفت که تو کشته نه‌ای در طرب آغشته نه‌ای &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; پیش رخ زنده‌کنش کشته و افکنده شدم  
> گفت که تو زیرککی مست خیالی و شکی &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; گول شدم هول شدم وز همه برکنده شدم  
> گفت که تو شمع شدی قبله‌ی این جمع شدی &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; جمع نیم شمع نیم دود پراکنده شدم  
> گفت که شیخی و سری پیش‌رو و راهبری &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; شیخ نیم پیش نیم امر تو را بنده شدم  
> گفت که با بال و پری من پر و بالت ندهم &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; در هوس بال و پرش بی‌پر و پرکنده شدم  
> گفت مرا دولت نو راه مرو رنجه مشو &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; زان که من از لطف و کرم سوی تو آینده شدم  
> گفت مرا عشق کهن از بر ما نقل مکن &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; گفتم آری نکنم ساکن و باشنده شدم  
> چشمه‌ی خورشید تویی سایه‌گه بید منم &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; چون که زدی بر سر من پست و گدازنده شدم  

### Line by line

- مرده بدم زنده شدم گریه بدم خنده شدم / دولت عشق آمد و من دولت پاینده شدم  
  *morde bodam zende shodam gerye bodam khande shodam / dowlat-e 'eshgh āmad o man dowlat-e pāyande shodam* — I was dead, I came alive; I was tears, I became laughter; / the good fortune of love came, and I became a fortune that lasts for ever.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  بدم *bodam* I was – short for بودم · مرده *morde* dead · گریه *gerye* weeping, tears · خنده *khande* laughter · دولت *dowlat* good fortune, luck (today 'government, state') · پاینده *pāyande* lasting, everlasting

  </details>
- دیده‌ی سیر است مرا جان دلیر است مرا / زهره‌ی شیر است مرا زهره‌ی تابنده شدم  
  *dide-ye sir ast marā jān-e dalir ast marā / zahre-ye shir ast marā zohre-ye tābande shodam* — My eyes want nothing more, my soul is bold, / I have a lion's courage – I have become the shining Venus.  
  _زهره is spelled the same but read two ways: *zahre* 'gall, courage' and *zohre* 'Venus'. A play on words like this is called جناس *jenās*._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  دیده‌ی سیر *dide-ye sir* a satisfied eye – one that wants nothing more · مرا … است *marā … ast* I have (lit. 'to me is') · دلیر *dalir* brave, bold · زهره *zahre* gall bladder – courage, nerve · زهره *zohre* the planet Venus · تابنده *tābande* shining

  </details>
- گفت که دیوانه نه‌ای لایق این خانه نه‌ای / رفتم و دیوانه شدم سلسله‌بندنده شدم  
  *goft ke divāne na-'i lāyegh-e in khāne na-'i / raftam o divāne shodam selsele-bandande shodam* — He said, 'You are not mad, you are not fit for this house'; / I went and went mad, and became one who wears chains.  
  _Madmen used to be kept in chains, so 'one in chains' means 'a real madman'._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  دیوانه *divāne* mad, insane · نه‌ای *na-'i* you are not – نیستی · لایق *lāyegh* worthy, fit for · سلسله *selsele* chain · سلسله‌بندنده *selsele-bandande* one bound in chains

  </details>
- گفت که سرمست نه‌ای رو که از این دست نه‌ای / رفتم و سرمست شدم وز طرب آکنده شدم  
  *goft ke sarmast na-'i row ke az in dast na-'i / raftam o sarmast shodam vaz tarab ākande shodam* — He said, 'You are not drunk; go away, you are not one of us'; / I went and got drunk, and was filled with joy.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  سرمست *sarmast* drunk, intoxicated · رو *row* go! – برو · از این دست *az in dast* of this kind, one of these · وز *vaz* and from – و + از · طرب *tarab* joy, delight · آکنده *ākande* filled, stuffed

  </details>
- گفت که تو کشته نه‌ای در طرب آغشته نه‌ای / پیش رخ زنده‌کنش کشته و افکنده شدم  
  *goft ke to koshte na-'i dar tarab āghoshte na-'i / pish-e rokh-e zende-kon-ash koshte vo afkande shodam* — He said, 'You have not been slain, you are not steeped in joy'; / before his life-giving face I fell, slain and laid low.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  کشته *koshte* killed, slain · آغشته *āghoshte* soaked, steeped · رخ *rokh* face, cheek · زنده‌کن *zende-kon* life-giving (lit. 'that makes alive') · افکنده *afkande* thrown down, fallen

  </details>
- گفت که تو زیرککی مست خیالی و شکی / گول شدم هول شدم وز همه برکنده شدم  
  *goft ke to zirakaki mast-e khiyāli vo shakki / gul shodam hol shodam vaz hame barkande shodam* — He said, 'You are too clever, drunk on fancies and doubts'; / I became a fool, a dazed one, and tore myself away from everyone.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  زیرککی *zirakaki* you are a clever little one – زیرک + ـک + ـی · خیال *khiyāl* fancy, imagination · شک *shakk* doubt · گول *gul* fool, simpleton (today 'trick') · هول *hol* flustered, dazed · برکنده *barkande* uprooted, torn away

  </details>
- گفت که تو شمع شدی قبله‌ی این جمع شدی / جمع نیم شمع نیم دود پراکنده شدم  
  *goft ke to sham' shodi gheble-ye in jam' shodi / jam' niyam sham' niyam dud-e parākande shodam* — He said, 'You have become a candle, the focus of this gathering'; / I am no gathering, I am no candle – I have become scattered smoke.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  شمع *sham'* candle · قبله *gheble* the direction of prayer; the focus everyone turns to · جمع *jam'* gathering, company · نیم *niyam* I am not – نیستم · دود *dud* smoke · پراکنده *parākande* scattered

  </details>
- گفت که شیخی و سری پیش‌رو و راهبری / شیخ نیم پیش نیم امر تو را بنده شدم  
  *goft ke sheykhi vo sari pish-row o rāhbari / sheykh niyam pish niyam amr-e to rā bande shodam* — He said, 'You are a sheikh and a chief, out in front, a guide'; / I am no sheikh, I am not in front – I have become the servant of your command.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  شیخ *sheykh* sheikh, a Sufi master · سر *sar* head, chief · پیش‌رو *pish-row* one who goes ahead, leader · راهبر *rāhbar* guide (today also 'leader') · امر *amr* command · بنده *bande* servant, slave

  </details>
- گفت که با بال و پری من پر و بالت ندهم / در هوس بال و پرش بی‌پر و پرکنده شدم  
  *goft ke bā bāl o pari man par o bāl-at nadaham / dar havas-e bāl o par-ash bi-par o parkande shodam* — He said, 'You already have wings and feathers; I will not give you mine'; / longing for his wings, I lost my feathers and was plucked bare.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  بال و پر *bāl o par* wings and feathers · با بال و پری *bā bāl o pari* you have wings and feathers – پری = پر + ی 'you are' · ندهم *nadaham* I will not give · هوس *havas* desire, longing · بی‌پر *bi-par* featherless · پرکنده *parkande* plucked

  </details>
- گفت مرا دولت نو راه مرو رنجه مشو / زان که من از لطف و کرم سوی تو آینده شدم  
  *goft marā dowlat-e now rāh marow ranje masho / zān ke man az lotf o karam su-ye to āyande shodam* — The new fortune said to me, 'Don't set out, don't trouble yourself, / for out of kindness and generosity I have come to you.'
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  دولت نو *dowlat-e now* new fortune, new happiness · راه مرو *rāh marow* don't go on the road – مرو = نرو · رنجه شدن *ranje shodan* to take trouble, to bother · زان که *zān ke* because · لطف و کرم *lotf o karam* kindness and generosity · آینده *āyande* coming (today 'future')

  </details>
- گفت مرا عشق کهن از بر ما نقل مکن / گفتم آری نکنم ساکن و باشنده شدم  
  *goft marā 'eshgh-e kohan az bar-e mā naghl makon / goftam āri nakonam sāken o bāshande shodam* — The old love said to me, 'Do not move away from us'; / I said, 'Yes, I will not' – and I settled down and stayed.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  کهن *kohan* old, ancient · از بر *az bar-e* from beside · نقل کردن *naghl kardan* to move house (today 'to tell, to quote') · آری *āri* yes – an old word (today آره, بله) · ساکن *sāken* settled, living in a place · باشنده *bāshande* one who stays, a resident

  </details>
- چشمه‌ی خورشید تویی سایه‌گه بید منم / چون که زدی بر سر من پست و گدازنده شدم  
  *cheshme-ye khorshid to-yi sāye-gah-e bid manam / chon ke zadi bar sar-e man past o godāzande shodam* — You are the fountain of the sun, I am the shade of the willow; / when you struck my head, I was laid low and melted away.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  چشمه *cheshme* spring, fountain · تویی *to-yi* you are – تو + ی · سایه‌گه *sāye-gah* shady place – سایه + گاه · بید *bid* willow · منم *manam* I am – من + م · پست *past* low, flat · گدازنده *godāzande* melting

  </details>

### Reading the poem

**The images.** The first line says it all: death to life, tears to laughter, a poor man to a lasting fortune. The rest of the poem tells how. The beloved keeps refusing: you are not mad enough, not drunk enough, not dead enough; you are too clever, too much of a leader. Each time Rumi does not argue – he simply becomes what he is asked to become, or less: mad, drunk, slain, a fool, scattered smoke, a servant, a plucked bird. Every loss is a gain. In the last couplets the roles turn round: the fortune comes to him, the old love asks him to stay, and he melts like the shade of a willow when the sun strikes it. Behind the beloved, readers have always seen both Shams and God.

**The rhythm.** The ghazal dances. Its metre (*rajaz*) breaks each half-line into four short, equal pieces, *DUM-da-da-DUM* four times, and Rumi makes the pieces rhyme inside the line: *mor-de ba-dam / zen-de sho-dam / ger-ye ba-dam / khan-de sho-dam*. Every couplet ends in ـنده شدم (*-ande shodam*): پاینده (*pāyande*), تابنده (*tābande*), آکنده (*ākande*), افکنده (*afkande*)… It is easy to imagine it chanted in a circle of dancers, which is probably how it was first heard.

**The grammar.** Rumi uses short, almost spoken forms: بدم (*bodam*) for بودم (*budam*) and نیم (*niyam*) for نیستم (*nistam*). نه‌ای (*na-'i*) is نیستی (*nisti*), مرو (*marow*) and مشو (*masho*) are نرو (*naro*) and نشو (*nasho*), and مرا (*marā*) is به من (*be man*) 'to me'. The pattern دیده‌ی سیر است مرا (*dide-ye sir ast marā*) 'to me is a full eye' means 'I have a satisfied eye'. Couplet 3 in modern prose: گفت تو دیوانه نیستی و لایق این خانه نیستی؛ رفتم و دیوانه شدم و به زنجیر بسته شدم (*goft to divāne nisti va lāyegh-e in khāne nisti; raftam va divāne shodam va be zanjir baste shodam*).

**Using it.** مرده بدم زنده شدم، گریه بدم خنده شدم (*morde bodam zende shodam, gerye bodam khande shodam*) is one of the most sung lines in Persian – classical and pop singers alike have set it – and people quote it about any happy transformation: a recovery, a new love, a fresh start.
