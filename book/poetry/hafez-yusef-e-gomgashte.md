# Lost Joseph will come back
## یوسف گم‌گشته
Hafez (حافظ), Divan, 14th century. [Listen on the website](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/poetry/hafez-yusef-e-gomgashte.html)

This is one of the best-loved ghazals of Hafez (about 1315–1390), and perhaps the most consoling poem in Persian: every couplet ends with the same two words, *gham makhor*, 'do not grieve'. It begins with the story of Joseph (Yusof), which Iranians know from the Quran and from poetry. His jealous brothers threw him into a well and told their father Jacob that a wolf had eaten him; Jacob wept in his 'house of sorrows' in Canaan until he went blind, and years later Joseph's shirt, sent from Egypt, gave him back his sight. Hafez turns that story into a promise: what is lost comes back, the wheel of time turns, and no road is without an end.

### The poem

> یوسف گم‌گشته باز آید به کنعان غم مخور &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; کلبه‌ی احزان شود روزی گلستان غم مخور  
> ای دل غمدیده حالت به شود دل بد مکن &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; وین سر شوریده باز آید به سامان غم مخور  
> گر بهار عمر باشد باز بر تخت چمن &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; چتر گل در سر کشی ای مرغ خوشخوان غم مخور  
> دور گردون گر دو روزی بر مراد ما نرفت &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; دائماً یکسان نباشد حال دوران غم مخور  
> هان مشو نومید چون واقف نه‌ای از سر غیب &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; باشد اندر پرده بازی‌های پنهان غم مخور  
> ای دل ار سیل فنا بنیاد هستی برکند &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; چون تو را نوح است کشتیبان ز طوفان غم مخور  
> در بیابان گر به شوق کعبه خواهی زد قدم &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; سرزنش‌ها گر کند خار مغیلان غم مخور  
> گرچه منزل بس خطرناک است و مقصد بس بعید &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; هیچ راهی نیست کان را نیست پایان غم مخور  
> حال ما در فرقت جانان و ابرام رقیب &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; جمله می‌داند خدای حال‌گردان غم مخور  
> حافظا در کنج فقر و خلوت شب‌های تار &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; تا بود وردت دعا و درس قرآن غم مخور  

### Line by line

- یوسف گم‌گشته باز آید به کنعان غم مخور / کلبه‌ی احزان شود روزی گلستان غم مخور  
  *yusof-e gom-gashte bāz āyad be kan'ān gham makhor / kolbe-ye ahzān shavad ruzi golestān gham makhor* — Lost Joseph will come back to Canaan – do not grieve; / the house of sorrows will one day be a rose garden – do not grieve.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  یوسف *yusof* Joseph, the most beautiful of men · گم‌گشته *gom-gashte* lost – گم گشتن = گم شدن · کنعان *kan'ān* Canaan, Jacob's homeland · غم مخور *gham makhor* do not grieve (today غم نخور) · کلبه *kolbe* hut, cottage · احزان *ahzān* sorrows (Arabic plural of حزن) · گلستان *golestān* rose garden

  </details>
- ای دل غمدیده حالت به شود دل بد مکن / وین سر شوریده باز آید به سامان غم مخور  
  *ey del-e ghamdide hāl-at beh shavad del bad makon / vin sar-e shuride bāz āyad be sāmān gham makhor* — O grieving heart, you will feel better – do not lose heart; / this troubled head will come back to its senses – do not grieve.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  غمدیده *ghamdide* grief-stricken (lit. 'that has seen sorrow') · به شدن *beh shodan* to get better · دل بد کردن *del bad kardan* to lose heart, to despair · وین *vin* and this – و + این · شوریده *shuride* troubled, distraught · سامان *sāmān* order, a settled state

  </details>
- گر بهار عمر باشد باز بر تخت چمن / چتر گل در سر کشی ای مرغ خوشخوان غم مخور  
  *gar bahār-e 'omr bāshad bāz bar takht-e chaman / chatr-e gol dar sar keshi ey morgh-e khoshkhān gham makhor* — If life brings spring again, on the throne of the meadow / you will hold a parasol of roses over your head, sweet singer – do not grieve.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  گر *gar* if – short for اگر · تخت *takht* throne · چمن *chaman* meadow, lawn · چتر *chatr* parasol, royal canopy · در سر کشیدن *dar sar keshidan* to draw over one's head · مرغ *morgh* bird (today usually 'hen') · خوشخوان *khoshkhān* sweet-singing

  </details>
- دور گردون گر دو روزی بر مراد ما نرفت / دائماً یکسان نباشد حال دوران غم مخور  
  *dowr-e gardun gar do ruzi bar morād-e mā naraft / dā'eman yeksān nabāshad hāl-e dowrān gham makhor* — If the turning sky has gone against us for a day or two, / the times are not always the same – do not grieve.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  دور *dowr* turn, revolution, cycle · گردون *gardun* the turning sky – fate · دو روزی *do ruzi* a day or two, a short while · بر مراد کسی رفتن *bar morād-e kasi raftan* to go as someone wishes · دائماً *dā'eman* always (Arabic) · یکسان *yeksān* the same, unchanging · دوران *dowrān* the age, the times

  </details>
- هان مشو نومید چون واقف نه‌ای از سر غیب / باشد اندر پرده بازی‌های پنهان غم مخور  
  *hān masho nowmid chon vāghef na-'i az serr-e gheyb / bāshad andar parde bāzi-hā-ye penhān gham makhor* — Take care, do not despair: you do not know the secret of the unseen; / there may be hidden games behind the curtain – do not grieve.  
  _«پشت پرده» 'behind the curtain' is still the everyday Persian for what goes on out of sight._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  هان *hān* take care!, look! – an old interjection · مشو *masho* do not become (today نشو) · نومید *nowmid* hopeless – short for ناامید · واقف *vāghef* aware, informed · نه‌ای *na-'i* you are not – نیستی · سر غیب *serr-e gheyb* the secret of the unseen · پرده *parde* curtain, veil

  </details>
- ای دل ار سیل فنا بنیاد هستی برکند / چون تو را نوح است کشتیبان ز طوفان غم مخور  
  *ey del ar seyl-e fanā bonyād-e hasti bar-kanad / chon to rā nuh ast kashtibān ze tufān gham makhor* — O heart, if the flood of death tears up the foundations of being, / with Noah as your boatman, do not grieve over the Flood.  
  _طوفان here is the Flood of Noah; today it means any storm._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  ار *ar* if – short for اگر · سیل *seyl* flood · فنا *fanā* annihilation, passing away · بنیاد *bonyād* foundation · برکندن *bar-kandan* to tear up, to uproot · نوح *nuh* Noah · کشتیبان *kashtibān* boatman, captain

  </details>
- در بیابان گر به شوق کعبه خواهی زد قدم / سرزنش‌ها گر کند خار مغیلان غم مخور  
  *dar biyābān gar be showgh-e ka'be khāhi zad ghadam / sarzanesh-hā gar konad khār-e moghilān gham makhor* — If you set out across the desert, longing for the Kaaba, / and the acacia thorns reproach you – do not grieve.  
  _Pilgrims who crossed the Arabian desert were torn by acacia thorns; in poetry the thorns are the hardships, and the critics, on the way to any goal._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  بیابان *biyābān* desert · شوق *showgh* longing, eagerness · کعبه *ka'be* the Kaaba in Mecca · قدم زدن *ghadam zadan* to step out, to walk · سرزنش *sarzanesh* reproach, blame · خار مغیلان *khār-e moghilān* the thorns of the desert acacia

  </details>
- گرچه منزل بس خطرناک است و مقصد بس بعید / هیچ راهی نیست کان را نیست پایان غم مخور  
  *garche manzel bas khatarnāk ast o maghsad bas ba'id / hich rāhi nist kān rā nist pāyān gham makhor* — Though the stages are very dangerous and the goal very far, / there is no road that has no end – do not grieve.
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  گرچه *garche* although · منزل *manzel* stage of a journey, stopping place · بس *bas* very · مقصد *maghsad* destination, goal · بعید *ba'id* far, distant · کان *kān* that … it – که + آن · پایان *pāyān* end

  </details>
- حال ما در فرقت جانان و ابرام رقیب / جمله می‌داند خدای حال‌گردان غم مخور  
  *hāl-e mā dar forghat-e jānān o ebrām-e raghib / jomle mi-dānad khodā-ye hāl-gardān gham makhor* — How we fare, far from the beloved and pestered by the rival – / God, who changes every state, knows it all; do not grieve.  
  _The prayer Iranians say at the moment the New Year begins calls God «محول الحول و الاحوال», 'the one who changes the years and the states' – the same God as Hafez's خدای حال‌گردان._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  فرقت *forghat* separation · ابرام *ebrām* insistence, pestering · رقیب *raghib* the rival in love, the watchful guard · جمله *jomle* all, entirely (today 'sentence') · خدای *khodāy* God – the older form of خدا · حال‌گردان *hāl-gardān* who turns (changes) states

  </details>
- حافظا در کنج فقر و خلوت شب‌های تار / تا بود وردت دعا و درس قرآن غم مخور  
  *hāfezā dar konj-e faghr o khalvat-e shab-hā-ye tār / tā bovad verd-at do'ā vo dars-e ghor'ān gham makhor* — Hafez, in your corner of poverty and in the solitude of dark nights, / as long as prayer and the Quran are your daily practice, do not grieve.  
  _Hafez's pen name means 'one who knows the Quran by heart', which he did._
  <details><summary>Word by word</summary>

  حافظا *hāfezā* O Hafez! – ـا of address · کنج *konj* corner, nook · فقر *faghr* poverty · خلوت *khalvat* solitude, privacy · تار *tār* dark · تا *tā* as long as · ورد *verd* a prayer said every day, a daily practice

  </details>

### Reading the poem

**The images.** Hafez gathers every picture of hope he can find. First the stories everyone knows: Joseph coming home to Canaan, Jacob's hut of sorrows turning into a rose garden, Noah steering his ark through the Flood. Then nature: a songbird that will sit enthroned in the meadow, under a parasol of roses, when spring comes back. Then the journey: a pilgrim crossing the desert to Mecca, torn by thorns, on a road that may be long and dangerous but must have an end. And above all the turning sky, دور گردون (*dowr-e gardun*), which never stays still – what has gone against us will come round again.

**The rhythm.** The ghazal is in *ramal*, a long, rolling metre: *DUM-da-DUM-DUM DUM-da-DUM-DUM DUM-da-DUM-DUM DUM-da-DUM*. Listen to *yu-so-fe gom-gash-te bā-zā-yad be kan-'ān gham ma-khor*. Every couplet ends in the same rhyme – کنعان (*kan'ān*), گلستان (*golestān*), سامان (*sāmān*), خوشخوان (*khoshkhān*), دوران (*dowrān*) – followed by the same refrain, غم مخور (*gham makhor*): a ردیف (*radif*) so strong that the whole poem feels like one long, patient reassurance.

**The grammar.** The language is close to today's. The old negative imperative uses م (*ma-*): مخور (*makhor*) 'don't eat', مشو (*masho*) 'don't become', مکن (*makon*) 'don't do'. گر (*gar*) and ار (*ar*) are short for اگر (*agar*); نه‌ای (*na-'i*) is نیستی (*nisti*) 'you are not'; نومید (*nowmid*) is ناامید (*nā-omid*). And غم خوردن (*gham khordan*), 'to eat sorrow', is still the everyday word for 'to worry'. Couplet 4 in modern prose: اگر چرخ روزگار دو روزی به میل ما نچرخید، غصه نخور؛ اوضاع دنیا همیشه یکسان نمی‌ماند (*agar charkh-e ruzgār do ruzi be meyl-e mā nacharkhid, ghosse nakhor; owzā'-e donyā hamishe yeksān nemi-mānad*).

**Using it.** This is the ghazal Iranians send to a friend in trouble, and many people know its first line by heart. یوسف گم‌گشته باز آید به کنعان (*yusof-e gom-gashte bāz āyad be kan'ān*) is said when someone long absent comes home, and هیچ راهی نیست کان را نیست پایان (*hich rāhi nist kān rā nist pāyān*) to anyone who thinks a hard time will never end.
