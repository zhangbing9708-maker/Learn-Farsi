# Time and dates: days, months and birthdays
## ساعت و تاریخ
*sā'at o tārikh* — Beginner (A1), lesson 12. [Open this lesson with audio and interactive exercises](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/course/a1-12-time-and-dates.html)

What time is it, what day is it, when is your birthday? In this lesson you learn to tell the time with sā'at, the seven days of the Iranian week – which starts on Saturday – and the twelve months of the Iranian solar calendar, from Farvardin in spring to Esfand in winter. You also find out why the Iranian year begins on the first day of spring, with Nowruz.

**In this lesson you will learn to:**

- ask and tell the time: sā'at chand ast? – sā'at do o nim ast
- name the days of the week, from shanbe to jom'e
- name the twelve Iranian months and their seasons
- say dates and birthdays: tavallod-e man dahom-e Dey ast
- ask 'when?' with key

### Two birthdays

_Tom and Mina meet in a corridor at the University of Tehran. Tom wants to know the time – and finds out something about Mina's birthday._

- **Tom:** ببخشید، مینا، ساعت چند است؟  
  *bebakhshid, Minā, sā'at chand ast?* — Excuse me, Mina, what time is it?  
  Spoken: ببخشید، مینا، ساعت چنده؟ *bebakhshid, Minā, sā'at chande?*
- **Mina:** ساعت ده و ربع است. کلاس من ساعت ده و نیم است!  
  *sā'at dah o rob' ast. kelās-e man sā'at-e dah o nim ast!* — It's a quarter past ten. My class is at half past ten!  
  Spoken: ساعت ده و ربعه. کلاسم ساعت ده و نیمه! *sā'at dah o rob'e. kelās-am sā'at-e dah o nime!*
- **Tom:** امروز چندشنبه است؟ دوشنبه؟  
  *emruz chand-shanbe ast? do-shanbe?* — What day is it today? Monday?
- **Mina:** نه، امروز سه‌شنبه است. سه‌شنبه‌ها ساعت ده و نیم کلاس دارم.  
  *na, emruz se-shanbe ast. se-shanbe-hā sā'at-e dah o nim kelās dāram.* — No, today is Tuesday. On Tuesdays I have a class at half past ten.
- **Tom:** راستی، تولد تو کِی است؟  
  *rāsti, tavallod-e to key ast?* — By the way, when is your birthday?  
  Spoken: راستی، تولدت کِیه؟ *rāsti, tavallod-et keye?*
- **Mina:** تولد من بیست و پنجم آبان است.  
  *tavallod-e man bist o panjom-e Ābān ast.* — My birthday is on the twenty-fifth of Aban.
- **Tom:** آبان کدام ماه است؟  
  *Ābān kodām māh ast?* — Which month is Aban?
- **Mina:** ماه هشتم سال، بعد از مهر. بیست و پنجم آبان معمولاً شانزدهم نوامبر است.  
  *māh-e hashtom-e sāl, ba'd az Mehr. bist o panjom-e Ābān ma'mulan shānzdahom-e novāmbr ast.* — The eighth month of the year, after Mehr. The twenty-fifth of Aban is usually the sixteenth of November.
- **Tom:** شانزدهم نوامبر؟ تولد من هم شانزدهم نوامبر است!  
  *shānzdahom-e novāmbr? tavallod-e man ham shānzdahom-e novāmbr ast!* — The sixteenth of November? My birthday is on the sixteenth of November too!
- **Mina:** واقعاً؟ پس روز تولد ما یکی است!  
  *vāghe'an? pas ruz-e tavallod-e mā yeki ast!* — Really? So our birthdays are on the same day!  
  Spoken: واقعاً؟ پس روز تولدمون یکیه! *vāghe'an? pas ruz-e tavallod-emun yeki-ye!*
- **Tom:** جشن می‌گیری؟  
  *jashn mi-giri?* — Are you having a party?
- **Mina:** بله، جمعه، ساعت هفت شب، در خانه‌ی ما. تو هم دعوتی!  
  *bale, jom'e, sā'at-e haft-e shab, dar khāne-ye mā. to ham da'vat-i!* — Yes, on Friday at seven in the evening, at our house. You're invited too!
- **Tom:** ممنون! اما الان ساعت ده و بیست و پنج دقیقه است!  
  *mamnun! ammā alān sā'at dah o bist o panj daghighe ast!* — Thanks! But it's twenty-five past ten now!
- **Mina:** وای، کلاس! خدا حافظ!  
  *vāy, kelās! khodā hāfez!* — Oh no, my class! Bye!

### Sā'at: hour, clock, watch

The word ساعت (*sā'at*) does a lot of work. It means *hour* (دو ساعت (*do sā'at*, ‘two hours’)), *clock* and *watch* (ساعت من (*sā'at-e man*, ‘my watch’)), and it is the word you put before a time: ساعت ده است. (*sā'at dah ast.*, ‘It is ten o'clock.’)

Tom's question word for time is کِی (*key*, ‘when’). In ordinary Persian it is written exactly like کی (*ki*, ‘who’), with no vowel mark, and the context tells you which one is meant. In this course we write it **کِی** to help you.

راستی (*rāsti*, ‘by the way’) (from *rāst*, true) is how Iranians change the subject. And when it is someone's birthday, you say تولدت مبارک! (*tavallod-at mobārak!*, ‘happy birthday!’) – literally *your birth is blessed*. مبارک (*mobārak*) works for every celebration: عید مبارک! (*eyd mobārak!*, ‘happy holiday!’), نوروز مبارک! (*nowruz mobārak!*, ‘happy Nowruz!’)

### What time is it? sā'at chand ast?

Ask ساعت چند است؟ (*sā'at chand ast?*, ‘what time is it?’) – literally *the hour is how many?* – and answer with **sā'at + number + ast**: ساعت سه است. (*sā'at se ast.*, ‘It's three o'clock.’) In speech the question is *sā'at chande?*

Minutes **past** the hour are added with و (*o*, ‘and’); minutes **to** the hour use به (*be*, ‘to’), just as in English: ساعت سه و ده دقیقه است. (*sā'at se o dah daghighe ast.*, ‘It's ten past three.’) – ساعت ده دقیقه به چهار است. (*sā'at dah daghighe be chahār ast.*, ‘It's ten to four.’) A quarter is ربع (*rob'*), half is نیم (*nim*).

To say *at* a time, link **sā'at** to the number with **-e**: ساعت سه (*sā'at-e se*, ‘at three’). The question works the same way: کلاس ساعت چند است؟ (*kelās sā'at-e chand ast?*, ‘What time is the class?’) Add the part of the day with another **-e**: ساعت هفت صبح (*sā'at-e haft-e sobh*, ‘at seven in the morning’), ساعت هشت شب (*sā'at-e hasht-e shab*, ‘at eight in the evening’).

| Persian | Time | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ساعت دو است. (*sā'at do ast.*) | 2:00 | It's two o'clock. |
| ساعت دو و ده دقیقه است. (*sā'at do o dah daghighe ast.*) | 2:10 | It's ten past two. |
| ساعت دو و ربع است. (*sā'at do o rob' ast.*) | 2:15 | It's a quarter past two. |
| ساعت دو و نیم است. (*sā'at do o nim ast.*) | 2:30 | It's half past two. |
| ساعت یک ربع به سه است. (*sā'at yek rob' be se ast.*) | 2:45 | It's a quarter to three. |
| ساعت ده دقیقه به سه است. (*sā'at dah daghighe be se ast.*) | 2:50 | It's ten to three. |

- ساعت چند است؟ ساعت هشت و نیم است.  
  *sā'at chand ast? sā'at hasht o nim ast.* — What time is it? It's half past eight.  
  Spoken: ساعت چنده؟ ساعت هشت و نیمه. *sā'at chande? sā'at hasht o nime.*
- کلاس ساعت چند است؟ ساعت نه.  
  *kelās sā'at-e chand ast? sā'at-e noh.* — What time is the class? At nine.
- من ساعت هفت صبح بیدار می‌شوم.  
  *man sā'at-e haft-e sobh bidār mi-shavam.* — I get up at seven in the morning.
- بانک از ساعت هشت تا ساعت دو باز است.  
  *bānk az sā'at-e hasht tā sā'at-e do bāz ast.* — The bank is open from eight to two.
- فیلم دو ساعت و نیم است.  
  *film do sā'at o nim ast.* — The film is two and a half hours long.

### The week: from shanbe to jom'e

The Iranian week begins on **Saturday**, شنبه (*shanbe*). The next five days are simply counted from it – *one-shanbe, two-shanbe…* – so with the numbers you already know, you know the week. Only Friday has a name of its own: جمعه (*jom'e*), *the day of gathering*, the weekly holiday.

To ask what day it is, Persian asks *how-many-shanbe?*: امروز چندشنبه است؟ (*emruz chand-shanbe ast?*, ‘What day is it today?’) For *on Monday* no preposition is needed – دوشنبه به اصفهان می‌روم. (*do-shanbe be Esfahān mi-ravam.*, ‘On Monday I'm going to Isfahan.’) – or you can say روز دوشنبه (*ruz-e do-shanbe*, ‘on Monday’). Add **-hā** for a habit: جمعه‌ها (*jom'e-hā*, ‘on Fridays’).

| Persian | Reading | English | Literally |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| شنبه (*shanbe*) | shanbe | Saturday | from 'Sabbath' |
| یک‌شنبه (*yek-shanbe*) | yek-shanbe | Sunday | one (after) shanbe |
| دوشنبه (*do-shanbe*) | do-shanbe | Monday | two (after) shanbe |
| سه‌شنبه (*se-shanbe*) | se-shanbe | Tuesday | three (after) shanbe |
| چهارشنبه (*chahār-shanbe*) | chahār-shanbe | Wednesday | four (after) shanbe |
| پنج‌شنبه (*panj-shanbe*) | panj-shanbe | Thursday | five (after) shanbe |
| جمعه (*jom'e*) | jom'e | Friday | (the day of) gathering |

- امروز چندشنبه است؟ امروز چهارشنبه است.  
  *emruz chand-shanbe ast? emruz chahār-shanbe ast.* — What day is it today? Today is Wednesday.
- فردا پنج‌شنبه است.  
  *fardā panj-shanbe ast.* — Tomorrow is Thursday.
- جمعه‌ها به خانه‌ی مادربزرگم می‌روم.  
  *jom'e-hā be khāne-ye mādar-bozorg-am mi-ravam.* — On Fridays I go to my grandmother's house.
- کلاس فارسی شنبه و دوشنبه است.  
  *kelās-e fārsi shanbe va do-shanbe ast.* — The Persian class is on Saturday and Monday.

### Months, dates and birthdays

The Iranian year has twelve months, and each season has exactly three. The year begins with spring, on the first day of فروردین (*Farvardin*) – about 21 March – which is نوروز (*nowruz*, ‘New Year’). The first six months have 31 days, the next five have 30, and اسفند (*Esfand*) has 29 (30 in a leap year).

For a date, turn the number into a *-th* word by adding **-om**: پنج (*panj*, ‘five’) → پنجم (*panjom*, ‘fifth’), ده (*dah*, ‘ten’) → دهم (*dahom*, ‘tenth’), بیست و یک (*bist o yek*, ‘twenty-one’) → بیست و یکم (*bist o yekom*, ‘twenty-first’). Two are slightly irregular: دوم (*dovvom*, ‘second’) and سوم (*sevvom*, ‘third’). Then link the date to the month with **-e**: پنجم مهر (*panjom-e Mehr*, ‘the fifth of Mehr’). The first of the month is usually اول (*avval*): اول فروردین (*avval-e Farvardin*, ‘the first of Farvardin’).

*In* a month is در (*dar*): در اسفند (*dar Esfand*, ‘in Esfand’). *What's the date today?* is امروز چندم است؟ (*emruz chandom ast?*) – literally *today is the how-many-th?*

| Month | No. | Season | Begins around |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| فروردین (*Farvardin*) | 1 | بهار (*bahār*) spring | 21 March |
| اردیبهشت (*Ordibehesht*) | 2 | spring | 21 April |
| خرداد (*Khordād*) | 3 | spring | 22 May |
| تیر (*Tir*) | 4 | تابستان (*tābestān*) summer | 22 June |
| مرداد (*Mordād*) | 5 | summer | 23 July |
| شهریور (*Shahrivar*) | 6 | summer | 23 August |
| مهر (*Mehr*) | 7 | پاییز (*pāyiz*) autumn | 23 September |
| آبان (*Ābān*) | 8 | autumn | 23 October |
| آذر (*Āzar*) | 9 | autumn | 22 November |
| دی (*Dey*) | 10 | زمستان (*zemestān*) winter | 22 December |
| بهمن (*Bahman*) | 11 | winter | 21 January |
| اسفند (*Esfand*) | 12 | winter | 20 February |

- امروز چندم است؟ امروز دهم دی است.  
  *emruz chandom ast? emruz dahom-e Dey ast.* — What's the date today? Today is the tenth of Dey.
- تولد تو کِی است؟ سوم خرداد.  
  *tavallod-e to key ast? sevvom-e Khordād.* — When is your birthday? The third of Khordad.  
  Spoken: تولدت کِیه؟ سوم خرداد. *tavallod-et keye? sevvom-e Khordād.*
- مدرسه‌ها اول مهر باز می‌شوند.  
  *madrese-hā avval-e Mehr bāz mi-shavand.* — Schools open on the first of Mehr.
- تولد من در تیر است، در تابستان.  
  *tavallod-e man dar Tir ast, dar tābestān.* — My birthday is in Tir, in the summer.
- تولدت مبارک!  
  *tavallod-at mobārak!* — Happy birthday!  
  Spoken: تولدت مبارک! *tavallod-et mobārak!*

### Nowruz

_How Iranians celebrate the new year. Read and answer the questions._

- نوروز روز اول سال ایرانی است.  
  *nowruz ruz-e avval-e sāl-e irāni ast.* — Nowruz is the first day of the Iranian year.
- سال نو اول فروردین، روز اول بهار، شروع می‌شود.  
  *sāl-e now avval-e Farvardin, ruz-e avval-e bahār, shoru' mi-shavad.* — The new year begins on the first of Farvardin, the first day of spring.

### One country, three calendars

Iran's official calendar is the solar هجری شمسی (*hejri-ye shamsi*) calendar. It counts the years from 622 CE, when the Prophet Muhammad moved from Mecca to Medina, but its years follow the sun, so it runs about 621 years behind the European calendar: the year ۱۴۰۵ (*1405*) began on 21 March 2026. Because the year starts at the spring equinox, the seasons always fall in the same months – which is why Iranians like to say their calendar is the most natural in the world.

The month names are far older than Islam. They come from the Zoroastrian calendar of ancient Iran: مهر (*Mehr*) is Mithra, the spirit of friendship and promises (the word still means *kindness*); آبان (*Ābān*) means *the waters*; آذر (*Āzar*) is *fire*; بهمن (*Bahman*) is *good thought*.

Two other calendars run alongside. The lunar Islamic calendar, قمری (*ghamari*), sets the religious holidays, which move about eleven days earlier every year; the European calendar, میلادی (*milādi*), is used for business abroad. An Iranian diary often shows all three dates on every page.

The biggest date of all is نوروز (*nowruz*, ‘new day’). The exact moment of the equinox, سال تحویل (*sāl-tahvil*), can fall at any hour, and families gather around the هفت‌سین (*haft-sin*) – a spread of seven things whose names begin with the letter *sin* (س) – to wait for it, sometimes in the middle of the night.

### Tips

- Don't mix up **ki** 'who' and **key** 'when': both are normally written کی. *ki mi-āyad?* 'who is coming?' – *key mi-āyad?* 'when is he coming?'
- The **'** in *sā'at* and *rob'* is a tiny catch in the throat, like the break in *uh-oh*. In fast speech it almost disappears: *sāat*.
- Timetables, the TV and airports use the 24-hour clock: *sā'at-e chahārdah* is 14:00. In conversation people use the 12-hour clock and add *sobh*, *ba'd az zohr* or *shab* when it is not obvious.
- Iranians often give dates in both calendars. To convert a year roughly, add 621 to the Iranian year (622 for dates between January and March) – and remember that the year turns on 21 March.
- Learn the months in their seasons, three at a time: *Farvardin, Ordibehesht, Khordād* for spring, and so on. Lesson 15 uses them again for the weather.

4. تولد تو ___ است؟ (*tavallod-e to ___ ast?*) — When is your birthday? (hint: when)
5. تولدت ___! (*tavallod-at ___!*) — Happy birthday! (hint: blessed)

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. و
2. چهارشنبه
3. فروردین
4. کِی
5. مبارک

</details>

### Put the words in order

**Put the words in order**

1. است؟ / تو / کِی / تولد — When is your birthday?
2. ساعت / سه / ربع / است. / و — It's a quarter past three.
3. به / جمعه‌ها / مادربزرگم / می‌روم. / خانه‌ی — On Fridays I go to my grandmother's house.
4. می‌شوند. / مهر / مدرسه‌ها / اول / باز — Schools open on the first of Mehr.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. تولد تو کِی است؟
2. ساعت سه و ربع است.
3. جمعه‌ها به خانه‌ی مادربزرگم می‌روم.
4. مدرسه‌ها اول مهر باز می‌شوند.

</details>
