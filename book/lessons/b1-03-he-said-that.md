# He said that…: reported speech
## گفت که می‌آید
*goft ke mi-āyad* — Intermediate (B1), lesson 3. [Open this lesson with audio and interactive exercises](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/course/b1-03-he-said-that.html)

English shifts the tense when it reports: 'I'm tired' becomes 'she said she was tired'. Persian doesn't. It keeps the original tense and simply changes the person: گفت که خسته است – literally 'she said that she is tired'. In this lesson you learn to pass on messages, report questions with آیا and 'or not', report requests and orders with the subjunctive, and use a dozen verbs that say more than گفتن: explain, promise, insist, deny, warn.

**In this lesson you will learn to:**

- report what someone said without changing the tense: گفت که می‌آید
- report yes/no questions with آیا or … یا نه, and wh-questions without changing the word order
- report requests and orders with خواستن and the subjunctive: از ما خواست که صبر کنیم
- use reporting verbs such as توضیح دادن، قول دادن، اصرار کردن، انکار کردن، هشدار دادن
- recognise the perfect as the tense of hearsay: گفت که دیروز رفته است

### The landlord called

_Tom and his flatmate Amir rent a flat in Tehran. Tom comes home, and Amir passes on a message from the landlord, Mr Karimi. Notice that every reported sentence keeps the tense the landlord used._

- **Amir:** تام، صاحب‌خانه زنگ زد.  
  *Tām, sāheb-khāne zang zad.* — Tom, the landlord rang.  
  Spoken: تام، صاحب‌خونه زنگ زد. *Tām, sāheb-khune zang zad.*
- **Tom:** آقای کریمی؟ چه گفت؟  
  *āghā-ye Karimi? che goft?* — Mr Karimi? What did he say?  
  Spoken: آقای کریمی؟ چی گفت؟ *āghā-ye Karimi? chi goft?*
- **Amir:** گفت که فردا ساعت ده برای دیدن آشپزخانه می‌آید.  
  *goft ke fardā sā'at-e dah barā-ye didan-e āshpazkhāne mi-āyad.* — He said he'd come tomorrow at ten to look at the kitchen.  
  Spoken: گفت فردا ساعت ده میاد آشپزخونه رو ببینه. *goft fardā sā'at-e dah miyād āshpazkhune ro bebine.*
- **Tom:** برای چه؟ آشپزخانه که مشکلی ندارد.  
  *barā-ye che? āshpazkhāne ke moshkel-i nadārad.* — What for? There's nothing wrong with the kitchen.  
  Spoken: واسه چی؟ آشپزخونه که مشکلی نداره. *vāse chi? āshpazkhune ke moshkel-i nadāre.*
- **Amir:** گفت همسایه‌ی طبقه‌ی پایین شکایت کرده است که از سقفش آب می‌چکد.  
  *goft hamsāye-ye tabaghe-ye pāyin shekāyat karde ast ke az saghf-ash āb mi-chekad.* — He said the neighbour downstairs has complained that water is dripping from his ceiling.  
  Spoken: گفت همسایه‌ی طبقه‌ی پایین شکایت کرده که از سقفش آب می‌چکه. *goft hamsāye-ye tabaghe-ye pāyin shekāyat karde ke az saghf-ash āb mi-cheke.*
- **Tom:** وای! دیگر چه گفت؟  
  *vāy! digar che goft?* — Oh no! What else did he say?  
  Spoken: وای! دیگه چی گفت؟ *vāy! dige chi goft?*
- **Amir:** پرسید که آیا اجاره‌ی این ماه را پرداخته‌ایم.  
  *porsid ke āyā ejāre-ye in māh rā pardākhte-im.* — He asked whether we had paid this month's rent.  
  Spoken: پرسید اجاره‌ی این ماهو دادیم یا نه. *porsid ejāre-ye in māh-o dādim yā na.*
- **Tom:** تو چه جواب دادی؟  
  *to che javāb dādi?* — What did you tell him?  
  Spoken: تو چی جواب دادی؟ *to chi javāb dādi?*
- **Amir:** گفتم که تو دیروز پول را به حسابش واریز کرده‌ای.  
  *goftam ke to diruz pul rā be hesāb-ash vāriz karde-i.* — I said you'd paid the money into his account yesterday.  
  Spoken: گفتم تو دیروز پولو ریختی به حسابش. *goftam to diruz pul-o rikhti be hesāb-esh.*
- **Tom:** درست است. چیز دیگری نپرسید؟  
  *dorost ast. chiz-e digar-i naporsid?* — That's right. Didn't he ask anything else?  
  Spoken: درسته. چیز دیگه‌ای نپرسید؟ *doroste. chiz-e dige-i naporsid?*
- **Amir:** چرا، پرسید کی قرارداد را تمدید می‌کنیم و گفت که از ماه بعد اجاره را بالا می‌برد.  
  *cherā, porsid key gharārdād rā tamdid mi-konim o goft ke az māh-e ba'd ejāre rā bālā mi-barad.* — Yes – he asked when we were renewing the contract, and said he's putting the rent up from next month.  
  Spoken: چرا، پرسید کی قراردادو تمدید می‌کنیم و گفت از ماه بعد اجاره رو می‌بره بالا. *cherā, porsid key gharārdād-o tamdid mi-konim o goft az māh-e ba'd ejāre ro mi-bare bālā.*
- **Tom:** همیشه همین را می‌گوید! به او بگو که اول باید سقف را درست کند.  
  *hamishe hamin rā mi-guyad! be u begu ke avval bāyad saghf rā dorost konad.* — He always says that! Tell him he has to fix the ceiling first.  
  Spoken: همیشه همینو می‌گه! بهش بگو اول باید سقفو درست کنه. *hamishe hamin-o mi-ge! behesh begu avval bāyad saghf-o dorost kone.*
- **Amir:** باشد، ولی فردا خودت به او بگو. من ساعت ده کلاس دارم!  
  *bāshad, vali fardā khod-at be u begu. man sā'at-e dah kelās dāram!* — OK, but tomorrow you tell him yourself. I've got a class at ten!  
  Spoken: باشه، ولی فردا خودت بهش بگو. من ساعت ده کلاس دارم! *bāshe, vali fardā khod-et behesh begu. man sā'at-e dah kelās dāram!*

### No tense shift

When English reports speech, it moves the verb back in time: *'I'm coming tomorrow'* → *he said he was coming*. Persian keeps the verb exactly as it was spoken and changes only the **person**:

- Mr Karimi: فردا می‌آیم. (*fardā mi-āyam.*, ‘I'm coming tomorrow.’)
- Amir: گفت که فردا می‌آید. (*goft ke fardā mi-āyad.*, ‘He said he would come tomorrow.’)

Literally, *he said that he comes tomorrow*. The word که (*ke*, ‘that’) is optional, and in speech it is usually dropped: گفت فردا میاد (*goft fardā miyād*).

This makes reported speech much easier than in English: no *was* for *is*, no *would* for *will*, no *had* for *has*. What you need instead is a good stock of **reporting verbs** – ask, answer, explain, promise, insist – and two small patterns for questions and requests.

### Same tense, new person

To report a statement, use گفتن (*goftan*) (or another reporting verb) + optional که (*ke*) + the sentence **in its original tense**. Change *I* to *he* or *she*, *you* to *I*, and so on – just as in English – but leave the tense alone.

One thing is different: when someone tells you about a **past** event, Persian very often reports it with the **perfect**: دیروز رفتم (*diruz raftam*, ‘I went yesterday’) → گفت که دیروز رفته است (*goft ke diruz rafte ast*, ‘he said he had gone yesterday’). The perfect is the Persian tense of hearsay – of things you know only because someone told you. The simple past (گفت که دیروز رفت (*goft ke diruz raft*)) is also correct.

In conversation people also quote directly, keeping the original *I*: گفت: «من فردا میام.» (*goft: «man fardā miyām.»*, ‘he said, 'I'm coming tomorrow'’). The person ending of the verb tells you which one you are hearing.

### Reporting questions, requests and orders

**Yes/no questions.** In writing, put آیا (*āyā*, ‘whether’) after پرسید که (*porsid ke*, ‘he asked’): پرسید که آیا می‌آیم (*porsid ke āyā mi-āyam*, ‘he asked whether I was coming’). In speech, leave out آیا and add یا نه (*yā na*, ‘or not’) at the end: پرسید میام یا نه (*porsid miyām yā na*).

**Wh-questions** keep their question word where it was – there is no change of word order, unlike English: کجا زندگی می‌کنی؟ (*kojā zendegi mi-koni?*, ‘where do you live?’) → پرسید کجا زندگی می‌کنم (*porsid kojā zendegi mi-konam*, ‘he asked where I lived’). Never use آیا here.

**Requests and orders** become a **subjunctive**: بنشین! (*beneshin!*, ‘sit down!’) → گفت که بنشینم (*goft ke beneshinam*, ‘he told me to sit down’); لطفاً صبر کنید (*lotfan sabr konid*, ‘please wait’) → از ما خواست که صبر کنیم (*az mā khāst ke sabr konim*, ‘she asked us to wait’).

Mind the prepositions: you ask a question *from* someone (از کسی پرسیدن (*az kas-i porsidan*)), ask *from* someone to do something (از کسی خواستن (*az kas-i khāstan*)), but tell *to* someone (به کسی گفتن (*be kas-i goftan*)).

| Direct question or order | Reported | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| «می‌آیی؟» (*«mi-āyi?»*) | پرسید که آیا می‌آیم. (*porsid ke āyā mi-āyam.*) | He asked whether I was coming. |
| «میای؟» (*«miyāy?»*) (spoken) | پرسید میام یا نه. (*porsid miyām yā na.*) | He asked if I was coming or not. |
| «کجا زندگی می‌کنی؟» (*«kojā zendegi mi-koni?»*) | پرسید کجا زندگی می‌کنم. (*porsid kojā zendegi mi-konam.*) | He asked where I lived. |
| «چرا دیر کردی؟» (*«cherā dir kardi?»*) | پرسید چرا دیر کرده‌ام. (*porsid cherā dir karde-am.*) | She asked why I was late. |
| «کی برمی‌گردی؟» (*«key bar-mi-gardi?»*) | پرسید کی برمی‌گردم. (*porsid key bar-mi-gardam.*) | He asked when I was coming back. |
| «چند سالت است؟» (*«chand sāl-at ast?»*) | پرسید چند سالم است. (*porsid chand sāl-am ast.*) | She asked how old I was. |
| «بنشین!» (*«beneshin!»*) | گفت که بنشینم. (*goft ke beneshinam.*) | He told me to sit down. |

### More precise than گفتن: reporting verbs

گفتن (*goftan*) can report anything, but good Persian – and every news bulletin – uses more precise verbs. Almost all of them are compounds with کردن (*kardan*) or دادن (*dādan*), and all can be followed by که (*ke*) and a clause.

Verbs that look forward to an action that has not happened yet – *promise, insist, suggest, ask (someone to do)* – take the **subjunctive**: قول داد که زود بیاید (*ghowl dād ke zud biyāyad*, ‘he promised to come early’). Verbs that report a fact – *explain, admit, deny, claim, announce* – keep the ordinary tense: اعتراف کرد که دروغ گفته است (*e'terāf kard ke dorugh gofte ast*, ‘he admitted that he had lied’).

| Verb | Pattern | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| گفتن (*goftan*) | به کسی گفت که… (*be kas-i goft ke…*) | to say; to tell (someone) |
| پرسیدن (*porsidan*) | از کسی پرسید که (آیا)… (*az kas-i porsid ke (āyā)…*) | to ask (a question) |
| خواستن (*khāstan*) | از کسی خواست که… (*az kas-i khāst ke…*) | to ask (someone to do something) – + subjunctive |
| جواب دادن (*javāb dādan*) | جواب داد که… (*javāb dād ke…*) | to answer |
| توضیح دادن (*towzih dādan*) | توضیح داد که… (*towzih dād ke…*) | to explain |
| قول دادن (*ghowl dādan*) | قول داد که… (*ghowl dād ke…*) | to promise – + subjunctive |
| اصرار کردن (*esrār kardan*) | اصرار کرد که… (*esrār kard ke…*) | to insist – + subjunctive |
| پیشنهاد کردن (*pishnahād kardan*) | پیشنهاد کرد که… (*pishnahād kard ke…*) | to suggest – + subjunctive |
| هشدار دادن (*hoshdār dādan*) | هشدار داد که… (*hoshdār dād ke…*) | to warn |
| ادعا کردن (*edde'ā kardan*) | ادعا کرد که… (*edde'ā kard ke…*) | to claim |
| اعتراف کردن (*e'terāf kardan*) | اعتراف کرد که… (*e'terāf kard ke…*) | to admit |
| انکار کردن (*enkār kardan*) | انکار کرد که… (*enkār kard ke…*) | to deny |
| اعلام کردن (*e'lām kardan*) | اعلام کرد که… (*e'lām kard ke…*) | to announce |
| تأکید کردن (*ta'kid kardan*) | تأکید کرد که… (*ta'kid kard ke…*) | to stress, emphasise |

- رضا قول داد که دیگر دیر نکند.  
  *Rezā ghowl dād ke digar dir nakonad.* — Reza promised not to be late again.  
  Spoken: رضا قول داد دیگه دیر نکنه. *Rezā ghowl dād dige dir nakone.*
- دوستم پیشنهاد کرد که با قطار به مشهد برویم.  
  *dust-am pishnahād kard ke bā ghatār be Mashhad beravim.* — My friend suggested we go to Mashhad by train.  
  Spoken: دوستم پیشنهاد داد با قطار بریم مشهد. *dust-am pishnahād dād bā ghatār berim Mashhad.*
- متهم انکار کرد که آن شب در خانه بوده است.  
  *mottaham enkār kard ke ān shab dar khāne bude ast.* — The accused denied that he had been at home that night.
- هواشناسی هشدار داد که فردا برف سنگینی می‌بارد.  
  *havāshenāsi hoshdār dād ke fardā barf-e sangin-i mi-bārad.* — The weather service warned that heavy snow will fall tomorrow.
- مهمان‌ها اصرار کردند که ما هم با آن‌ها شام بخوریم.  
  *mehmān-hā esrār kardand ke mā ham bā ān-hā shām bokhorim.* — The guests insisted that we have dinner with them too.  
  Spoken: مهمونا اصرار کردن ما هم باهاشون شام بخوریم. *mehmunā esrār kardan mā ham bāhāshun shām bokhorim.*
- وزیر آموزش و پرورش اعلام کرد که مدرسه‌ها شنبه باز می‌شوند.  
  *vazir-e āmuzesh o parvaresh e'lām kard ke madrese-hā shanbe bāz mi-shavand.* — The education minister announced that schools would open on Saturday.
- بالاخره اعتراف کرد که اشتباه کرده است.  
  *bel-akhare e'terāf kard ke eshtebāh karde ast.* — In the end he admitted that he had made a mistake.  
  Spoken: آخرش اعتراف کرد که اشتباه کرده. *ākhar-esh e'terāf kard ke eshtebāh karde.*

### The interview

_Leila tells the story of her job interview – entirely in reported speech. Notice آیا in the yes/no questions, the subjunctive after خواستن and قول دادن, and the perfect for things that had already happened._

- لیلا دیروز برای کار در یک شرکت نرم‌افزاری مصاحبه داشت.  
  *Leylā diruz barā-ye kār dar yek sherkat-e narm-afzāri mosāhebe dāsht.* — Yesterday Leila had an interview for a job at a software company.
- مدیر شرکت اول از او پرسید که چند سال سابقه‌ی کار دارد.  
  *modir-e sherkat avval az u porsid ke chand sāl sābeghe-ye kār dārad.* — The manager first asked her how many years of work experience she had.
- لیلا گفت که پنج سال در یک بانک کار کرده است.  
  *Leylā goft ke panj sāl dar yek bānk kār karde ast.* — Leila said she had worked in a bank for five years.
- مدیر پرسید چرا کار قبلی‌اش را رها کرده است.  
  *modir porsid cherā kār-e ghabli-ash rā rahā karde ast.* — The manager asked why she had left her previous job.
- لیلا توضیح داد که در آن بانک فرصتی برای پیشرفت نداشته است.  
  *Leylā towzih dād ke dar ān bānk forsat-i barā-ye pishraft nadāshte ast.* — Leila explained that at the bank she had had no chance to move up.
- مدیر پرسید که آیا می‌تواند گاهی آخر هفته‌ها هم کار کند.  
  *modir porsid ke āyā mi-tavānad gāhi ākhar-e hafte-hā ham kār konad.* — The manager asked whether she could sometimes work at weekends too.
- لیلا جواب داد که اگر لازم باشد، مشکلی ندارد.  
  *Leylā javāb dād ke agar lāzem bāshad, moshkel-i nadārad.* — Leila answered that if it was necessary, she didn't mind.
- بعد از او خواستند که درباره‌ی بزرگ‌ترین اشتباهش صحبت کند.  
  *ba'd az u khāstand ke darbāre-ye bozorg-tarin eshtebāh-ash sohbat konad.* — Then they asked her to talk about her biggest mistake.
- لیلا با خنده تعریف کرد که یک بار یک ایمیل خصوصی را اشتباهی برای همه‌ی همکارانش فرستاده است.  
  *Leylā bā khande ta'rif kard ke yek bār yek imeyl-e khosusi rā eshtebāhi barā-ye hame-ye hamkārān-ash ferestāde ast.* — Laughing, Leila told them that she had once sent a private email to all her colleagues by mistake.
- در پایان، مدیر قول داد که تا آخر هفته به او خبر بدهد.  
  *dar pāyān, modir ghowl dād ke tā ākhar-e hafte be u khabar bedahad.* — At the end, the manager promised to let her know by the end of the week.
- امروز صبح منشی شرکت زنگ زد و گفت که لیلا را استخدام کرده‌اند.  
  *emruz sobh monshi-ye sherkat zang zad o goft ke Leylā rā estekhdām karde-and.* — This morning the company's secretary rang and said they had hired Leila.

  *monshi porsid ke āyā mi-tavānad az shanbe-ye āyande shoru' konad.* — The secretary asked whether she could start next Saturday.

### Passing on messages – and greetings

Persian has a small grammar of its own for passing things on. When someone cannot talk to you directly, they ask a friend to سلام برساند (*salām beresānad*, ‘deliver their greeting’), and your friend reports it: مینا سلام رساند (*Minā salām resānd*, ‘Mina sends her regards’). As you leave a house you will hear به خانواده سلام برسانید (*be khānevāde salām beresānid*, ‘give my regards to your family’), and you answer چشم، حتماً (*cheshm, hatman*, ‘I will, of course’).

In the news, reported speech has its own fixed phrases. A report rarely begins with گفت; instead you read به گفته‌ی پلیس… (*be gofte-ye polis…*, ‘according to the police…’), به نقل از خبرگزاری… (*be naghl az khabargozāri…*, ‘quoting the news agency…’) or وی افزود که… (*vey afzud ke…*, ‘he added that…’) – with وی (*vey*), the formal written word for *he* or *she*, which you will almost never hear in conversation.

And when Iranians tell a story among friends, they love direct speech, often in the present tense for drama: بهش می‌گم: «چرا؟» می‌گه: «همین‌طوری!» (*behesh mi-gam: «cherā?» mi-ge: «hamin-towri!»*, ‘so I say to him, 'Why?' And he says, 'No reason!'’)

### Tips

- Don't shift the tense: 'he said he was tired' is گفت خسته است. گفت خسته بود would mean that he had been tired before.
- آیا belongs to writing and formal speech. In conversation, report a yes/no question with … یا نه: پرسید میای یا نه.
- Never use آیا with a question word: پرسید کجا می‌روم, not *پرسید آیا کجا می‌روم.
- Ask someone a question: از کسی پرسیدن. Ask someone to do something: از کسی خواستن که + subjunctive. Tell someone: به کسی گفتن.
- The perfect (رفته است) is also the tense of rumour and inference: ظاهراً دیروز رفته است 'apparently he left yesterday'. If you hear it in a story, the speaker probably didn't see it happen.
- In speech که is usually dropped (گفت فردا میاد); in writing it is usually kept (گفت که فردا می‌آید).

### How do you report it?

**Choose the right answer**

1. «من ایرانی هستم.» (*«man irāni hastam.»*) How do you report it – 'He said he was Iranian'? — گفت که ایرانی بود. / گفت که ایرانی است. / گفت که ایرانی هستم.
