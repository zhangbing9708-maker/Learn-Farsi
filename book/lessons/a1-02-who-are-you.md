# Who are you? Names and where you are from
## شما کی هستید؟
*shomā ki hastid?* — Beginner (A1), lesson 2. [Open this lesson with audio and interactive exercises](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/course/a1-02-who-are-you.html)

Now that you can say hello, it is time to say who you are. In this lesson you introduce yourself, ask someone's name, say where you are from and what you are – and you meet the full set of Persian pronouns and the verb 'to be'.

**In this lesson you will learn to:**

- say and ask names: esm-e man … ast, esm-e shomā chist?
- say where you are from and your nationality
- use all the pronouns: man, to, u, mā, shomā, ān-hā
- use hastam/hasti/hast… and the negative nistam

### First day at the language class

_Tom, a student from Australia, arrives at a Persian class in Tehran. Sara, an Iranian student, sits next to him._

- **Sara:** سلام! من سارا هستم. اسم شما چیست؟  
  *salām! man Sārā hastam. esm-e shomā chist?* — Hello! I'm Sara. What is your name?  
  Spoken: سلام! من سارام. اسمتون چیه؟ *salām! man Sārā-m. esm-etun chi-ye?*
- **Tom:** سلام. اسم من تام است.  
  *salām. esm-e man Tām ast.* — Hello. My name is Tom.
- **Sara:** خوشبختم، تام. شما ایرانی هستید؟  
  *khosh-bakht-am, Tām. shomā irāni hastid?* — Nice to meet you, Tom. Are you Iranian?
- **Tom:** نه، من ایرانی نیستم. من استرالیایی هستم.  
  *na, man irāni nistam. man ostorāliyāyi hastam.* — No, I'm not Iranian. I'm Australian.
- **Sara:** اهل کجا هستید؟  
  *ahl-e kojā hastid?* — Where are you from?  
  Spoken: اهل کجایید؟ *ahl-e kojā-yid?*
- **Tom:** اهل سیدنی هستم. شما اهل تهران هستید؟  
  *ahl-e Sidni hastam. shomā ahl-e Tehrān hastid?* — I'm from Sydney. Are you from Tehran?
- **Sara:** نه، من اهل شیراز هستم، اما در تهران دانشجو هستم.  
  *na, man ahl-e Shirāz hastam, ammā dar Tehrān dāneshju hastam.* — No, I'm from Shiraz, but I'm a student in Tehran.
- **Tom:** آن آقا کیست؟  
  *ān āghā kist?* — Who is that man?  
  Spoken: اون آقا کیه؟ *un āghā ki-ye?*
- **Sara:** او آقای کریمی است. او معلم ماست.  
  *u āghā-ye Karimi ast. u mo'allem-e mā-st.* — He is Mr Karimi. He is our teacher.
- **Tom:** و آن‌ها؟  
  *va ān-hā?* — And them?
- **Sara:** آن‌ها هم دانشجو هستند. لیلا و امیر.  
  *ān-hā ham dāneshju hastand. Leylā o Amir.* — They are students too. Leila and Amir.

### Introducing yourself

The simplest way to say who you are is **man … hastam**: من سارا هستم (*man Sārā hastam*, ‘I am Sara’). To say your name more formally, use اسم (*esm*, ‘name’): اسم من تام است (*esm-e man Tām ast*, ‘my name is Tom’) – literally *name-of me Tom is*. The little **-e** after *esm* is the **ezafe**, the sound that links a noun to what belongs to it; you will meet it again and again.

To ask a name, say اسم شما چیست؟ (*esm-e shomā chist?*) – *name-of you what-is?* In speech this becomes *esm-etun chi-ye?*

When you learn someone's name, answer خوشبختم (*khosh-bakht-am*, ‘I am pleased (to meet you)’), literally *I am fortunate*. The reply is من هم خوشبختم (*man ham khosh-bakht-am*, ‘I am pleased too’).

### The pronouns

Persian has six personal pronouns. There is **one word for he, she and it**: او (*u*). Nothing in Persian grammar tells you whether a person is a man or a woman – a relief after French or German.

For *they*, Iranians write آن‌ها (*ān-hā*) (literally *those*) and say *un-hā*. ایشان (*ishān*) is a polite *he/she* for someone you respect – your teacher, a stranger, a grandparent.

| Persian | Reading | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| من (*man*) | man | I |
| تو (*to*) | to | you (one person, informal) |
| او (*u*) | u | he / she / it |
| ما (*mā*) | mā | we |
| شما (*shomā*) | shomā | you (plural, or one person politely) |
| آن‌ها (*ān-hā*) | ān-hā | they |

- او ایرانی است.  
  *u irāni ast.* — He / she is Iranian.
- ما دانشجو هستیم.  
  *mā dāneshju hastim.* — We are students.
- آن‌ها اهل تبریز هستند.  
  *ān-hā ahl-e Tabriz hastand.* — They are from Tabriz.  
  Spoken: اون‌ها اهل تبریزن. *un-hā ahl-e Tabriz-an.*

### 'To be': the long form hastam and the short endings

In lesson 1 you met the short endings (*khub-am*). Persian also has a full verb *to be*, هستن (*hastan*), used when the word before it is a noun or when you want to stress: من دانشجو هستم (*man dāneshju hastam*, ‘I am a student’). The short and long forms mean the same thing; من دانشجو هستم (*man dāneshju hastam*) and من دانشجوام (*man dāneshju-am*) are both correct – the long form is clearer and is what you will see in textbooks.

For *he/she/it is*, both است (*ast*) and هست (*hast*) exist. **ast** is the neutral word (*u mo'allem ast*); **hast** stresses existence – *there is*: چای هست؟ (*chāy hast?*, ‘Is there tea?’)

The negative is a separate word: نیستم (*nistam*, ‘I am not’), نیستی (*nisti*), نیست (*nist*), نیستیم (*nistim*), نیستید (*nistid*), نیستند (*nistand*).

| Pronoun | am / are / is | am not / are not / is not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| من (*man*) | هستم (*hastam*) | نیستم (*nistam*) |
| تو (*to*) | هستی (*hasti*) | نیستی (*nisti*) |
| او (*u*) | است (*ast*) / هست (*hast*) | نیست (*nist*) |
| ما (*mā*) | هستیم (*hastim*) | نیستیم (*nistim*) |
| شما (*shomā*) | هستید (*hastid*) | نیستید (*nistid*) |
| آن‌ها (*ān-hā*) | هستند (*hastand*) | نیستند (*nistand*) |

- من معلم نیستم، دانشجو هستم.  
  *man mo'allem nistam, dāneshju hastam.* — I am not a teacher, I am a student.
- تو اهل کجا هستی؟  
  *to ahl-e kojā hasti?* — Where are you from? (to a friend)
- آقای کریمی این‌جا نیست.  
  *āghā-ye Karimi in-jā nist.* — Mr Karimi is not here.
- لیلا و امیر دوست هستند.  
  *Leylā o Amir dust hastand.* — Leila and Amir are friends.  
  Spoken: لیلا و امیر دوستن. *Leylā o Amir dust-an.*

### Nationalities: add -i

Add ی (*-i*) to a country and you have its person and its language: ایران (*Irān*) → ایرانی (*irāni*), انگلیس (*Engelis*) → انگلیسی (*engelisi*) (English), چین (*Chin*) → چینی (*chini*). If the country ends in a vowel, a *y* slips in: استرالیا (*Ostorāliyā*) → استرالیایی (*ostorāliyāyi*), آمریکا (*Āmrikā*) → آمریکایی (*āmrikāyi*), فرانسه (*Farānse*) → فرانسوی (*farānsavi*) (irregular).

Adjectives never change for gender or number: *one Iranian woman, three Iranian men* – all ایرانی (*irāni*).

- تام استرالیایی است.  
  *Tām ostorāliyāyi ast.* — Tom is Australian.
- ما ایرانی هستیم.  
  *mā irāni hastim.* — We are Iranian.
- شما آلمانی هستید یا فرانسوی؟  
  *shomā ālmāni hastid yā farānsavi?* — Are you German or French?

### Tom's class

_Tom writes a few lines about his new class._

- اسم من تام است.  
  *esm-e man Tām ast.* — My name is Tom.
- من استرالیایی هستم و اهل سیدنی هستم.  
  *man ostorāliyāyi hastam o ahl-e Sidni hastam.* — I am Australian and I am from Sydney.
- حالا در تهران دانشجو هستم.  
  *hālā dar Tehrān dāneshju hastam.* — Now I am a student in Tehran.
- سارا دوست من است. او اهل شیراز است.  
  *Sārā dust-e man ast. u ahl-e Shirāz ast.* — Sara is my friend. She is from Shiraz.
- آقای کریمی معلم ماست. او خیلی خوب است.  
  *āghā-ye Karimi mo'allem-e mā-st. u kheyli khub ast.* — Mr Karimi is our teacher. He is very good.
- لیلا و امیر هم در کلاس ما هستند.  
  *Leylā o Amir ham dar kelās-e mā hastand.* — Leila and Amir are in our class too.
- آن‌ها ایرانی هستند، اما من ایرانی نیستم.  
  *ān-hā irāni hastand, ammā man irāni nistam.* — They are Iranian, but I am not Iranian.

**Choose the right answer**

1. Where is Tom from? — Tehran / Sydney / Shiraz
2. Who is Mr Karimi? — Tom's friend / a student / the teacher
3. Which sentence is true? — Sara is from Tehran. / Leila and Amir are Iranian. / Tom is Iranian.

1. Sydney
2. the teacher
3. Leila and Amir are Iranian.

### Names in Iran

Iranian first names come from three worlds: the Shahnameh and ancient Persia (داریوش (*Dāriyush*), کوروش (*Kurosh*), رستم (*Rostam*), سارا (*Sārā*)), Islam (محمد (*Mohammad*), علی (*Ali*), فاطمه (*Fāteme*), زهرا (*Zahrā*)) and nature and poetry (مینا (*Minā*, ‘enamel; a bird’), لیلا (*Leylā*), نسرین (*Nasrin*, ‘wild rose’), بهار (*Bahār*, ‘spring’)). Many names work for men and women in sound but not in use, so listen for the آقا (*āghā*) or خانم (*khānom*) people add.

Surnames are young – they were made compulsory only in 1925 – and often tell you a home town (شیرازی (*Shirāzi*), تهرانی (*Tehrāni*)) or an ancestor's job. Adults address each other by surname plus *āghā-ye* or *khānom*: آقای کریمی (*āghā-ye Karimi*), خانم احمدی (*khānom-e Ahmadi*). Friends use first names, often with جان (*jān*, ‘dear’): *Sārā jān*.

### Watch out

- **ast** is written as a separate word but pronounced as if glued to the word before it: *mo'allem-e mā-st* for معلم ماست, *Sārā-st* for سارا است. In writing you will see both ماست and ما است.
- The ezafe **-e** (*esm-e man*) is not written after a consonant – you have to know it is there. After a vowel it is written as ی: *khāne-ye man*.
- **u** (او) is one syllable, like English *oo*; **to** (تو) rhymes with *go*.
- Stress the last syllable of nouns, but **has**-tam, **nis**-tam – stress stays on the stem in these verbs.

**Choose the right answer**

3. Which word means 'he or she'? — او / آن‌ها / تو

5. آمریکایی — After a vowel, -yi: āmrikāyi.
