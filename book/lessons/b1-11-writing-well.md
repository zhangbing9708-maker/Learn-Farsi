# Writing well: paragraphs, connectors and punctuation
## خوب نوشتن
*khub neveshtan* — Intermediate (B1), lesson 11. [Open this lesson with audio and interactive exercises](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/course/b1-11-writing-well.html)

At B1 you can write correct sentences; now it is time to write good paragraphs. In this lesson you learn how a Persian paragraph is built, the connectors that hold an argument together (بنابراین، با این حال، علاوه بر این، از یک سو … از سوی دیگر…), and how Persian punctuation works – including where the comma goes and where it doesn't. You study a model three-paragraph essay on learning languages, then the same ideas written badly, sentence by sentence, and corrected.

**In this lesson you will learn to:**

- build a paragraph with a topic sentence, support and a conclusion
- link ideas with بنابراین، با این حال، علاوه بر این، از یک سو … از سوی دیگر، در نتیجه، به عبارت دیگر and در حالی که
- use Persian punctuation – ، ؛ « » ؟ – and put commas in the right places
- spot and fix the commonest mistakes in a learner's essay

### Model essay: why learn a foreign language?

_A short essay in three paragraphs, of the kind Iranian students write at school. After the title, paragraph 1 (sentences 2–4) introduces the topic, paragraph 2 (5–10) gives the arguments, and paragraph 3 (11–14) concludes. The notes show what each sentence does._

- چرا باید زبان خارجی یاد بگیریم؟  
  *cherā bāyad zabān-e khāreji yād begirim?* — Why should we learn a foreign language?  
  _The title: a question that the essay will answer._
- امروزه یادگیری زبان خارجی دیگر یک سرگرمی نیست، بلکه یک ضرورت است.  
  *emruze yādgiri-ye zabān-e khāreji digar yek sargarmi nist, balke yek zarurat ast.* — Today learning a foreign language is no longer a hobby but a necessity.  
  _Paragraph 1. The topic sentence: it states the main idea of the whole essay. … نیست، بلکه … – 'not …, but rather …'._
- در جهانی که مرزها روزبه‌روز کم‌رنگ‌تر می‌شوند، زبان پلی میان انسان‌ها و فرهنگ‌هاست.  
  *dar jahān-i ke marz-hā ruz-be-ruz kam-rang-tar mi-shavand, zabān pol-i miyān-e ensān-hā va farhang-hā-st.* — In a world where borders grow fainter every day, language is a bridge between people and cultures.  
  _Background: why the topic matters. Notice the comma after the long opening clause._
- در این نوشته، به سه دلیل مهم برای یادگیری زبان خارجی اشاره می‌کنم.  
  *dar in neveshte, be se dalil-e mohem barā-ye yādgiri-ye zabān-e khāreji eshāre mi-konam.* — In this essay I point to three important reasons for learning a foreign language.  
  _The plan: it tells the reader what is coming._
- نخست اینکه زبان خارجی درهای تازه‌ای به روی ما باز می‌کند.  
  *nokhost inke zabān-e khāreji dar-hā-ye tāze-i be ru-ye mā bāz mi-konad.* — First, a foreign language opens new doors for us.  
  _Paragraph 2. The first argument, announced by نخست اینکه – the written equivalent of اول اینکه._
- برای مثال، بسیاری از شرکت‌ها به کارمندانی نیاز دارند که با مشتریان خارجی ارتباط برقرار کنند.  
  *barā-ye mesāl, besyār-i az sherkat-hā be kārmandān-i niyāz dārand ke bā moshtariyān-e khāreji ertebāt bar-gharār konand.* — For example, many companies need employees who can communicate with foreign customers.  
  _Support: an example for the first argument._
- علاوه بر این، یادگیری زبان ذهن را فعال نگه می‌دارد.  
  *alāve bar in, yādgiri-ye zabān zehn rā fa'āl negah mi-dārad.* — Moreover, learning a language keeps the mind active.  
  _The second argument, introduced by علاوه بر این – 'in addition, moreover'._
- به گفته‌ی برخی پژوهشگران، یادگیری زبان دوم حافظه و تمرکز را تقویت می‌کند.  
  *be gofte-ye barkhi pazhuheshgarān, yādgiri-ye zabān-e dovvom hāfeze va tamarkoz rā taghviyat mi-konad.* — According to some researchers, learning a second language strengthens memory and concentration.  
  _Evidence for the second argument, with its source._
- مهم‌تر از همه، زبان ما را با فرهنگ دیگران آشنا می‌کند.  
  *mohem-tar az hame, zabān mā rā bā farhang-e digarān āshnā mi-konad.* — Most important of all, a language introduces us to other people's culture.  
  _The third and strongest argument is kept for last._
- کسی که شعر حافظ را به فارسی می‌خواند، چیزی را می‌فهمد که در هیچ ترجمه‌ای پیدا نمی‌شود.  
  *kas-i ke she'r-e Hāfez rā be fārsi mi-khānad, chiz-i rā mi-fahmad ke dar hich tarjome-i peydā nemi-shavad.* — Someone who reads Hafez in Persian understands something that cannot be found in any translation.  
  _An example for the third argument. The comma after a subject that contains a whole clause helps the reader find the verb._
- البته یادگیری زبان کار آسانی نیست و به صبر و تمرین نیاز دارد.  
  *albatte yādgiri-ye zabān kār-e āsān-i nist va be sabr va tamrin niyāz dārad.* — Of course, learning a language is not easy, and it takes patience and practice.  
  _Paragraph 3. A concession: admit the other side before you conclude._
- با این حال، فایده‌های آن بسیار بیشتر از سختی‌هایش است.  
  *bā in hāl, fāyede-hā-ye ān besyār bish-tar az sakhti-hā-yash ast.* — Nevertheless, its benefits far outweigh its difficulties.  
  _با این حال turns back to the main idea after the concession._
- بنابراین، هر کس که فرصت دارد، باید دست‌کم یک زبان خارجی یاد بگیرد.  
  *banābar-in, har kas ke forsat dārad, bāyad dast-e-kam yek zabān-e khāreji yād begirad.* — Therefore, everyone who has the chance should learn at least one foreign language.  
  _The conclusion, introduced by بنابراین._
- به عبارت دیگر، یادگیری زبان سرمایه‌گذاری برای آینده است.  
  *be ebārat-e digar, yādgiri-ye zabān sarmāye-gozāri barā-ye āyande ast.* — In other words, learning a language is an investment in the future.  
  _A last sentence that restates the main idea in a new, memorable form._

**Choose the right answer**

1. What is the main idea of the essay? — Languages are hard to learn. / Learning a foreign language is a necessity today. / Hafez is impossible to translate.
2. Which connector introduces the second argument? — برای مثال / علاوه بر این / بنابراین
3. What does the last paragraph do? — It gives a new argument. / It admits a difficulty, then concludes. / It tells a story.

1. Learning a foreign language is a necessity today.
2. علاوه بر این

### What makes Persian prose good

Good Persian writing – نثر (*nasr*, ‘prose’) – values the same things as good English writing: one idea per paragraph, clear links between sentences, no padding. But a few habits are particular to Persian:

- **The paragraph** (بند (*band*) or پاراگراف (*pārāgrāf*)) opens with a **topic sentence** (جمله‌ی اصلی (*jomle-ye asli*)) that states its idea; the following sentences explain it or give examples and evidence; the last one rounds it off or leads on to the next.
- **Keep the verb in sight.** Persian puts the verb last, so a long sentence keeps the reader waiting. If a sentence runs past two lines, split it.
- **Don't repeat.** Persian style dislikes the same word – above all the same verb – twice in a row. Vary with a synonym, a different construction, or by joining two sentences with a connector.
- **Choose one register.** An essay is written in book Persian from start to finish: no خوبه (*khub-e*), no می‌خوام (*mi-khām*) (lesson 8).
- **Connectors** are the joints of an argument. Learn a dozen and your writing will at once read as more mature.

Iranian editors also warn against two over-formal habits: using می‌باشد (*mi-bāshad*) where است (*ast*) will do, and the passive with توسط (*tavassot-e*, ‘by’) (*کتاب توسط او نوشته شد*) where a simple active sentence is clearer: او کتاب را نوشت (*u ketāb rā nevesht*, ‘he wrote the book’).

### Connectors: the joints of an argument

A connector tells the reader how the next sentence relates to the last one: *more of the same*, *the opposite*, *the result*, *an example*. In writing, a connector at the start of a sentence is followed by a comma. Speech has its own, simpler set – the third column – which does not belong in an essay.

Two of them need a note. از یک سو (*az yek su*) always waits for its partner از سوی دیگر (*az su-ye digar*), like *on the one hand … on the other*. And در حالی که (*dar hāl-i ke*) means both *while* (two things at the same time) and *whereas* (a contrast); it joins two clauses inside one sentence instead of starting a new one.

| Connector | Meaning | Spoken equivalent | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| بنابراین (*banābar-in*) | therefore, so | پس (*pas*) | result, conclusion |
| در نتیجه (*dar natije*) | as a result | واسه همین (*vāse hamin*) | result |
| به همین دلیل (*be hamin dalil*) | for this reason | واسه همین (*vāse hamin*) | cause and effect |
| با این حال (*bā in hāl*) | nevertheless, however | ولی خب (*vali khob*) | contrast after a concession |
| اما (*ammā*)، ولی (*vali*) | but | ولی (*vali*) | contrast |
| علاوه بر این (*alāve bar in*) | moreover, in addition | تازه (*tāze*) | adding a point |
| همچنین (*hamchenin*) | also, likewise | هم (*ham*) | adding a point |
| از یک سو … از سوی دیگر (*az yek su … az su-ye digar*) | on the one hand … on the other | از یه طرف … از یه طرف دیگه (*az ye taraf … az ye taraf-e dige*) | weighing two sides |
| به عبارت دیگر (*be ebārat-e digar*) | in other words | یعنی (*ya'ni*) | restating |
| در حالی که (*dar hāl-i ke*) | while; whereas | در حالی که (*dar hāl-i ke*) | two things at once; contrast |
| برای مثال (*barā-ye mesāl*)، برای نمونه (*barā-ye nemune*) | for example | مثلاً (*masalan*) | example |
| نخست (*nokhost*) … سپس (*sepas*) … در پایان (*dar pāyān*) | first … then … finally | اول (*avval*) … بعد (*ba'd*) … آخرش (*ākhar-esh*) | order |
| در مجموع (*dar majmu'*)، به طور خلاصه (*be tor-e kholāse*) | all in all, in short | خلاصه (*kholāse*) | summing up |
| زیرا (*zirā*) | because | چون (*chon*) | cause, inside the sentence |

- هوا خیلی سرد بود؛ بنابراین، مسابقه به هفته‌ی بعد موکول شد.  
  *havā kheyli sard bud; banābar-in, mosābeghe be hafte-ye ba'd mowkul shod.* — It was very cold; therefore the match was postponed to the following week.  
  Spoken: هوا خیلی سرد بود، واسه همین مسابقه رو انداختن هفته‌ی بعد. *havā kheyli sard bud, vāse hamin mosābeghe ro andākhtan hafte-ye ba'd.*
- این روش گران است. با این حال، نتیجه‌ی بهتری دارد.  
  *in ravesh gerān ast. bā in hāl, natije-ye behtar-i dārad.* — This method is expensive. Nevertheless, it gives a better result.  
  Spoken: این روش گرونه. ولی خب، نتیجه‌ش بهتره. *in ravesh gerun-e. vali khob, natije-sh behtar-e.*
- این آپارتمان به مترو نزدیک است. علاوه بر این، پارکینگ هم دارد.  
  *in āpārtemān be metro nazdik ast. alāve bar in, pārking ham dārad.* — This flat is close to the metro. In addition, it has parking.  
  Spoken: این آپارتمان به مترو نزدیکه. تازه، پارکینگم داره. *in āpārtemān be metro nazdik-e. tāze, pārking-am dāre.*
- از یک سو، شهرهای بزرگ امکانات بیشتری دارند؛ از سوی دیگر، زندگی در آن‌ها گران‌تر است.

  Spoken: از یه طرف، شهرای بزرگ امکانات بیشتری دارن؛ از یه طرف دیگه، زندگی توشون گرون‌تره. *az ye taraf, shahr-ā-ye bozorg emkānāt-e bishtar-i dāran; az ye taraf-e dige, zendegi tu-shun gerun-tar-e.*
- او ساعت‌ها تمرین کرد و در نتیجه، در مسابقه اول شد.  
  *u sā'at-hā tamrin kard va dar natije, dar mosābeghe avval shod.* — He practised for hours, and as a result he came first in the competition.  
  Spoken: ساعت‌ها تمرین کرد، واسه همین تو مسابقه اول شد. *sā'at-hā tamrin kard, vāse hamin tu mosābeghe avval shod.*
- او بسیار کم حرف می‌زند؛ به عبارت دیگر، اهل تعارف نیست.  
  *u besyār kam harf mi-zanad; be ebārat-e digar, ahl-e ta'ārof nist.* — He says very little; in other words, he is not one for ta'arof.  
  Spoken: خیلی کم حرف می‌زنه؛ یعنی اهل تعارف نیست. *kheyli kam harf mi-zane; ya'ni ahl-e ta'ārof nist.*
- مینا درس می‌خواند، در حالی که برادرش تلویزیون تماشا می‌کرد.  
  *Minā dars mi-khānd, dar hāl-i ke barādar-ash televiziyon tamāshā mi-kard.* — Mina was studying while her brother was watching television.  
  Spoken: مینا داشت درس می‌خوند، داداشش داشت تلویزیون نگاه می‌کرد. *Minā dāsht dars mi-khund, dādāsh-esh dāsht televiziyon negāh mi-kard.*
- در شمال ایران باران زیاد می‌بارد، در حالی که جنوب کشور خشک است.  
  *dar shomāl-e Irān bārān ziyād mi-bārad, dar hāl-i ke jonub-e keshvar khoshk ast.* — In the north of Iran it rains a lot, whereas the south of the country is dry.

### Persian punctuation – and where the comma goes

Persian borrowed its punctuation from European languages about a century ago, and turned some marks round to suit right-to-left writing: the comma (،) and the semicolon (؛) are upside down, the question mark (؟) is mirrored, and quotation marks are the guillemets « ». The full stop, the colon and the exclamation mark are the same.

**Where the comma goes:**

1. after an introductory phrase or a connector: بنابراین، (*banābar-in,*) به نظر من، (*be nazar-e man,*)
2. after a long clause that comes first: وقتی به خانه رسیدم، باران شروع شد. (*vaghti be khāne residam, bārān shoru' shod.*)
3. between the items of a list – but **not** before the last و (*va*): سیب، پرتقال و انار (*sib, porteghāl va anār*)
4. around an inserted phrase: حافظ، شاعر بزرگ شیراز، در قرن هشتم زندگی می‌کرد. (*Hāfez, shā'er-e bozorg-e Shirāz, dar gharn-e hashtom zendegi mi-kard.*)
5. after the name of the person you are addressing: سارا، بیا این‌جا! (*Sārā, biyā in-jā!*)

**Where it does not go:** between a simple subject and its verb, and before که (*ke*) when it introduces a short clause (می‌دانم که می‌آید (*mi-dānam ke mi-āyad*)). After a subject that contains a whole clause, though – کسی که شعر حافظ را می‌خواند،… (*kas-i ke she'r-e Hāfez rā mi-khānad,…*) – a comma helps the reader find the verb.

The **semicolon** joins two complete sentences that belong together, and Persian uses it more than English does. **Spacing**: no space before a mark, one space after it – and inside words use the zero-width non-joiner, which Iranians call نیم‌فاصله (*nim-fāsele*, ‘half-space’): می‌روم (*mi-ravam*), not *میروم* or *می روم*.

| Mark | Name | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ، | ویرگول (*virgul*)، کاما (*kāmā*) | comma: pauses, lists, after connectors |
| ؛ | نقطه‌ویرگول (*noghte-virgul*) | semicolon: joins two related sentences |
| . | نقطه (*noghte*) | full stop – also after an indirect question |
| ؟ | علامت سؤال (*alāmat-e so'āl*) | question mark – direct questions only |
| ! | علامت تعجب (*alāmat-e ta'ajjob*) | exclamation mark |
| « » | گیومه (*giyume*) | quotations, titles of books and articles, special terms |
| : | دونقطه (*do-noghte*) | before a quotation, a list or an explanation |
| ( ) | پرانتز (*parāntez*) | brackets |
| … | سه‌نقطه (*se-noghte*) | an unfinished thought |
| – | خط تیره (*khatt-e tire*) | dash: an aside, or between numbers (۱۴۰۰–۱۴۰۵) |

- به نظر من، یادگیری زبان از کودکی آسان‌تر است.  
  *be nazar-e man, yādgiri-ye zabān az kudaki āsān-tar ast.* — In my opinion, learning a language is easier from childhood.  
  _A comma after an introductory phrase._
- وقتی به ایستگاه رسیدیم، قطار رفته بود.  
  *vaghti be istgāh residim, ghatār rafte bud.* — When we reached the station, the train had gone.  
  _A comma after a long first clause._
- در بازار سیب، پرتقال و انار خریدیم.  
  *dar bāzār sib, porteghāl va anār kharidim.* — At the market we bought apples, oranges and pomegranates.  
  _No comma before the last و._
- فردوسی، شاعر بزرگ ایران، شاهنامه را در سی سال سرود.  
  *Ferdowsi, shā'er-e bozorg-e Irān, Shāhnāme rā dar si sāl sorud.* — Ferdowsi, Iran's great poet, composed the Shahnameh over thirty years.  
  _Commas around an inserted phrase. A book title can also go in « »: «شاهنامه»._
- استاد گفت: «فردا امتحان داریم.»  
  *ostād goft: «fardā emtehān dārim.»* — The teacher said: 'We have an exam tomorrow.'  
  _A colon and « » for a direct quotation; the full stop goes inside._
- نمی‌دانم چرا دیر کرد.  
  *nemi-dānam cherā dir kard.* — I don't know why he was late.  
  _An indirect question ends with a full stop, not ؟._
- هوا سرد بود؛ با این حال، همه به کوه رفتند.  
  *havā sard bud; bā in hāl, hame be kuh raftand.* — It was cold; nevertheless, everyone went to the mountains.  
  _A semicolon between two related sentences, and a comma after the connector._
- مینا، لطفاً پنجره را ببند!  
  *Minā, lotfan panjare rā beband!* — Mina, please close the window!  
  _A comma after the name of the person addressed._

### Building a paragraph

A good Persian paragraph has the same skeleton as a good English one. The **topic sentence** says what the paragraph is about; the **supporting sentences** explain it with reasons, examples or facts; the **closing sentence** sums up or leads on to the next paragraph. Each slot has its typical openings – learn two or three for each. The examples below build one complete paragraph, sentence by sentence.

| Part of the paragraph | Useful openings | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| topic sentence | یکی از … این است که… (*yek-i az … in ast ke…*) | one of … is that… |
| topic sentence | امروزه (*emruze*)، در دنیای امروز (*dar donyā-ye emruz*) | nowadays, in today's world |
| first point | نخست اینکه (*nokhost inke*)، اول آنکه (*avval ānke*) | first(ly) |
| further points | دوم اینکه (*dovvom inke*)، علاوه بر این (*alāve bar in*) | second(ly), moreover |
| example | برای مثال (*barā-ye mesāl*)، به عنوان نمونه (*be onvān-e nemune*) | for example |
| evidence | پژوهش‌ها نشان می‌دهد که… (*pazhuhesh-hā neshān mi-dahad ke…*) | research shows that… |
| concession | البته (*albatte*)، هرچند (*harchand*) | of course, although |
| conclusion | بنابراین (*banābar-in*)، در مجموع (*dar majmu'*)، در پایان (*dar pāyān*) | therefore, all in all, finally |
| restating | به عبارت دیگر (*be ebārat-e digar*)، خلاصه اینکه (*kholāse inke*) | in other words, in short |

- یکی از بهترین راه‌های رفت‌وآمد در تهران مترو است.  
  *yek-i az behtarin rāh-hā-ye raft-o-āmad dar Tehrān metro ast.* — One of the best ways of getting around Tehran is the metro.  
  _Topic sentence._
- نخست اینکه مترو از ترافیک سنگین خیابان‌ها سریع‌تر است.  
  *nokhost inke metro az terāfik-e sangin-e khiyābān-hā sari'-tar ast.* — First, the metro is faster than the heavy traffic in the streets.  
  _The first supporting point._
- علاوه بر این، بلیت آن ارزان است و آلودگی هوا را کم می‌کند.  
  *alāve bar in, belit-e ān arzān ast va āludegi-ye havā rā kam mi-konad.* — Moreover, its tickets are cheap and it reduces air pollution.  
  _The second point._
- برای مثال، سفر از تجریش تا میدان آزادی با ماشین گاهی دو ساعت طول می‌کشد، در حالی که با مترو کمتر از یک ساعت است.  
  *barā-ye mesāl, safar az Tajrish tā meydān-e Āzādi bā māshin gāhi do sā'at tul mi-keshad, dar hāl-i ke bā metro kam-tar az yek sā'at ast.* — For example, the trip from Tajrish to Azadi Square sometimes takes two hours by car, whereas by metro it is under an hour.  
  _An example, with a contrast introduced by در حالی که._
- البته مترو در ساعت‌های شلوغ بسیار پرجمعیت است.  
  *albatte metro dar sā'at-hā-ye sholugh besyār por-jam'iyyat ast.* — Of course, the metro is very crowded at rush hour.  
  _A concession._
- با این حال، برای بیشتر تهرانی‌ها مترو سریع‌ترین و ارزان‌ترین گزینه است.  
  *bā in hāl, barā-ye bishtar-e Tehrāni-hā metro sari'-tarin va arzān-tarin gozine ast.* — Nevertheless, for most Tehranis the metro is the fastest and cheapest option.  
  _The closing sentence takes us back to the main idea._

### The same ideas, written badly – and corrected

_Tom wrote a first draft of the same essay. His Persian is correct word by word, but the writing is weak: spoken forms, endless sentences, no connectors, English punctuation, missing half-spaces and repetition. Each draft sentence (Draft) is followed by the version his teacher Ms Ahmadi suggested (Corrected); read only the corrected sentences and you have a good paragraph._

- زبان خارجی خیلی خوبه و آدم باید یاد بگیره و من هم دارم فارسی یاد میگیرم و خیلی دوستش دارم.  
  *zabān-e khāreji kheyli khub-e va ādam bāyad yād begire va man ham dāram fārsi yād migiram va kheyli dust-esh dāram.* — Foreign languages are really good and you should learn them and I'm learning Persian too and I really like it.  
  _Draft. Spoken forms (خوبه، بگیره) in a written text; four clauses chained with و; میگیرم has no half-space; and no clear main idea._
- یادگیری زبان خارجی امروزه یک ضرورت است و من نیز به همین دلیل فارسی می‌آموزم.  
  *yādgiri-ye zabān-e khāreji emruze yek zarurat ast va man niz be hamin dalil fārsi mi-āmuzam.* — Learning a foreign language is a necessity today, and that is why I am learning Persian.  
  _Corrected. One clear main idea, all in the written register (است، نیز، می‌آموزم), with the half-space._
- شرکت‌ها کارمند زبان‌دان میخواهند.  
  *sherkat-hā kārmand-e zabān-dān mikhāhand.* — Companies want staff who know languages.  
  _Draft. A reason with no signpost – the reader can't tell it is the first argument – and no example. میخواهند is missing its half-space._
- نخست اینکه بسیاری از شرکت‌ها، برای مثال شرکت‌های گردشگری، به کارمندان زبان‌دان نیاز دارند.  
  *nokhost inke besyār-i az sherkat-hā, barā-ye mesāl sherkat-hā-ye gardeshgari, be kārmandān-e zabān-dān niyāz dārand.* — First, many companies – travel companies, for example – need staff who know languages.  
  _Corrected. The argument is announced with نخست اینکه, and an example is set off by commas._
- زبان برای مغز خوب است و زبان حافظه را خوب می‌کند و زبان تمرکز را هم خوب می‌کند.  
  *zabān barā-ye maghz khub ast va zabān hāfeze rā khub mi-konad va zabān tamarkoz rā ham khub mi-konad.* — Language is good for the brain and language makes memory good and language makes concentration good too.  
  _Draft. Repetition: زبان three times, خوب three times. Persian style dislikes this even more than English does._
- علاوه بر این، یادگیری زبان حافظه و تمرکز را تقویت می‌کند.  
  *alāve bar in, yādgiri-ye zabān hāfeze va tamarkoz rā taghviyat mi-konad.* — Moreover, learning a language strengthens memory and concentration.  
  _Corrected. One clause instead of three, a precise verb (تقویت کردن) instead of خوب کردن, and a connector that links it to the first point._
- بنابراین فرهنگ هم مهم است ، مثلاً حافظ.  
  *banābar-in farhang ham mohem ast , masalan Hāfez.* — Therefore culture is also important, for example Hafez.  
  _Draft. The wrong connector – nothing here follows from the sentence before, so بنابراین ('therefore') makes no sense; a space before the comma; and مثلاً حافظ is not a sentence._
- از همه مهم‌تر، زبان ما را با فرهنگ دیگران آشنا می‌کند؛ برای مثال، شعر حافظ را فقط به فارسی می‌توان به‌درستی فهمید.  
  *az hame mohem-tar, zabān mā rā bā farhang-e digarān āshnā mi-konad; barā-ye mesāl, she'r-e Hāfez rā faghat be fārsi mi-tavān be-dorosti fahmid.* — Most importantly, a language introduces us to other cultures; for example, Hafez can only be properly understood in Persian.  
  _Corrected. A connector that fits (از همه مهم‌تر, 'most importantly'), a semicolon between the claim and the example, and a complete example sentence._
- یاد گرفتن زبان سخت است ولی ارزش دارد چون فایده هایش زیاد است?  
  *yād gereftan-e zabān sakht ast vali arzesh dārad chon fāyede hā-yash ziyād ast?* — Learning a language is hard but it's worth it because its benefits are many?  
  _Draft. A Latin question mark (?) – on a statement, which needs a full stop; فایده هایش needs a half-space, not a space; and the contrast is buried in the middle of a long sentence._
- یادگیری زبان کار سختی است؛ با این حال، فایده‌هایش بسیار بیشتر از سختی‌هایش است.  
  *yādgiri-ye zabān kār-e sakht-i ast; bā in hāl, fāyede-hā-yash besyār bish-tar az sakhti-hā-yash ast.* — Learning a language is hard work; nevertheless, its benefits far outweigh its difficulties.  
  _Corrected. The contrast gets its own clause after a semicolon and با این حال; فایده‌هایش has its half-space; the sentence ends with a full stop._
- در آخر من فکر میکنم همه باید زبان یاد بگیرند چون "زبان پل است".  
  *dar ākhar man fekr mikonam hame bāyad zabān yād begirand chon "zabān pol ast".* — In the end I think everyone should learn languages because "language is a bridge".  
  _Draft. English quotation marks instead of « »; میکنم without the half-space; and a conclusion that just repeats a phrase instead of summing up the argument._
- بنابراین، هر کس که فرصت دارد، باید دست‌کم یک زبان خارجی بیاموزد؛ به عبارت دیگر، زبان سرمایه‌ای برای آینده است.  
  *banābar-in, har kas ke forsat dārad, bāyad dast-e-kam yek zabān-e khāreji biyāmuzad; be ebārat-e digar, zabān sarmāye-i barā-ye āyande ast.* — Therefore, everyone who has the chance should learn at least one foreign language; in other words, a language is an investment in the future.  
  _Corrected. بنابراین now really draws a conclusion; commas after the connector and after the subject clause هر کس که فرصت دارد; and a last phrase that sums up the whole essay._

### Enshā: the Iranian school essay

Every Iranian remembers انشا (*enshā*), the composition class of primary and secondary school, and its famous topics. The most famous of all is علم بهتر است یا ثروت؟ (*elm behtar ast yā servat?*, ‘Is knowledge better, or wealth?’) – a topic so overused that it has become a joke for anything predictable. Others are تابستان خود را چگونه گذراندید؟ (*tābestān-e khod rā chegune gozarāndid?*, ‘How did you spend your summer?’) and descriptions of spring or of 'my mother'.

School essays follow the three-part plan of this lesson – مقدمه (*moghaddame*, ‘introduction’), بدنه (*badane*, ‘body’), نتیجه‌گیری (*natije-giri*, ‘conclusion’) – and many teachers like a line of poetry or a proverb at the start or the end. That habit is worth copying, sparingly: a well-placed verse of Saadi is the Persian essay's equivalent of a good quotation.

For adults, the classic guide to good written Persian is غلط ننویسیم (*ghalat na-nevisim*, ‘Let's not write incorrectly’) (1987) by the translator and editor Abolhassan Najafi, which campaigned against needless loan structures – among them می‌باشد (*mi-bāshad*) for است and the passive with توسط (*tavassot-e*). The Academy of Persian Language and Literature, فرهنگستان زبان و ادب فارسی (*farhangestān-e zabān va adab-e fārsi*), publishes the official spelling rules, including when to use the half-space.

### Tips

- Write the topic sentence first, then the conclusion, then fill in the middle. It keeps every paragraph on one idea.
- Read your text aloud. If you run out of breath before the verb, the sentence is too long – split it.
- Use the half-space (نیم‌فاصله): on most Persian keyboards it is Shift+Space, and on phones it usually sits next to the space bar. می‌روم، کتاب‌ها، خانه‌ی من.
- Don't carry English punctuation over: no comma before و in a list, « » instead of " ", and the Persian ، ؛ ؟.
- Vary your connectors, but don't overdo them: one every two or three sentences is plenty.
- Collect good sentences. When you read a Persian text you like, copy two or three sentences by hand – the old way Iranian students learned to write.

### Choose the connector

**Fill in the missing word**

### Punctuation and connectors

**Choose the right answer**

1. Which sentence is punctuated correctly? — سیب، پرتقال، و انار خریدم. / سیب، پرتقال و انار خریدم. / سیب پرتقال، و انار خریدم.
2. Which mark goes at the end of نمی‌دانم کجا رفت (*nemi-dānam kojā raft*)? — ؟ / . / !
3. Which is the Persian way to quote someone? — او گفت: "می‌آیم." / او گفت: «می‌آیم.» / او گفت، می‌آیم؟
4. از یک سو (*az yek su*) must be followed later by… — از سوی دیگر / بنابراین / برای مثال
5. Which connector brings you back to your point after a concession ('…; nevertheless, …')? — علاوه بر این / با این حال / به عبارت دیگر
6. Which is correctly written? — کتابها / کتاب‌ها / کتاب ها

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. سیب، پرتقال و انار خریدم. — Commas between the items, but none before the last و.
2. . — It is an indirect question – really a statement – so it ends with a full stop.
3. او گفت: «می‌آیم.»
4. از سوی دیگر
5. با این حال
6. کتاب‌ها — The plural ـها is joined with a half-space: neither glued on nor after a full space.

</details>
