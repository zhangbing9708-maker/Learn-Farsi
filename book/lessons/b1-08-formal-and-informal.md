# Formal and informal: registers and respect
## رسمی و خودمانی
*rasmi o khodemāni* — Intermediate (B1), lesson 8. [Open this lesson with audio and interactive exercises](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/course/b1-08-formal-and-informal.html)

Every Iranian moves between different kinds of Persian many times a day: the formal language of letters, forms and the news, the everyday written language, and the spoken Persian of the street – and, on a separate scale, between the respectful Persian used with strangers, elders and officials and the familiar Persian used with friends. In this lesson you put the three levels side by side, learn the honorifics جناب، سرکار، حضرت‌عالی and بنده, the plural of respect (ایشان آمدند for one person), the polite verbs فرمودن، تشریف آوردن، میل کردن and عرض کردن, and how to open and close a formal letter – then you read a formal email and the same message as a text to a friend.

**In this lesson you will learn to:**

- move between formal written, everyday written and spoken Tehrani Persian
- use بنده، جناب، سرکار and حضرت‌عالی, and the plural of respect: ایشان آمدند
- choose the right polite verb – فرمودن and تشریف آوردن for others, عرض کردن for yourself
- write a formal email, and the same message as a text to a friend

### First day at the office

_Mina is starting a new job as an editor at a publishing house in Tehran. Ms Ahmadi, the office manager, welcomes her; Mr Karimi, the director, is in a meeting. Everyone is being formal – listen for بنده, ایشان, تشریف, فرمودید and عرض کنم. Notice that formal Persian is still *spoken* Persian: the spoken line shows how it really sounds._

- **Ms Ahmadi:** سلام، بفرمایید. امری داشتید؟  
  *salām, befarmāyid. amr-i dāshtid?* — Hello, please come in. How can I help you? (lit. did you have an order?)  
  Spoken: سلام، بفرمایین. امری داشتین؟ *salām, befarmāyin. amr-i dāshtin?*
- **Mina:** سلام. بنده مینا صادقی هستم. امروز اولین روز کارم است.  
  *salām. bande Minā Sādeghi hastam. emruz avvalin ruz-e kār-am ast.* — Hello. I'm Mina Sadeghi. Today is my first day at work.  
  Spoken: سلام. بنده مینا صادقی هستم. امروز اولین روز کارمه. *salām. bande Minā Sādeghi hastam. emruz avvalin ruz-e kār-am-e.*
- **Ms Ahmadi:** خیلی خوش آمدید! جناب آقای کریمی منتظرتان بودند، ولی الان در جلسه تشریف دارند.  
  *kheyli khosh āmadid! jenāb-e āghā-ye Karimi montazer-etān budand, vali alān dar jalase tashrif dārand.* — Welcome! Mr Karimi was expecting you, but he's in a meeting at the moment.  
  Spoken: خیلی خوش اومدین! جناب آقای کریمی منتظرتون بودن، ولی الان تو جلسه تشریف دارن. *kheyli khosh umadin! jenāb-e āghā-ye Karimi montazer-etun budan, vali alān tu jalase tashrif dāran.*
- **Mina:** اشکالی ندارد، منتظر می‌مانم.  
  *eshkāl-i na-dārad, montazer mi-mānam.* — That's all right, I'll wait.  
  Spoken: اشکالی نداره، منتظر می‌مونم. *eshkāl-i na-dāre, montazer mi-munam.*
- **Ms Ahmadi:** بفرمایید بنشینید. چای میل دارید یا قهوه؟  
  *befarmāyid beneshinid. chāy meyl dārid yā ghahve?* — Please, have a seat. Would you like tea or coffee?  
  Spoken: بفرمایین بشینین. چای میل دارین یا قهوه؟ *befarmāyin beshinin. chāy meyl dārin yā ghahve?*
- **Mina:** خیلی ممنون، زحمت نکشید.  
  *kheyli mamnun, zahmat na-keshid.* — Thank you very much – please don't go to any trouble.  
  Spoken: خیلی ممنون، زحمت نکشین. *kheyli mamnun, zahmat na-keshin.*
- **Mr Karimi:** سلام خانم صادقی، خوش آمدید! ببخشید که منتظر ماندید.  
  *salām khānom-e Sādeghi, khosh āmadid! bebakhshid ke montazer māndid.* — Hello Ms Sadeghi, welcome! I'm sorry to have kept you waiting.  
  Spoken: سلام خانم صادقی، خوش اومدین! ببخشید که منتظر موندین. *salām khānom-e Sādeghi, khosh umadin! bebakhshid ke montazer mundin.*
- **Mina:** خواهش می‌کنم، اختیار دارید. بنده تازه رسیدم.  
  *khāhesh mi-konam, ekhtiyār dārid. bande tāze residam.* — Not at all, please. I've only just arrived.  
  Spoken: خواهش می‌کنم، اختیار دارین. بنده تازه رسیدم. *khāhesh mi-konam, ekhtiyār dārin. bande tāze residam.*
- **Mr Karimi:** خانم احمدی گفتند که شما سه سال در یک انتشارات دیگر کار کرده‌اید.  
  *khānom-e Ahmadi goftand ke shomā se sāl dar yek enteshārāt-e digar kār karde-id.* — Ms Ahmadi tells me you've worked at another publisher for three years.  
  Spoken: خانم احمدی گفتن که شما سه سال تو یه انتشارات دیگه کار کردین. *khānom-e Ahmadi goftan ke shomā se sāl tu ye enteshārāt-e dige kār kardin.*
- **Mina:** بله، همان‌طور که فرمودید. عرض کنم که بیشتر در بخش ویرایش کار می‌کردم.  
  *bale, hamān-tor ke farmudid. arz konam ke bishtar dar bakhsh-e virāyesh kār mi-kardam.* — Yes, just as you say. I should mention that I worked mostly in editing.  
  Spoken: بله، همون‌طور که فرمودین. عرض کنم که بیشتر تو بخش ویرایش کار می‌کردم. *bale, hamun-tor ke farmudin. arz konam ke bishtar tu bakhsh-e virāyesh kār mi-kardam.*
- **Mr Karimi:** عالی است. خانم احمدی، لطفاً میزشان را به ایشان نشان بدهید.  
  *āli ast. khānom-e Ahmadi, lotfan miz-eshān rā be ishān neshān bedahid.* — Excellent. Ms Ahmadi, please show her to her desk.  
  Spoken: عالیه. خانم احمدی، لطفاً میزشونو به ایشون نشون بدین. *āli-ye. khānom-e Ahmadi, lotfan miz-eshun-o be ishun neshun bedin.*
- **Ms Ahmadi:** چشم، همین الان. بفرمایید از این طرف.  
  *chashm, hamin alān. befarmāyid az in taraf.* — Certainly, right away. This way, please.  
  Spoken: چشم، همین الان. بفرمایین از این طرف. *chashm, hamin alān. befarmāyin az in taraf.*
- **Mina:** خیلی لطف کردید. با اجازه‌تان.  
  *kheyli lotf kardid. bā ejāze-tān.* — That's very kind of you. Excuse me (lit. with your permission).  
  Spoken: خیلی لطف کردین. با اجازه‌تون. *kheyli lotf kardin. bā ejāze-tun.*
- **Mr Karimi:** خواهش می‌کنم. موفق باشید.  
  *khāhesh mi-konam. movaffagh bāshid.* — You're welcome. Good luck (lit. be successful).  
  Spoken: خواهش می‌کنم. موفق باشین. *khāhesh mi-konam. movaffagh bāshin.*

### Two scales: written or spoken, respectful or familiar

Register in Persian runs along two separate scales, and it helps to keep them apart.

**Written or spoken.** Iranians write فارسی نوشتاری (*fārsi-ye neveshtāri*, ‘written Persian’) and speak فارسی گفتاری (*fārsi-ye goftāri*, ‘spoken Persian’), whose standard is the speech of Tehran. You met the sound changes in A2 lesson 16: است (*ast*) becomes *-e*, می‌روم (*mi-ravam*) becomes می‌رم (*mi-ram*), خانه (*khāne*) becomes خونه (*khune*). This lesson adds a level on top: the **formal written** Persian of official letters, the news and speeches, which has words of its own such as اکنون (*aknun*, ‘now’) and می‌باشد (*mi-bāshad*, ‘is’).

**Respectful or familiar.** Quite separately, Persian shows how much respect you are paying. With friends, family and children you use تو (*to*) and plain verbs. With strangers, elders, officials – anyone you want to honour – you use شما (*shomā*) with a plural verb, and a special vocabulary: بنده (*bande*, ‘I (humble)’), ایشان (*ishān*, ‘he, she (respectful)’), فرمودن (*farmudan*, ‘to say (of others)’), تشریف آوردن (*tashrif āvardan*, ‘to come (of others)’).

The two scales combine freely. Ms Ahmadi in the dialogue speaks *spoken* Persian – بفرمایین (*befarmāyin*), داشتین (*dāshtin*) – that is also highly *respectful*: امری داشتید؟ (*amr-i dāshtid?*), تشریف دارند (*tashrif dārand*). An official letter is written and respectful; a text to your brother is written down, but familiar and spelled the way it sounds.

### Three shelves: formal, everyday and spoken

Think of Persian vocabulary as three shelves.

- On the **top shelf** is formal written Persian: official letters, the news, speeches, academic writing. It has words of its own, many of them Arabic or literary – می‌باشد (*mi-bāshad*), اکنون (*aknun*), زیرا (*zirā*), نیز (*niz*), وی (*vey*) – and it loves compound verbs built on a noun of action: مراجعه کردن (*morāje'e kardan*, ‘to go (to an office) in person’), دریافت کردن (*daryāft kardan*, ‘to receive’).
- On the **middle shelf** is everyday written Persian – the main line of this course, novels, most of the internet.
- On the **bottom shelf** is spoken Tehrani, which changes the *sounds* of the middle shelf (A2 lesson 16) and swaps in a few words of its own: الان (*alān*), آره (*āre*), واسه (*vāse*).

The top shelf is not 'better' Persian, only more formal: an essay full of می‌باشد sounds pompous, and editors cross it out. And **don't mix shelves in one sentence**: *اکنون می‌رم خونه* sounds about as natural as 'I shall now pop home, mate'.

### Honorifics and the plural of respect

In A2 you met شما with a plural verb, and ایشان (*ishān*). Here is the whole system.

**The plural of respect.** To speak *about* someone you respect – a teacher, a doctor, a manager, a parent or grandparent, anyone older or important – use ایشان (*ishān*) (spoken *ishun*) instead of او (*u*), and a **plural verb** for one person: ایشان آمدند (*ishān āmadand*, ‘he/she has come’), پدرم گفتند (*pedar-am goftand*, ‘my father said’). Do it most of all when the person is present: pointing at someone with او sounds rude.

**The humble I.** The other side of respect is modesty. بنده (*bande*) – literally *servant* – replaces من (*man*) in polite speech and in letters; the verb stays singular: بنده موافقم (*bande movāfegh-am*, ‘I agree’).

**Titles.** For a man, آقای (*āghā-ye*) (Mr) becomes جناب آقای (*jenāb-e āghā-ye*) in letters, speeches and introductions; for a woman, خانم (*khānom*) becomes سرکار خانم (*sarkār-e khānom*). Professional titles stack on top: جناب آقای دکتر کریمی (*jenāb-e āghā-ye doktor Karimi*). For *you*, the most deferential forms are حضرت‌عالی (*hazrat-āli*) and جناب‌عالی (*jenāb-āli*) – roughly *Your Excellency*. Between friends they are a joke.

| تو (*to*) | شما (*shomā*) + plural verb | you – to anyone who is not family or a close friend |

| پدرم گفت (*pedar-am goft*) | پدرم گفتند (*pedar-am goftand*) | my father said – parents and grandparents, in many families |

### Polite verbs: lifting others, lowering yourself

Persian has pairs of verbs for everyday actions: a **respectful** one for what *other* people do, and a **humble** one for what *you* do. Using a respectful verb about yourself is the classic learner's mistake – it sounds as if you think rather highly of yourself.

- فرمودن (*farmudan*) – *to say, to command*, of others. Its present stem فرما (*farmā*) gives بفرمایید (*befarmāyid*), the most useful word in polite Persian.
- تشریف آوردن (*tashrif āvardan*) – literally *to bring one's honour* – is *to come*; تشریف بردن (*tashrif bordan*) is *to go*, تشریف داشتن (*tashrif dāshtan*) *to be there*.
- میل کردن (*meyl kardan*) – *to eat, to drink* – is for offering: چای میل دارید؟ (*chāy meyl dārid?*) For yourself, just eat: می‌خورم (*mi-khoram*) (though میل ندارم (*meyl na-dāram*, ‘I don't feel like any’) is fine).
- عرض کردن (*arz kardan*) – *to say*, of yourself: عرض کنم که… (*arz konam ke…*, ‘let me say that…’), عرض کردم (*arz kardam*, ‘I said’), and as a noun, عرضی داشتم (*arz-i dāshtam*, ‘I had something to say’).
- خدمت رسیدن (*khedmat residan*) – *to come and see you* – and مرخص شدن (*morakkhas shodan*) – *to take my leave* – are the humble verbs of arriving and leaving.

### Opening and closing a letter

Formal Persian letters and emails are built from fixed formulas. You don't need to invent anything – choose the right piece for each slot.

## A formal letter or email

1. **To whom**: جناب آقای دکتر کریمی (*jenāb-e āghā-ye doktor Karimi*) or سرکار خانم احمدی (*sarkār-e khānom-e Ahmadi*), often followed by the position: مدیر محترم انتشارات (*modir-e mohtaram-e enteshārāt*, ‘the respected director of the publishing house’).
2. **Greeting**: با سلام و احترام، (*bā salām va ehterām,*, ‘with greetings and respect’) – the Persian *Dear Sir or Madam*.
3. **First line**: احتراماً، به استحضار می‌رساند که… (*ehterāman, be estehzār mi-resānad ke…*, ‘respectfully, this is to inform you that…’)
4. **A request**: خواهشمند است… (*khāheshmand ast…*, ‘it is requested that… (please…)’) or, more personally, خواهشمندم… (*khāheshmand-am…*, ‘I would be grateful if…’)
5. **Attachments**: به پیوست (*be peyvast*, ‘attached’)
6. **Thanks**: پیشاپیش از همکاری شما سپاسگزارم. (*pishāpish az hamkāri-ye shomā sepāsgozār-am.*, ‘thank you in advance for your cooperation’)
7. **Closing**: با تشکر و احترام، (*bā tashakkor va ehterām,*, ‘with thanks and respect’), با سپاس، (*bā sepās,*, ‘with thanks’) or ارادتمند، (*erādatmand,*, ‘yours sincerely’) – then your full name and position.

## A message to a friend

- Opening: سلام سارا جان! (*salām Sārā jān!*, ‘hi Sara dear!’), سلام عزیزم! (*salām aziz-am!*, ‘hi dear!’), سلام، خوبی؟ (*salām, khub-i?*, ‘hi, how are you?’)
- Closing: قربانت (*ghorbān-at*) (spoken قربونت (*ghorbun-et*), 'love you'), فعلاً (*fe'lan*, ‘bye for now’), مراقب خودت باش (*morāgheb-e khod-at bāsh*, ‘take care’) – and between close friends and family, می‌بوسمت (*mi-busam-at*, ‘kisses’).

### A formal email

_A week later Mina falls ill and has to miss Monday's meeting. This is her email to the director. Almost every line is a fixed formula of formal correspondence._

- موضوع: درخواست تغییر زمان جلسه  
  *mowzu': darkhāst-e taghyir-e zamān-e jalase* — Subject: Request to change the time of the meeting

- جناب آقای کریمی، مدیر محترم انتشارات

  *ehterāman, be estehzār mi-resānad ke bande be dalil-e bimāri ghāder be hozur dar jalase-ye ruz-e doshanbe nistam.* — I am writing to inform you that, because of illness, I am unable to attend Monday's meeting.  
  _احتراماً، به استحضار می‌رساند – literally 'respectfully, (this letter) brings to (your) attention' – is the classic first line of an official letter. قادر به … نیستم is the formal way to say نمی‌توانم._
- متأسفانه پزشک سه روز استراحت تجویز کرده است.  
  *mota'assefāne pezeshk se ruz esterāhat tajviz karde ast.* — Unfortunately the doctor has prescribed three days' rest.
- خواهشمند است در صورت امکان، جلسه به روز چهارشنبه موکول شود.  
  *khāheshmand ast dar surat-e emkān, jalase be ruz-e chahārshanbe mowkul shavad.* — If possible, I would ask that the meeting be postponed to Wednesday.

### Titles, ta'arof and the grammar of respect

Politeness in Persian lives in the grammar as much as in the words. Behind it is تعارف (*ta'ārof*), the art of offering, insisting and declining that you met in A1 – and the same instinct produces the plural of respect, the humble بنده and the verbs that lift the other person up while lowering yourself.

A few things that surprise learners:

- **Titles are everywhere.** Anyone with an engineering degree is مهندس (*mohandes*), anyone with a doctorate or a medical degree is دکتر (*doktor*), a teacher or a master craftsman is استاد (*ostād*), and an older man who has made the pilgrimage to Mecca is حاج آقا (*hāj āghā*). People are addressed by the title alone: ببخشید، آقای مهندس… (*bebakhshid, āghā-ye mohandes…*)
- **Parents and grandparents.** In many families children say شما to their parents and grandparents and use the plural verb about them; in others تو is normal. Both are warm.
- **Mock humility is a joke between friends.** چاکرم (*chāker-am*, ‘I'm your humble servant’) and نوکرتم (*nowkar-et-am*, ‘I'm your servant’) are slangy, affectionate answers to a compliment, and calling a friend حضرت‌عالی (*hazrat-āli*) means something like 'Your Majesty forgot to call me back'.
- **Letters are full of formulas.** احتراماً (*ehterāman*), به استحضار می‌رساند (*be estehzār mi-resānad*) and خواهشمند است (*khāheshmand ast*) come from the long tradition of Persian chancery prose, which reached its most elaborate under the Qajar kings: letters from their court at the Golestan Palace in Tehran could open with several lines of titles before getting to the point.

The safe rule for a learner is **start formal**. If an Iranian wants you to switch to تو, they will say so – often with راحت باش (*rāhat bāsh*, ‘relax, make yourself at home’).

### Tips

- When in doubt, use شما and the plural verb. Too much respect never offends; too little can.
- Respectful verbs are for other people, humble ones for yourself. Never say *من فرمودم* ('I deigned to say') – and never tell someone *شما عرض کردید*, which makes them your servant.
- ایشان is the polite way to point to someone who is standing next to you: ایشان همکار من هستند – 'this is my colleague'. Using او with the person right there sounds blunt.
- بفرمایید does a dozen jobs: 'come in', 'sit down', 'help yourself', 'go ahead', 'here you are', 'after you' – and, on an office phone, 'hello, how can I help?'.
- Keep each sentence on one shelf. اکنون می‌رم خونه mixes a formal word with spoken forms and sounds comic; say الان می‌رم خونه, or write اکنون به منزل می‌روم.
- Copy the other person. If an email to you begins با سلام و احترام, answer the same way; if a colleague texts you in spoken spelling, you can do the same.

3. کی تشریف می‌آورید؟ — *key tashrif mi-āvarid?*
4. می‌خواستم عرض کنم که موافقم. — *mi-khāstam arz konam ke movāfegh-am.*
5. پیشاپیش از همکاری شما سپاسگزارم. — *pishāpish az hamkāri-ye shomā sepāsgozār-am.*
6. با اجازه‌ی شما، مرخص می‌شوم. — *bā ejāze-ye shomā, morakkhas mi-shavam.*

</details>

### Put the words in order

**Put the words in order**

1. ایشان / هستند. / استاد / ما — She is our professor.
2. چای / میل / بفرمایید، / کنید. — Please, have some tea.
3. خواهشمند / است / موکول / به / شود. / جلسه / چهارشنبه — Please postpone the meeting to Wednesday.
4. نظر / بنده / شما / با / موافقم. — I agree with your view.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. ایشان استاد ما هستند.
2. بفرمایید، چای میل کنید.
3. خواهشمند است جلسه به چهارشنبه موکول شود.
4. بنده با نظر شما موافقم.

</details>

### Match the spoken form with its meaning

**Give the meaning**

1. می‌رم — ?
2. خونه — ?
3. اون — ?
4. بهش — ?
5. می‌خوام — ?
6. دیگه — ?
7. ایشون — ?
8. تموم — ?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. می‌رم = I go (written می‌روم)
2. خونه = house, home (written خانه)
3. اون = that (written آن)
4. بهش = to him, to her (written به او)
5. می‌خوام = I want (written می‌خواهم)
6. دیگه = any more; other (written دیگر)
7. ایشون = he, she – respectful (written ایشان)
8. تموم = finished (written تمام)

</details>

### Listen and choose

**Listening**

1. (Listen on the website.) They have already left. / He hasn't arrived yet. / She is still asleep.
2. (Listen on the website.) Please have some pastries. / Please sit down. / Please don't go to any trouble.
3. (Listen on the website.) You said you would come tomorrow. / He said he would call tomorrow. / I said I would come and see you tomorrow.
4. (Listen on the website.) May I come in? / With your permission, I'll take my leave. / Please give me permission to speak.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. ایشان هنوز تشریف نیاورده‌اند. — He hasn't arrived yet.
2. بفرمایید، شیرینی میل کنید. — Please have some pastries.
3. عرض کردم که فردا خدمت می‌رسم. — I said I would come and see you tomorrow.
4. با اجازه‌ی شما، مرخص می‌شوم. — With your permission, I'll take my leave.

</details>
