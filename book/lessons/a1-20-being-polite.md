# Being polite: ta'arof, invitations and phone calls
## تعارف نکنید!
*ta'ārof na-konid!* — Beginner (A1), lesson 20. [Open this lesson with audio and interactive exercises](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/course/a1-20-being-polite.html)

Iranians are famous for their courtesy, and it comes with a whole set of phrases: what you say when you arrive, when you are offered tea, when someone has cooked for you, when someone is working, when you leave. Behind them lies تعارف – ta'arof, the elaborate politeness of offering and refusing. In this last A1 lesson you learn the formulas, how to accept an invitation, and how to get through a simple phone call.

**In this lesson you will learn to:**

- use the everyday formulas: بفرمایید، خواهش می‌کنم، ببخشید، چشم
- thank a host and answer thanks: دست شما درد نکند، نوش جان، قابل ندارد
- greet someone at work with خسته نباشید
- understand ta'arof: when 'no' means 'ask me again'
- invite someone, accept an invitation and make a simple phone call

### Dinner at the Karimis'

_Mr and Mrs Karimi have invited Tom to their home for dinner. He arrives a little late, with flowers._

- **Mr Karimi:** سلام تام جان، خوش آمدی! بفرما تو.  
  *salām Tām jān, khosh āmadi! befarmā tu.* — Hello Tom, welcome! Come in.  
  Spoken: سلام تام جان، خوش اومدی! بفرما تو. *salām Tām jān, khosh umadi! befarmā tu.*
- **Tom:** سلام، ببخشید دیر کردم. این گل‌ها برای شماست.  
  *salām, bebakhshid dir kardam. in gol-hā barā-ye shomāst.* — Hello, sorry I'm late. These flowers are for you.
- **Mr Karimi:** وای، چرا زحمت کشیدی؟  
  *vāy, cherā zahmat keshidi?* — Oh, you shouldn't have!  
  Literally: why did you take trouble?
- **Tom:** قابل شما را ندارد.  
  *ghābel-e shomā rā na-dārad.* — It's nothing.  
  Spoken: قابل شما رو نداره. *ghābel-e shomā ro na-dāre.*  
  Literally: it is not worthy of you
- **Mrs Karimi:** بفرمایید بنشینید. چای می‌خورید؟  
  *befarmāyid beneshinid. chāy mi-khorid?* — Please sit down. Will you have some tea?  
  Spoken: بفرمایید بشینید. چای می‌خورین؟ *befarmāyid beshinid. chāy mi-khorin?*
- **Tom:** نه، ممنون. زحمت نکشید.  
  *na, mamnun. zahmat na-keshid.* — No, thank you. Please don't go to any trouble.  
  Spoken: نه، ممنون. زحمت نکشین. *na, mamnun. zahmat na-keshin.*
- **Mrs Karimi:** چه زحمتی؟ تعارف نکنید!  
  *che zahmat-i? ta'ārof na-konid!* — What trouble? Don't be shy!  
  Spoken: چه زحمتی؟ تعارف نکنین! *che zahmat-i? ta'ārof na-konin!*
- **Tom:** چشم، پس یک چای می‌خورم.  
  *chashm, pas yek chāy mi-khoram.* — All right then, I'll have a tea.  
  Spoken: چشم، پس یه چای می‌خورم. *chashm, pas ye chāy mi-khoram.*
- **Mrs Karimi:** بفرمایید، شام حاضر است.  
  *befarmāyid, shām hāzer ast.* — Please come, dinner is ready.  
  Spoken: بفرمایید، شام حاضره. *befarmāyid, shām hāzere.*
- **Tom:** خیلی خوشمزه بود. دستتان درد نکند!  
  *kheyli khoshmaze bud. dast-etān dard na-konad!* — It was delicious. Thank you so much!  
  Spoken: خیلی خوشمزه بود. دستتون درد نکنه! *kheyli khoshmaze bud. dast-etun dard na-kone!*  
  Literally: may your hand not hurt
- **Mrs Karimi:** نوش جان! باز هم بخورید.  
  *nush-e jān! bāz ham bekhorid.* — Enjoy! Have some more.  
  Spoken: نوش جان! باز هم بخورین. *nush-e jān! bāz ham bekhorin.*
- **Tom:** نه، دیگر سیر شدم. ممنون.  
  *na, digar sir shodam. mamnun.* — No, I'm full now. Thank you.  
  Spoken: نه، دیگه سیر شدم. ممنون. *na, dige sir shodam. mamnun.*
- **Mr Karimi:** خواهش می‌کنم. باز هم به ما سر بزنید.  
  *khāhesh mi-konam. bāz ham be mā sar bezanid.* — You're welcome. Come and see us again.  
  Spoken: خواهش می‌کنم. باز هم به ما سر بزنین. *khāhesh mi-konam. bāz ham be mā sar bezanin.*
- **Tom:** حتماً. خیلی ممنون، خدا حافظ!  
  *hatman. kheyli mamnun, khodā hāfez!* — Of course. Thank you very much, goodbye!

### Ta'arof in one minute

Look at what happened in that dialogue. Tom brought flowers and Mr Karimi said چرا زحمت کشیدی؟ (*cherā zahmat keshidi?*, ‘why did you go to the trouble?’) – not a complaint, just good manners. Tom answered with the classic قابل شما را ندارد (*ghābel-e shomā rā na-dārad*, ‘it is not worthy of you’). Mrs Karimi offered tea, Tom said no once, she insisted – تعارف نکنید! (*ta'ārof na-konid!*, ‘don't stand on ceremony!’) – and then he said yes.

This polite dance of offering, refusing and insisting is تعارف (*ta'ārof*). It is part of every visit, every meal and even every taxi ride. You don't need to master it now, but you do need the formulas: Iranians use them dozens of times a day, and they are delighted when a foreigner uses them too.

Almost all of them are wishes or images: *may your hand not hurt*, *may you not be tired*, *sweet to your soul*. Learn them as whole phrases, like *bless you* in English.

### The everyday formulas – and their answers

Each formula has its moment, and many have a fixed answer. Don't translate them word for word: دست شما درد نکند (*dast-e shomā dard na-konad*) is simply *thank you* for something someone has made or done for you, and خسته نباشید (*khaste na-bāshid*) is what you say to a shopkeeper, a teacher at the end of class or a friend who has just finished a job.

The formulas with a verb use the be- / na- forms you know from the imperative and from باید: نکند (*na-konad*, ‘may it not do’), نباشید (*na-bāshid*, ‘may you not be’). To a friend, change *shomā* to *to* and the endings to the singular: دستت درد نکند (*dast-at dard na-konad*), خسته نباشی (*khaste na-bāshi*).

| Say | when | literally | the answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| بفرمایید (*befarmāyid*) | come in, sit down, help yourself, here you are, after you | please do | ممنون (*mamnun*) |
| خوش آمدید (*khosh āmadid*) | a guest arrives | you came happily | خوش باشید (*khosh bāshid*) |
| ببخشید (*bebakhshid*) | excuse me, sorry | forgive | خواهش می‌کنم (*khāhesh mi-konam*) |
| چشم (*chashm*) | yes, of course (to a request) | on my eye | چشمتان بی‌بلا (*chashm-etān bi-balā*) |
| قابل ندارد (*ghābel na-dārad*) | giving a gift; a seller asked the price | it is not worthy | صاحبش قابل است (*sāheb-ash ghābel ast*) |

### The ta'arof dance: offering, refusing, insisting

When you are offered something – tea, fruit, a second helping, the front seat – the polite pattern is: refuse once, let the host insist, then accept. Hosts expect to offer at least twice, so a first *no* is never the end of the matter.

Most of the phrases are polite imperatives, and the refusals are **negative** imperatives with **na-** (lesson 16): زحمت نکشید (*zahmat na-keshid*, ‘don't trouble yourself’), تعارف نکنید (*ta'ārof na-konid*, ‘don't stand on ceremony’). A very polite word for *eat* or *have* is میل کردن (*meyl kardan*): میل کنید (*meyl konid*, ‘please have some’).

If you really mean no, say it with a smile and a reason: نه، واقعاً ممنون. سیر شدم. (*na, vāghe'an mamnun. sir shodam.*, ‘No, really, thank you. I'm full.’)

| Step | Persian | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. the offer | بفرمایید، میل کنید. (*befarmāyid, meyl konid.*) | Please, have some. |
| 2. the first refusal | نه، ممنون. زحمت نکشید. (*na, mamnun. zahmat na-keshid.*) | No, thank you. Don't go to any trouble. |
| 3. the host insists | تعارف نکنید! خواهش می‌کنم. (*ta'ārof na-konid! khāhesh mi-konam.*) | Don't be shy! I insist. |
| 4. accepting | چشم، پس کمی. (*chashm, pas kami.*) | All right, just a little then. |
| 5. thanking | دستتان درد نکند. (*dast-etān dard na-konad.*) | Thank you. |
| 6. leaving | ببخشید، زحمت دادیم. (*bebakhshid, zahmat dādim.*) | Sorry for all the trouble. |
| 7. the host's answer | زحمت نبود، رحمت بود. (*zahmat na-bud, rahmat bud.*) | It was no trouble, it was a blessing. |

- میوه میل کنید.  
  *mive meyl konid.* — Please have some fruit.  
  Spoken: میوه میل کنین. *mive meyl konin.*
- نه، ممنون. زحمت نکشید.  
  *na, mamnun. zahmat na-keshid.* — No, thank you. Don't go to any trouble.
- تعارف نکنید، بفرمایید!  
  *ta'ārof na-konid, befarmāyid!* — Don't be shy, help yourself!  
  Spoken: تعارف نکنین، بفرمایین! *ta'ārof na-konin, befarmāyin!*
- ببخشید، خیلی زحمت دادیم.  
  *bebakhshid, kheyli zahmat dādim.* — Sorry, we've caused you a lot of trouble.
- زحمت نبود، رحمت بود.  
  *zahmat na-bud, rahmat bud.* — It was no trouble, it was a blessing.  
  _zahmat and rahmat rhyme – a favourite piece of politeness_

### On the phone, and invitations

Iranians answer the phone with الو؟ (*alo?*) – or, at work, with بفرمایید. (*befarmāyid.*) Two things are new. First, when you ask for a respected person, Persian uses the **plural** verb out of respect, even for one person: آقای کریمی هستند؟ (*āghā-ye Karimi hastand?*, ‘Is Mr Karimi there?’) Second, *hold the line* is an image: گوشی دستتان باشد (*gushi dast-etān bāshad*), literally *let the receiver stay in your hand*.

To invite someone, the simplest way is مهمان ما باشید (*mehmān-e mā bāshid*, ‘be our guest(s)’) – باشید (*bāshid*) is the be- form of *to be*. Accept with با کمال میل (*bā kamāl-e meyl*, ‘with the greatest pleasure’) or حتماً (*hatman*, ‘definitely’). And on the phone, goodbye comes in a long string: قربان شما، خدا حافظ! (*ghorbān-e shomā, khodā hāfez!*)

| Persian | English | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| الو؟ (*alo?*) | Hello? | answering the phone |
| سلام، من تام هستم. (*salām, man Tām hastam.*) | Hello, it's Tom. | saying who you are |
| ببخشید، آقای کریمی هستند؟ (*bebakhshid, āghā-ye Karimi hastand?*) | Excuse me, is Mr Karimi there? | plural of respect |
| گوشی دستتان باشد. (*gushi dast-etān bāshad.*) | Hold the line, please. | let the receiver be in your hand |
| ایشان نیستند. (*ishān nistand.*) | He / she isn't here. | ایشان: respectful he / she |
| بعداً زنگ می‌زنم. (*ba'dan zang mi-zanam.*) | I'll call back later. |  |
| اشتباه گرفتید. (*eshtebāh gereftid.*) | You've got the wrong number. | you took (it) wrong |
| فردا شب مهمان ما باشید. (*fardā shab mehmān-e mā bāshid.*) | Be our guests tomorrow night. | an invitation |
| با کمال میل! (*bā kamāl-e meyl!*) | With great pleasure! | accepting |
| قربان شما، خدا حافظ! (*ghorbān-e shomā, khodā hāfez!*) | Thank you so much, goodbye! | I am your sacrifice |

- الو، سلام. ببخشید، خانم احمدی هستند؟  
  *alo, salām. bebakhshid, khānom-e Ahmadi hastand?* — Hello. Excuse me, is Ms Ahmadi there?  
  Spoken: الو، سلام. ببخشید، خانم احمدی هستن؟ *alo, salām. bebakhshid, khānom-e Ahmadi hastan?*
- گوشی دستتان باشد، الان صدایش می‌کنم.  
  *gushi dast-etān bāshad, alān sedā-yash mi-konam.* — Hold the line, I'll call her now.  
  Spoken: گوشی دستتون باشه، الان صداش می‌کنم. *gushi dast-etun bāshe, alān sedā-sh mi-konam.*
- جمعه ناهار مهمان ما باشید.  
  *jom'e nāhār mehmān-e mā bāshid.* — Be our guests for lunch on Friday.  
  Spoken: جمعه ناهار مهمون ما باشین. *jom'e nāhār mehmun-e mā bāshin.*
- با کمال میل! حتماً می‌آیم.  
  *bā kamāl-e meyl! hatman mi-āyam.* — With great pleasure! I'll definitely come.  
  Spoken: با کمال میل! حتماً میام. *bā kamāl-e meyl! hatman miyām.*

### Tom and the taxi driver

_Tom's first lesson in ta'arof did not happen at a dinner table. Read what happened._

- تام در تهران با یک تاکسی به خانه رفت.  
  *Tām dar Tehrān bā yek tāksi be khāne raft.* — In Tehran, Tom took a taxi home.
- او از راننده پرسید: «چقدر می‌شود؟»  
  *u az rānande porsid: «cheghadr mi-shavad?»* — He asked the driver: "How much is it?"
- راننده گفت: «قابل ندارد.»  
  *rānande goft: «ghābel na-dārad.»* — The driver said: "It's nothing."
- تام گفت: «ممنون!» و از ماشین پیاده شد.  
  *Tām goft: «mamnun!» o az māshin piyāde shod.* — Tom said "Thanks!" and got out of the car.
- راننده تعجب کرد و گفت: «آقا، پول!»  
  *rānande ta'ajjob kard o goft: «āghā, pul!»* — The driver was surprised and said: "Sir, the money!"
- تام خندید، پول داد و گفت: «ببخشید، تعارف بود؟»  
  *Tām khandid, pul dād o goft: «bebakhshid, ta'ārof bud?»* — Tom laughed, paid and said: "Sorry – was that ta'arof?"
- حالا تام می‌داند که «قابل ندارد» تعارف است.  
  *hālā Tām mi-dānad ke «ghābel na-dārad» ta'ārof ast.* — Now Tom knows that "ghābel na-dārad" is ta'arof.

### Ta'arof: politeness as an art

تعارف (*ta'ārof*) is the Iranian art of putting the other person first. At a door, two people may stand for a while saying بفرمایید (*befarmāyid*, ‘after you’) – نه، شما بفرمایید (*na, shomā befarmāyid*, ‘no, after you’). A host keeps filling your plate and your tea glass; a guest praises the food and leaves a little on the plate to show there was more than enough. A shopkeeper or a taxi driver who says قابل ندارد (*ghābel na-dārad*) is being gracious, not generous – you insist, and you pay.

The language is full of exaggerated humility: قربان شما (*ghorbān-e shomā*, ‘I am your sacrifice’) means *thanks, bye*, and چاکرم (*chāker-am*, ‘I'm your servant’) is a cheerful *thanks* among Tehran men. It isn't insincere; it is a way of showing respect, احترام (*ehterām*), and of keeping social life smooth.

Foreigners are forgiven almost everything. A safe rule: refuse an offer once, accept the second time, thank the cook with *dast-etān dard na-konad*, and when you leave, say *bebakhshid, zahmat dādim*.

### Tips

- **befarmāyid** is the Swiss army knife of Persian politeness: come in, sit down, help yourself, here you are, after you, go ahead, I'm listening. The situation tells you which.
- A first 'no' is often just politeness. If you are the host, offer again; if you are the guest and really want something, accept the second offer.
- **chashm** (eye) is the polite yes to a request – from children to parents, from staff to customers. The kind answer is *chashm-at bi-balā*, 'may your eye be safe from harm'.
- Spoken and friendly forms: *dast-et dard nakone* (to a friend), *dast-etun dard nakone* (polite); *khaste nabāshi* / *khaste nabāshin*.
- In shops and taxis *ghābel na-dārad* never means it's free. Say *khāhesh mi-konam, befarmāyid* and hand over the money.

3. You ask a shopkeeper the price and he says قابل ندارد. You… — take it for free / insist and pay / leave the shop
4. You walk into a workshop where people are busy. You greet them with… — نوش جان! / خسته نباشید! / قابل ندارد!
5. On the phone, 'Hold the line, please' is… — گوشی دستتان باشد. / اشتباه گرفتم. / بعداً زنگ می‌زنم.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. دستتان درد نکند! — dast-etān dard na-konad – 'may your hand not hurt' – thanks someone for what they made or did.
2. نوش جان! — nush-e jān: 'may it be sweet to your soul'.
3. insist and pay — It is ta'arof: a polite formula, not a gift. Insist – khāhesh mi-konam – and he will tell you the price.
4. خسته نباشید! — khaste na-bāshid, 'may you not be tired', greets people at work.
5. گوشی دستتان باشد. — gushi dast-etān bāshad: 'let the receiver stay in your hand'.

</details>
