# A place to live: renting a flat
## خانه‌ی اجاره‌ای
*khāne-ye ejāre-i* — Elementary (A2), lesson 13. [Open this lesson with audio and interactive exercises](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/course/a2-13-a-place-to-live.html)

Finding a flat in an Iranian city means learning a whole new set of numbers. In this lesson you describe a home – its rooms, its floor, its size – with ordinal numbers like اول، دوم، سوم and words like دوخوابه, and you find out how renting works in Iran: a monthly اجاره plus a very large deposit called رهن. Since flats are priced in millions of tomans, you also learn to read and say big numbers.

**In this lesson you will learn to:**

- describe a flat or a house: rooms, floor, size and features
- use ordinal numbers: اول، دوم، سوم … and اولین، دومین
- say which floor: طبقه‌ی سوم، همکف، زیرزمین
- read and say big numbers: هزار، میلیون، میلیارد
- understand how اجاره and رهن work

### Viewing a flat

_Tom and Reza share a flat, but they need more space. An agent from the local estate agency shows them a flat in a new building nearby._

- **Agent:** بفرمایید، این هم آپارتمان. طبقه‌ی سوم است و آسانسور دارد.  
  *befarmāyid, in ham āpārtemān. tabaghe-ye sevvom ast o āsānsor dārad.* — Here we are, this is the flat. It's on the third floor, and there's a lift.  
  Spoken: بفرمایید، اینم آپارتمان. طبقه‌ی سومه و آسانسور داره. *befarmāyid, in-am āpārtemān. tabaghe-ye sevvom-e o āsānsor dāre.*
- **Reza:** چند متر است؟  
  *chand metr ast?* — How big is it?  
  Spoken: چند متره؟ *chand metr-e?*  
  Literally: how many metres is it?
- **Agent:** هشتاد و پنج متر، دوخوابه. پذیرایی‌اش هم خیلی نورگیر است.  
  *hashtād o panj metr, do-khābe. pazirāyi-ash ham kheyli nur-gir ast.* — Eighty-five square metres, two bedrooms. And the living room gets a lot of light.
- **Tom:** این اتاق اول است؟ برای من کمی کوچک است.  
  *in otāgh-e avval ast? barā-ye man kam-i kuchak ast.* — Is this the first bedroom? It's a bit small for me.  
  Spoken: این اتاق اوله؟ برام یه کم کوچیکه. *in otāgh-e avval-e? barā-m ye kam kuchik-e.*
- **Agent:** اتاق دوم بزرگ‌تر است و بالکن دارد.  
  *otāgh-e dovvom bozorg-tar ast o bālkon dārad.* — The second room is bigger and has a balcony.  
  Spoken: اتاق دوم بزرگ‌تره و بالکن داره. *otāgh-e dovvom bozorg-tar-e o bālkon dāre.*
- **Reza:** خوب است. پارکینگ و انباری هم دارد؟  
  *khub ast. pārking o anbāri ham dārad?* — Good. Does it come with a parking space and a storeroom?  
  Spoken: خوبه. پارکینگ و انباری هم داره؟ *khub-e. pārking o anbāri ham dāre?*
- **Agent:** بله، هر دو در زیرزمین هستند.  
  *bale, har do dar zir-zamin hastand.* — Yes, both are in the basement.
- **Tom:** اجاره‌اش چقدر است؟  
  *ejāre-ash cheghadr ast?* — How much is the rent?  
  Spoken: اجاره‌ش چقدره؟ *ejāre-sh cheghadr-e?*
- **Agent:** سیصد میلیون تومان رهن و ماهی دوازده میلیون اجاره.  
  *sisad milyun tomān rahn o māh-i davāzdah milyun ejāre.* — A deposit of three hundred million tomans, and twelve million a month in rent.  
  Spoken: سیصد میلیون رهن، ماهی دوازده میلیون اجاره. *sisad milyun rahn, māh-i davāzdah milyun ejāre.*
- **Tom:** سیصد میلیون؟! من این‌قدر پول ندارم!  
  *sisad milyun?! man in-ghadr pul na-dāram!* — Three hundred million?! I don't have that much money!
- **Reza:** نگران نباش، رهن را آخر قرارداد پس می‌دهند. پولت گم نمی‌شود!  
  *negarān na-bāsh, rahn rā ākhar-e gharārdād pas mi-dahand. pul-at gom nemi-shavad!* — Don't worry, you get the deposit back at the end of the contract. Your money doesn't disappear!  
  Spoken: نگران نباش، رهنو آخر قرارداد پس می‌دن. پولت گم نمی‌شه! *negarān na-bāsh, rahn-o ākhar-e gharārdād pas mi-dan. pul-et gom nemi-she!*
- **Reza:** آقا، اگر رهن را کمتر کنیم، اجاره چقدر می‌شود؟  
  *āghā, agar rahn rā kam-tar konim, ejāre cheghadr mi-shavad?* — If we pay a smaller deposit, how much will the rent be?  
  Spoken: آقا، اگه رهنو کمتر کنیم، اجاره چقدر می‌شه؟ *āghā, age rahn-o kam-tar konim, ejāre cheghadr mi-she?*
- **Agent:** اجاره بیشتر می‌شود. اما صاحب‌خانه آدم خوبی است؛ امروز با او صحبت می‌کنم.  
  *ejāre bishtar mi-shavad. ammā sāheb-khāne ādam-e khub-i ast; emruz bā u sohbat mi-konam.* — The rent goes up. But the landlord is a nice man; I'll talk to him today.  
  Spoken: اجاره بیشتر می‌شه. ولی صاحب‌خونه آدم خوبیه؛ امروز باهاش صحبت می‌کنم. *ejāre bishtar mi-she. vali sāheb-khune ādam-e khub-i-ye; emruz bā-hāsh sohbat mi-konam.*
- **Tom:** خیلی ممنون. من این آپارتمان را دوست دارم، مخصوصاً بالکنش را!  
  *kheyli mamnun. man in āpārtemān rā dust dāram, makhsusan bālkon-ash rā!* — Thanks very much. I like this flat – especially the balcony!

### Renting the Iranian way

When Iranians rent a home, they talk about two sums of money, not one.

- اجاره (*ejāre*, ‘rent’) is what you pay every month: ماهی دوازده میلیون (*māh-i davāzdah milyun*, ‘twelve million a month’). Notice the ـی (*-i*) again: ماهی (*māh-i*) means *per month*, just as روزی (*ruz-i*) meant *per day* at the pharmacy.
- رهن (*rahn*, ‘deposit’) is a large lump sum you give the landlord when you move in. It is not a fee: you get every toman back when you leave. The bigger the deposit, the lower the monthly rent – and if you pay a very large one, a رهن کامل (*rahn-e kāmel*, ‘full deposit’), there is no monthly rent at all.

Because flats cost hundreds of millions of tomans, house-hunting is excellent practice for big numbers. And because flats are stacked in tall buildings, you need ordinal numbers to say where things are: طبقه‌ی سوم (*tabaghe-ye sevvom*, ‘the third floor’), اتاق دوم (*otāgh-e dovvom*, ‘the second room’).

### First, second, third: ordinal numbers

To make *fourth, fifth, sixth…*, add ـم (*-om*) to the number: چهار (*chahār*, ‘four’) → چهارم (*chahārom*, ‘fourth’). Two are a little irregular: دوم (*dovvom*, ‘second’) and سوم (*sevvom*, ‘third’) are spelled with a و and said with a double *v*. *First* has its own word, اول (*avval*); یکم (*yekom*) is used in dates and in compound numbers like بیست و یکم (*bist o yekom*, ‘twenty-first’).

The ordinal goes **after** the noun, with the ezafe: طبقه‌ی سوم (*tabaghe-ye sevvom*, ‘the third floor’), اتاق دوم (*otāgh-e dovvom*, ‘the second room’). Add ـین (*-in*) and it moves **before** the noun, with no ezafe: سومین طبقه (*sevvomin tabaghe*, ‘the third floor’). The *-in* form is a little more emphatic, and it is the usual one with بار (*bār*, ‘time’): اولین بار (*avvalin bār*, ‘the first time’).

Floors are counted the British way: همکف (*hamkaf*) is the ground floor, طبقه‌ی اول (*tabaghe-ye avval*) is one floor up, and the basement is زیرزمین (*zir-zamin*).

| سه (*se*) | سوم (*sevvom*) | سومین (*sevvomin*) | third |

| شش (*shesh*) | ششم (*sheshom*) | ششمین (*sheshomin*) | sixth |
| هفت (*haft*) | هفتم (*haftom*) | هفتمین (*haftomin*) | seventh |
| هشت (*hasht*) | هشتم (*hashtom*) | هشتمین (*hashtomin*) | eighth |
| نه (*noh*) | نهم (*nohom*) | نهمین (*nohomin*) | ninth |

### Big numbers: هزار، میلیون، میلیارد

House prices are where Persian numbers get big. You already know صد (*sad*, ‘hundred’) and هزار (*hezār*, ‘thousand’); add میلیون (*milyun*, ‘million’) and میلیارد (*milyārd*, ‘billion, a thousand million’). Say the biggest unit first and join the parts with و (*o*), exactly as you did with smaller numbers: دو میلیون و پانصد هزار (*do milyun o pānsad hezār*, ‘2,500,000’).

After a number the noun stays singular – سیصد میلیون تومان (*sisad milyun tomān*, ‘300 million tomans’) – and *millions of* is میلیون‌ها (*milyun-hā*): میلیون‌ها نفر (*milyun-hā nafar*, ‘millions of people’).

Remember that prices are *said* in tomans, although official prices are written in rials (one toman is ten rials), and that in speech people often leave out the unit altogether: ماهی دوازده میلیون (*māh-i davāzdah milyun*) simply means twelve million tomans a month. In Persian texts, long numbers are grouped in threes with the separator ٬ – ۲٬۵۰۰٬۰۰۰ – and, like all Persian numbers, they are written from left to right.

| ۳۰۰٬۰۰۰٬۰۰۰ | سیصد میلیون (*sisad milyun*) | three hundred million |
| ۱٬۰۰۰٬۰۰۰٬۰۰۰ | یک میلیارد (*yek milyārd*) | a billion |

### Describing a flat: دوخوابه، ۸۰ متری، پنج‌طبقه

Flat adverts pack a lot into a few words, using numbers to build adjectives.

- number + noun + ـه (*-e*): دوخوابه (*do-khābe*, ‘two-bedroom’) (literally *two-sleep*), پنج‌طبقه (*panj-tabaghe*, ‘five-storey’), ده‌ساله (*dah-sāle*, ‘ten years old’).
- number + متری (*metri*), with the adjective ـی (*-i*): آپارتمان ۸۰ متری (*āpārtemān-e hashtād metri*, ‘an 80-square-metre flat’). Flats are always measured in square metres, and متر (*metr*) on its own means a square metre here.

A few more words do the rest: نوساز (*now-sāz*, ‘newly built’), نورگیر (*nur-gir*, ‘bright’), مبله (*moble*, ‘furnished’), and شمالی (*shomāli*, ‘northern’) – which in a flat advert means that the building stands on the north side of its plot, so its windows face south, into the sun. Iranians pay more for that.

| Persian | English | Literally |
| --- | --- | --- |
| یک‌خوابه (*yek-khābe*) | one-bedroom | one-sleep |
| دوخوابه (*do-khābe*) | two-bedroom | two-sleep |
| ۸۰ متری (*hashtād metri*) | 80 square metres | 80-metre |
| پنج‌طبقه (*panj-tabaghe*) | five-storey | five-floor |
| ده‌ساله (*dah-sāle*) | ten years old | ten-year |
| نوساز (*now-sāz*) | newly built | new-built |
| نورگیر (*nur-gir*) | bright, sunny | light-catching |
| مبله (*moble*) | furnished | from French meublé |
| شمالی (*shomāli*) | on the north of its plot, facing the sun | northern |
| نزدیک مترو (*nazdik-e metro*) | near the metro | near metro |

- یک آپارتمان دوخوابه‌ی ۸۰ متری می‌خواهیم.  
  *yek āpārtemān-e do-khābe-ye hashtād metri mi-khāhim.* — We want a two-bedroom flat of 80 square metres.
- ساختمان پنج‌طبقه است و آسانسور دارد.  
  *sākhtemān panj-tabaghe ast o āsānsor dārad.* — The building has five floors and a lift.
- خانه نوساز و خیلی نورگیر است.  
  *khāne now-sāz o kheyli nur-gir ast.* — The flat is new and very bright.
- این آپارتمان مبله است؟  
  *in āpārtemān moble ast?* — Is this flat furnished?  
  Spoken: این آپارتمان مبله‌ست؟ *in āpārtemān moble-st?*
- ما در یک ساختمان ده‌ساله زندگی می‌کنیم.  
  *mā dar yek sākhtemān-e dah-sāle zendegi mi-konim.* — We live in a ten-year-old building.
- خانه‌ی ما کوچک است، اما نزدیک مترو است.  
  *khāne-ye mā kuchak ast, ammā nazdik-e metro ast.* — Our flat is small, but it's near the metro.

  *barā-ye rahn, az bānk vām gereftim.* — We took out a bank loan for the deposit.
- همسایه‌های ما خیلی مهربان هستند.

### Rahn, moving day and the dish that never goes back empty

Renting in Iran works differently from almost anywhere else. Instead of a small bond, the tenant hands the landlord a very large deposit, the رهن (*rahn*) – in contracts called ودیعه (*vadi'e*) – which comes back in full at the end of the lease. For the landlord it is money to invest; for the tenant it means a much smaller monthly rent. Leases usually run for a year, and the busiest moving season is summer, before the new school year starts in مهر (*Mehr*) (late September). Most deals go through a neighbourhood بنگاه (*bongāh*, ‘estate agency’), and both sides pay the agent a fee.

Modern Tehran is a city of flats, but the traditional Iranian house looked inward: rooms around a حیاط (*hayāt*, ‘courtyard’) with trees and a pool, the حوض (*howz*), behind plain walls that showed nothing to the street. The merchants' houses of Kashan, like the Tabatabaei House, are the grandest examples.

One custom moved from the old houses into the new buildings. When neighbours bring you food – a bowl of آش (*āsh*) on moving day, نذری (*nazri*, ‘votive food’) on a religious holiday – you never return the dish empty. Wash it, put something in it, even a few sweets, and give it back: ظرف خالی پس نمی‌دهند (*zarf-e khāli pas nemi-dahand*, ‘people don't give back an empty dish’).

### Tips

- **The ordinal goes after the noun, the -in form before it**: **طبقه‌ی سوم** or **سومین طبقه** – never *سوم طبقه*. With بار, the -in form is usual: **اولین بار**, the first time.
- **دوم** and **سوم** are said **dovvom** and **sevvom**, with a double v. **سی‌ام** (thirtieth) is said **si-yom**.
- **The first floor** in Iran means one floor up, as in Britain. Street level is **همکف**, and the basement is **زیرزمین**.
- Flat prices are in **millions of tomans**: say the millions first, then the thousands – **دو میلیون و پانصد هزار**. In speech the word تومان is often left out.
- In speech **خانه** becomes **خونه** and **صاحب‌خانه** becomes **صاحب‌خونه**; **ماهی** (a month, per month) stays the same.
