# Where is it? Places and positions
## کجاست؟
*kojā-st?* — Beginner (A1), lesson 8. [Open this lesson with audio and interactive exercises](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/course/a1-08-where-is-it.html)

Where is the bank? Is there a pharmacy near here? And where are my keys? In this lesson you learn the places of an Iranian neighbourhood and the things in a flat, the little words that say where things are – on, under, next to, behind, in front of, between – and how to say 'there is' and 'there isn't'.

**In this lesson you will learn to:**

- ask where something is: … kojā-st?
- say where things are with dar, ru-ye, zir-e, kenār-e, posht-e, jelo-ye, beyn-e, nazdik-e
- say 'there is / there are' with hast and 'there isn't' with nist
- name places in a city and things in a home

### A new flat

_Tom has just moved into a flat in Mina's building. Mina comes by to say hello, and Tom asks her about the neighbourhood._

- **Tom:** ببخشید، مینا خانم. این نزدیکی بانک هست؟  
  *bebakhshid, Minā khānom. in nazdiki bānk hast?* — Excuse me, Ms Mina. Is there a bank near here?
- **Mina:** بله، یک بانک در خیابان اصلی هست، کنار داروخانه.  
  *bale, yek bānk dar khiyābān-e asli hast, kenār-e dāru-khāne.* — Yes, there's a bank on the main street, next to the pharmacy.
- **Tom:** داروخانه کجاست؟  
  *dāru-khāne kojā-st?* — Where is the pharmacy?  
  Spoken: داروخونه کجاست؟ *dāru-khune kojā-st?*
- **Mina:** روبه‌روی پارک است. پارک خیلی نزدیک است.  
  *ru-be-ru-ye pārk ast. pārk kheyli nazdik ast.* — Opposite the park. The park is very close.  
  Spoken: روبه‌روی پارکه. پارک خیلی نزدیکه. *ru-be-ru-ye pārk-e. pārk kheyli nazdik-e.*
- **Tom:** سوپرمارکت هم هست؟  
  *supermārket ham hast?* — Is there a supermarket too?
- **Mina:** بله، پشت مسجد یک سوپرمارکت هست.  
  *bale, posht-e masjed yek supermārket hast.* — Yes, there's a supermarket behind the mosque.  
  Spoken: آره، پشت مسجد یه سوپرمارکت هست. *āre, posht-e masjed ye supermārket hast.*
- **Tom:** و ایستگاه مترو؟  
  *va istgāh-e metro?* — And the metro station?
- **Mina:** ایستگاه مترو کمی دور است، جلوی بیمارستان.  
  *istgāh-e metro kami dur ast, jelo-ye bimārestān.* — The metro station is a bit far, in front of the hospital.  
  Spoken: ایستگاه مترو یه کم دوره، جلوی بیمارستان. *istgāh-e metro ye kam dur-e, jelo-ye bimārestān.*
- **Tom:** ممنون. راستی، کلید من کجاست؟  
  *mamnun. rāsti, kelid-e man kojā-st?* — Thanks. By the way – where's my key?
- **Mina:** روی میز نیست؟  
  *ru-ye miz nist?* — Isn't it on the table?  
  Spoken: رو میز نیست؟ *ru miz nist?*
- **Tom:** نه، روی میز نیست. زیر مبل هم نیست.  
  *na, ru-ye miz nist. zir-e mobl ham nist.* — No, it's not on the table. It's not under the sofa either.
- **Mina:** آن چیست، کنار در؟  
  *ān chist, kenār-e dar?* — What's that, next to the door?  
  Spoken: اون چیه، کنار در؟ *un chi-ye, kenār-e dar?*
- **Tom:** آهان! کلیدم اینجاست! ممنون، مینا خانم.  
  *āhān! kelid-am injā-st! mamnun, Minā khānom.* — Aha! My key is here! Thank you, Ms Mina.

### Where is it? Is there one?

Two questions will get you round any Iranian neighbourhood. To ask where a particular place is, add *ast* to *kojā*: داروخانه کجاست؟ (*dāru-khāne kojā-st?*, ‘where is the pharmacy?’) To ask whether there is one at all, use هست (*hast*): این نزدیکی بانک هست؟ (*in nazdiki bānk hast?*, ‘is there a bank near here?’) The answer is بله، هست (*bale, hast*, ‘yes, there is’) or نه، نیست (*na, nist*, ‘no, there isn't’). این نزدیکی (*in nazdiki*) means *round here, nearby*.

The answers use small place words – روبه‌روی (*ru-be-ru-ye*, ‘opposite’), پشت (*posht-e*, ‘behind’), جلوی (*jelo-ye*, ‘in front of’), کنار (*kenār-e*, ‘next to’) – and almost all of them are followed by the ezafe. That is the main grammar point of this lesson.

Notice also راستی (*rāsti*, ‘by the way’), the word Iranians use to change the subject.

### On, under, next to: place words and the ezafe

Most Persian place words are really nouns – رو (*ru*, ‘top, face’), زیر (*zir*, ‘underside’), پشت (*posht*, ‘back’), کنار (*kenār*, ‘side, edge’) – so they link to the next word with the **ezafe**: روی میز (*ru-ye miz*, ‘on the table’) is literally *the top of the table*, and پشت در (*posht-e dar*, ‘behind the door’) is *the back of the door*. After a vowel the ezafe is written ی: *ru-ye*, *jelo-ye*.

Only a handful of little words take **no** ezafe – در (*dar*, ‘in’), به (*be*, ‘to’), از (*az*, ‘from’), با (*bā*, ‘with’): در اتاق (*dar otāgh*, ‘in the room’).

بین (*beyn-e*, ‘between’) takes two things joined by *o*: بین بانک و پارک (*beyn-e bānk o pārk*, ‘between the bank and the park’). With a pronoun you can use the endings from lesson 5: کنارم (*kenār-am*, ‘next to me’), زیرش (*zir-ash*, ‘under it’).

| Place word | Meaning | Example | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| در (*dar*) | in, at | در اتاق (*dar otāgh*) | in the room |
| روی (*ru-ye*) | on | روی میز (*ru-ye miz*) | on the table |
| زیر (*zir-e*) | under | زیر تخت (*zir-e takht*) | under the bed |
| کنار (*kenār-e*) | next to, beside | کنار پنجره (*kenār-e panjare*) | next to the window |
| پشت (*posht-e*) | behind | پشت در (*posht-e dar*) | behind the door |
| جلوی (*jelo-ye*) | in front of | جلوی خانه (*jelo-ye khāne*) | in front of the house |
| روبه‌روی (*ru-be-ru-ye*) | opposite | روبه‌روی بانک (*ru-be-ru-ye bānk*) | opposite the bank |
| بین (*beyn-e*) | between | بین میز و تخت (*beyn-e miz o takht*) | between the table and the bed |
| نزدیک (*nazdik-e*) | near | نزدیک مسجد (*nazdik-e masjed*) | near the mosque |

- کتاب روی میز است.  
  *ketāb ru-ye miz ast.* — The book is on the table.
- کیف من زیر صندلی است.  
  *kif-e man zir-e sandali ast.* — My bag is under the chair.  
  Spoken: کیفم زیر صندلیه. *kif-am zir-e sandali-ye.*
- بانک بین داروخانه و نانوایی است.  
  *bānk beyn-e dāru-khāne o nānvāyi ast.* — The bank is between the pharmacy and the bakery.
- مدرسه نزدیک پارک است.  
  *madrese nazdik-e pārk ast.* — The school is near the park.

### Where is it? – kojā-st

*Where is …?* is کجاست؟ (*kojā-st?*) – *kojā* + *ast*, written as one word: بانک کجاست؟ (*bānk kojā-st?*, ‘where is the bank?’) For more than one thing, use the full verb: کلیدها کجا هستند؟ (*kelid-hā kojā hastand?*, ‘where are the keys?’)

The answers follow the same pattern: اینجاست (*injā-st*, ‘it's here’), آنجاست (*ānjā-st*, ‘it's there’) (spoken *unjā-st*), or a place word + *ast*: کنار پارک است (*kenār-e pārk ast*, ‘it's next to the park’). You don't need a word for *it* – the verb says it all.

Start with ببخشید (*bebakhshid*) when you ask a stranger, and finish with ممنون (*mamnun*). In speech *ast* becomes *-e*: *kenār-e pārk-e*, *nazdik-e*.

| Persian | Reading | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| بانک کجاست؟ (*bānk kojā-st?*) | bānk kojā-st? | Where is the bank? |
| اینجاست. (*injā-st.*) | injā-st. | It's here. |
| آنجاست. (*ānjā-st.*) | ānjā-st. | It's there. |
| کلیدها کجا هستند؟ (*kelid-hā kojā hastand?*) | kelid-hā kojā hastand? | Where are the keys? |
| روی میز هستند. (*ru-ye miz hastand.*) | ru-ye miz hastand. | They're on the table. |

- ببخشید، داروخانه کجاست؟  
  *bebakhshid, dāru-khāne kojā-st?* — Excuse me, where is the pharmacy?
- مترو آنجاست، جلوی بیمارستان.  
  *metro ānjā-st, jelo-ye bimārestān.* — The metro is over there, in front of the hospital.  
  Spoken: مترو اونجاست، جلوی بیمارستان. *metro unjā-st, jelo-ye bimārestān.*
- بچه‌ها کجا هستند؟ در پارک هستند.  
  *bacche-hā kojā hastand? dar pārk hastand.* — Where are the children? They're in the park.
- خانه‌ی شما کجاست؟  
  *khāne-ye shomā kojā-st?* — Where is your home?

### There is, there are: hast and nist

To say that something **exists** somewhere – *there is, there are* – Persian uses هست (*hast*), the form of *to be* you met in lesson 2, and usually puts the place first: روی میز یک کتاب هست (*ru-ye miz yek ketāb hast*, ‘there is a book on the table’). Things in the plural still take *hast*: در اتاق دو صندلی هست (*dar otāgh do sandali hast*, ‘there are two chairs in the room’); people usually take *hastand*.

The negative is نیست (*nist*): اینجا بانک نیست (*injā bānk nist*, ‘there is no bank here’).

Compare the two: *ast* tells you **where** a known thing is; *hast* tells you **that** something is there.

| Persian | English | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| کتاب روی میز است. (*ketāb ru-ye miz ast.*) | The book is on the table. | where the book (that we know) is |
| روی میز یک کتاب هست. (*ru-ye miz yek ketāb hast.*) | There is a book on the table. | that a book is there |
| بانک کجاست؟ (*bānk kojā-st?*) | Where is the bank? | I know there is one |
| این نزدیکی بانک هست؟ (*in nazdiki bānk hast?*) | Is there a bank near here? | is there one at all? |
| نه، این نزدیکی بانک نیست. (*na, in nazdiki bānk nist.*) | No, there's no bank near here. | there isn't one |

- در خیابان ما دو نانوایی هست.  
  *dar khiyābān-e mā do nānvāyi hast.* — There are two bakeries on our street.
- این نزدیکی داروخانه هست؟  
  *in nazdiki dāru-khāne hast?* — Is there a pharmacy near here?
- در اتاق من یک تخت، یک میز و دو صندلی هست.  
  *dar otāgh-e man yek takht, yek miz o do sandali hast.* — In my room there is a bed, a table and two chairs.
- در کافه ده نفر هستند.  
  *dar kāfe dah nafar hastand.* — There are ten people in the café.

### Tom's neighbourhood

_Tom writes to his sister about his new neighbourhood. Read, then answer the questions._

- آپارتمان من در یک کوچه‌ی کوچک است.  
  *āpārtemān-e man dar yek kuche-ye kuchak ast.* — My flat is in a small lane.
- سر کوچه یک نانوایی و یک سوپرمارکت هست.  
  *sar-e kuche yek nānvāyi o yek supermārket hast.* — At the end of the lane there is a bakery and a supermarket.
- بانک در خیابان اصلی است، کنار داروخانه.  
  *bānk dar khiyābān-e asli ast, kenār-e dāru-khāne.* — The bank is on the main street, next to the pharmacy.
- روبه‌روی داروخانه یک پارک بزرگ هست.  
  *ru-be-ru-ye dāru-khāne yek pārk-e bozorg hast.* — Opposite the pharmacy there is a big park.
- بین پارک و مسجد یک مدرسه هست.  
  *beyn-e pārk o masjed yek madrese hast.* — Between the park and the mosque there is a school.
- ایستگاه مترو کمی دور است، اما بیمارستان خیلی نزدیک است.  
  *istgāh-e metro kami dur ast, ammā bimārestān kheyli nazdik ast.* — The metro station is a bit far, but the hospital is very close.
- محله‌ی من همه چیز دارد!  
  *mahalle-ye man hame chiz dārad!* — My neighbourhood has everything!

### Squares, streets and lanes: finding an address

Iranian addresses read like directions. They start big and get smaller: the city, a well-known square or main street, a smaller street, then the lane – کوچه (*kuche*) – and the house number, sometimes with the floor and the flat. A taxi driver needs the landmarks more than the number, so you will often hear an address given as *Valiasr Street, after the square, opposite the mosque, the second lane on the left*.

The great landmarks of Tehran are its squares, میدان (*meydān*): Azadi Square in the west with its famous white tower, Enghelab in the centre, Vanak and Tajrish in the north. People arrange to meet سر کوچه (*sar-e kuche*, ‘at the head of the lane’) or جلوی نانوایی (*jelo-ye nānvāyi*, ‘in front of the bakery’) – exactly the language of this lesson.

If you get lost, ask. Iranians give directions generously – often with a long explanation, and sometimes by walking you there themselves.

### Tips

- Almost every place word takes the ezafe: *ru-ye, zir-e, kenār-e, posht-e, jelo-ye, beyn-e, nazdik-e*. Forgetting it is the classic beginner's mistake – *dar* is the main exception.
- In speech *ru-ye* often shrinks to *ru*, and *dar* becomes *tu*: *ru miz-e* 'it's on the table', *tu otāgh-e* 'it's in the room'.
- **در** is *dar* 'in' and also *dar* 'door' – *posht-e dar* is 'behind the door', *dar otāgh* is 'in the room'.
- *nazdik* and *dur* also work on their own: *bānk nazdik ast* 'the bank is near'. 'Round here' is *in nazdiki*.
- *ast* says where a known thing is (*bānk kojā-st?*); *hast* says that something exists (*bānk hast?*). Choose the verb by what you want to know.
