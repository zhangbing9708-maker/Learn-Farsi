# Passive and impersonal: 'it was built', 'they say'
## ساخته شد، می‌گویند
*sākhte shod, mi-guyand* — Intermediate (B1), lesson 1. [Open this lesson with audio and interactive exercises](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/course/b1-01-passive-and-impersonal.html)

Persian can say that something was done without saying who did it – but it does so far less often than English. In this lesson you learn how the passive is built with شدن, when Iranians actually use it, and the three everyday ways of being impersonal: 'they say', 'one can', and 'a person'.

**In this lesson you will learn to:**

- form the passive of any verb with شدن in every tense
- understand why Persian prefers 'they built it' to 'it was built'
- use می‌گویند، می‌شود and آدم to speak impersonally
- read a short history text written in the formal passive style

### A language that would rather say who did it

English loves the passive: *the bridge was built, the letter was sent, mistakes were made.* Persian has a passive too, but in everyday speech Iranians usually prefer an **active sentence with a vague subject**: پل را ساختند (*pol rā sākhtand*, ‘they built the bridge’) rather than پل ساخته شد (*pol sākhte shod*, ‘the bridge was built’). Both are correct; the second simply sounds more formal and written.

So you will meet the passive constantly in **newspapers, history books, signs and official notices** – and much less in conversation. Learn to form it so you can read; learn the impersonal 'they' so you can speak.

The passive is built from two pieces you already know: the **past participle** (the past stem plus ـه (*-e*): ساخته (*sākhte*, ‘built’), دیده (*dide*, ‘seen’), گفته (*gofte*, ‘said’)) and the verb شدن (*shodan*, ‘to become’), which carries the tense. Literally, *the bridge became built*.

### Building the passive: past participle + شدن

Take the past stem, add ـه (*-e*) to get the past participle, then conjugate شدن (*shodan*) in whatever tense you need. The participle never changes; شدن does all the work. Here is ساختن (*sākhtan*, ‘to build’) in every tense you have met:

| Tense | Persian | Reading | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| simple past | ساخته شد (*sākhte shod*) | sākhte shod | it was built |
| present | ساخته می‌شود (*sākhte mi-shavad*) | sākhte mi-shavad | it is (being) built |
| past continuous | ساخته می‌شد (*sākhte mi-shod*) | sākhte mi-shod | it was being built, used to be built |
| perfect | ساخته شده است (*sākhte shode ast*) | sākhte shode ast | it has been built |
| past perfect | ساخته شده بود (*sākhte shode bud*) | sākhte shode bud | it had been built |
| future | ساخته خواهد شد (*sākhte khāhad shod*) | sākhte khāhad shod | it will be built |
| subjunctive | ساخته شود (*sākhte shavad*) | sākhte shavad | (that) it be built |
| negative | ساخته نشد (*sākhte na-shod*) | sākhte na-shod | it was not built |

- این نامه دیروز فرستاده شد.  
  *in nāme diruz ferestāde shod.* — This letter was sent yesterday.
- در ایران هر سال میلیون‌ها تن گندم تولید می‌شود.  
  *dar Irān har sāl milyun-hā ton gandom towlid mi-shavad.* — Millions of tonnes of wheat are produced in Iran every year.
- کتاب‌های او به بیست زبان ترجمه شده‌اند.  
  *ketāb-hā-ye u be bist zabān tarjome shode-and.* — His books have been translated into twenty languages.
- وقتی رسیدیم، در بسته شده بود.  
  *vaghti residim, dar baste shode bud.* — When we arrived, the door had been closed.
- نتیجه‌ی امتحان فردا اعلام خواهد شد.  
  *natije-ye emtehān fardā e'lām khāhad shod.* — The exam result will be announced tomorrow.
- باید این مشکل زودتر حل شود.  
  *bāyad in moshkel zud-tar hal shavad.* — This problem must be solved sooner.
- این خانه هنوز فروخته نشده است.  
  *in khāne hanuz forukhte na-shode ast.* — This house has not been sold yet.  
  Spoken: این خونه هنوز فروخته نشده. *in khune hanuz forukhte na-shode.*

### Compound verbs: just swap کردن for شدن

Most Persian verbs are compounds – a noun or adjective plus کردن (*kardan*, ‘to do’). Their passive is wonderfully simple: **replace کردن with شدن**. No participle is needed, because the noun part stays as it is.

The same trick turns an action into a change of state: باز کردن (*bāz kardan*, ‘to open (something)’) → باز شدن (*bāz shodan*, ‘to open, to be opened’); خراب کردن (*kharāb kardan*, ‘to break (something)’) → خراب شدن (*kharāb shodan*, ‘to break down’).

By whom? Persian rarely says it. If you must, use به دست (*be dast-e*, ‘by the hand of’) or توسط (*tavassot-e*, ‘by’) (formal): این پل به دست شاه عباس ساخته شد (*in pol be dast-e Shāh Abbās sākhte shod*). In speech people simply switch to the active.

| Active (کردن) | Passive (شدن) | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| تمام کردن (*tamām kardan*) | تمام شدن (*tamām shodan*) | to finish → to be finished, run out |
| شروع کردن (*shoru' kardan*) | شروع شدن (*shoru' shodan*) | to start → to begin |
| پیدا کردن (*peydā kardan*) | پیدا شدن (*peydā shodan*) | to find → to be found |
| تعمیر کردن (*ta'mir kardan*) | تعمیر شدن (*ta'mir shodan*) | to repair → to be repaired |
| آماده کردن (*āmāde kardan*) | آماده شدن (*āmāde shodan*) | to prepare → to get ready |
| گم کردن (*gom kardan*) | گم شدن (*gom shodan*) | to lose → to get lost |
| برگزار کردن (*bargozār kardan*) | برگزار شدن (*bargozār shodan*) | to hold (an event) → to be held |

- کلاس ساعت نه شروع می‌شود.  
  *kelās sā'at-e noh shoru' mi-shavad.* — The class starts at nine.
- کلیدم پیدا شد!  
  *kelid-am peydā shod!* — My key has been found! (My key turned up!)
- ماشین هنوز تعمیر نشده است.  
  *māshin hanuz ta'mir na-shode ast.* — The car has not been repaired yet.  
  Spoken: ماشین هنوز تعمیر نشده. *māshin hanuz ta'mir na-shode.*
- جشنواره هر سال در شهریور برگزار می‌شود.  
  *jashnvāre har sāl dar Shahrivar bargozār mi-shavad.* — The festival is held every year in Shahrivar.
- در بازار اصفهان گم شدیم.  
  *dar bāzār-e Esfahān gom shodim.* — We got lost in the Isfahan bazaar.
- شام آماده شد؟ بله، همه چیز آماده است.  
  *shām āmāde shod? bale, hame chiz āmāde ast.* — Is dinner ready? Yes, everything is ready.

### Three ways to be impersonal

**1. They say… – the third person plural.** When the doer is 'people in general', Persian uses the verb in the *they* form with no subject at all: می‌گویند که… (*mi-guyand ke…*, ‘they say that…’), پل را ساختند (*pol rā sākhtand*, ‘they built the bridge’). This is the conversational substitute for the passive.

**2. One can… – می‌شود / می‌توان.** می‌شود (*mi-shavad*) (spoken *mishe*) plus a verb means *it is possible to, one can*: این‌جا می‌شود پارک کرد؟ (*in-jā mi-shavad pārk kard?*, ‘can one park here?’) – note the short infinitive (past stem) after it in formal style. In writing you also see می‌توان (*mi-tavān*): می‌توان گفت که… (*mi-tavān goft ke…*, ‘one can say that…’)

**3. A person… – آدم / یکی / کسی.** آدم (*ādam*) means *a human being* and works like English *you* in *you never know*: آدم نمی‌داند چه بگوید (*ādam nemi-dānad che beguyad*, ‘one doesn't know what to say’).

- می‌گویند این غذا را اول در شیراز پختند.  
  *mi-guyand in ghazā rā avval dar Shirāz pokhtand.* — They say this dish was first cooked in Shiraz.  
  Spoken: می‌گن این غذا رو اول شیراز پختن. *mi-gan in ghazā ro avval Shirāz pokhtan.*
- در این خیابان نمی‌شود پارک کرد.  
  *dar in khiyābān nemi-shavad pārk kard.* — One can't park on this street.  
  Spoken: این خیابون نمی‌شه پارک کرد. *in khiyābun nemi-she pārk kard.*
- می‌توان گفت که زبان فارسی تغییر کمی کرده است.  
  *mi-tavān goft ke zabān-e fārsi taghyir-e kam-i karde ast.* — One can say that the Persian language has changed little.
- آدم وقتی خسته است، زود عصبانی می‌شود.  
  *ādam vaghti khaste ast, zud asabāni mi-shavad.* — When one is tired, one gets angry quickly.
- یکی زنگ زد و اسم شما را پرسید.  
  *yek-i zang zad o esm-e shomā rā porsid.* — Someone called and asked your name.
- این‌جا را تمیز کرده‌اند.  
  *in-jā rā tamiz karde-and.* — This place has been cleaned. (lit. they have cleaned here)

### At the garage

_Reza's car broke down. He talks to the mechanic, Mr Karimi. Listen to how often شدن appears in normal talk about things that happen to things._

- **Reza:** سلام آقای کریمی. ماشینم دیروز وسط خیابان خراب شد.  
  *salām āghā-ye Karimi. māshin-am diruz vasat-e khiyābān kharāb shod.* — Hello Mr Karimi. My car broke down in the middle of the street yesterday.  
  Spoken: سلام آقای کریمی. ماشینم دیروز وسط خیابون خراب شد. *salām āghā-ye Karimi. māshin-am diruz vasat-e khiyābun kharāb shod.*
- **Mr Karimi:** چه شد؟ یک‌دفعه خاموش شد؟  
  *che shod? yek-daf'e khāmush shod?* — What happened? Did it suddenly switch off?  
  Spoken: چی شد؟ یه‌دفعه خاموش شد؟ *chi shod? ye-daf'e khāmush shod?*
- **Reza:** بله. و دیگر روشن نشد.  
  *bale. o digar roshan na-shod.* — Yes. And it wouldn't start again.  
  Spoken: آره. دیگه روشن نشد. *āre. dige roshan na-shod.*
- **Mr Karimi:** باتری چک شده است؟  
  *bātri chek shode ast?* — Has the battery been checked?  
  Spoken: باتری چک شده؟ *bātri chek shode?*
- **Reza:** بله، پارسال تعویض شد.  
  *bale, pārsāl ta'viz shod.* — Yes, it was replaced last year.
- **Mr Karimi:** خب، باید ماشین به تعمیرگاه آورده شود. فردا صبح آماده می‌شود.  
  *khob, bāyad māshin be ta'mirgāh āvarde shavad. fardā sobh āmāde mi-shavad.* — Well, the car must be brought to the workshop. It will be ready tomorrow morning.  
  Spoken: خب، ماشین باید بیاد تعمیرگاه. فردا صبح آماده می‌شه. *khob, māshin bāyad biyād ta'mirgāh. fardā sobh āmāde mi-she.*
- **Reza:** هزینه‌اش چقدر می‌شود؟  
  *hazine-ash cheghadr mi-shavad?* — How much will it cost?  
  Spoken: هزینه‌ش چقدر می‌شه؟ *hazine-sh cheghadr mi-she?*
- **Mr Karimi:** هنوز نمی‌شود گفت. اول باید دیده شود.  
  *hanuz nemi-shavad goft. avval bāyad dide shavad.* — One can't say yet. First it has to be looked at.  
  Spoken: هنوز نمی‌شه گفت. اول باید دیده شه. *hanuz nemi-she goft. avval bāyad dide she.*
- **Reza:** می‌گویند شما بهترین مکانیک محله هستید.  
  *mi-guyand shomā behtarin mekānik-e mahalle hastid.* — They say you are the best mechanic in the neighbourhood.  
  Spoken: می‌گن شما بهترین مکانیک محله‌این. *mi-gan shomā behtarin mekānik-e mahalle-in.*
- **Mr Karimi:** لطف دارید. نگران نباشید، درست می‌شود.  
  *lotf dārid. negarān na-bāshid, dorost mi-shavad.* — You're kind. Don't worry, it will be fixed.  
  Spoken: لطف دارین. نگران نباشین، درست می‌شه. *lotf dārin. negarān na-bāshin, dorost mi-she.*

### Reading the signs

Once you know the passive, Iranian streets become readable. Shop windows and official notices use it everywhere, usually in the present tense with می‌شود:

- این‌جا کلید ساخته می‌شود (*in-jā kelid sākhte mi-shavad*, ‘keys cut here’) (literally *here key is made*)
- به قیمت مناسب فروخته می‌شود (*be gheymat-e monāseb forukhte mi-shavad*, ‘sold at a reasonable price’)
- خواهشمند است از کشیدن سیگار خودداری شود (*khāheshmand ast az keshidan-e sigār khoddāri shavad*, ‘please refrain from smoking’) – the most formal register of all, with a subjunctive passive.
- ورود ممنوع (*vorud mamnu'*, ‘no entry’) – no verb at all; Arabic participles like ممنوع (*forbidden*) do the passive's job.

In Isfahan, Si-o-se-pol is where the city goes for its evening walk. Under the arches, people sing: the vaulted brick makes a natural echo chamber, and on summer nights you can hear old songs and poems of Hafez sung by strangers – a tradition that is, as the reading says, گفته می‌شود, 'said to be' as old as the bridge.

### Tips

- If you are speaking, not writing, try the *they* form first: کتاب را چاپ کردند sounds more natural than کتاب چاپ شد.
- Not every شدن is a passive. In خسته شدم ('I got tired') or بزرگ شد ('he grew up'), شدن just means 'to become'. Ask yourself: could I add 'by someone'? If not, it is a change of state, not a passive.
- Intransitive verbs have no passive: you cannot say *was gone* in Persian any more than in English. Verbs like آمدن، رفتن، خوابیدن never take شدن.
- After می‌شود / نمی‌شود in the sense 'one can / one can't', the formal written style uses the short infinitive (the past stem): نمی‌شود گفت. In speech you will also hear the subjunctive: نمی‌شه بگیم.
- Spoken Persian shortens شد to شد but شده است to just شده: فروخته شده 'it's been sold'.

### Passive or just 'become'?

**Choose the right answer**

1. در باز شد. (*dar bāz shod.*) – Is this a passive (someone opened it) or a change of state? — Only passive / Only 'became open' / It can be either – Persian does not distinguish
2. Which sentence is the natural **spoken** way to say 'The house was sold'? — خانه به فروش رسانده شد. / خانه را فروختند. / خانه توسط آن‌ها فروخته گشت.
3. Which verb cannot be made passive? — خواندن / خوابیدن / نوشتن
4. نمی‌شود این‌جا نشست. (*nemi-shavad in-jā neshast.*) means… — Nobody sat here. / One can't sit here. / It wasn't sitting here.
5. How do you say 'by Shah Abbas' in a formal passive sentence? — با شاه عباس / به دست شاه عباس / برای شاه عباس

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. It can be either – Persian does not distinguish — باز شدن covers both 'was opened (by someone)' and 'opened (by itself)'. Context decides.
2. خانه را فروختند. — The 'they' form is what people say; the others are written or archaic (گشت).
3. خوابیدن — خوابیدن 'to sleep' has no object, so there is nothing to make the subject of a passive.
4. One can't sit here. — نمی‌شود + short infinitive = 'it is not possible to'.
5. به دست شاه عباس — به دست (or توسط) marks the agent; با means 'together with / using'.

</details>

### Say it in Persian

**Say it in Persian**

1. This bridge was built four hundred years ago.
2. They say the weather will be cold tomorrow.
3. The shop is opened at eight every morning.
4. One can't say anything yet.
5. The letter has not been written yet.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. این پل چهارصد سال پیش ساخته شد. — *in pol chahārsad sāl pish sākhte shod.*
2. می‌گویند هوا فردا سرد می‌شود. — *mi-guyand havā fardā sard mi-shavad.*
3. مغازه هر روز صبح ساعت هشت باز می‌شود. — *maghāze har ruz sobh sā'at-e hasht bāz mi-shavad.*
4. هنوز نمی‌شود چیزی گفت. — *hanuz nemi-shavad chiz-i goft.*
5. نامه هنوز نوشته نشده است. — *nāme hanuz neveshte na-shode ast.*

</details>

### Put the words in order

**Put the words in order**

1. این / ساخته / شد. / پل / در / اصفهان — This bridge was built in Isfahan.
2. چند / او / کتاب‌های / شده‌اند. / به / زبان / ترجمه — His books have been translated into several languages.
3. او / می‌گویند / مکانیک / بهترین / شهر / است. — They say he is the best mechanic in town.
4. این / نمی‌شود / کرد. / خیابان / در / پارک — One can't park on this street.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. این پل در اصفهان ساخته شد.
2. کتاب‌های او به چند زبان ترجمه شده‌اند.
3. می‌گویند او بهترین مکانیک شهر است.
4. در این خیابان نمی‌شود پارک کرد.

</details>

### Match active and passive

**Give the meaning**

1. تمام شدن — ?
2. گم شدن — ?
3. برگزار شدن — ?
4. تعمیر شدن — ?
5. ثبت شدن — ?
6. شنیده شدن — ?
7. فروخته شدن — ?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. تمام شدن = to be finished, run out
2. گم شدن = to get lost
3. برگزار شدن = to be held (event)
4. تعمیر شدن = to be repaired
5. ثبت شدن = to be registered
6. شنیده شدن = to be heard
7. فروخته شدن = to be sold

</details>
