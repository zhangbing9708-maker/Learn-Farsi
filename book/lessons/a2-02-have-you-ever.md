# Have you ever…? The present perfect
## تا حالا به شیراز رفته‌ای؟
*tā hālā be Shirāz rafte-i?* — Elementary (A2), lesson 2. [Open this lesson with audio and interactive exercises](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/course/a2-02-have-you-ever.html)

Have you ever been to Shiraz? Have you tried fesenjan? Not yet? This lesson is about experiences – the things you have and haven't done so far. Persian builds its present perfect from two pieces you already have: the past stem from A1 plus -e, and the short endings of 'to be'. You will also learn ever, never, not yet and just, and how spoken Persian squeezes رفته‌ام into something that sounds almost like رفتم.

**In this lesson you will learn to:**

- talk about experiences with the present perfect: رفته‌ام، دیده‌ام
- ask 'have you ever…?' with تا حالا
- say 'never', 'not yet' and 'just' with هیچ‌وقت، هنوز، تازه
- say how often: یک بار، دو بار، چند بار
- choose between the simple past (پارسال رفتم) and the perfect (رفته‌ام)

### Have you ever been to Shiraz?

_Mahsa and Tom are having tea after class. Mahsa loves Shiraz; Tom has been in Iran for six months._

- **Mahsa:** تام، تا حالا به شیراز رفته‌ای؟  
  *Tām, tā hālā be Shirāz rafte-i?* — Tom, have you ever been to Shiraz?  
  Spoken: تام، تا حالا شیراز رفتی؟ *Tām, tā hālā Shirāz rafti?*
- **Tom:** نه، هنوز نرفته‌ام. اما اصفهان و یزد را دیده‌ام.  
  *na, hanuz na-rafte-am. ammā Esfahān o Yazd rā dide-am.* — No, not yet. But I've seen Isfahan and Yazd.  
  Spoken: نه، هنوز نرفتم. ولی اصفهان و یزد رو دیدم. *na, hanuz na-raftam. vali Esfahān o Yazd ro didam.*
- **Mahsa:** یزد را دوست داشتی؟  
  *Yazd rā dust dāshti?* — Did you like Yazd?  
  Spoken: یزد رو دوست داشتی؟ *Yazd ro dust dāshti?*
- **Tom:** خیلی! من هیچ‌وقت شهری مثل یزد ندیده‌ام.  
  *kheyli! man hich-vaght shahr-i mesl-e Yazd na-dide-am.* — Very much! I've never seen a city like Yazd.  
  Spoken: خیلی! من هیچ‌وقت شهری مثل یزد ندیدم. *kheyli! man hich-vaght shahr-i mesl-e Yazd na-didam.*
- **Mahsa:** شیراز هم خیلی قشنگ است. من سه بار آن‌جا رفته‌ام.  
  *Shirāz ham kheyli ghashang ast. man se bār ān-jā rafte-am.* — Shiraz is very beautiful too. I've been there three times.  
  Spoken: شیراز هم خیلی قشنگه. من سه بار اون‌جا رفتم. *Shirāz ham kheyli ghashange. man se bār un-jā raftam.*
- **Tom:** آرامگاه حافظ را دیده‌ای؟  
  *ārāmgāh-e Hāfez rā dide-i?* — Have you seen the tomb of Hafez?  
  Spoken: آرامگاه حافظ رو دیدی؟ *ārāmgāh-e Hāfez ro didi?*
- **Mahsa:** البته! هر بار به آن‌جا رفته‌ام و فال حافظ گرفته‌ام.  
  *albatte! har bār be ān-jā rafte-am o fāl-e Hāfez gerefte-am.* — Of course! Every time I've gone there and had a Hafez fortune reading.  
  Spoken: البته! هر دفعه رفتم اون‌جا و فال حافظ گرفتم. *albatte! har daf'e raftam un-jā o fāl-e Hāfez gereftam.*
- **Tom:** فال حافظ؟ آن چیست؟  
  *fāl-e Hāfez? ān chist?* — A Hafez fortune? What's that?  
  Spoken: فال حافظ؟ اون چیه؟ *fāl-e Hāfez? un chiye?*
- **Mahsa:** نیت می‌کنی، دیوان حافظ را باز می‌کنی و شعر جواب تو را می‌دهد. هنوز امتحان نکرده‌ای؟  
  *niyyat mi-koni, divān-e Hāfez rā bāz mi-koni o she'r javāb-e to rā mi-dahad. hanuz emtehān na-karde-i?* — You make a wish, open the Divan of Hafez, and the poem gives you your answer. You still haven't tried it?  
  Spoken: نیت می‌کنی، دیوان حافظ رو باز می‌کنی و شعر جوابتو می‌ده. هنوز امتحان نکردی؟ *niyyat mi-koni, divān-e Hāfez ro bāz mi-koni o she'r javāb-eto mi-de. hanuz emtehān na-kardi?*
- **Tom:** نه، هیچ‌وقت. باید امتحان کنم!  
  *na, hich-vaght. bāyad emtehān konam!* — No, never. I must try it!
- **Mahsa:** پس ماه بعد با هم به شیراز برویم!  
  *pas māh-e ba'd bā ham be Shirāz beravim!* — Then let's go to Shiraz together next month!  
  Spoken: پس ماه بعد با هم بریم شیراز! *pas māh-e ba'd bā ham berim Shirāz!*
- **Tom:** عالی است! تا حالا با اتوبوس شبانه سفر نکرده‌ام.  
  *āli ast! tā hālā bā otobus-e shabāne safar na-karde-am.* — Great! I've never travelled on a night bus.  
  Spoken: عالیه! تا حالا با اتوبوس شبونه سفر نکردم. *āliye! tā hālā bā otobus-e shabune safar na-kardam.*

### Two pieces you already know

English *have you ever been* is Persian *until now have you gone*: تا حالا رفته‌ای؟ (*tā hālā rafte-i?*) The new tense, the **present perfect** (ماضی نقلی (*māzi-ye naghli*)), is built from two things you learned at A1.

1. The **past participle**: the past stem plus **-e** (written ه). رفت (*raft*) → رفته (*rafte*, ‘gone’), دید (*did*) → دیده (*dide*, ‘seen’), خورد (*khord*) → خورده (*khorde*, ‘eaten’). It is the Persian *gone, seen, eaten*.
2. The short endings of *to be*: **-am, -i, -im, -id, -and**, joined to the participle with a ZWNJ: رفته‌ام (*rafte-am*, ‘I have gone’). Only *he / she* takes the full word است (*ast*), written separately: رفته است (*rafte ast*).

You use it, as in English, for experiences (*I've been to Yazd*), for things not yet done (*I haven't tried it*) and for a past action whose result matters now (*I've lost my key*).

### The present perfect: رفته‌ام، دیده‌ام

Participle + short ending, with a ZWNJ between them. For the negative, put **na-** on the participle: نرفته‌ام (*na-rafte-am*, ‘I haven't gone’). In compound verbs, na- goes on the verb part: سفر نکرده‌ام (*safar na-karde-am*, ‘I haven't travelled’).

The he / she form is written رفته است (*rafte ast*), but in speech and in much informal writing the است (*ast*) disappears: رفته (*rafte*, ‘he has gone’).

One use English doesn't share: Persian often uses the perfect for facts about the past that are still 'on record' – in history books, the news and reported information: حافظ در شیراز به دنیا آمده است. (*Hāfez dar Shirāz be donyā āmade ast.*, ‘Hafez was born in Shiraz.’)

| Person | go: rafte | see: dide | negative | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | رفته‌ام (*rafte-am*) | دیده‌ام (*dide-am*) | نرفته‌ام (*na-rafte-am*) | I have gone / seen; I haven't gone |
| you | رفته‌ای (*rafte-i*) | دیده‌ای (*dide-i*) | نرفته‌ای (*na-rafte-i*) | you have gone / seen |
| he / she | رفته است (*rafte ast*) | دیده است (*dide ast*) | نرفته است (*na-rafte ast*) | he / she has gone / seen |
| we | رفته‌ایم (*rafte-im*) | دیده‌ایم (*dide-im*) | نرفته‌ایم (*na-rafte-im*) | we have gone / seen |
| you (polite, pl.) | رفته‌اید (*rafte-id*) | دیده‌اید (*dide-id*) | نرفته‌اید (*na-rafte-id*) | you have gone / seen |
| they | رفته‌اند (*rafte-and*) | دیده‌اند (*dide-and*) | نرفته‌اند (*na-rafte-and*) | they have gone / seen |

- من دو بار به مشهد رفته‌ام.  
  *man do bār be Mashhad rafte-am.* — I've been to Mashhad twice.  
  Spoken: من دو بار رفتم مشهد. *man do bār raftam Mashhad.*
- سارا این فیلم را سه بار دیده است.  
  *Sārā in film rā se bār dide ast.* — Sara has seen this film three times.  
  Spoken: سارا این فیلم رو سه بار دیده. *Sārā in film ro se bār dide.*
- ما قبلاً همدیگر را دیده‌ایم؟  
  *mā ghablan hamdigar rā dide-im?* — Have we met before?  
  Spoken: ما قبلاً همدیگه رو دیدیم؟ *mā ghablan hamdige ro didim?*
- حافظ در شیراز به دنیا آمده است.  
  *Hāfez dar Shirāz be donyā āmade ast.* — Hafez was born in Shiraz.  
  _a fact 'on record': the perfect, where English uses the simple past_
- آن‌ها هنوز شام نخورده‌اند.  
  *ān-hā hanuz shām na-khorde-and.* — They haven't had dinner yet.  
  Spoken: اونا هنوز شام نخوردن. *unā hanuz shām na-khordan.*

### Ever, never, not yet, just: تا حالا، هیچ‌وقت، هنوز، تازه

- تا حالا (*tā hālā*, ‘until now’) in a question means **ever**; in a statement, **so far**: تا حالا سه کتاب فارسی خوانده‌ام. (*tā hālā se ketāb-e fārsi khānde-am.*, ‘So far I've read three Persian books.’) A more formal version is تا به حال (*tā be hāl*).
- هیچ‌وقت (*hich-vaght*, ‘never’) always goes with a **negative** verb – Persian doubles the negative, and that is correct: هیچ‌وقت نرفته‌ام (*hich-vaght na-rafte-am*, ‘I've never been’). The same goes for هیچ‌کس (*hich-kas*, ‘nobody’) and هیچ‌جا (*hich-jā*, ‘nowhere’).
- هنوز (*hanuz*) with a positive verb means **still**; with a negative verb, **not yet**. On its own, هنوز نه (*hanuz na*) is *not yet*.
- تازه (*tāze*) with the perfect means **just**: تازه رسیده‌ام (*tāze reside-am*, ‘I've just arrived’).
- To count: یک بار (*yek bār*, ‘once’), دو بار (*do bār*, ‘twice’), چند بار؟ (*chand bār?*, ‘how many times?’)

|  | Persian | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the question | تا حالا کباب خورده‌ای؟ (*tā hālā kabāb khorde-i?*) | Have you ever eaten kebab? |
| yes, often | بله، خیلی خورده‌ام. (*bale, kheyli khorde-am.*) | Yes, lots of times. |
| yes, once | بله، یک بار خورده‌ام. (*bale, yek bār khorde-am.*) | Yes, once. |
| not yet | نه، هنوز نخورده‌ام. (*na, hanuz na-khorde-am.*) | No, not yet. |
| never | نه، هیچ‌وقت نخورده‌ام. (*na, hich-vaght na-khorde-am.*) | No, never. |
| just | تازه خورده‌ام! (*tāze khorde-am!*) | I've just eaten! |
| how often? | چند بار به اصفهان رفته‌ای؟ (*chand bār be Esfahān rafte-i?*) | How many times have you been to Isfahan? |

- تا حالا دوغ خورده‌اید؟  
  *tā hālā dugh khorde-id?* — Have you ever drunk doogh?  
  Spoken: تا حالا دوغ خوردین؟ *tā hālā dugh khordin?*
- من هیچ‌وقت برف ندیده‌ام.  
  *man hich-vaght barf na-dide-am.* — I've never seen snow.  
  Spoken: من هیچ‌وقت برف ندیدم. *man hich-vaght barf na-didam.*
- هنوز فارسی را خوب یاد نگرفته‌ام.  
  *hanuz fārsi rā khub yād na-gerefte-am.* — I haven't learned Persian properly yet.  
  Spoken: هنوز فارسی رو خوب یاد نگرفتم. *hanuz fārsi ro khub yād na-gereftam.*
- رضا هنوز خواب است.  
  *Rezā hanuz khāb ast.* — Reza is still asleep.  
  _hanuz + positive verb = still_
- تازه از سفر برگشته‌ایم.  
  *tāze az safar bargashte-im.* — We've just come back from a trip.  
  Spoken: تازه از سفر برگشتیم. *tāze az safar bargashtim.*

### Spoken Persian, and perfect or simple past?

In speech the **-e** of the participle melts into the ending: رفته‌ام (*rafte-am*) becomes *raftam*, دیده‌ای (*dide-i*) becomes *didi*. That looks exactly like the simple past – informal writing even spells it the same – but the **stress** is different. In the simple past the stress is on the stem; in the spoken perfect it is on the very end:

- *RAF-tam* – I went
- *raf-TAM* – I have gone (I've been)

Which tense? Exactly as in English: if you name a **finished time** – دیروز (*diruz*), پارسال (*pārsāl*), سه سال پیش (*se sāl pish*) – use the simple past. For experience with no time, or for *ever, never, yet, just*, use the perfect. *Yesterday I have gone* is as wrong in Persian as in English.

| Written | Spoken | stress | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| رفته‌ام (*rafte-am*) | رفتم (*raftam*) | raf-TAM | I have gone |
| رفته‌ای (*rafte-i*) | رفتی (*rafti*) | raf-TI | you have gone |
| رفته است (*rafte ast*) | رفته (*rafte*) | raf-TE | he / she has gone |
| رفته‌ایم (*rafte-im*) | رفتیم (*raftim*) | raf-TIM | we have gone |
| رفته‌اید (*rafte-id*) | رفتین (*raftin*) | raf-TIN | you have gone |
| رفته‌اند (*rafte-and*) | رفتن (*raftan*) | raf-TAN | they have gone |
| compare: رفتم (*raftam*) | رفتم (*raftam*) | RAF-tam | I went (simple past) |

- پارسال به شیراز رفتم.  
  *pārsāl be Shirāz raftam.* — Last year I went to Shiraz.  
  _a finished time: simple past_
- به شیراز رفته‌ام، اما به کرمان نرفته‌ام.  
  *be Shirāz rafte-am, ammā be Kermān na-rafte-am.* — I've been to Shiraz, but I haven't been to Kerman.  
  Spoken: شیراز رفتم، ولی کرمان نرفتم. *Shirāz raftam, vali Kermān na-raftam.*
- کلیدم را گم کرده‌ام!  
  *kelid-am rā gom karde-am!* — I've lost my key!  
  Spoken: کلیدم رو گم کردم! *kelid-am ro gom kardam!*  
  _the result matters now_
- دیروز کجا رفتی؟  
  *diruz kojā rafti?* — Where did you go yesterday?  
  _with diruz, never the perfect_

### Ms Ahmadi has seen it all

_Ms Ahmadi teaches geography – and she has been everywhere in Iran. Almost every verb here is in the present perfect._

- خانم احمدی معلم جغرافی است و خیلی سفر کرده است.  
  *khānom-e Ahmadi mo'allem-e joghrāfi ast o kheyli safar karde ast.* — Ms Ahmadi is a geography teacher and has travelled a lot.
- او به همه‌ی استان‌های ایران رفته است.  
  *u be hame-ye ostān-hā-ye Irān rafte ast.* — She has been to every province of Iran.
- در کویر لوت زیر آسمان پر از ستاره خوابیده است.  
  *dar kavir-e Lut zir-e āsmān-e por az setāre khābide ast.* — In the Lut desert she has slept under a sky full of stars.
- دو بار به قله‌ی دماوند رفته است.  
  *do bār be gholle-ye Damāvand rafte ast.* — She has climbed to the top of Damavand twice.
- در جزیره‌ی هرمز ساحل‌های قرمز را دیده است.  
  *dar jazire-ye Hormoz sāhel-hā-ye ghermez rā dide ast.* — On Hormuz Island she has seen the red beaches.
- اما او هنوز به خارج سفر نکرده است.  
  *ammā u hanuz be khārej safar na-karde ast.* — But she hasn't travelled abroad yet.
- تا حالا هیچ‌وقت سوار هواپیما نشده است!  
  *tā hālā hich-vaght savār-e havāpeymā na-shode ast!* — She has never been on a plane!
- او می‌گوید: «چرا به خارج بروم؟ ایران خودش یک دنیاست!»  
  *u mi-guyad: «cherā be khārej beravam? Irān khod-ash yek donyā-st!»* — She says: "Why go abroad? Iran is a world of its own!"
- شاگردانش می‌پرسند: «خانم، تا حالا کجا را بیشتر از همه دوست داشته‌اید؟»  
  *shāgerdān-ash mi-porsand: «khānom, tā hālā kojā rā bishtar az hame dust dāshte-id?»* — Her pupils ask: "Miss, which place have you liked best so far?"
- او همیشه جواب می‌دهد: «جایی که هنوز ندیده‌ام!»  
  *u hamishe javāb mi-dahad: «jā-yi ke hanuz na-dide-am!»* — She always answers: "The place I haven't seen yet!"

### Asking Hafez

Ask an Iranian تا حالا فال حافظ گرفته‌ای؟ (*tā hālā fāl-e Hāfez gerefte-i?*, ‘have you ever had a Hafez reading?’) and the answer is almost always yes. Hafez, the 14th-century poet of Shiraz, is called لسان‌الغیب (*Lesān-ol-Gheyb*, ‘the Tongue of the Unseen’), and his دیوان (*divān*) – his collected ghazals – is in nearly every Iranian home, often next to the Quran.

The ritual is simple. You make a silent wish or ask a question in your heart, the نیت (*niyyat*), open the Divan at random and read the ghazal on the page: the poem is Hafez's answer. Families do it together on شب یلدا (*shab-e Yaldā*), the longest night of the year, and at Nowruz. At the poet's tomb in Shiraz, the حافظیه (*Hāfeziye*), men with small birds in cages earn a living from it: the bird picks a card with a verse for you.

Whether or not you believe in it, a *fāl* is a wonderful way into Persian poetry – and a good question to ask Iranian friends: *tā hālā fāl-e Hāfez gerefte-id? chi umad?* – what came up?

### Watch out

- The participle ends in a silent ه, so the endings are joined with a ZWNJ: رفته‌ام، دیده‌ای، خورده‌اند. Only the he / she form is two words: رفته است.
- Double negatives are correct and necessary: *hich-vaght na-rafte-am* is literally 'never I haven't gone'. Without na- it is wrong.
- In speech the perfect sounds like the simple past with the stress on the end: *raf-TAM* (I have gone) vs *RAF-tam* (I went). Listen for the stress.
- History books and the news use the perfect for past facts: *in masjed dar gharn-e shānzdahom sākhte shode ast* – this mosque was built in the 16th century.
- *hanuz na* on its own is 'not yet': *shām khordi? – hanuz na.* (Have you eaten? – Not yet.)

### Match the forms

**Give the meaning**

1. رفته‌ام — ?
2. دیده‌ای — ?
3. خورده است — ?
4. نرفته‌ایم — ?
5. سفر کرده‌اند — ?
6. یاد گرفته‌اید — ?
7. هنوز نه — ?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. رفته‌ام = I have gone
2. دیده‌ای = you have seen
3. خورده است = he has eaten
4. نرفته‌ایم = we haven't gone
5. سفر کرده‌اند = they have travelled
6. یاد گرفته‌اید = you (pl.) have learned
7. هنوز نه = not yet

</details>

### Choose the right form

**Choose the right answer**

1. 'Have you ever been to Yazd?' — تا حالا به یزد رفته‌ای؟ / دیروز به یزد رفتی؟ / به یزد می‌روی؟
2. 'I've never seen snow.' — همیشه برف دیده‌ام. / هیچ‌وقت برف ندیده‌ام. / هیچ‌وقت برف دیده‌ام.
3. Which verb goes with دیروز (yesterday)? — رفته‌ام / رفتم / نرفته‌ام
4. هنوز نیامده است means… — He has still come. / He hasn't come yet. / He never comes.
5. In spoken Persian, رفته‌اید becomes… — رفتین / می‌رفتین / رفته‌این

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. تا حالا به یزد رفته‌ای؟ — tā hālā (ever) + the present perfect rafte-i.
2. هیچ‌وقت برف ندیده‌ام. — hich-vaght needs a negative verb: na-dide-am.
3. رفتم — A finished time takes the simple past: diruz raftam.
4. He hasn't come yet. — hanuz + a negative verb = not yet.
5. رفتین — rafte-id → raftin, with the stress at the end: raf-TIN.

</details>
