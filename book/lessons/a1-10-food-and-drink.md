# Food and drink: ordering and liking
## خوراک و نوشیدنی
*khorāk o nushidani* — Beginner (A1), lesson 10. [Open this lesson with audio and interactive exercises](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/course/a1-10-food-and-drink.html)

Persian food is one of the great pleasures of learning the language – and one of the easiest things to talk about. In this lesson you order in a restaurant and a café, ask for the bill, and say what you like and dislike with the verb dust dāshtan. Along the way you meet the dishes you will see on every menu in Iran.

**In this lesson you will learn to:**

- order food and drink politely with lotfan and … bedahid
- say what you like and don't like with dust dāshtan
- say what you want with mi-khāham + noun
- ask for the price and the bill
- name the main Iranian dishes and drinks

### At a kababi in Tehran

_Sara and Tom have lunch at a kababi (a kebab restaurant). The waiter takes their order._

- **Waiter:** سلام، خوش آمدید. بفرمایید.  
  *salām, khosh āmadid. befarmāyid.* — Hello, welcome. Please (what would you like?).
- **Sara:** سلام. یک چلوکباب کوبیده لطفاً.  
  *salām. yek chelow-kabāb-e kubide lotfan.* — Hello. One chelow kabab koobideh, please.
- **Waiter:** با نوشیدنی؟  
  *bā nushidani?* — With a drink?
- **Sara:** بله، یک دوغ.  
  *bale, yek dugh.* — Yes, one doogh.
- **Waiter:** شما چه می‌خواهید؟  
  *shomā che mi-khāhid?* — What would you like?  
  Spoken: شما چی می‌خواین؟ *shomā chi mi-khāyn?*
- **Tom:** من کباب نمی‌خورم. غذای بدون گوشت دارید؟  
  *man kabāb nemi-khoram. ghazā-ye bedun-e gusht dārid?* — I don't eat kebab. Do you have food without meat?
- **Waiter:** بله. کشک بادمجان و میرزاقاسمی داریم. با نان یا با برنج؟  
  *bale. kashk-e bādemjān o mirzā-ghāsemi dārim. bā nān yā bā berenj?* — Yes. We have kashk-e bademjan and mirza ghasemi. With bread or with rice?
- **Tom:** میرزاقاسمی با نان، لطفاً. و یک نوشابه.  
  *mirzā-ghāsemi bā nān, lotfan. va yek nushābe.* — Mirza ghasemi with bread, please. And one soft drink.
- **Waiter:** سالاد هم می‌خواهید؟  
  *sālād ham mi-khāhid?* — Do you want salad too?
- **Sara:** بله، یک سالاد شیرازی برای دو نفر.  
  *bale, yek sālād-e shirāzi barā-ye do nafar.* — Yes, one Shirazi salad for two people.
- **Waiter:** چشم. نوش جان!  
  *chashm. nush-e jān!* — Certainly. Enjoy!
- **Tom:** ببخشید، صورت‌حساب لطفاً. چقدر شد؟  
  *bebakhshid, surat-hesāb lotfan. cheghadr shod?* — Excuse me, the bill please. How much is it?  
  Spoken: ببخشید، صورت‌حساب لطفاً. چقدر شد؟ *bebakhshid, surat-hesāb lotfan. cheghadr shod?*
- **Waiter:** سیصد و بیست هزار تومان.  
  *sisad o bist hezār tumān.* — Three hundred and twenty thousand tomans.

### Three ways to ask for something

The simplest order is the thing plus لطفاً (*lotfan*, ‘please’): یک چای لطفاً (*yek chāy lotfan*). Adding a polite verb makes it fuller: یک چای بدهید (*yek chāy bedahid*, ‘give (me) a tea’) – *bedahid* is the polite command 'give', and Iranians use it constantly in shops and cafés. Thirdly, you can say what you want: یک چای می‌خواهم (*yek chāy mi-khāham*, ‘I want a tea’).

The waiter's two words are worth learning now: بفرمایید (*befarmāyid*) (here: *go ahead, what would you like?*) and چشم (*chashm*), literally *eye*, meaning *certainly, at once* – the standard way of saying yes to a request.

For 'how much did it come to?' Persian uses the past tense: چقدر شد؟ (*cheghadr shod?*, ‘how much did it become?’)

### I like / I don't like: dust dāshtan

*To like* is a two-word verb: دوست (*dust*, ‘friend’) plus داشتن (*dāshtan*, ‘to have’). Only *dāshtan* changes; *dust* stays. The thing you like goes before *dust*: من چای دوست دارم (*man chāy dust dāram*, ‘I like tea’). The negative is دوست ندارم (*dust na-dāram*).

To like doing something, put the verb in its *-an* form (the infinitive) in the same slot: من خوردن دوست دارم (*man khordan dust dāram*, ‘I like eating’).

A stronger word for love is عاشق … بودن (*āshegh-e … budan*, ‘to be in love with’): من عاشق فسنجانم (*man āshegh-e fesenjān-am*, ‘I love fesenjan’). Iranians use it for food without blushing.

| Persian | Reading | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| من دوغ دوست دارم. (*man dugh dust dāram.*) | man dugh dust dāram. | I like doogh. |
| تو قهوه دوست داری؟ (*to ghahve dust dāri?*) | to ghahve dust dāri? | Do you like coffee? |
| او ماهی دوست ندارد. (*u māhi dust na-dārad.*) | u māhi dust na-dārad. | She doesn't like fish. |
| ما غذای تند دوست داریم. (*mā ghazā-ye tond dust dārim.*) | mā ghazā-ye tond dust dārim. | We like spicy food. |

- کباب دوست داری؟  
  *kabāb dust dāri?* — Do you like kebab?
- بله، خیلی دوست دارم.  
  *bale, kheyli dust dāram.* — Yes, I like it a lot.
- من گوشت دوست ندارم. سبزی دوست دارم.  
  *man gusht dust na-dāram. sabzi dust dāram.* — I don't like meat. I like vegetables.
- تام عاشق چای ایرانی است.  
  *Tām āshegh-e chāy-e irāni ast.* — Tom loves Iranian tea.  
  Spoken: تام عاشق چای ایرانیه. *Tām āshegh-e chāy-e irāni-ye.*

### I want … : khāstan, and polite orders

خواستن (*khāstan*, ‘to want’) has the stem **khāh-**, so *I want* is می‌خواهم (*mi-khāham*) (spoken: *mi-khām*). With a noun it is a complete order: یک قهوه می‌خواهم (*yek ghahve mi-khāham*, ‘I want a coffee’). Ask the other person with چه می‌خواهید؟ (*che mi-khāhid?*, ‘what do you want?’)

The polite 'give me' is بدهید (*bedahid*) (spoken *bedid*): یک نان بدهید (*yek nān bedahid*, ‘give me one bread’). You will learn how such command forms are built in lesson 16; for now use *bedahid* as a fixed word. Note the word order: **thing + lotfan / bedahid / mi-khāham** – the verb is always last.

| Persian | Reading | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| می‌خواهم (*mi-khāham*) | mi-khāham | I want |
| می‌خواهی (*mi-khāhi*) | mi-khāhi | you want (friend) |
| می‌خواهد (*mi-khāhad*) | mi-khāhad | he / she wants |
| می‌خواهیم (*mi-khāhim*) | mi-khāhim | we want |
| می‌خواهید (*mi-khāhid*) | mi-khāhid | you want (polite) |
| می‌خواهند (*mi-khāhand*) | mi-khāhand | they want |

- دو تا چای بدهید، لطفاً.  
  *do tā chāy bedahid, lotfan.* — Give us two teas, please.  
  Spoken: دو تا چای بدین، لطفاً. *do tā chāy bedin, lotfan.*
- من آب می‌خواهم، نوشابه نمی‌خواهم.  
  *man āb mi-khāham, nushābe nemi-khāham.* — I want water, I don't want a soft drink.
- چه می‌خواهید؟ یک سالاد، لطفاً.  
  *che mi-khāhid? yek sālād, lotfan.* — What would you like? A salad, please.
- منو را بدهید، لطفاً.  
  *menu rā bedahid, lotfan.* — Give me the menu, please.

### Breakfast at Reza's house

_A typical Iranian breakfast. Read and answer the questions._

- صبح است و خانواده‌ی رضا صبحانه می‌خورند.  
  *sobh ast o khānevāde-ye Rezā sobhāne mi-khorand.* — It is morning and Reza's family are having breakfast.
- روی میز نان سنگک، پنیر، گردو و مربا است.  
  *ru-ye miz nān-e sangak, panir, gerdu o morabbā ast.* — On the table there is sangak bread, cheese, walnuts and jam.
- رضا نان و پنیر دوست دارد.  
  *Rezā nān o panir dust dārad.* — Reza likes bread and cheese.
- خواهرش تخم‌مرغ می‌خواهد.  
  *khāhar-ash tokhm-e-morgh mi-khāhad.* — His sister wants an egg.
- همه چای می‌خورند، اما مادر قهوه دوست دارد.  
  *hame chāy mi-khorand, ammā mādar ghahve dust dārad.* — Everyone drinks tea, but mother likes coffee.
- پدر می‌گوید: «نوش جان!»  
  *pedar mi-guyad: «nush-e jān!»* — Father says: "Enjoy!"

**Choose the right answer**

1. What kind of bread is on the table? — lavash / sangak / barbari
2. Who wants an egg? — Reza / his sister / his father
3. What does the mother like? — tea / doogh / coffee

1. sangak
2. his sister
3. coffee

### What is on the menu

The national dish is چلوکباب (*chelow-kabāb*): steamed rice (چلو (*chelow*)) with grilled meat. کوبیده (*kubide*) is minced lamb, برگ (*barg*) is a fillet, جوجه کباب (*juje kabāb*) is chicken. It comes with a grilled tomato, a pat of butter, raw onion, and a shaker of سماق (*somāgh*, ‘sumac’) – the dark red powder you sprinkle over the meat.

The great home dishes are the خورش (*khoresh*, ‘stews’) eaten over rice: قورمه‌سبزی (*ghorme-sabzi*) (herbs, beans and dried limes), قیمه (*gheyme*) (split peas, tomato, fried potatoes) and فسنجان (*fesenjān*) (walnut and pomegranate). Vegetarians look for کشک بادمجان (*kashk-e bādemjān*) (aubergine with whey) and میرزاقاسمی (*mirzā-ghāsemi*) (smoked aubergine, tomato and egg, from the Caspian coast).

To drink: دوغ (*dugh*), the salty yoghurt drink that goes with kebab, and endless چای (*chāy*). The salad is almost always سالاد شیرازی (*sālād-e shirāzi*) – cucumber, tomato and onion in lime juice.

### Tips

- Persian has one verb, **khordan**, for eating and drinking: *chāy mi-khoram* 'I drink tea'. There is a separate *nushidan* 'to drink', but it is formal.
- Ezafe links a dish and its kind: *chelow-kabāb-e kubide*, *sālād-e shirāzi*, *ghazā-ye irāni* – the little **-e** you learned in lesson 3.
- **nush-e jān** 'may it nourish your soul' is said before, during and after a meal, and even when you thank the cook. Answer with *nush-e jān* too, or *mamnun*.
- **gh** (غ / ق) in *ghazā*, *ghahve*, *ghorme* is a deep sound made at the back of the throat, like a French *r* said hard. Do not turn it into English *g*.

### Choose the right answer

**Choose the right answer**

1. You want to order a tea politely. You say… — یک چای بدهید، لطفاً. / یک چای دوست دارم. / چای چقدر شد؟
2. The waiter says چشم (*chashm*). He means… — Look! / Certainly. / How many?
3. 'She doesn't like fish' is… — او ماهی دوست دارد. / او ماهی دوست ندارد. / او ماهی نمی‌خواهد.
4. Which word means both 'to eat' and 'to drink'? — خواستن / خوردن / داشتن
5. چقدر شد؟ (*cheghadr shod?*) means… — How much is it? / What is it? / Where is it?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. یک چای بدهید، لطفاً. — bedahid, lotfan = 'please give (me)'.
2. Certainly. — chashm, literally 'eye', means 'certainly, right away'.
3. او ماهی دوست ندارد. — dust na-dārad = doesn't like.
4. خوردن — khordan covers both.
5. How much is it? — Literally 'how much did it become?' – asking for the total.

</details>
