# In my opinion: agreeing, disagreeing and giving reasons
## به نظر من
*be nazar-e man* — Elementary (A2), lesson 10. [Open this lesson with audio and interactive exercises](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/course/a2-10-in-my-opinion.html)

So far you have described, compared and asked. Now you learn to say what you think – and why. You give your opinion with به نظر من and فکر می‌کنم که, agree and disagree without offending anyone, and back up your view with چون, زیرا and برای اینکه. The test case is an argument every Iranian family has had: is life better in the city or in the village?

**In this lesson you will learn to:**

- give your opinion with به نظر من and فکر می‌کنم که
- ask other people what they think
- agree, partly agree and disagree – politely
- give reasons with چون، زیرا and برای اینکه
- argue for and against city and village life

### City or village?

_Mina has just spent a week at her grandmother's in a village near Kashan. Back in Tehran, over tea with Reza and Tom, she has some news._

- **Mina:** تهران دیگر برای من خیلی شلوغ است. فکر می‌کنم که زندگی در روستا بهتر است.  
  *Tehrān digar barā-ye man kheyli sholugh ast. fekr mi-konam ke zendegi dar rustā behtar ast.* — Tehran is just too crowded for me now. I think life in a village is better.  
  Spoken: تهران دیگه برام خیلی شلوغه. فکر می‌کنم زندگی تو روستا بهتره. *Tehrān dige barā-m kheyli sholugh-e. fekr mi-konam zendegi tu rustā behtar-e.*
- **Reza:** جدی می‌گویی؟ به نظر من تهران بهترین شهر دنیاست!  
  *jeddi mi-guyi? be nazar-e man Tehrān behtarin shahr-e donyā-st!* — Are you serious? In my opinion Tehran is the best city in the world!  
  Spoken: جدی می‌گی؟ به نظرم تهران بهترین شهر دنیاست! *jeddi mi-gi? be nazar-am Tehrān behtarin shahr-e donyā-st!*
- **Mina:** چرا؟ هوای تهران آلوده است و همه جا ترافیک است.  
  *cherā? havā-ye Tehrān ālude ast o hame jā terāfik ast.* — Why? Tehran's air is polluted and there's traffic everywhere.  
  Spoken: چرا؟ هوای تهران آلوده‌ست و همه جا ترافیکه. *cherā? havā-ye Tehrān ālude-st o hame jā terāfik-e.*
- **Reza:** درست است، اما تهران همه چیز دارد: کار، دانشگاه، سینما و رستوران‌های خوب.  
  *dorost ast, ammā Tehrān hame chiz dārad: kār, dāneshgāh, sinamā o restorān-hā-ye khub.* — True, but Tehran has everything: work, universities, cinemas and good restaurants.  
  Spoken: درسته، ولی تهران همه چی داره: کار، دانشگاه، سینما و رستورانای خوب. *dorost-e, vali Tehrān hame chi dāre: kār, dāneshgāh, sinamā o restorān-ā-ye khub.*
- **Tom:** من تا حدی با رضا موافقم، چون در شهر فرصت‌های بیشتری برای کار هست.  
  *man tā hadd-i bā Rezā movāfegh-am, chon dar shahr forsat-hā-ye bishtar-i barā-ye kār hast.* — I agree with Reza up to a point, because there are more job opportunities in the city.
- **Mina:** ببخشید، ولی من مخالفم. کار مهم است، اما سلامتی مهم‌تر است.  
  *bebakhshid, vali man mokhālef-am. kār mohem ast, ammā salāmati mohem-tar ast.* — Sorry, but I disagree. Work is important, but health is more important.
- **Reza:** حق با توست، هوای پاک خیلی مهم است. ولی در روستا چه کار می‌کنی؟  
  *hagh bā to-st, havā-ye pāk kheyli mohem ast. vali dar rustā che kār mi-koni?* — You're right, clean air matters a lot. But what would you do in a village?  
  Spoken: حق با توئه، هوای پاک خیلی مهمه. ولی تو روستا چی کار می‌کنی؟ *hagh bā to-e, havā-ye pāk kheyli mohem-e. vali tu rustā chi kār mi-koni?*
- **Mina:** من می‌توانم از راه دور کار کنم. این روزها خیلی‌ها همین کار را می‌کنند.  
  *man mi-tavānam az rāh-e dur kār konam. in ruz-hā kheyli-hā hamin kār rā mi-konand.* — I can work remotely. Lots of people do that these days.  
  Spoken: من می‌تونم از راه دور کار کنم. این روزا خیلیا همین کارو می‌کنن. *man mi-tunam az rāh-e dur kār konam. in ruz-ā kheyli-ā hamin kār-o mi-konan.*
- **Tom:** اینترنت روستا چطور است؟ به نظرت به اندازه‌ی کافی سریع است؟  
  *internet-e rustā chetor ast? be nazar-at be andāze-ye kāfi sari' ast?* — What's the internet like in the village? Do you think it's fast enough?  
  Spoken: اینترنت روستا چطوره؟ به نظرت به اندازه‌ی کافی سریعه؟ *internet-e rustā chetor-e? be nazar-et be andāze-ye kāfi sari'-e?*
- **Mina:** مطمئن نیستم. شاید حق با تو باشد. اما می‌خواهم امتحان کنم.  
  *motma'en nistam. shāyad hagh bā to bāshad. ammā mi-khāham emtehān konam.* — I'm not sure. Maybe you're right. But I want to give it a try.  
  Spoken: مطمئن نیستم. شاید حق با تو باشه. ولی می‌خوام امتحان کنم. *motma'en nistam. shāyad hagh bā to bāshe. vali mi-khām emtehān konam.*
- **Reza:** باشد، ولی قول بده هر ماه به تهران بیایی!  
  *bāshad, vali ghowl bedeh har māh be Tehrān biyāyi!* — All right, but promise you'll come to Tehran every month!  
  Spoken: باشه، ولی قول بده هر ماه بیای تهران! *bāshe, vali ghowl bede har māh biyāy Tehrān!*
- **Tom:** من هم موافقم! و من هم برای هوای پاک به روستا می‌آیم.  
  *man ham movāfegh-am! va man ham barā-ye havā-ye pāk be rustā mi-āyam.* — I agree too! And I'll come to the village for the clean air.  
  Spoken: منم موافقم! منم واسه هوای پاک میام روستا. *man-am movāfegh-am! man-am vāse havā-ye pāk miyām rustā.*

### Three tools for an argument

Every opinion in Persian is built from three small tools.

1. **Saying what you think.** به نظر من (*be nazar-e man*, ‘in my opinion’) literally means *to my view*. Just as common is فکر می‌کنم که… (*fekr mi-konam ke…*, ‘I think that…’) – and in speech the که (*ke*) is usually dropped.
2. **Agreeing and disagreeing.** موافق (*movāfegh*, ‘agreeing’) and مخالف (*mokhālef*, ‘opposed’) are adjectives, so they take the to-be endings you learned in A1: موافقم (*movāfegh-am*, ‘I agree’), مخالفم (*mokhālef-am*, ‘I disagree’).
3. **Giving a reason.** چون (*chon*, ‘because’) for every day, زیرا (*zirā*, ‘because’) for writing, and برای اینکه (*barā-ye inke*, ‘because’) when you answer a چرا؟ (*cherā?*, ‘why?’).

One more thing matters as much as the grammar: tone. Iranians argue with gusto, but a flat *you're wrong* sounds rude. Listen to how Mina says ببخشید، ولی… (*bebakhshid, vali…*, ‘sorry, but…’) before she disagrees, and how Reza starts with حق با توست (*hagh bā to-st*, ‘you're right’) before his *but*.

### In my opinion: به نظر من and فکر می‌کنم که

نظر (*nazar*) means *view, opinion*, so به نظر من (*be nazar-e man*) is *to my view*. Swap the pronoun to talk about anyone: به نظر او (*be nazar-e u*, ‘in his / her opinion’), به نظر شما (*be nazar-e shomā*, ‘in your opinion’). In speech the possessive endings are more common: به نظرم (*be nazar-am*), به نظرت (*be nazar-at*), به نظرش (*be nazar-ash*).

فکر کردن (*fekr kardan*, ‘to think’) is an ordinary compound verb, and what you think follows in a clause with که (*ke*, ‘that’): فکر می‌کنم که دیر است (*fekr mi-konam ke dir ast*, ‘I think (that) it's late’). As in English, *that* is often left out.

**I don't think…** – when you doubt something, the verb after فکر نمی‌کنم (*fekr nemi-konam*) usually goes into the subjunctive you learned in lesson 4: فکر نمی‌کنم بیاید (*fekr nemi-konam biyāyad*, ‘I don't think he'll come’). The same happens after شاید (*shāyad*, ‘maybe’).

| Persian | Literally | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| به نظر من… (*be nazar-e man…*) | to my view | in my opinion – the everyday phrase |
| به نظرم… (*be nazar-am…*) | to-my-view | the same, shorter; very common in speech |
| فکر می‌کنم که… (*fekr mi-konam ke…*) | thought I-do that | I think that… |
| فکر نمی‌کنم که… (*fekr nemi-konam ke…*) | thought I-don't-do that | I don't think that… (+ subjunctive) |
| به عقیده‌ی من… (*be aghide-ye man…*) | to my belief | in my view – formal, in writing and speeches |
| مطمئنم که… (*motma'en-am ke…*) | sure-I-am that | I'm sure that… |
| به نظر می‌رسد که… (*be nazar mi-resad ke…*) | it reaches the view that | it seems that… |
| نظر شما چیست؟ (*nazar-e shomā chist?*) | your view is what? | What's your opinion? |
| به نظر شما…؟ (*be nazar-e shomā…?*) | to your view…? | In your opinion…? Do you think…? |
| شما چه فکر می‌کنید؟ (*shomā che fekr mi-konid?*) | you what thought do? | What do you think? |

- به نظر من زندگی در شهر راحت‌تر است.  
  *be nazar-e man zendegi dar shahr rāhat-tar ast.* — In my opinion life in the city is easier.
- فکر می‌کنم که فردا باران می‌آید.  
  *fekr mi-konam ke fardā bārān mi-āyad.* — I think it's going to rain tomorrow.  
  Spoken: فکر کنم فردا بارون میاد. *fekr konam fardā bārun miyād.*  
  _in speech فکر کنم (literally 'let me think') is a casual 'I guess'_
- فکر نمی‌کنم که او امروز بیاید.  
  *fekr nemi-konam ke u emruz biyāyad.* — I don't think he'll come today.  
  _the subjunctive بیاید after 'I don't think'_
- به نظر شما کدام شهر قشنگ‌تر است، اصفهان یا شیراز؟  
  *be nazar-e shomā kodām shahr ghashang-tar ast, Esfahān yā Shirāz?* — In your opinion, which city is more beautiful, Isfahan or Shiraz?
- مطمئنم که این فیلم را دوست داری.  
  *motma'en-am ke in film rā dust dāri.* — I'm sure you'll love this film.
- به نظر می‌رسد که همه خسته هستند.  
  *be nazar mi-resad ke hame khaste hastand.* — It seems everyone is tired.

### I agree, I disagree: موافقم، مخالفم

موافق (*movāfegh*) and مخالف (*mokhālef*) are adjectives, so you add the short to-be endings: موافقم (*movāfegh-am*, ‘I agree’), موافقی؟ (*movāfegh-i?*, ‘do you agree?’), موافق است (*movāfegh ast*, ‘he / she agrees’), موافقیم (*movāfegh-im*, ‘we agree’), موافقید (*movāfegh-id*, ‘you agree’), موافق‌اند (*movāfegh-and*, ‘they agree’). The person or idea you agree with takes با (*bā*, ‘with’): با شما موافقم (*bā shomā movāfegh-am*, ‘I agree with you’).

مخالفم (*mokhālef-am*) is strong – *I'm against it*. Most people soften it to موافق نیستم (*movāfegh nistam*, ‘I don't agree’), or, more gently still, agree with part of what was said and then add اما (*ammā*) or ولی (*vali*). The scale below runs from full agreement to a flat no:

| Persian | English | How strong? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| کاملاً موافقم. (*kāmelan movāfegh-am.*) | I completely agree. | + + + |
| موافقم. (*movāfegh-am.*) | I agree. | + + |
| حق با شماست. (*hagh bā shomā-st.*) | You're right. | + + |
| درست است، اما… (*dorost ast, ammā…*) | That's true, but… | + / − |
| تا حدی موافقم. (*tā hadd-i movāfegh-am.*) | I agree up to a point. | + / − |
| مطمئن نیستم. (*motma'en nistam.*) | I'm not sure. | ? |
| من این‌طور فکر نمی‌کنم. (*man in-towr fekr nemi-konam.*) | I don't think so. | − |
| موافق نیستم. (*movāfegh nistam.*) | I don't agree. | − − |
| مخالفم. (*mokhālef-am.*) | I disagree. / I'm against it. | − − − |

- با شما کاملاً موافقم.  
  *bā shomā kāmelan movāfegh-am.* — I completely agree with you.
- ببخشید، ولی من با این نظر موافق نیستم.  
  *bebakhshid, vali man bā in nazar movāfegh nistam.* — Sorry, but I don't agree with this view.
- پدرم با سفر من مخالف است.  
  *pedar-am bā safar-e man mokhālef ast.* — My father is against my trip.
- درست است، اما یک مشکل هست.  
  *dorost ast, ammā yek moshkel hast.* — That's true, but there's one problem.  
  Spoken: درسته، ولی یه مشکلی هست. *dorost-e, vali ye moshkel-i hast.*
- شاید حق با شما باشد، ولی من این‌طور فکر نمی‌کنم.  
  *shāyad hagh bā shomā bāshad, vali man in-towr fekr nemi-konam.* — Maybe you're right, but I don't think so.
- شما موافقید یا مخالف؟  
  *shomā movāfegh-id yā mokhālef?* — Are you for or against?

### Because: چون، زیرا، برای اینکه

Persian has three common words for *because*, and they differ in style more than in meaning.

- چون (*chon*) is the everyday one. It can come in the middle or at the very start: چون خسته بودم، زود خوابیدم. (*chon khaste budam, zud khābidam.*, ‘Because I was tired, I went to bed early.’)
- زیرا (*zirā*) belongs to writing, the news and speeches. It only joins two halves of a sentence – it never opens one.
- برای اینکه (*barā-ye inke*) (literally *for this that*) is the natural answer to چرا؟ (*cherā?*, ‘why?’). In Tehrani speech it is واسه اینکه (*vāse inke*).

Before a **noun**, use به خاطر (*be khāter-e*, ‘because of’): به خاطر ترافیک (*be khāter-e terāfik*, ‘because of the traffic’). And to give the result instead of the reason, use برای همین (*barā-ye hamin*, ‘that's why’) or به همین دلیل (*be hamin dalil*, ‘for this reason’).

| Persian | English | Followed by | Style |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| چون (*chon*) | because | a clause | everyday; can start the sentence |
| زیرا (*zirā*) | because | a clause | formal, written; never first |
| برای اینکه (*barā-ye inke*) | because | a clause | everyday; the answer to چرا؟ |
| به خاطر (*be khāter-e*) | because of | a noun | everyday |
| برای همین (*barā-ye hamin*) | that's why, so | a clause | spoken |
| به همین دلیل (*be hamin dalil*) | for this reason | a clause | neutral, written |
| پس (*pas*) | so, then | a clause | everyday |

- امروز به پارک نمی‌رویم، چون هوا خیلی سرد است.  
  *emruz be pārk nemi-ravim, chon havā kheyli sard ast.* — We're not going to the park today, because it's very cold.
- چون ماشین نداشتم، با مترو آمدم.  
  *chon māshin na-dāshtam, bā metro āmadam.* — Because I didn't have a car, I came by metro.
- بسیاری از جوان‌ها به شهر می‌روند، زیرا در روستا کار کم است.  
  *besyāri az javān-hā be shahr mi-ravand, zirā dar rustā kār kam ast.* — Many young people move to the city, because there is little work in the villages.
- برای اینکه ترافیک خیلی سنگین بود.  
  *barā-ye inke terāfik kheyli sangin bud.* — Because the traffic was really heavy.  
  Spoken: واسه اینکه ترافیک خیلی سنگین بود. *vāse inke terāfik kheyli sangin bud.*  
  _an answer to چرا دیر آمدی؟ – why are you late?_
- به خاطر آلودگی هوا، مدرسه‌ها تعطیل شدند.  
  *be khāter-e āludegi-ye havā, madrese-hā ta'til shodand.* — Because of the air pollution, the schools were closed.
- مادربزرگم در روستا زندگی می‌کند، برای همین هر سال به آن‌جا می‌رویم.  
  *mādar-bozorg-am dar rustā zendegi mi-konad, barā-ye hamin har sāl be ān-jā mi-ravim.* — My grandmother lives in a village – that's why we go there every year.

### City or village? Tom's essay

_Mr Karimi asked his class to write an انشا – a school essay – on an old question. Here is Tom's. Notice how he gives both sides with از یک طرف … از طرف دیگر (on the one hand … on the other) before his own opinion._

- زندگی در شهر بهتر است یا زندگی در روستا؟  
  *zendegi dar shahr behtar ast yā zendegi dar rustā?* — Is life better in the city or in the village?
- بسیاری از جوان‌های ایرانی به شهرهای بزرگ می‌روند، زیرا در شهر کار و دانشگاه هست.  
  *besyāri az javān-hā-ye Irāni be shahr-hā-ye bozorg mi-ravand, zirā dar shahr kār o dāneshgāh hast.* — Many young Iranians move to the big cities, because that is where the jobs and universities are.
- از یک طرف، شهر امکانات بیشتری دارد: بیمارستان، مترو، سینما و کتاب‌فروشی.  
  *az yek taraf, shahr emkānāt-e bishtar-i dārad: bimārestān, metro, sinamā o ketāb-forushi.* — On the one hand, the city has more facilities: hospitals, the metro, cinemas and bookshops.
- از طرف دیگر، زندگی در شهر گران و شلوغ است.  
  *az taraf-e digar, zendegi dar shahr gerān o sholugh ast.* — On the other hand, city life is expensive and crowded.
- در تهران هوا آلوده است و مردم ساعت‌ها در ترافیک می‌مانند.  
  *dar Tehrān havā ālude ast o mardom sā'at-hā dar terāfik mi-mānand.* — In Tehran the air is polluted and people sit in traffic for hours.
- در روستا زندگی آرام‌تر است و مردم همدیگر را می‌شناسند.  
  *dar rustā zendegi ārām-tar ast o mardom hamdigar rā mi-shenāsand.* — In a village life is calmer, and people know each other.
- اما به نظر من روستا یک مشکل بزرگ دارد: کار کم است.  
  *ammā be nazar-e man rustā yek moshkel-e bozorg dārad: kār kam ast.* — But in my opinion the village has one big problem: there isn't much work.
- دوستم مینا فکر می‌کند که با اینترنت در روستا هم می‌تواند کار کند.  
  *dust-am Minā fekr mi-konad ke bā internet dar rustā ham mi-tavānad kār konad.* — My friend Mina thinks that with the internet she can work in a village too.
- من فقط تا حدی با او موافقم، چون اینترنت روستاها هنوز کند است.  
  *man faghat tā hadd-i bā u movāfegh-am, chon internet-e rustā-hā hanuz kond ast.* — I only partly agree with her, because the internet in villages is still slow.  
  _کند here is kond, 'slow' – the same letters as konad, 'does'_
- پس به نظر من بهترین راه این است که در شهر کار کنیم و آخر هفته‌ها به روستا برویم.  
  *pas be nazar-e man behtarin rāh in ast ke dar shahr kār konim o ākhar-e hafte-hā be rustā beravim.* — So in my opinion the best way is to work in the city and go to the village at weekends.
- شما چه فکر می‌کنید؟  
  *shomā che fekr mi-konid?* — What do you think?

**Choose the right answer**

1. Why do many young Iranians move to the big cities? — for the clean air / because the jobs and universities are there / because life there is cheap
2. What is the village's big problem, in Tom's opinion? — there isn't much work / pollution / traffic
3. What does Tom suggest in the end? — living in a village all year / moving back to Australia / working in the city and going to the village at weekends

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. because the jobs and universities are there
2. there isn't much work
3. working in the city and going to the village at weekends

</details>

### From the village to the city – and back for Nowruz

Two generations ago most Iranians lived in villages: in the census of 1956 fewer than a third of the population was urban. Today about three Iranians in four live in towns and cities, and Tehran alone has more than eight million people. Yet almost every city family still has a village in its story – the ده (*deh*, ‘village’) of a grandfather – and at Nowruz the roads out of Tehran fill with people going back to it.

Some villages have become sights in their own right: ماسوله (*Māsule*) in the green hills of Gilan, built so steeply that the roof of one house is the yard of the house above, or red-earth ابیانه (*Abyāne*) near Kashan, where older women still wear long white headscarves printed with flowers.

The argument itself is a school classic. Generations of Iranian children have had to write an انشا (*enshā*, ‘essay’) on the question علم بهتر است یا ثروت؟ (*elm behtar ast yā servat?*, ‘Is knowledge better, or wealth?’) – so many that the title has become shorthand for a tired school topic. *City or village?* is its close cousin, and every Iranian has an opinion on it.

### Tips

- **Disagree softly.** A flat **مخالفم** can sound rude to an older person or a guest. Start with **درست است، اما…** (that's true, but…), **ببخشید، ولی…** (sorry, but…) or **شاید حق با شما باشد، ولی…** – and then say what you like.
- **زیرا never starts a sentence.** To put the reason first, use **چون**: *chon khaste budam, zud khābidam* – because I was tired, I went to bed early.
- **موافقم is one word with two parts**: movāfegh + -am (I am). Change the ending for other people: **موافقی؟** (do you agree?), **موافق‌اند** (they agree).
- **کند** can be *konad* (does) or *kond* (slow) – the same letters. The sentence tells you which.
- In speech **به نظر من** becomes **به نظرم**, **برای اینکه** becomes **واسه اینکه**, and **فکر می‌کنم** often becomes **فکر کنم** – a casual *I guess*.

### Choose the right answer

**Choose the right answer**

1. 'I agree with you.' — با شما موافقم. / شما موافقم. / با شما مخالفم.
2. Which 'because' belongs in a newspaper article? — واسه اینکه / زیرا / برای همین
3. You want to disagree politely with an older colleague. The best way to start: — مخالفم! / اشتباه می‌کنید! / درست است، اما…
4. 'What do you think?' — شما چه کار می‌کنید؟ / شما چه فکر می‌کنید؟ / شما کجا زندگی می‌کنید؟
5. 'I don't think he'll come.' — فکر نمی‌کنم بیاید. / فکر می‌کنم نمی‌آمد. / فکر نمی‌کنم آمد.
6. Which word can NOT start a sentence? — چون / به نظر من / زیرا

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. با شما موافقم. — The person you agree with takes با: bā shomā movāfegh-am.
2. زیرا — zirā is formal and written; vāse inke is spoken Tehrani, and barā-ye hamin means 'that's why'.
3. درست است، اما… — Agree with part of what was said, then add your 'but'.
4. شما چه فکر می‌کنید؟ — fekr kardan = to think; che kār mi-konid? = what are you doing?
5. فکر نمی‌کنم بیاید. — After 'I don't think' Persian normally uses the subjunctive: biyāyad.
6. زیرا — zirā only joins the two halves of a sentence; start with chon instead.

</details>
