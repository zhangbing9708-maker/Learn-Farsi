# What do you do? Work and study
## چه‌کار می‌کنید؟
*che-kār mi-konid?* — Beginner (A1), lesson 7. [Open this lesson with audio and interactive exercises](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/course/a1-07-jobs.html)

After 'where are you from?', the next question Iranians ask is 'what do you do?' In this lesson you learn to name jobs and workplaces, to say where you work or study, and to ask other people about their work – with lots of practice of the present tense from lesson 6.

**In this lesson you will learn to:**

- name common jobs and workplaces
- ask and say what people do: che-kār mi-konid?
- say where you work or study with kār kardan, dars khāndan and dar
- use the question words che-kār, kojā, ki and che

### At Sara's house

_Sara has invited some friends home. Tom meets her brother Dariush, whom you saw in the family photo in lesson 5._

- **Sara:** تام، این برادرم داریوش است.  
  *Tām, in barādar-am Dāriyush ast.* — Tom, this is my brother Dariush.
- **Tom:** خوشبختم! شما چه‌کار می‌کنید؟  
  *khosh-bakht-am! shomā che-kār mi-konid?* — Nice to meet you! What do you do?  
  Spoken: خوشبختم! شما چی‌کار می‌کنین؟ *khosh-bakht-am! shomā chi-kār mi-konin?*
- **Dariush:** من هم خوشبختم. من مهندس هستم. در یک شرکت کار می‌کنم.  
  *man ham khosh-bakht-am. man mohandes hastam. dar yek sherkat kār mi-konam.* — Nice to meet you too. I'm an engineer. I work for a company.
- **Tom:** شرکت شما کجاست؟  
  *sherkat-e shomā kojā-st?* — Where is your company?
- **Dariush:** در خیابان ولیعصر. شما هم کار می‌کنید؟  
  *dar khiyābān-e Vali'asr. shomā ham kār mi-konid?* — On Valiasr Street. Do you work too?
- **Tom:** نه، من دانشجو هستم. در دانشگاه تهران فارسی می‌خوانم.  
  *na, man dāneshju hastam. dar dāneshgāh-e Tehrān fārsi mi-khānam.* — No, I'm a student. I study Persian at the University of Tehran.  
  Spoken: نه، دانشجوام. تو دانشگاه تهران فارسی می‌خونم. *na, dāneshju-am. tu dāneshgāh-e Tehrān fārsi mi-khunam.*
- **Dariush:** چه خوب! همسرم هم در دانشگاه کار می‌کند. او استاد است.  
  *che khub! hamsar-am ham dar dāneshgāh kār mi-konad. u ostād ast.* — How nice! My wife works at a university too. She's a professor.
- **Tom:** او چه درسی می‌دهد؟  
  *u che dars-i mi-dahad?* — What does she teach?  
  Spoken: چی درس می‌ده؟ *chi dars mi-de?*
- **Dariush:** تاریخ. شما فقط درس می‌خوانید یا کار هم می‌کنید؟  
  *tārikh. shomā faghat dars mi-khānid yā kār ham mi-konid?* — History. Do you only study, or do you work too?
- **Tom:** هفته‌ای دو روز در یک کافه کار می‌کنم.  
  *hafte-i do ruz dar yek kāfe kār mi-konam.* — I work in a café two days a week.  
  Spoken: هفته‌ای دو روز تو یه کافه کار می‌کنم. *hafte-i do ruz tu ye kāfe kār mi-konam.*
- **Sara:** کافه‌ی تام نزدیک دانشگاه است. قهوه‌اش خیلی خوب است!  
  *kāfe-ye Tām nazdik-e dāneshgāh ast. ghahve-ash kheyli khub ast!* — Tom's café is near the university. Its coffee is very good!
- **Dariush:** پس شما هم دانشجو هستید، هم گارسون!  
  *pas shomā ham dāneshju hastid, ham gārson!* — So you're a student and a waiter!
- **Tom:** بله، و خیلی خسته!  
  *bale, va kheyli khaste!* — Yes – and very tired!

### What do you do?

The everyday question is چه‌کار می‌کنید؟ (*che-kār mi-konid?*, ‘what do you do?’) – literally *what work do you do?* In speech it sounds like *chi-kār mi-konin?* On forms and in interviews you will see شغل شما چیست؟ (*shoghl-e shomā chist?*, ‘what is your occupation?’), and friends ask چه‌کاره‌ای؟ (*che-kāre-i?*, ‘what's your line of work?’)

To answer, name the job with *hastam* – there is no word for *a*: من مهندس هستم (*man mohandes hastam*, ‘I am an engineer’). Or say where you work with کار کردن (*kār kardan*, ‘to work’): در یک شرکت کار می‌کنم (*dar yek sherkat kār mi-konam*, ‘I work for a company’). Students say دانشجو هستم (*dāneshju hastam*) and name their subject with خواندن (*khāndan*, ‘to read, to study’): فارسی می‌خوانم (*fārsi mi-khānam*, ‘I study Persian’).

In Iran a job is also a title. People greet a doctor as آقای دکتر (*āghā-ye doktor*) or خانم دکتر (*khānom-e doktor*), an engineer as مهندس (*mohandes*), and a teacher or a master of any craft as استاد (*ostād*).

### Present tense practice: kār mi-konam, dars mi-khānam

You built the present tense in lesson 6: *mi-* + stem + ending. Two verbs carry most talk about work and study.

کار کردن (*kār kardan*, ‘to work’) is a compound, like *zendegi kardan*: کار می‌کنم (*kār mi-konam*, ‘I work’). خواندن (*khāndan*) means *to read* and also *to study* a subject: پزشکی می‌خوانم (*pezeshki mi-khānam*, ‘I study medicine’). With درس (*dars*, ‘lesson’) it means *to study* in general: درس می‌خوانم (*dars mi-khānam*, ‘I am studying’). Its stem is *khān-*, written خوان – with the silent و you met in خواهر.

The teacher's side of the desk is درس دادن (*dars dādan*, ‘to teach’), literally *to give a lesson*: درس می‌دهم (*dars mi-daham*, ‘I teach’), درس می‌دهد (*dars mi-dahad*, ‘he / she teaches’).

|  | kār kardan – to work | khāndan – to read, to study |
| --- | --- | --- |
| من (*man*) | کار می‌کنم (*kār mi-konam*) | می‌خوانم (*mi-khānam*) |
| تو (*to*) | کار می‌کنی (*kār mi-koni*) | می‌خوانی (*mi-khāni*) |
| او (*u*) | کار می‌کند (*kār mi-konad*) | می‌خواند (*mi-khānad*) |
| ما (*mā*) | کار می‌کنیم (*kār mi-konim*) | می‌خوانیم (*mi-khānim*) |
| شما (*shomā*) | کار می‌کنید (*kār mi-konid*) | می‌خوانید (*mi-khānid*) |
| آن‌ها (*ān-hā*) | کار می‌کنند (*kār mi-konand*) | می‌خوانند (*mi-khānand*) |

- مادرم در بانک کار می‌کند.  
  *mādar-am dar bānk kār mi-konad.* — My mother works in a bank.  
  Spoken: مامانم تو بانک کار می‌کنه. *māmān-am tu bānk kār mi-kone.*
- سارا در دانشگاه تاریخ می‌خواند.  
  *Sārā dar dāneshgāh tārikh mi-khānad.* — Sara studies history at university.
- بچه‌ها در مدرسه درس می‌خوانند.  
  *bacche-hā dar madrese dars mi-khānand.* — The children study at school.
- آقای کریمی فارسی درس می‌دهد.  
  *āghā-ye Karimi fārsi dars mi-dahad.* — Mr Karimi teaches Persian.

### Asking questions: che-kār, kojā, ki, che

Persian question words stand in the place of the answer – the rest of the sentence does not move: شما در بانک کار می‌کنید (*shomā dar bānk kār mi-konid*, ‘you work in a bank’) → شما کجا کار می‌کنید؟ (*shomā kojā kār mi-konid?*, ‘where do you work?’)

چه‌کار (*che-kār*) is also written as two words, چه کار, and in speech it is *chi-kār*.

Careful with کی (*ki*): the same spelling is *ki* 'who' and *key* 'when' (lesson 12). The context – and the voice – tell them apart.

| Question word | Example | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| چه‌کار (*che-kār*) – what (work) | چه‌کار می‌کنید؟ (*che-kār mi-konid?*) | What do you do? |
| کجا (*kojā*) – where | کجا کار می‌کنید؟ (*kojā kār mi-konid?*) | Where do you work? |
| کی (*ki*) – who | کی اینجا کار می‌کند؟ (*ki injā kār mi-konad?*) | Who works here? |
| چه (*che*) – what | چه می‌خوانید؟ (*che mi-khānid?*) | What are you studying? |
| چه درسی (*che dars-i*) – which subject | چه درسی می‌دهید؟ (*che dars-i mi-dahid?*) | What do you teach? |

- برادرتان چه‌کار می‌کند؟  
  *barādar-etān che-kār mi-konad?* — What does your brother do?
- مهسا کجا کار می‌کند؟  
  *Mahsā kojā kār mi-konad?* — Where does Mahsa work?
- شما چه می‌خوانید؟  
  *shomā che mi-khānid?* — What do you study?  
  Spoken: شما چی می‌خونین؟ *shomā chi mi-khunin?*
- کی در این مغازه کار می‌کند؟  
  *ki dar in maghāze kār mi-konad?* — Who works in this shop?

### In, at: dar – and jobs without 'a'

در (*dar*) covers both *in* and *at*: در بیمارستان (*dar bimārestān*, ‘in / at the hospital’), در یک شرکت (*dar yek sherkat*, ‘at a company’), در تهران (*dar Tehrān*, ‘in Tehran’). Unlike the place words of lesson 8, *dar* takes no ezafe. In speech it often becomes تو (*tu*) or disappears: *bimārestān kār mi-konam*.

A job needs no word for *a*: او پرستار است (*u parastār ast*, ‘she is a nurse’). To say what kind, add the ezafe: معلم انگلیسی (*mo'allem-e engelisi*, ‘English teacher’), راننده‌ی تاکسی (*rānande-ye tāksi*, ‘taxi driver’), کارمند بانک (*kārmand-e bānk*, ‘bank clerk’), دانشجوی مهندسی (*dāneshju-ye mohandesi*, ‘engineering student’), استاد تاریخ (*ostād-e tārikh*, ‘history professor’).

| Job | Where | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| پزشک (*pezeshk*) | در بیمارستان (*dar bimārestān*) | a doctor – in a hospital |
| معلم (*mo'allem*) | در مدرسه (*dar madrese*) | a teacher – at a school |
| استاد (*ostād*) | در دانشگاه (*dar dāneshgāh*) | a professor – at a university |
| کارمند (*kārmand*) | در اداره (*dar edāre*) | an employee – in an office |
| فروشنده (*forushande*) | در مغازه (*dar maghāze*) | a shop assistant – in a shop |
| آشپز (*āshpaz*) | در رستوران (*dar resturān*) | a cook – in a restaurant |

- مهسا پزشک است. او در بیمارستان کار می‌کند.  
  *Mahsā pezeshk ast. u dar bimārestān kār mi-konad.* — Mahsa is a doctor. She works in a hospital.
- آقای کریمی معلم فارسی است.  
  *āghā-ye Karimi mo'allem-e fārsi ast.* — Mr Karimi is a Persian teacher.
- پدر رضا راننده‌ی تاکسی است.  
  *pedar-e Rezā rānande-ye tāksi ast.* — Reza's father is a taxi driver.
- من کارمند بانک هستم.  
  *man kārmand-e bānk hastam.* — I am a bank clerk.

### Doctor, engineer – and the konkur

Ask Iranian parents what they hope their children will become, and you will often hear two words: دکتر (*doktor*) and مهندس (*mohandes*). Medicine and engineering carry great prestige, and the titles stay with people for life – the man who comes to fix your lift may be greeted as *āghā-ye mohandes*.

The gate to university is the کنکور (*konkur*), a national entrance exam taken by around a million young people every year. Months of private classes and practice tests lead up to it, and the results decide who studies what, and where. Iran has a very large student population, and for many years women have made up about half of it – at times more.

In daily life, many Iranians have more than one job. A teacher may also give private lessons, and an office worker may drive a taxi in the evening – so the driver who asks you *che-kār mi-konid?* may well be an engineer himself.

### Tips

- No 'a' before a job: *man mohandes hastam* – 'I am an engineer'. Use *yek* only when you add an adjective: *u yek mohandes-e khub ast* – 'he is a good engineer'.
- *dars khāndan* is 'to study' (literally 'to read a lesson') and *dars dādan* is 'to teach' ('to give a lesson'): the same *dars*, on opposite sides of the desk.
- **کی** is both *ki* 'who' and *key* 'when'. Listen for the vowel.
- خواندن has a silent و: می‌خوانم is *mi-khānam*, and in speech *mi-khunam*.
- Many job words are a stem plus an ending: *forush-ande* 'seller' (from *forukhtan*, to sell), *rān-ande* 'driver' (from *rāndan*, to drive), *āsh-paz* 'cook' (*āsh* 'soup' + *paz* 'cooking').

3. My sister is a doctor in Shiraz.
4. Where do you study? (to a friend)

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. شما چه‌کار می‌کنید؟ — *shomā che-kār mi-konid?*
2. من مهندس هستم. در یک شرکت کار می‌کنم. — *man mohandes hastam. dar yek sherkat kār mi-konam.*
3. خواهرم در شیراز پزشک است. — *khāhar-am dar Shirāz pezeshk ast.*
4. تو کجا درس می‌خوانی؟ — *to kojā dars mi-khāni?*
