# Travelling: the station, the airport and the hotel
## سفر به اصفهان
*safar be Esfahān* — Elementary (A2), lesson 11. [Open this lesson with audio and interactive exercises](https://zhangbing9708-maker.github.io/Learn-Farsi/course/a2-11-travelling.html)

Iran is a big country, and sooner or later you will take a plane, a night train or a long bus ride. This lesson gets you through the station, the airport and the hotel desk – booking, checking in, asking about breakfast and check-out – and introduces the real Persian future tense, خواهم رفت. You will mostly read it on tickets, screens and in the news; in conversation people simply use the present.

**In this lesson you will learn to:**

- understand travel announcements, tickets and timetables
- form and recognise the formal future: خواهم رفت
- talk about plans the everyday way, with the present and قرار است
- check in at a hotel and ask the questions travellers need

### At the hotel desk in Isfahan

_Tom and Amir have come to Isfahan by train for the Nowruz holidays. It is evening when they reach a small hotel near Naqsh-e Jahan Square._

- **Receptionist:** سلام، خوش آمدید! بفرمایید.  
  *salām, khosh āmadid! befarmāyid.* — Hello, welcome! How can I help?
- **Amir:** سلام. ما یک اتاق دوتخته برای سه شب رزرو کرده‌ایم.  
  *salām. mā yek otāgh-e do-takhte barā-ye se shab rezerv karde-im.* — Hello. We've booked a twin room for three nights.  
  Spoken: سلام. ما یه اتاق دوتخته برای سه شب رزرو کردیم. *salām. mā ye otāgh-e do-takhte barā-ye se shab rezerv kardim.*
- **Receptionist:** بله، اتاق شما آماده است. لطفاً کارت ملی یا گذرنامه‌تان را بدهید.  
  *bale, otāgh-e shomā āmāde ast. lotfan kārt-e melli yā gozarnāme-tān rā bedahid.* — Yes, your room is ready. Your ID cards or passports, please.  
  Spoken: بله، اتاقتون آماده‌ست. لطفاً کارت ملی یا پاسپورتتونو بدین. *bale, otāgh-etun āmāde-st. lotfan kārt-e melli yā pāsport-etun-o bedin.*
- **Tom:** بفرمایید، این گذرنامه‌ی من است. صبحانه هم دارد؟  
  *befarmāyid, in gozarnāme-ye man ast. sobhāne ham dārad?* — Here you are, this is my passport. Is breakfast included?  
  Spoken: بفرمایید، اینم پاسپورت من. صبحونه هم داره؟ *befarmāyid, in-am pāsport-e man. sobhune ham dāre?*
- **Receptionist:** بله، صبحانه از ساعت هفت تا ده است، در طبقه‌ی اول.  
  *bale, sobhāne az sā'at-e haft tā dah ast, dar tabaghe-ye avval.* — Yes, breakfast is from seven to ten, on the first floor.
- **Amir:** ببخشید، هتل وای‌فای دارد؟  
  *bebakhshid, hotel vāy-fāy dārad?* — Excuse me, does the hotel have wifi?  
  Spoken: ببخشید، هتل وای‌فای داره؟ *bebakhshid, hotel vāy-fāy dāre?*
- **Receptionist:** بله، رمزش روی این کارت است. این هم کلید شما: اتاق ۲۰۴، طبقه‌ی دوم.  
  *bale, ramz-ash ru-ye in kārt ast. in ham kelid-e shomā: otāgh-e devist-o-chahār, tabaghe-ye dovvom.* — Yes, the password is on this card. And here is your key: room 204, second floor.  
  Spoken: بله، رمزش رو این کارته. اینم کلیدتون: اتاق ۲۰۴، طبقه‌ی دوم. *bale, ramz-esh ru in kārt-e. in-am kelid-etun: otāgh-e devist-o-chahār, tabaghe-ye dovvom.*
- **Tom:** ممنون. فردا می‌خواهیم میدان نقش جهان را ببینیم. از این‌جا دور است؟  
  *mamnun. fardā mi-khāhim meydān-e Naghsh-e Jahān rā bebinim. az in-jā dur ast?* — Thanks. Tomorrow we want to see Naqsh-e Jahan Square. Is it far from here?  
  Spoken: ممنون. فردا می‌خوایم میدون نقش جهان رو ببینیم. از این‌جا دوره؟ *mamnun. fardā mi-khāym meydun-e Naghsh-e Jahān ro bebinim. az in-jā dur-e?*
- **Receptionist:** نه، پیاده ده دقیقه راه است.  
  *na, piyāde dah daghighe rāh ast.* — No, it's ten minutes on foot.  
  Spoken: نه، پیاده ده دقیقه راهه. *na, piyāde dah daghighe rāh-e.*
- **Amir:** یک سؤال دیگر: روز آخر ساعت چند باید اتاق را تحویل بدهیم؟  
  *yek so'āl-e digar: ruz-e ākhar sā'at-e chand bāyad otāgh rā tahvil bedahim?* — One more question: on the last day, what time do we have to check out?  
  Spoken: یه سؤال دیگه: روز آخر ساعت چند باید اتاقو تحویل بدیم؟ *ye so'āl-e dige: ruz-e ākhar sā'at-e chand bāyad otāgh-o tahvil bedim?*
- **Receptionist:** تا ساعت دوازده. اگر قطارتان عصر حرکت می‌کند، می‌توانید چمدان‌ها را این‌جا بگذارید.  
  *tā sā'at-e davāzdah. agar ghatār-etān asr harekat mi-konad, mi-tavānid chamedān-hā rā in-jā begozārid.* — By twelve. If your train leaves in the afternoon, you can leave your suitcases here.  
  Spoken: تا ساعت دوازده. اگه قطارتون عصر حرکت می‌کنه، می‌تونین چمدونا رو این‌جا بذارین. *tā sā'at-e davāzdah. age ghatār-etun asr harekat mi-kone, mi-tunin chamedun-ā ro in-jā bezārin.*
- **Tom:** عالی است! قطار ما ساعت پنج حرکت می‌کند.  
  *āli ast! ghatār-e mā sā'at-e panj harekat mi-konad.* — Great! Our train leaves at five.  
  Spoken: عالیه! قطارمون ساعت پنج حرکت می‌کنه. *āli-ye! ghatār-emun sā'at-e panj harekat mi-kone.*
- **Receptionist:** پس امیدوارم سفر خوبی داشته باشید. شب به خیر!  
  *pas omidvār-am safar-e khub-i dāshte bāshid. shab be kheyr!* — Then I hope you have a lovely trip. Good night!

### Will: three ways to talk about the future

English has *I'll go*, *I'm going to go* and *I'm going*. Persian has three ways too, but they differ in **style** rather than meaning:

- **The formal future** – خواهم رفت (*khāham raft*, ‘I will go’). You read it on tickets and departure boards, hear it in the news and in station announcements, and use it in writing and in solemn promises.
- **The present** – فردا می‌روم (*fardā mi-ravam*, ‘I'm going tomorrow’). This is what people actually say: a time word shows that it is the future. In Tehrani speech, فردا می‌رم (*fardā mi-ram*).
- **قرار است + subjunctive** – قرار است فردا برویم (*gharār ast fardā beravim*, ‘we're due to go tomorrow’), for anything arranged or timetabled.

In the dialogue nobody uses the formal future – it would sound like a news bulletin. But on the way to Isfahan, Tom and Amir heard it on the station loudspeaker: قطار تهران به اصفهان ساعت هفت حرکت خواهد کرد. (*ghatār-e Tehrān be Esfahān sā'at-e haft harekat khāhad kard.*, ‘The Tehran–Isfahan train will depart at seven.’)

### The formal future: خواهم رفت

Persian has a true future tense, but you will mostly *read* it rather than hear it. Take the present of خواستن (*khāstan*, ‘to want’) **without** می (*mi*) – خواهم (*khāham*), خواهی (*khāhi*), خواهد (*khāhad*) … – and add the **past stem** of the main verb, which is the infinitive without *-an*: رفتن → رفت (*raft*). Only *khāh-* changes for the person; the main verb stays the same.

**Negative:** ن (*na*) goes on *khāh-*: نخواهم رفت (*na-khāham raft*, ‘I will not go’).

**Compound verbs:** the future goes between the two parts: حرکت خواهد کرد (*harekat khāhad kard*, ‘it will depart’), تماس خواهیم گرفت (*tamās khāhim gereft*, ‘we will be in touch’).

**Don't confuse** it with *want*: می‌خواهم بروم (*mi-khāham beravam*, ‘I want to go’) has **mi-** and the subjunctive; the future has no mi- and the past stem.

| تو (*to*) | خواهی رفت (*khāhi raft*) | نخواهی رفت (*na-khāhi raft*) | you will (not) go |
| او (*u*) | خواهد رفت (*khāhad raft*) | نخواهد رفت (*na-khāhad raft*) | he / she will (not) go |

### What people actually say: the present for the future

In conversation Iranians hardly ever say *khāham raft*. They use the ordinary present tense you learned in A1, and a time word does the rest: فردا می‌روم (*fardā mi-ravam*, ‘I'm going tomorrow, I'll go tomorrow’). In Tehrani speech that is فردا می‌رم (*fardā mi-ram*), and the destination often moves to the end: فردا می‌رم اصفهان (*fardā mi-ram Esfahān*).

For something fixed in advance – a timetable, an appointment, a plan made with other people – use قرار است (*gharār ast*, ‘it is arranged’) + the subjunctive: قرار است ساعت هشت حرکت کنیم (*gharār ast sā'at-e hasht harekat konim*, ‘we're due to leave at eight’). In speech: قراره (*gharāre*).

The same plan in three styles:

| Formal: news, tickets, brochures | Everyday written | Spoken Tehrani | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| خواهد آمد (*khāhad āmad*) | می‌آید (*mi-āyad*) | میاد (*miyād*) | he / she'll come |
| حرکت خواهد کرد (*harekat khāhad kard*) | حرکت می‌کند (*harekat mi-konad*) | حرکت می‌کنه (*harekat mi-kone*) | it'll leave |
| خواهیم رسید (*khāhim resid*) | می‌رسیم (*mi-resim*) | می‌رسیم (*mi-resim*) | we'll arrive |
| خواهید دید (*khāhid did*) | می‌بینید (*mi-binid*) | می‌بینین (*mi-binin*) | you'll see |
| نخواهند ماند (*na-khāhand mānd*) | نمی‌مانند (*nemi-mānand*) | نمی‌مونن (*nemi-munan*) | they won't stay |

- فردا صبح به اصفهان می‌رویم.  
  *fardā sobh be Esfahān mi-ravim.* — We're going to Isfahan tomorrow morning.  
  Spoken: فردا صبح می‌ریم اصفهان. *fardā sobh mi-rim Esfahān.*
- قرار است قطار ساعت هشت حرکت کند.  
  *gharār ast ghatār sā'at-e hasht harekat konad.* — The train is due to leave at eight.  
  Spoken: قراره قطار ساعت هشت حرکت کنه. *gharār-e ghatār sā'at-e hasht harekat kone.*
- هفته‌ی بعد پدر و مادرم از استرالیا می‌آیند.  
  *hafte-ye ba'd pedar o mādar-am az Ostrāliyā mi-āyand.* — Next week my parents are coming from Australia.  
  Spoken: هفته‌ی بعد بابا مامانم از استرالیا میان. *hafte-ye ba'd bābā māmān-am az Ostrāliyā miyān.*

  *shab be hotel mi-resim o zud mi-khābim.* — We'll get to the hotel at night and go straight to bed.
- نگران نباش، فردا زنگ می‌زنم.  
  *negarān na-bāsh, fardā zang mi-zanam.* — Don't worry, I'll ring tomorrow.

### Questions for the hotel and the station

Travel questions are built from words you know, with the verb at the end. Three things make them polite: start with ببخشید (*bebakhshid*, ‘excuse me’); use the plural شما (*shomā*) forms; and for requests ask *is it possible…?* – می‌شود…؟ (*mi-shavad…?*) or the more formal ممکن است…؟ (*momken ast…?*) – with the subjunctive.

In writing and in very formal speech a yes/no question can begin with آیا (*āyā*). It adds no meaning – it is simply a question mark you can hear: آیا اتاق خالی دارید؟ (*āyā otāgh-e khāli dārid?*, ‘Do you have a free room?’)

| Persian | English |
| --- | --- |
| اتاق خالی دارید؟ (*otāgh-e khāli dārid?*) | Do you have a free room? |
| برای یک شب چقدر می‌شود؟ (*barā-ye yek shab cheghadr mi-shavad?*) | How much is it for one night? |
| صبحانه هم دارد؟ (*sobhāne ham dārad?*) | Is breakfast included? |
| صبحانه از ساعت چند است؟ (*sobhāne az sā'at-e chand ast?*) | What time is breakfast from? |
| ساعت چند باید اتاق را تحویل بدهیم؟ (*sā'at-e chand bāyad otāgh rā tahvil bedahim?*) | What time is check-out? |
| می‌شود چمدان‌ها را این‌جا بگذاریم؟ (*mi-shavad chamedān-hā rā in-jā begozārim?*) | Can we leave our suitcases here? |
| ممکن است یک تاکسی برای ما بگیرید؟ (*momken ast yek tāksi barā-ye mā begirid?*) | Could you get us a taxi? |
| قطار اصفهان از کدام سکو حرکت می‌کند؟ (*ghatār-e Esfahān az kodām sakku harekat mi-konad?*) | Which platform does the Isfahan train leave from? |
| تا میدان نقش جهان چقدر راه است؟ (*tā meydān-e Naghsh-e Jahān cheghadr rāh ast?*) | How far is it to Naqsh-e Jahan Square? |

- ببخشید، آیا اتاق خالی دارید؟  
  *bebakhshid, āyā otāgh-e khāli dārid?* — Excuse me, do you have a free room?
- برای دو نفر، دو شب، چقدر می‌شود؟  
  *barā-ye do nafar, do shab, cheghadr mi-shavad?* — How much is it for two people for two nights?  
  Spoken: برای دو نفر، دو شب، چقدر می‌شه؟ *barā-ye do nafar, do shab, cheghadr mi-she?*
- ممکن است ساعت شش به اتاق ما زنگ بزنید؟

- پرواز مشهد از کدام ترمینال است؟  
  *parvāz-e Mashhad az kodām terminal ast?* — Which terminal does the Mashhad flight leave from?
- قطار چند ساعت تأخیر دارد؟  
  *ghatār chand sā'at ta'khir dārad?* — How late is the train?  
  Literally: the train has how many hours of delay?

### Three days in Isfahan: a tour programme

_A travel agency's programme, written the way such leaflets are – in the formal future. Find every خواهد and خواهید, then try saying the same thing the everyday way._

- قطار تهران به اصفهان روز پنج‌شنبه ساعت هفت صبح حرکت خواهد کرد.  
  *ghatār-e Tehrān be Esfahān ruz-e panj-shanbe sā'at-e haft-e sobh harekat khāhad kard.* — The Tehran to Isfahan train will leave on Thursday at seven in the morning.
- مسافران حدود ساعت یک بعد از ظهر به اصفهان خواهند رسید.  
  *mosāferān hodud-e sā'at-e yek-e ba'd az zohr be Esfahān khāhand resid.* — Passengers will reach Isfahan at about one in the afternoon.
- بعد از ناهار در هتل، از میدان نقش جهان دیدن خواهید کرد.  
  *ba'd az nāhār dar hotel, az meydān-e Naghsh-e Jahān didan khāhid kard.* — After lunch at the hotel, you will visit Naqsh-e Jahan Square.  
  _از … دیدن کردن – to visit (a place), a formal compound verb_
- این میدان یکی از بزرگ‌ترین میدان‌های دنیاست.  
  *in meydān yek-i az bozorg-tarin meydān-hā-ye donyā-st.* — This square is one of the largest squares in the world.
- شب در بازار اصفهان خرید خواهید کرد و چای خواهید خورد.  
  *shab dar bāzār-e Esfahān kharid khāhid kard o chāy khāhid khord.* — In the evening you will shop in the Isfahan bazaar and drink tea.
- روز دوم به سی‌وسه‌پل و پل خواجو خواهید رفت.  
  *ruz-e dovvom be Si-o-se-pol o Pol-e Khāju khāhid raft.* — On the second day you will go to Si-o-se Pol and the Khaju Bridge.
- عصر از کلیسای وانک در محله‌ی جلفا دیدن خواهید کرد.

### Night trains, Nowruz and water behind the traveller

The راه‌آهن سراسری (*rāh-āhan-e sarāsari*, ‘Trans-Iranian Railway’), built between 1927 and 1938, climbs from the Caspian Sea over the Alborz mountains and runs all the way down to the Persian Gulf; since 2021 it has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Trains are cheap and comfortable, and the overnight train to مشهد (*Mashhad*) is a small society of its own: strangers in a کوپه (*kupe*, ‘compartment’) share tea from a flask, fruit and life stories, and by morning they have usually swapped phone numbers.

The busiest travel time of the year is نوروز (*nowruz*), the Iranian New Year in late March, when schools close for two weeks and millions of families take to the roads. Mashhad, with the shrine of Imam Reza, receives more visitors than any other city in Iran.

Setting off on a journey has its own rituals. The family holds a Qur'an up for the traveller to pass under, and after the car has gone someone pours a bowl of water on the ground behind it – آب پشت سر مسافر (*āb posht-e sar-e mosāfer*, ‘water behind the traveller’) – so that the journey goes as smoothly as water and the traveller comes back. As people leave you say سفر به خیر! (*safar be kheyr!*, ‘have a good trip!’), and when they return, رسیدن به خیر! (*residan be kheyr!*, ‘welcome back!’)

### Tips

- **Want or will?** **می‌خواهم بروم** = I want to go; **خواهم رفت** = I will go. The future has no می and takes the short past stem.
- On screens and tickets: **حرکت** = departure, **ورود** = arrival, **سکو** = platform, **تأخیر** = delay, **لغو شد** = cancelled.
- **بلیت** or **بلیط**? You will see both spellings; **بلیت** is the one the Academy of Persian Language recommends.
- Use the formal future in conversation and you will sound like a newsreader. For your own plans, say **فردا می‌روم** – or, Tehrani style, **فردا می‌رم**.
- To a traveller: **سفر به خیر!** (have a good trip) or **خوش بگذرد!** (have a great time – spoken **خوش بگذره!**).

### Will, want or go?

**Choose the right answer**

1. 'I will go' (formal) — می‌خواهم بروم / خواهم رفت / خواهم بروم
2. Which means 'I want to go'? — خواهم رفت / می‌روم / می‌خواهم بروم
3. The negative of خواهد آمد is… — نخواهد آمد / خواهد نیامد / نمی‌خواهد آمد
4. A station announcement: 'The train will depart at eight.' — قطار ساعت هشت خواهد حرکت کرد. / قطار ساعت هشت حرکت خواهد کرد. / قطار ساعت هشت حرکت کرد.
5. A friend asks when you're going to Mashhad. The most natural answer: — هفته‌ی بعد به مشهد خواهم رفت. / هفته‌ی بعد به مشهد می‌روم. / هفته‌ی بعد به مشهد رفتم.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. خواهم رفت — khāh- without mi, plus the past stem: khāham raft.
2. می‌خواهم بروم — 'Want' has mi- and is followed by the subjunctive.
3. نخواهد آمد — na- goes on khāh-: na-khāhad āmad.
4. قطار ساعت هشت حرکت خواهد کرد. — In a compound verb the future goes between the two parts: harekat khāhad kard.
5. هفته‌ی بعد به مشهد می‌روم. — In conversation, use the present for the future; khāham raft sounds like a news bulletin.

</details>
