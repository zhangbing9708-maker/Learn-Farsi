# Phrasebook

Where the two differ, every phrase is given twice: first in standard written Persian – correct, polite and understood everywhere – and then, marked 🗣, in the everyday spoken Persian of Tehran, which is what you will actually hear in shops, taxis and homes. Learn to say the spoken form and to recognise both. Persian has two words for 'you': shomā, for anyone you don't know well, anyone older than you and any official (it is also the plural), and to, for friends, family and children. Phrases marked formal use shomā – verbs end in -id, spoken -in – or the polite register; the others use to – verbs end in -i – and are for people you are close to. When in doubt, use shomā: nobody minds too much respect, and when an Iranian friend starts calling you to, you can answer in kind. Tap a phrase to hear it.

## Greetings and goodbyes — سلام و خداحافظی

Salām works at any hour and with anyone, and the person who arrives says it first – a younger person greets an older one. Questions about your health and family follow straight away; they are part of the greeting, so answer briefly and ask back. Goodbyes take time: expect several rounds of good wishes at the door.

- سلام!  
  *salām!* — Hello!  
  _Works at any time of day and with anyone – shopkeepers, drivers, neighbours, officials._
- سلام علیکم.  
  *salām aleykom.* — Peace be upon you (a respectful hello).  
  _More traditional, especially with older people. The reply is aleykom assalām._
- صبح به خیر.  
  *sobh be kheyr.* — Good morning.
- عصر به خیر.  
  *asr be kheyr.* — Good afternoon. / Good evening.  
  _From late afternoon until dark._
- شب به خیر.  
  *shab be kheyr.* — Good night.  
  _Said when you leave in the evening or go to bed – not as a greeting._
- حال شما چطور است؟  
  *hāl-e shomā chetor ast?* — How are you?  
  Spoken: حالتون چطوره؟ *hāl-etun chetor-e?*
- چطوری؟  
  *chetor-i?* — How's it going? (to a friend)
- خوبم، ممنون. شما چطورید؟  
  *khub-am, mamnun. shomā chetor-id?* — I'm fine, thanks. And you?  
  Spoken: خوبم، مرسی. شما چطورین؟ *khub-am, mersi. shomā chetor-in?*
- خیلی خوبم، خدا را شکر.  
  *kheyli khub-am, khodā rā shokr.* — Very well, thank God.  
  Spoken: خیلی خوبم، خدا رو شکر. *kheyli khub-am, khodā ro shokr.*  
  _Iranians of every background say khodā ro shokr – it is as much habit as faith._
- بد نیستم.  
  *bad nistam.* — Not bad.
- چه خبر؟  
  *che khabar?* — What's new?  
  _A casual follow-up to hello._
- خبری نیست، سلامتی.  
  *khabar-i nist, salāmati.* — Nothing much – all's well.  
  _salāmati, 'health', is the standard answer to che khabar?_
- خانواده خوب هستند؟  
  *khānevāde khub hastand?* — Is the family well?  
  Spoken: خانواده خوبن؟ *khānevāde khub-an?*
- خیلی وقت است شما را ندیده‌ام!  
  *kheyli vaght ast shomā rā na-dide-am!* — Long time no see!  
  Spoken: خیلی وقته ندیدمتون! *kheyli vaght-e na-didam-etun!*
- کم پیدایی!  
  *kam peydā-yi!* — Where have you been hiding?  
  _Literally 'you are rarely seen' – a friendly tease._
- سلام برسانید.  
  *salām be-resānid.* — Give everyone my regards.  
  Spoken: سلام برسونین. *salām be-resunin.*
- خدا حافظ.  
  *khodā hāfez.* — Goodbye.  
  Spoken: خدافظ. *khodāfez.*  
  _Literally 'God (be your) protector'._
- خدا نگهدار.  
  *khodā negahdār.* — Goodbye. (God keep you.)  
  _A warm alternative to khodā hāfez, often said in reply to it._
- فعلاً!  
  *fe'lan!* — See you! / Bye for now!  
  _Very casual, among friends._
- روز خوبی داشته باشید!  
  *ruz-e khub-i dāshte bāshid!* — Have a good day!  
  Spoken: روز خوبی داشته باشین! *ruz-e khub-i dāshte bāshin!*
- مواظب خودتان باشید.  
  *movāzeb-e khod-etān bāshid.* — Take care of yourself.  
  Spoken: مواظب خودتون باشین. *movāzeb-e khod-etun bāshin.*

## Introductions — معرفی و آشنایی

Iranians ask personal questions early – your job, your age, whether you are married, what you think of Iran. It is friendliness, not nosiness; answer as much as you like. Use shomā with everyone you meet for the first time.

- اسم شما چیست؟  
  *esm-e shomā chist?* — What's your name?  
  Spoken: اسمتون چیه؟ *esm-etun chi-ye?*
- اسمت چیست؟  
  *esm-at chist?* — What's your name? (to a child or a friend)  
  Spoken: اسمت چیه؟ *esm-et chi-ye?*
- اسم من سارا است.  
  *esm-e man Sārā ast.* — My name is Sara.  
  Spoken: اسمم ساراست. *esm-am Sārā-st.*
- از آشنایی با شما خوشوقتم.  
  *az āshnāyi bā shomā khoshvaght-am.* — Pleased to meet you.  
  Spoken: از آشنایی‌تون خوشوقتم. *az āshnāyi-tun khoshvaght-am.*  
  _Younger people often say khoshbakht-am._
- همچنین.  
  *hamchenin.* — Likewise.
- معرفی می‌کنم، دوستم علی.  
  *mo'arrefi mi-konam, dust-am Ali.* — Let me introduce my friend Ali.
- اهل کجا هستید؟  
  *ahl-e kojā hastid?* — Where are you from?  
  Spoken: اهل کجایین؟ *ahl-e kojā-yin?*
- اهل انگلستان هستم.  
  *ahl-e Engelestān hastam.* — I'm from England.  
  Spoken: اهل انگلیسم. *ahl-e Engelis-am.*  
  _In speech Engelis means England – and often the whole of Britain._
- من آمریکایی هستم.  
  *man āmrikāyi hastam.* — I'm American.  
  Spoken: من آمریکایی‌ام. *man āmrikāyi-am.*
- کجا زندگی می‌کنید؟  
  *kojā zendegi mi-konid?* — Where do you live?  
  Spoken: کجا زندگی می‌کنین؟ *kojā zendegi mi-konin?*
- در تهران زندگی می‌کنم.  
  *dar Tehrān zendegi mi-konam.* — I live in Tehran.  
  Spoken: تهران زندگی می‌کنم. *Tehrān zendegi mi-konam.*
- شغل شما چیست؟  
  *shoghl-e shomā chist?* — What do you do for a living?  
  Spoken: شغلتون چیه؟ *shoghl-etun chi-ye?*  
  _You will also hear chi-kāre-in? – 'what do you do?'_
- من معلم هستم.  
  *man mo'allem hastam.* — I'm a teacher.  
  Spoken: من معلمم. *man mo'allem-am.*
- مهندس نرم‌افزار هستم.  
  *mohandes-e narm-afzār hastam.* — I'm a software engineer.  
  Spoken: مهندس نرم‌افزارم. *mohandes-e narm-afzār-am.*
- دانشجو هستم.  
  *dāneshju hastam.* — I'm a university student.  
  Spoken: دانشجوام. *dāneshju-am.*
- برای کار این‌جا هستم.  
  *barā-ye kār in-jā hastam.* — I'm here for work.  
  Spoken: برای کار اومدم. *barā-ye kār umadam.*
- برای سفر آمده‌ام.  
  *barā-ye safar āmade-am.* — I'm here on holiday.  
  Spoken: برای سفر اومدم. *barā-ye safar umadam.*
- چند سال دارید؟  
  *chand sāl dārid?* — How old are you?  
  Spoken: چند سالتونه؟ *chand sāl-etun-e?*  
  _A normal question in Iran, though asked less often to women._
- سی و دو سال دارم.  
  *si o do sāl dāram.* — I'm thirty-two.  
  Spoken: سی و دو سالمه. *si o do sāl-am-e.*
- متأهل هستید؟  
  *mota'ahhel hastid?* — Are you married?  
  Spoken: متأهلین؟ *mota'ahhel-in?*  
  _Questions about marriage and children are ordinary small talk._
- نه، مجرد هستم.  
  *na, mojarrad hastam.* — No, I'm single.  
  Spoken: نه، مجردم. *na, mojarrad-am.*

## Courtesy and ta'ārof — ادب و تعارف

Ta'ārof is the ritual politeness that runs through Iranian life: offering what you don't expect to be accepted, refusing what you would like, insisting that the other person go first. The rule of thumb is to refuse an offer once or twice and accept the third time – and when a shopkeeper or driver says your money is 'not worthy', thank them and pay anyway.

- ممنون.  
  *mamnun.* — Thanks.  
  _The everyday thank-you, fine with anyone._
- مرسی.  
  *mersi.* — Thanks.  
  _Borrowed from French; heard everywhere in the cities, a touch casual._
- خیلی متشکرم.  
  *kheyli motshakker-am.* — Thank you very much.
- دستتان درد نکند.  
  *dast-etān dard na-konad.* — Thank you (for all your trouble).  
  Spoken: دستتون درد نکنه. *dast-etun dard na-kone.*  
  Literally: may your hand not hurt  
  _Thanks for something someone has made or done for you – a meal, a favour, a gift._
- خواهش می‌کنم.  
  *khāhesh mi-konam.* — You're welcome.  
  _Also 'please' when you ask a favour._
- قابل شما را ندارد.  
  *ghābel-e shomā rā na-dārad.* — It's nothing – please, it's yours.  
  Spoken: قابل نداره. *ghābel na-dāre.*  
  Literally: it is not worthy of you  
  _Said when giving a present – and by shopkeepers and drivers when you ask the price. It is courtesy: insist, and pay._
- لطف دارید. چقدر شد؟  
  *lotf dārid. cheghadr shod?* — That's kind of you. How much is it?  
  Spoken: لطف دارین. چقدر شد؟ *lotf dārin. cheghadr shod?*  
  _The right answer to ghābel na-dāre: thank them, then ask again._
- اختیار دارید!  
  *ekhtiyār dārid!* — Not at all! / Don't say that!  
  Spoken: اختیار دارین! *ekhtiyār dārin!*  
  _The polite protest when someone praises you or puts themselves down._
- ببخشید.  
  *be-bakhshid.* — Excuse me. / Sorry.  
  _To get attention, to apologise, or to squeeze past._
- معذرت می‌خواهم.  
  *ma'zerat mi-khāham.* — I apologise.  
  Spoken: معذرت می‌خوام. *ma'zerat mi-khām.*
- شرمنده‌ام.  
  *sharmande-am.* — I'm so sorry. (I'm embarrassed.)  
  Spoken: شرمنده. *sharmande.*  
  _Very common: for being late, for having no change, for a host's 'simple' dinner._
- اشکالی ندارد.  
  *eshkāl-i na-dārad.* — No problem. / Never mind.  
  Spoken: اشکال نداره. *eshkāl na-dāre.*
- بفرمایید.  
  *be-farmāyid.* — Please – go ahead / come in / sit down / help yourself.  
  Spoken: بفرمایین. *be-farmāyin.*  
  _The all-purpose polite invitation: when you hear it, step forward, sit, or take what is offered._
- اول شما بفرمایید.  
  *avval shomā be-farmāyid.* — After you.  
  Spoken: اول شما بفرمایین. *avval shomā be-farmāyin.*  
  _Iranians insist on going last through a door. After two rounds, go first and say be-bakhshid._
- با اجازه.  
  *bā ejāze.* — Excuse me. (With your permission.)  
  _Said when you leave a room, take the last piece, or finally accept after ta'ārof._
- تعارف نکنید.  
  *ta'ārof na-konid.* — Please don't be polite – really, take it.  
  Spoken: تعارف نکنین. *ta'ārof na-konin.*  
  _A host's way of saying the offer is sincere._
- زحمت کشیدید.  
  *zahmat keshidid.* — You went to so much trouble. (Thank you.)  
  Spoken: زحمت کشیدین. *zahmat keshidin.*
- زحمت نکشید.  
  *zahmat na-keshid.* — Please don't go to any trouble.  
  Spoken: زحمت نکشین. *zahmat na-keshin.*
- خسته نباشید!  
  *khaste na-bāshid!* — Thank you for your hard work!  
  Spoken: خسته نباشین! *khaste na-bāshin!*  
  Literally: may you not be tired  
  _Said to anyone working or just finished – a cashier, a driver at the end of the ride, a teacher after class._
- سلامت باشید.  
  *salāmat bāshid.* — Thank you.  
  Spoken: سلامت باشین. *salāmat bāshin.*  
  Literally: may you be healthy  
  _The reply to khaste na-bāshid._
- قربان شما.  
  *ghorbān-e shomā.* — Thank you so much. (very warm)  
  Spoken: قربون شما. *ghorbun-e shomā.*  
  Literally: (I'd be) your sacrifice  
  _Also a warm way to end a phone call._
- چشم.  
  *chashm.* — Certainly. / Will do.  
  Literally: (on my) eye  
  _The polite yes to a request or an instruction._

## Understanding each other — حرف زدن و فهمیدن

Iranians are delighted when a foreigner tries Persian, and most will happily slow down, repeat and teach you a new word. Don't be shy about asking – and expect plenty of people to try out their English on you in return.

- فارسی بلد نیستم.  
  *fārsi balad nistam.* — I don't speak Persian.
- کمی فارسی بلدم.  
  *kam-i fārsi balad-am.* — I speak a little Persian.  
  Spoken: یه کم فارسی بلدم. *ye kam fārsi balad-am.*
- انگلیسی بلدید؟  
  *engelisi balad-id?* — Do you speak English?  
  Spoken: انگلیسی بلدین؟ *engelisi balad-in?*
- کسی این‌جا انگلیسی بلد است؟  
  *kas-i in-jā engelisi balad ast?* — Does anyone here speak English?  
  Spoken: کسی این‌جا انگلیسی بلده؟ *kas-i in-jā engelisi balad-e?*
- متوجه نمی‌شوم.  
  *motavajjeh nemi-shavam.* — I don't understand.  
  Spoken: متوجه نمی‌شم. *motavajjeh nemi-sham.*
- نفهمیدم.  
  *na-fahmidam.* — I didn't catch that.
- متوجه شدید؟  
  *motavajjeh shodid?* — Do you understand? / Did you get that?  
  Spoken: متوجه شدین؟ *motavajjeh shodin?*
- بله، فهمیدم.  
  *bale, fahmidam.* — Yes, I understand.  
  Spoken: آره، فهمیدم. *āre, fahmidam.*
- لطفاً آهسته‌تر صحبت کنید.  
  *lotfan āheste-tar sohbat konid.* — Please speak more slowly.  
  Spoken: لطفاً یواش‌تر حرف بزنین. *lotfan yavāsh-tar harf be-zanin.*
- ممکن است تکرار کنید؟  
  *momken ast tekrār konid?* — Could you say that again?  
  Spoken: می‌شه تکرار کنین؟ *mi-she tekrār konin?*
- یک بار دیگر، لطفاً.  
  *yek bār-e digar, lotfan.* — Once more, please.  
  Spoken: یه بار دیگه، لطفاً. *ye bār-e dige, lotfan.*
- ببخشید، چه گفتید؟  
  *be-bakhshid, che goftid?* — Sorry, what did you say?  
  Spoken: ببخشید، چی گفتین؟ *be-bakhshid, chi goftin?*
- جانم؟  
  *jān-am?* — Pardon? / Yes?  
  _The warm, polite way to say 'pardon?' or to answer when someone calls you – much kinder than che? ('what?')._
- منظورتان چیست؟  
  *manzur-etān chist?* — What do you mean?  
  Spoken: منظورتون چیه؟ *manzur-etun chi-ye?*
- «دربست» یعنی چه؟  
  *«darbast» ya'ni che?* — What does 'darbast' mean?  
  Spoken: «دربست» یعنی چی؟ *«darbast» ya'ni chi?*
- این را به فارسی چه می‌گویند؟  
  *in rā be fārsi che mi-guyand?* — What's this called in Persian?  
  Spoken: اینو به فارسی چی می‌گن؟ *in-o be fārsi chi mi-gan?*
- «کتاب» به انگلیسی چه می‌شود؟  
  *«ketāb» be engelisi che mi-shavad?* — What's 'ketāb' in English?  
  Spoken: «کتاب» به انگلیسی چی می‌شه؟ *«ketāb» be engelisi chi mi-she?*
- این کلمه را چطور تلفظ می‌کنید؟  
  *in kalame rā chetor talaffoz mi-konid?* — How do you pronounce this word?  
  Spoken: این کلمه رو چطوری تلفظ می‌کنین؟ *in kalame ro chetori talaffoz mi-konin?*
- می‌شود برایم بنویسید؟  
  *mi-shavad barā-yam be-nevisid?* — Could you write it down for me?  
  Spoken: می‌شه برام بنویسین؟ *mi-she barā-m be-nevisin?*
- ببخشید، فارسی‌ام خوب نیست.  
  *be-bakhshid, fārsi-am khub nist.* — Sorry, my Persian isn't very good.  
  Spoken: ببخشید، فارسیم خوب نیست. *be-bakhshid, fārsi-m khub nist.*

## Numbers and money — عدد و پول

Prices are written in rials but almost always spoken in tomans: one toman is ten rials, so a tag that says 500,000 rials means 50,000 tomans. In speech the 'thousand' is usually dropped as well, so if a price sounds impossibly low, ask 'tomans or rials?'. Foreign bank cards do not work in Iran: visitors bring cash to change, or buy a prepaid local card.

- این چند است؟  
  *in chand ast?* — How much is this?  
  Spoken: این چنده؟ *in chand-e?*
- قیمتش چقدر است؟  
  *gheymat-ash cheghadr ast?* — What's the price?  
  Spoken: قیمتش چقدره؟ *gheymat-esh cheghadr-e?*
- چقدر شد؟  
  *cheghadr shod?* — How much does it come to?  
  _At the till, at the end of a taxi ride or a meal._
- پنجاه هزار تومان.  
  *panjāh hezār tumān.* — Fifty thousand tomans.  
  Spoken: پنجاه تومن. *panjāh tomen.*  
  _In speech the 'thousand' is usually dropped: panjāh tomen means 50,000 tomans._
- تومان یا ریال؟  
  *tumān yā riyāl?* — Tomans or rials?  
  Spoken: تومن یا ریال؟ *tomen yā riyāl?*
- یک میلیون تومان.  
  *yek milyun tumān.* — One million tomans.  
  Spoken: یه میلیون تومن. *ye milyun tomen.*
- یک، دو، سه، چهار، پنج  
  *yek, do, se, chahār, panj* — one, two, three, four, five  
  _In speech chahār is often chār._
- شش، هفت، هشت، نه، ده  
  *shesh, haft, hasht, noh, dah* — six, seven, eight, nine, ten
- بیست، سی، چهل، پنجاه، صد  
  *bist, si, chehel, panjāh, sad* — twenty, thirty, forty, fifty, a hundred
- هزار، ده هزار، صد هزار  
  *hezār, dah hezār, sad hezār* — a thousand, ten thousand, a hundred thousand
- می‌شود با کارت پرداخت کنم؟  
  *mi-shavad bā kārt pardākht konam?* — Can I pay by card?  
  Spoken: می‌شه کارت بکشم؟ *mi-she kārt be-kesham?*  
  _Spoken, literally 'can I swipe a card?'. Card machines are everywhere, but they take only Iranian cards._
- کارت‌خوان دارید؟  
  *kārt-khān dārid?* — Do you have a card machine?  
  Spoken: کارت‌خون دارین؟ *kārt-khun dārin?*
- فقط پول نقد دارم.  
  *faghat pul-e naghd dāram.* — I only have cash.
- کارت به کارت می‌کنم.  
  *kārt be kārt mi-konam.* — I'll send it by card-to-card transfer.  
  _Iranians often pay each other by instant transfer to a card number._
- پول خرد ندارم.  
  *pul-e khord na-dāram.* — I don't have any change.
- بقیه‌اش مال خودتان.  
  *baghiyye-ash māl-e khod-etān.* — Keep the change.  
  Spoken: بقیه‌ش مال خودتون. *baghiyye-sh māl-e khod-etun.*
- رسید می‌دهید؟  
  *resid mi-dahid?* — Could I have a receipt?  
  Spoken: رسید می‌دین؟ *resid mi-din?*
- صرافی کجاست؟  
  *sarrāfi kojā-st?* — Where is the exchange office?
- دلار را چند می‌خرید؟  
  *dolār rā chand mi-kharid?* — What's your rate for dollars?  
  Spoken: دلار رو چند می‌خرین؟ *dolār ro chand mi-kharin?*  
  Literally: how much do you buy the dollar for?
- می‌خواهم یورو تبدیل کنم.  
  *mi-khāham yuro tabdil konam.* — I'd like to change some euros.  
  Spoken: می‌خوام یورو تبدیل کنم. *mi-khām yuro tabdil konam.*
- خودپرداز کجاست؟  
  *khodpardāz kojā-st?* — Where is there a cash machine?  
  _Iranian ATMs only work with Iranian cards._

## Time and dates — وقت و تاریخ

Iran keeps its own solar calendar: the year begins at Nowruz, on the spring equinox around 21 March, and the twelve months have Persian names – Farvardin, Ordibehesht, Khordād and so on. Ask for the milādi (Western) date when you need it. The week starts on Saturday, and Friday is the day off.

- ساعت چند است؟  
  *sā'at chand ast?* — What time is it?  
  Spoken: ساعت چنده؟ *sā'at chand-e?*
- ساعت سه و نیم است.  
  *sā'at se o nim ast.* — It's half past three.  
  Spoken: ساعت سه و نیمه. *sā'at se o nim-e.*
- ساعت هشت و ربع است.  
  *sā'at hasht o rob' ast.* — It's a quarter past eight.  
  Spoken: ساعت هشت و ربعه. *sā'at hasht o rob'-e.*
- یک ربع به ده.  
  *yek rob' be dah.* — A quarter to ten.  
  Spoken: یه ربع به ده. *ye rob' be dah.*
- ساعت چند باز می‌کنید؟  
  *sā'at-e chand bāz mi-konid?* — What time do you open?  
  Spoken: ساعت چند باز می‌کنین؟ *sā'at-e chand bāz mi-konin?*
- تا ساعت ده شب باز هستیم.  
  *tā sā'at-e dah-e shab bāz hastim.* — We're open until ten at night.  
  Spoken: تا ساعت ده شب بازیم. *tā sā'at-e dah-e shab bāz-im.*
- کی؟  
  *key?* — When?  
  _Spelled like ki?, 'who?' – the context tells you which._
- امروز، فردا، دیروز  
  *emruz, fardā, diruz* — today, tomorrow, yesterday
- صبح، ظهر، عصر، شب  
  *sobh, zohr, asr, shab* — morning, midday, late afternoon, night  
  _zohr is lunchtime; in smaller towns many shops close from zohr until about five._
- امروز چندشنبه است؟  
  *emruz chand-shanbe ast?* — What day is it today?  
  Spoken: امروز چندشنبه‌ست؟ *emruz chand-shanbe-st?*
- امروز سه‌شنبه است.  
  *emruz se-shanbe ast.* — Today is Tuesday.  
  Spoken: امروز سه‌شنبه‌ست. *emruz se-shanbe-st.*
- شنبه، یک‌شنبه، دوشنبه، سه‌شنبه  
  *shanbe, yek-shanbe, do-shanbe, se-shanbe* — Saturday, Sunday, Monday, Tuesday  
  _The week starts on Saturday, and the next days are counted from it: 'one-shanbe', 'two-shanbe'…_
- چهارشنبه، پنج‌شنبه، جمعه  
  *chahār-shanbe, panj-shanbe, jom'e* — Wednesday, Thursday, Friday  
  _Friday (jom'e) is the weekly day off._
- امروز چندم است؟  
  *emruz chandom ast?* — What's the date today?  
  Spoken: امروز چندمه؟ *emruz chandom-e?*
- امروز پنجم مهر است.  
  *emruz panjom-e Mehr ast.* — Today is the 5th of Mehr.  
  Spoken: امروز پنجم مهره. *emruz panjom-e Mehr-e.*  
  _Mehr, the seventh month, begins around 23 September._
- به میلادی چندم است؟  
  *be milādi chandom ast?* — What's the date in the Western calendar?  
  Spoken: به میلادی چندمه؟ *be milādi chandom-e?*
- چقدر طول می‌کشد؟  
  *cheghadr tul mi-keshad?* — How long does it take?  
  Spoken: چقدر طول می‌کشه؟ *cheghadr tul mi-keshe?*
- حدود نیم ساعت.  
  *hodud-e nim sā'at.* — About half an hour.
- پنج دقیقه‌ی دیگر می‌آیم.  
  *panj daghighe-ye digar mi-āyam.* — I'll be there in five minutes.  
  Spoken: پنج دقیقه دیگه میام. *panj daghighe dige miyām.*
- ببخشید، دیر کردم.  
  *be-bakhshid, dir kardam.* — Sorry I'm late.
- هفته‌ی بعد، ماه بعد، سال بعد  
  *hafte-ye ba'd, māh-e ba'd, sāl-e ba'd* — next week, next month, next year

## Asking the way — راه و نشانی

Iranians give directions generously – sometimes even when they are not sure – so it is worth asking twice. Addresses run from big to small: the main street, then the side street (kuche), then the alley, then the number; squares (meydān) and well-known landmarks matter more than house numbers.

- ببخشید، نزدیک‌ترین ایستگاه مترو کجاست؟  
  *be-bakhshid, nazdik-tarin istgāh-e metro kojā-st?* — Excuse me, where's the nearest metro station?
- چطور به بازار بروم؟  
  *chetor be bāzār be-ravam?* — How do I get to the bazaar?  
  Spoken: چطوری برم بازار؟ *chetori be-ram bāzār?*
- میدان آزادی از کدام طرف است؟  
  *meydān-e Āzādi az kodām taraf ast?* — Which way is Azadi Square?  
  Spoken: میدون آزادی از کدوم طرفه؟ *meydun-e Āzādi az kodum taraf-e?*
- می‌توانید روی نقشه نشانم بدهید؟  
  *mi-tavānid ru-ye naghshe neshān-am be-dahid?* — Can you show me on the map?  
  Spoken: می‌تونین رو نقشه نشونم بدین؟ *mi-tunin ru naghshe neshun-am be-din?*
- آدرس این را بلدید؟  
  *ādres-e in rā balad-id?* — Do you know this address?  
  Spoken: آدرس اینو بلدین؟ *ādres-e in-o balad-in?*  
  _Show the address written in Persian – street names in Latin letters mean little to most people._
- دور است؟  
  *dur ast?* — Is it far?  
  Spoken: دوره؟ *dur-e?*
- نه، نزدیک است. پنج دقیقه پیاده.  
  *na, nazdik ast. panj daghighe piyāde.* — No, it's close – five minutes on foot.  
  Spoken: نه، نزدیکه. پنج دقیقه پیاده. *na, nazdik-e. panj daghighe piyāde.*
- از این‌جا چقدر راه است؟  
  *az in-jā cheghadr rāh ast?* — How far is it from here?  
  Spoken: از این‌جا چقدر راهه؟ *az in-jā cheghadr rāh-e?*
- پیاده می‌شود رفت؟  
  *piyāde mi-shavad raft?* — Can I walk there?  
  Spoken: پیاده می‌شه رفت؟ *piyāde mi-she raft?*
- مستقیم بروید.  
  *mostaghim be-ravid.* — Go straight on.  
  Spoken: مستقیم برین. *mostaghim be-rin.*
- سر چهارراه به راست بپیچید.  
  *sar-e chahār-rāh be rāst be-pichid.* — Turn right at the crossroads.  
  Spoken: سر چهارراه بپیچین راست. *sar-e chahār-rāh be-pichin rāst.*
- دست چپ، دست راست  
  *dast-e chap, dast-e rāst* — on the left, on the right
- همین خیابان را بگیرید و بروید.  
  *hamin khiyābān rā be-girid o be-ravid.* — Just keep following this street.  
  Spoken: همین خیابون رو بگیرین برین. *hamin khiyābun ro be-girin be-rin.*
- بعد از پل، اولین کوچه.  
  *ba'd az pol, avvalin kuche.* — After the bridge, the first side street.
- ته کوچه، دست راست.  
  *tah-e kuche, dast-e rāst.* — At the end of the alley, on the right.
- روبه‌روی بانک است.  
  *ru-be-ru-ye bānk ast.* — It's opposite the bank.  
  Spoken: روبه‌روی بانکه. *ru-be-ru-ye bānk-e.*
- پشت این ساختمان است.  
  *posht-e in sākhtemān ast.* — It's behind this building.  
  Spoken: پشت این ساختمونه. *posht-e in sākhtemun-e.*
- گم شده‌ام.  
  *gom shode-am.* — I'm lost.  
  Spoken: گم شدم. *gom shodam.*
- دستشویی کجاست؟  
  *dast-shuyi kojā-st?* — Where's the toilet?  
  _Public toilets are often the squat kind; carry tissues._
- شمال شهر یا جنوب شهر؟  
  *shomāl-e shahr yā jonub-e shahr?* — North or south of the city?  
  _In Tehran, north also means uphill, towards the mountains._

## Taxis and the metro — تاکسی و مترو

Most people in Tehran get around by ride-hailing app – Snapp or Tapsi – or by metro. Street taxis work in two ways: shared taxis run along fixed routes and pick up passengers on the way, while darbast means you hire the whole car. With a street taxi, agree the fare before you get in.

- دربست!  
  *darbast!* — Private hire! (I'll take the whole car.)  
  _Call it to a passing taxi; otherwise the driver may treat you as a shared-taxi passenger._
- دربست تا میدان ونک چقدر می‌شود؟  
  *darbast tā meydān-e Vanak cheghadr mi-shavad?* — How much is a private ride to Vanak Square?  
  Spoken: دربست تا میدون ونک چقدر می‌شه؟ *darbast tā meydun-e Vanak cheghadr mi-she?*
- آقا، تجریش می‌روید؟  
  *āghā, Tajrish mi-ravid?* — Are you going to Tajrish? (to a shared-taxi driver)  
  Spoken: آقا، تجریش می‌رین؟ *āghā, Tajrish mi-rin?*
- کرایه چقدر است؟  
  *kerāye cheghadr ast?* — How much is the fare?  
  Spoken: کرایه چقدره؟ *kerāye cheghadr-e?*
- یک اسنپ می‌گیرم.  
  *yek Esnap mi-giram.* — I'll get a Snapp.  
  Spoken: یه اسنپ می‌گیرم. *ye Esnap mi-giram.*  
  _Snapp and Tapsi are Iran's ride-hailing apps; the name is used like 'an Uber'._
- ببخشید، اسنپ هستید؟  
  *be-bakhshid, Esnap hastid?* — Excuse me, are you my Snapp?  
  Spoken: ببخشید، اسنپین؟ *be-bakhshid, Esnap-in?*
- من دم در منتظرم.  
  *man dam-e dar montazer-am.* — I'm waiting at the door.
- عجله دارم.  
  *ajale dāram.* — I'm in a hurry.
- لطفاً این‌جا نگه دارید.  
  *lotfan in-jā negah dārid.* — Please stop here.  
  Spoken: لطفاً این‌جا نگه دارین. *lotfan in-jā negah dārin.*
- سر کوچه پیاده می‌شوم.  
  *sar-e kuche piyāde mi-shavam.* — I'll get out at the corner.  
  Spoken: سر کوچه پیاده می‌شم. *sar-e kuche piyāde mi-sham.*
- بلیت مترو از کجا بخرم؟  
  *belit-e metro az kojā be-kharam?* — Where do I buy a metro ticket?
- یک کارت بلیت می‌خواهم.  
  *yek kārt-e belit mi-khāham.* — I'd like a travel card.  
  Spoken: یه کارت بلیت می‌خوام. *ye kārt-e belit mi-khām.*
- می‌خواهم کارتم را شارژ کنم.  
  *mi-khāham kārt-am rā shārzh konam.* — I'd like to top up my card.  
  Spoken: می‌خوام کارتمو شارژ کنم. *mi-khām kārt-am-o shārzh konam.*
- برای رفتن به تجریش کدام خط را سوار شوم؟  
  *barā-ye raftan be Tajrish kodām khat rā savār shavam?* — Which line do I take for Tajrish?  
  Spoken: برای تجریش کدوم خط رو سوار شم؟ *barā-ye Tajrish kodum khat ro savār sham?*
- کجا باید عوض کنم؟  
  *kojā bāyad avaz konam?* — Where do I have to change?
- ایستگاه بعدی کجاست؟  
  *istgāh-e ba'di kojā-st?* — What's the next station?
- این قطار به تئاتر شهر می‌رود؟  
  *in ghatār be Te'ātr-e Shahr mi-ravad?* — Does this train go to Teatr-e Shahr?  
  Spoken: این قطار می‌ره تئاتر شهر؟ *in ghatār mi-re Te'ātr-e Shahr?*
- واگن بانوان  
  *vāgon-e bānovān* — women's carriage  
  _On Tehran metro trains the carriages at each end are reserved for women; women may also ride in the others._
- این صندلی خالی است؟  
  *in sandali khāli ast?* — Is this seat free?  
  Spoken: این صندلی خالیه؟ *in sandali khāli-ye?*
- اتوبوس فرودگاه از کجا حرکت می‌کند؟  
  *otobus-e forudgāh az kojā harekat mi-konad?* — Where does the airport bus leave from?  
  Spoken: اتوبوس فرودگاه از کجا حرکت می‌کنه؟ *otobus-e forudgāh az kojā harekat mi-kone?*

## At the hotel — در هتل

Hotels ask for your passport at check-in. In the old cities – Yazd, Kashan, Isfahan – look for a traditional-house hotel, a restored courtyard home; on a budget, ask for a mosāferkhāne, a simple guest house.

- یک اتاق رزرو کرده‌ام.  
  *yek otāgh rezerv karde-am.* — I've booked a room.  
  Spoken: یه اتاق رزرو کردم. *ye otāgh rezerv kardam.*
- به اسم اسمیت.  
  *be esm-e Esmit.* — In the name of Smith.
- اتاق خالی دارید؟  
  *otāgh-e khāli dārid?* — Do you have a room free?  
  Spoken: اتاق خالی دارین؟ *otāgh-e khāli dārin?*
- یک اتاق دو تخته برای سه شب می‌خواهم.  
  *yek otāgh-e do takhte barā-ye se shab mi-khāham.* — I'd like a room for two for three nights.  
  Spoken: یه اتاق دو تخته برای سه شب می‌خوام. *ye otāgh-e do takhte barā-ye se shab mi-khām.*  
  _A single room is yek takhte._
- شبی چند است؟  
  *shab-i chand ast?* — How much is it per night?  
  Spoken: شبی چنده؟ *shab-i chand-e?*
- صبحانه هم دارد؟  
  *sobhāne ham dārad?* — Is breakfast included?  
  Spoken: صبحونه هم داره؟ *sobhune ham dāre?*
- صبحانه از ساعت چند است؟  
  *sobhāne az sā'at-e chand ast?* — What time does breakfast start?  
  Spoken: صبحونه از ساعت چنده؟ *sobhune az sā'at-e chand-e?*
- پاسپورتتان را لطف می‌کنید؟  
  *pāsport-etān rā lotf mi-konid?* — May I have your passport, please?  
  Spoken: پاسپورتتونو لطف می‌کنین؟ *pāsport-etun-o lotf mi-konin?*  
  _lotf mi-konid? – 'will you do the kindness?' – is the polite way to ask for something._
- رمز وای‌فای چیست؟  
  *ramz-e vāy-fāy chist?* — What's the Wi-Fi password?  
  Spoken: رمز وای‌فای چیه؟ *ramz-e vāy-fāy chi-ye?*
- کولر اتاق کار نمی‌کند.  
  *kuler-e otāgh kār nemi-konad.* — The air conditioning in my room doesn't work.  
  Spoken: کولر اتاق کار نمی‌کنه. *kuler-e otāgh kār nemi-kone.*
- آب گرم نمی‌آید.  
  *āb-e garm nemi-āyad.* — There's no hot water.  
  Spoken: آب گرم نمیاد. *āb-e garm nemiyād.*
- می‌شود حوله‌ی تمیز بیاورید؟  
  *mi-shavad hole-ye tamiz biyāvarid?* — Could you bring a clean towel?  
  Spoken: می‌شه حوله‌ی تمیز بیارین؟ *mi-she hole-ye tamiz biyārin?*
- اتاق ساکت‌تری دارید؟  
  *otāgh-e sāket-tar-i dārid?* — Do you have a quieter room?  
  Spoken: اتاق ساکت‌تری دارین؟ *otāgh-e sāket-tar-i dārin?*
- کلید اتاق سیصد و دوازده، لطفاً.  
  *kelid-e otāgh-e sisad o davāzdah, lotfan.* — The key to room 312, please.
- ساعت تحویل اتاق چند است؟  
  *sā'at-e tahvil-e otāgh chand ast?* — What time is check-out?  
  Spoken: ساعت تحویل اتاق چنده؟ *sā'at-e tahvil-e otāgh chand-e?*
- می‌توانم چمدانم را این‌جا بگذارم؟  
  *mi-tavānam chamedān-am rā in-jā be-gozāram?* — Can I leave my suitcase here?  
  Spoken: می‌تونم چمدونمو این‌جا بذارم؟ *mi-tunam chamedun-am-o in-jā be-zāram?*
- لطفاً یک تاکسی برایم بگیرید.  
  *lotfan yek tāksi barā-yam be-girid.* — Please call me a taxi.  
  Spoken: لطفاً یه تاکسی برام بگیرین. *lotfan ye tāksi barā-m be-girin.*
- این نزدیکی مسافرخانه‌ی ارزان هست؟  
  *in nazdiki mosāfer-khāne-ye arzān hast?* — Is there a cheap guest house near here?  
  Spoken: این نزدیکی مسافرخونه‌ی ارزون هست؟ *in nazdiki mosāfer-khune-ye arzun hast?*
- همه چیز عالی بود، ممنون.  
  *hame chiz āli bud, mamnun.* — Everything was great, thank you.
