عددها و تقویمNumbers and the calendar

adad-hā va taghvim

Iran writes its own digits and lives by its own calendar, in which the year begins with spring and 2026 is mostly 1405. Persian digits, the solar hijri calendar and its twelve months, Nowruz, and how to say dates, times and days of the week.

Facts

  • The Iranian year 1405 runs from 21 March 2026 to 20 March 2027.
  • The Jalali calendar of 1079, the ancestor of today's calendar, was worked out for the Seljuk sultan Malik-Shah by astronomers including Omar Khayyam.
  • In 1976 the Shah replaced the hijri year count with an 'imperial' one counting from Cyrus the Great, turning 1355 into 2535; it was dropped in 1978.
  • The school year begins on 1 Mehr, around 23 September.
  • Because the first six months have 31 days, the Iranian summer months are long and Esfand is the shortest month of the year.
  • The official Nowruz holiday is 1–4 Farvardin; 12 and 13 Farvardin are public holidays too.
  • Persian writes ۴, ۵ and ۶ differently from Arabic, so the two sets of digits are not quite the same.

Persian digits

Persian has its own digits: ۰ ۱ ۲ ۳ ۴ ۵ ۶ ۷ ۸ ۹. They are cousins of the numerals used in the West – both families came from India by way of the Arab world. Watch out for ۵ panj five, which looks like an upside-down heart, and ۰ sefr zero, a simple dot. Although Persian is written from right to left, numbers run from left to right: ۱۴۰۵ hezār o chahārsad o panj 1405. Western digits are common on phones and websites, but banknotes and car number plates use Persian ones.

Counting is regular: یک yek, دو do, سه se, چهار chahār, پنج panj, شش shesh, هفت haft, هشت hasht, نه noh, ده dah. Bigger numbers are joined with o, 'and': بیست و پنج bist o panj 25. In everyday speech things are counted with تا tā: سه تا چای se tā chāy three teas.

The solar hijri calendar

Iran uses the هجری شمسی hejri-ye shamsi solar hijri calendar. Like the Islamic calendar, it counts the years from the Prophet's migration to Medina in 622 – but its years are solar, so they keep step with the seasons. The year 1405 began in March 2026.

The calendar descends from the Jalali calendar, reformed in 1079 by a team of astronomers that included Omar Khayyam, and it is one of the most accurate in the world: the year begins at the exact moment of the spring equinox. Its modern form, with Persian month names, was adopted by parliament in 1925. Iranian calendars also show the Islamic lunar date, which sets religious holidays such as Ramadan and Muharram, and the Western date, called میلادی milādi.

Twelve months

The months carry old Zoroastrian names: مهر Mehr is Mithra, آذر Āzar is fire and آبان Ābān the waters. The first six months have 31 days, the next five 30, and the last 29, or 30 in a leap year. Each season has three:

  • Spring, from about 21 March: فروردین Farvardin, اردیبهشت Ordibehesht, خرداد Khordād
  • Summer, from about 22 June: تیر Tir, مرداد Mordād, شهریور Shahrivar
  • Autumn, from about 23 September: مهر Mehr, آبان Ābān, آذر Āzar
  • Winter, from about 22 December: دی Dey, بهمن Bahman, اسفند Esfand

The year begins with نوروز nowruz new day on 1 Farvardin. Because it starts at the equinox itself, the moment of تحویل سال tahvil-e sāl the turn of the year can fall at any hour, and families gather round the haft-sin to wait for it.

Dates and times

Persian dates go day, month, year, with an ordinal number ending in -om: بیست و ششم مهر bist o sheshom-e Mehr 26 Mehr. The first of the month is اول avval: اول فروردین avval-e Farvardin 1 Farvardin. Written in figures, dates usually run year/month/day: ۱۴۰۵/۰۷/۲۶.

To ask the time, say ساعت چند است؟ sā'at chand ast? what time is it? – in speech sā'at chande? Answers: سه و نیم se o nim half past three, سه و ربع se o rob' quarter past three, یک ربع به چهار yek rob' be chahār quarter to four. The day runs from صبح sobh morning through ظهر zohr noon and عصر asr late afternoon to شب shab night.

The week and the weekend

The week starts on شنبه shanbe Saturday, and the next days are simply counted from it: یک‌شنبه yek-shanbe Sunday, دوشنبه do-shanbe Monday, سه‌شنبه se-shanbe Tuesday, چهارشنبه chahār-shanbe Wednesday, پنج‌شنبه panj-shanbe Thursday. Friday, جمعه jom'e, is the day of rest and of Friday prayers. Many offices and schools also close on Thursday or work only half the day, so for many people the weekend is Thursday and Friday; making Saturday a day off instead has been debated in recent years.

امروز چندم است؟

emruz chandom ast?

What's the date today?

Spoken امروز چندمه؟ emruz chandom-e?

امروز بیست و ششم مهر است.

emruz bist o sheshom-e Mehr ast.

Today is 26 Mehr.

Spoken امروز بیست و ششم مهره. emruz bist o sheshom-e Mehr-e.

سال نو در اول فروردین شروع می‌شود.

sāl-e now dar avval-e Farvardin shoru' mi-shavad.

The new year begins on 1 Farvardin.

Spoken سال نو اول فروردین شروع می‌شه. sāl-e now avval-e Farvardin shoru' mi-she.

مدرسه‌ها اول مهر باز می‌شوند.

madrese-hā avval-e Mehr bāz mi-shavand.

Schools open on 1 Mehr.

Spoken مدرسه‌ها اول مهر باز می‌شن. madrese-hā avval-e Mehr bāz mi-shan.

ساعت چند است؟

sā'at chand ast?

What time is it?

Spoken ساعت چنده؟ sā'at chand-e?

ساعت سه و نیم است.

sā'at se o nim ast.

It's half past three.

Spoken ساعت سه و نیمه. sā'at se o nim-e.

کلاس ساعت یک ربع به نه شروع می‌شود.

kelās sā'at-e yek rob' be noh shoru' mi-shavad.

The class starts at a quarter to nine.

Spoken کلاس ساعت یه ربع به نه شروع می‌شه. kelās sā'at-e ye rob' be noh shoru' mi-she.

جمعه‌ها تعطیل است.

jom'e-hā ta'til ast.

Fridays are a day off.

Spoken جمعه‌ها تعطیله. jom'e-hā ta'til-e.

پنج‌شنبه عصر وقت دارید؟

panj-shanbe asr vaght dārid?

Are you free on Thursday afternoon?

Spoken پنج‌شنبه عصر وقت دارین؟ panj-shanbe asr vaght dārin?

من متولد ۱۳۷۵ هستم.

man motevalled-e hezār-o-sisad-o-haftād-o-panj hastam.

I was born in 1375 (1996–97).

تولدت کی است؟

tavallod-at key ast?

When is your birthday?

Spoken تولدت کیه؟ tavallod-et key-e?

تولد من پانزدهم اسفند است.

tavallod-e man pānzdahom-e Esfand ast.

My birthday is on 15 Esfand.

Spoken تولد من پونزدهم اسفنده. tavallod-e man punzdahom-e Esfand-e.
