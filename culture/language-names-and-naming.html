نام‌ها و نام‌گذاریNames and naming

nām-hā va nām-gozāri

Rostam and Sohrab, Mohammad-Reza and Fatemeh, Tehrani and Najjar: Iranian names come from the Shahnameh, from religion and from family history. Where first names and surnames come from, and how to address people politely.

Facts

  • A law of 1925 made family names compulsory in Iran; Reza Khan, then prime minister, chose the surname Pahlavi.
  • Iranian women keep their own surname when they marry.
  • The surname ending -pur, 'son', goes back to Old Persian puça; -zāde means 'born of'.
  • Descendants of the Prophet are called seyyed, and many names and surnames show it, such as Mir-Hoseyni.
  • Doctors are addressed as āghā-ye doktor or khānom-e doktor, often without a surname at all.
  • Siyavash, the innocent prince of the Shahnameh who rode through fire to prove his innocence, is a popular boy's name.
  • Iran's civil registry keeps lists of approved first names and can refuse a name it considers unsuitable.

Names from the Book of Kings

Iranian first names have three great sources: the Shahnameh, religion, and the poetry of nature. Ferdowsi's Book of Kings supplies heroes and heroines: رستم Rostam, سهراب Sohrāb, سیاوش Siyāvash, بیژن Bizhan, فریدون Fereydun and کاوه Kāve for boys; تهمینه Tahmine, منیژه Manizhe and رودابه Rudābe for girls. Ancient kings are popular too – کوروش Kurosh Cyrus and داریوش Dāriush Darius – and so are names with Zoroastrian roots such as آناهیتا Ānāhitā and بهرام Bahrām.

Religious names and flower names

The most common names are religious: محمد Mohammad, علی Ali, حسین Hoseyn, حسن Hasan, رضا Rezā and مهدی Mahdi for boys; فاطمه Fāteme, زهرا Zahrā, زینب Zeynab and مریم Maryam for girls. They are often combined – محمدرضا Mohammad-Rezā, امیرحسین Amir-Hoseyn – or built with غلام gholām servant, as in غلامحسین Gholām-Hoseyn servant of Hoseyn, and with Arabic abd 'servant', as in عبدالله Abdollāh servant of God.

Girls are also named after flowers and the sky: نسرین Nasrin wild rose, یاسمن Yāsaman jasmine, لاله Lāle tulip, ستاره Setāre star, مهتاب Mahtāb moonlight.

Surnames since 1925

Until the 20th century most Iranians had no family name. People were known as 'Hasan son of Ali', by a title, or by their home town. In 1925 a civil registration law required every family to choose a surname for its شناسنامه shenāsnāme ID booklet. The prime minister, Reza Khan, chose پهلوی Pahlavi, and before the year was out he was shah.

Families chose in a few typical ways. Many took their town: تهرانی Tehrāni, شیرازی Shirāzi, کرمانی Kermāni. Others took a trade: نجار Najjār carpenter, آهنگر Āhangar blacksmith, زرگر Zargar goldsmith, عطار Attār apothecary. Many added -i to an ancestor's name – احمدی Ahmadi, حسینی Hoseyni – or -zāde 'born of' and -pur 'son of': حسن‌زاده Hasan-zāde, علی‌پور Ali-pur. Others picked an epithet they liked: دلاور Delāvar brave, شادمان Shādmān cheerful.

Āghā and khānom

Titles matter. آقا āghā means 'Mr' and 'sir', خانم khānom 'Mrs, Ms' and 'madam'. Before a surname they take the ezafe: آقای احمدی āghā-ye Ahmadi Mr Ahmadi, خانم احمدی khānom-e Ahmadi Ms Ahmadi. After a first name they sound warm but still respectful: علی آقا Ali āghā, مریم خانم Maryam khānom. Professional titles are used freely: دکتر doktor for doctors and PhDs, مهندس mohandes engineer for anyone with a technical degree, استاد ostād master, professor for teachers and artists, and حاج آقا hāj āghā for an older man who has made the pilgrimage to Mecca – or simply for a respected elder.

Nicknames, and what to call people

Among family and friends, names shrink and soften. Tehranis turn Mohammad into ممد Mamad, Fatemeh into فاطی Fāti and Maryam into مری Mari, and almost any name can take جان jān dear, in speech jun: سارا جون Sārā jun. Family words are used for strangers too: a child calls any older man عمو amu uncle and any older woman خاله khāle aunt.

As a learner, say شما shomā you (polite) and use āghā or khānom with the surname until you are invited to use first names. In a shop or a taxi, a simple آقا، ببخشید āghā, bebakhshid excuse me, sir or خانم، ببخشید khānom, bebakhshid excuse me, madam is perfect.

اسم شما چیست؟

esm-e shomā chist?

What is your name?

Spoken اسمتون چیه؟ esm-etun chiye?

اسم من سهراب است، مثل پسر رستم در شاهنامه.

esm-e man Sohrāb ast, mesl-e pesar-e Rostam dar Shāhnāme.

My name is Sohrab, like Rostam's son in the Shahnameh.

Spoken اسمم سهرابه، مثل پسر رستم تو شاهنامه. esm-am Sohrāb-e, mesl-e pesar-e Rostam tu Shāhnāme.

نام خانوادگی شما چیست؟

nām-e khānevādegi-ye shomā chist?

What is your surname?

Spoken فامیلیتون چیه؟ fāmili-tun chiye?

پدربزرگم نام خانوادگی‌اش را از شهرش انتخاب کرد.

pedar-bozorg-am nām-e khānevādegi-ash rā az shahr-ash entekhāb kard.

My grandfather chose his surname from his home town.

Spoken پدربزرگم فامیلیشو از شهرش انتخاب کرد. pedar-bozorg-am fāmili-sh-o az shahr-esh entekhāb kard.

«زاده» یعنی «فرزند»؛ حسن‌زاده یعنی فرزند حسن.

«zāde» ya'ni «farzand»; Hasan-zāde ya'ni farzand-e Hasan.

Zāde means 'child'; Hasanzadeh means 'child of Hasan'.

آقای احمدی، خوش آمدید.

āghā-ye Ahmadi, khosh āmadid.

Welcome, Mr Ahmadi.

Spoken آقای احمدی، خوش اومدین. āghā-ye Ahmadi, khosh umadin.

مریم خانم، حالتان چطور است؟

Maryam khānom, hāl-etān chetowr ast?

How are you, Maryam khanom?

Spoken مریم خانم، حالتون چطوره؟ Maryam khānom, hāl-etun chetor-e?

دوستانش به او «ممد» می‌گویند.

dustān-ash be u «Mamad» mi-guyand.

His friends call him Mamad.

Spoken دوستاش بهش می‌گن «ممد». dust-ā-sh behesh mi-gan «Mamad».

لطفاً مرا به اسم کوچکم صدا بزنید.

lotfan ma-rā be esm-e kuchak-am sedā be-zanid.

Please call me by my first name.

Spoken لطفاً منو با اسم کوچیکم صدا کنین. lotfan man-o bā esm-e kuchik-am sedā konin.

بچه‌ها به همه‌ی مردهای بزرگ‌تر «عمو» می‌گویند.

bacche-hā be hame-ye mard-hā-ye bozorg-tar «amu» mi-guyand.

Children call all older men 'uncle'.

Spoken بچه‌ها به همه‌ی مردای بزرگ‌تر می‌گن «عمو». bacche-hā be hame-ye mard-ā-ye bozorg-tar mi-gan «amu».

اسم «کوروش» از تاریخ ایران باستان می‌آید.

esm-e «Kurosh» az tārikh-e Irān-e bāstān mi-āyad.

The name Kourosh comes from the history of ancient Iran.

Spoken اسم «کوروش» از تاریخ ایران باستان میاد. esm-e «Kurosh» az tārikh-e Irān-e bāstān mi-yād.

ببخشید آقا، این اتوبوس به میدان آزادی می‌رود؟

bebakhshid āghā, in otobus be meydān-e Āzādi mi-ravad?

Excuse me, sir, does this bus go to Azadi Square?

Spoken ببخشید آقا، این اتوبوس می‌ره میدون آزادی؟ bebakhshid āghā, in otobus mi-re meydun-e Āzādi?
