Culture / Festivals

شب یلداYalda, the longest night

shab-e yaldā

On the winter solstice Iranian families sit up together through the longest night of the year, with pomegranates and watermelon on the table and a fortune read from the poems of Hafez.

Facts

  • Yalda is the night between 30 Azar and 1 Dey, around 20–21 December – the winter solstice.
  • Its other name, Shab-e Chelle, marks the start of the 'great chelle', the forty coldest days of winter.
  • Yalda is a Syriac word meaning 'birth'.
  • UNESCO inscribed 'Yalda/Chella' on its intangible heritage list in 2022, on a joint nomination by Iran and Afghanistan.
  • Pomegranates and watermelon are eaten for their red colour, the colour of dawn and of life.
  • For a Hafez fortune the Divan is opened at random; the ghazal on the page is read as the answer to your wish.

The longest night

شب یلدا shab-e yaldā Yalda night is the night between the last day of autumn and the first day of winter – from the evening of 30 آذر Āzar to the morning of 1 دی Dey, around 20–21 December. It is the longest night of the year, and from the next day the light begins to win again. Its other name, شب چله shab-e chelle the night of the forty, comes from the چله‌ی بزرگ chelle-ye bozorg great forty, the forty coldest days of winter, which begin that night.

Where it comes from

The word یلدا yaldā is Syriac and means 'birth'; Syriac-speaking Christians brought it to Iran, where it came to stand for the rebirth of the sun. In old Iranian belief the long darkness belonged to the forces of evil, so people stayed awake together until dawn, keeping fires and lamps burning. Many Iranians today also connect the night with the birth of مهر Mehr Mithra, the ancient god of light, although historians debate how old that link really is. In 2022 UNESCO added 'Yalda/Chella' to its intangible heritage list, on a joint nomination from Iran and Afghanistan.

Red fruit for a dark night

Families gather in the evening at the home of the eldest – often the grandparents – and sit up past midnight. The table is red, the colour of dawn and of life: انار anār pomegranates, seeded into bowls and sprinkled with salt or گلپر golpar ground hogweed seed, and هندوانه hendevāne watermelon, because whoever eats watermelon on Yalda night, people say, will not catch cold all winter. Around them go آجیل ājil nuts and dried fruit, خرمالو khormālu persimmons, sweets and pots of tea. In the old days everyone sat with their legs under the کرسی korsi a low table over a brazier, covered with a quilt.

Hafez at midnight

The high point of the evening is the فال حافظ fāl-e Hāfez Hafez fortune. Each person makes a silent wish, a نیت niyyat intention; then the eldest opens the دیوان divān collected poems of Hafez at random and reads the ghazal on the page aloud. Its verses are taken as the poet's answer – and since Hafez can be read in many ways, everyone usually finds what they hoped for. Before opening the book, many people address the poet: 'O Hafez of Shiraz, you who know every secret.' Some families also read from the Shahnameh, and the grandparents tell old stories.

Gifts and good wishes

Yalda is also a night for the newly engaged: the groom's family often sends the bride a decorated خوانچه khānche tray of fruit, sweets and presents, sometimes with a watermelon carved like a flower basket. For a visitor the rules are easy: bring pomegranates, sweets or nuts, expect to stay late, and be ready to have your own fortune read. The greeting is شب یلدا مبارک shab-e yaldā mobārak happy Yalda night.

شب یلدا مبارک!

shab-e yaldā mobārak!

Happy Yalda night!

یلدایتان مبارک!

yaldā-yetān mobārak!

Happy Yalda to you all!

Spoken یلداتون مبارک! yaldā-tun mobārak!

امشب طولانی‌ترین شب سال است.

emshab tulāni-tarin shab-e sāl ast.

Tonight is the longest night of the year.

همه در خانه‌ی مادربزرگ جمع می‌شویم.

hame dar khāne-ye mādar-bozorg jam' mi-shavim.

We all gather at Grandma's house.

تا نیمه‌شب بیدار می‌مانیم و قصه می‌گوییم.

tā nime-shab bidār mi-mānim va ghesse mi-guyim.

We stay up till midnight telling stories.

انار را دانه کردم؛ بفرمایید!

anār rā dāne kardam; befarmāyid!

I've seeded the pomegranate – help yourselves!

می‌گویند هر کس شب یلدا هندوانه بخورد، در زمستان سرما نمی‌خورد.

mi-guyand har kas shab-e yaldā hendevāne bo-khorad, dar zemestān sarmā nemi-khorad.

They say whoever eats watermelon on Yalda night won't catch a cold all winter.

یک فال حافظ برایم بگیر.

yek fāl-e Hāfez barā-yam be-gir.

Read me a fortune from Hafez.

Spoken یه فال حافظ برام بگیر. ye fāl-e Hāfez barā-m be-gir.

اول نیت کن، بعد کتاب را باز کن.

avval niyyat kon, ba'd ketāb rā bāz kon.

First make a wish, then open the book.

ای حافظ شیرازی، تو محرم هر رازی!

ey Hāfez-e Shirāzi, to mahram-e har rāz-i!

O Hafez of Shiraz, you who know every secret!

Said to the poet before opening the Divan for a fortune.

از فردا روزها بلندتر می‌شوند.

az fardā ruz-hā boland-tar mi-shavand.

From tomorrow the days get longer.

داماد برای عروس خوانچه‌ی شب یلدا فرستاد.

dāmād barā-ye arus khānche-ye shab-e yaldā ferestād.

The groom sent his bride a Yalda gift tray.
