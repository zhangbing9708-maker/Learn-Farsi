محرم، عاشورا و نذریMuharram, Ashura and nazri

Moharram, āshurā va nazri

Muharram is the month in which Shia Iranians mourn Imam Hussein, killed at Karbala in AD 680. It is a time of black banners, processions, passion plays – and of nazri, food cooked in great pots and shared with everyone.

Facts

  • Muharram is the first month of the Islamic lunar calendar, so it begins about eleven days earlier each year.
  • Imam Hussein, the Prophet Muhammad's grandson, was killed at Karbala on 10 Muharram 61 AH – AD 680.
  • Tasua and Ashura, the 9th and 10th of Muharram, are public holidays in Iran.
  • In ta'zieh, Hussein's side traditionally wears green and sings, while the enemy side wears red and declaims.
  • UNESCO inscribed the ritual dramatic art of ta'zieh on its intangible heritage list in 2010.
  • Arbaeen, forty days after Ashura, draws huge crowds of pilgrims to Karbala, many of them Iranians who walk the last stretch from Najaf.
  • Nazri is food given away in fulfilment of a vow; it is shared with anyone who comes, neighbour or stranger.

Karbala

محرم Moharram Muharram is the first month of the Islamic lunar calendar, and for Shia Muslims – the great majority of Iranians – it is the month of mourning. On its tenth day, in AD 680, امام حسین Emām Hoseyn Imam Hussein, grandson of the Prophet Muhammad, was killed with his family and companions at کربلا Karbalā Karbala, in today's Iraq, after refusing to pledge allegiance to the caliph Yazid. For Shia Muslims his stand is the great example of sacrifice and of resistance to injustice, and the story is retold every year in sermons, songs, paintings and plays. Because the lunar year is shorter than the solar one, Muharram comes about eleven days earlier each year.

The first ten days

As the month begins, the cities change colour. Black banners hang from mosques and shops, many people wear black, and each neighbourhood's هیئت hey'at mourning association holds evening gatherings in a mosque or a حسینیه hoseyniye Muharram hall. A preacher recounts the events of Karbala, and singers lead نوحه nowhe laments while the men beat their chests in rhythm, سینه‌زنی sine-zani chest-beating. Many families put off weddings and parties until the mourning is over.

Tasua and Ashura

The ninth and tenth days, تاسوعا tāsu'ā Tasua and عاشورا āshurā Ashura, are public holidays. Processions of mourners, دسته daste dasteh, move through the streets to drums and cymbals, carrying a great steel standard, the علم alam alam, and green, red and black flags. Some groups strike their backs with bundles of light chains in time with the music. On the evening of Ashura, the شام غریبان shām-e gharibān evening of the strangers, lights are dimmed and people sit with candles, remembering the women and children of Hussein's family, left alone after the battle.

Ta'zieh

The most theatrical form of mourning is تعزیه ta'ziye ta'zieh, a sung passion play that tells the story of Karbala. It is performed in the round, in a courtyard or a special building, the تکیه takiye takyeh, with the audience close around the actors. Colours tell the story: Hussein and his followers wear green and sing their lines, while their enemies wear red and declaim theirs. Women's parts are traditionally played by men. In 2010 UNESCO recognised ta'zieh as part of the intangible heritage of humanity.

Nazri: food for everyone

Muharram is also the season of نذری nazri votive food. Someone who has made a نذر nazr vow – for a sick relative to recover, for an exam, or simply out of devotion – fulfils it by cooking and giving food away. Neighbours gather around huge pots, دیگ dig cauldrons, from before dawn; each person takes a turn stirring and makes a wish. The classic Muharram dish is قیمه gheyme split-pea stew with rice, but there is also شله‌زرد shole-zard saffron rice pudding decorated with cinnamon, حلیم halim wheat-and-meat porridge, and free tea and sherbet at roadside stands. Anyone may receive nazri: join the queue, take your portion and say thank you.

What to say and do

This is a time of mourning, not a festival, so don't wish anyone a happy day. People say قبول باشد ghabul bāshad may it be accepted to someone who has given nazri or prayed, and التماس دعا eltemās-e do'ā pray for me when they part. Visitors are welcome to watch the processions: dress soberly, ask before photographing people close up, and accept tea or food when it is offered. Many restaurants and shops close on Tasua and Ashura.

التماس دعا.

eltemās-e do'ā.

Please remember me in your prayers.

Said when parting, especially at religious times and places.

قبول باشد.

ghabul bāshad.

May it be accepted (by God).

Spoken قبول باشه. ghabul bāshe.

Said to someone who has prayed, fasted or given nazri.

نذرتان قبول باشد.

nazr-etān ghabul bāshad.

May your vow be accepted.

Spoken نذرتون قبول باشه. nazr-etun ghabul bāshe.

تاسوعا و عاشورا در ایران تعطیل رسمی است.

tāsu'ā va āshurā dar Irān ta'til-e rasmi ast.

Tasua and Ashura are public holidays in Iran.

در دهه‌ی اول محرم، بسیاری از مردم سیاه می‌پوشند.

dar dahe-ye avval-e Moharram, besyāri az mardom siyāh mi-pushand.

In the first ten days of Muharram many people wear black.

دسته‌های عزاداری با طبل و سنج از خیابان می‌گذرند.

daste-hā-ye azādāri bā tabl va sanj az khiyābān mi-gozarand.

Mourning processions pass down the street with drums and cymbals.

امام حسین در روز عاشورا در کربلا شهید شد.

Emām Hoseyn dar ruz-e āshurā dar Karbalā shahid shod.

Imam Hussein was martyred at Karbala on the day of Ashura.

Shahid shodan, 'to be martyred', is how Shia Iranians speak of his death.

تعزیه داستان کربلا را نمایش می‌دهد.

ta'ziye dāstān-e Karbalā rā namāyesh mi-dahad.

Ta'zieh acts out the story of Karbala.

همسایه‌ها با هم قیمه‌ی نذری می‌پزند.

hamsāye-hā bā ham gheyme-ye nazri mi-pazand.

The neighbours cook gheymeh nazri together.

هر کس دیگ را هم می‌زند و آرزویی می‌کند.

har kas dig rā ham mi-zanad va ārezu-yi mi-konad.

Everyone gives the pot a stir and makes a wish.

برایتان شله‌زرد نذری آورده‌ام.

barā-yetān shole-zard-e nazri āvarde-am.

I've brought you some nazri sholeh zard.

Spoken براتون شله‌زرد نذری آوردم. barā-tun shole-zard-e nazri āvordam.

در ایستگاه صلواتی به همه چای می‌دهند.

dar istgāh-e salavāti be hame chāy mi-dahand.

At the roadside stand they give everyone free tea.
