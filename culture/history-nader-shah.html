نادرشاهNader Shah

Nāder-shāh

A herdsman's son from Khorasan who drove the Afghans and Ottomans out of Iran, crowned himself shah, sacked Delhi and brought home the Peacock Throne – and was murdered by his own officers eleven years after his coronation.

Facts

  • Nader was born around 1688 in northern Khorasan into the Afshar tribe; his dynasty is called the Afsharids.
  • He was proclaimed shah on the Moghan plain in 1736.
  • At the battle of Karnal in February 1739 he defeated the Mughal emperor Muhammad Shah.
  • The Peacock Throne was made for the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan; Nader took it from Delhi in 1739.
  • The Darya-ye Noor diamond is kept in the National Jewelry Treasury in Tehran; the Koh-i-Noor is in the British Crown Jewels.
  • Nader Shah was assassinated by his own officers in June 1747 near Quchan.
  • The name Nader means 'rare' or 'unique' in Persian.

An empire in pieces

In 1722 an Afghan army led by Mahmud Hotak besieged and took اصفهان Esfahān Isfahan, the Safavid capital. The Safavid state collapsed. Ottoman armies moved into the west of Iran, Russia took the Caspian provinces, and a surviving Safavid prince, Tahmasp II, wandered the north with a small following. For a few years it looked as if Iran might simply be divided among its neighbours.

The man who put it back together came from nowhere.

From herdsman's son to general

نادر Nāder Nader was born around 1688 in northern Khorasan, into a clan of the Turkic ایل افشار il-e Afshār Afshar tribe. His family was poor: Persian tradition likes to say that his father was a چوپان chupān shepherd, and some sources make him a maker of sheepskin coats. Nader grew up as a soldier and a leader of armed bands, and by the 1720s he controlled much of Khorasan from the fortress of کلات Kalāt Kalat.

In 1726 he joined Tahmasp II and took the name Tahmasp Qoli Khan, 'the slave of Tahmasp'. In 1729 he crushed the Afghans in a series of battles and retook Isfahan. In the following years he pushed the Ottomans back across the western frontier, and Russia gave back the Caspian provinces by treaty. He was the best فرمانده farmānde commander Iran had seen for centuries.

A new shah

In 1732 Nader deposed Tahmasp and put his baby son on the throne, ruling in his name. In 1736 he gathered the leading men of the country on the دشت مغان Dasht-e Moghān Moghan plain and had himself proclaimed shah. One of his conditions was that Iranians stop cursing the first caliphs, a practice that offended Sunni neighbours: Nader hoped to end the bitter religious quarrel between Iran and the Ottomans, though the plan had little lasting effect.

Delhi and the Peacock Throne

In 1738 Nader took Kandahar and then crossed into Mughal India. At Karnal, north of Delhi, in February 1739 his army routed the much larger Mughal force of Muhammad Shah, and the Persians entered دهلی Dehli Delhi. When a rumour that Nader was dead led to attacks on his soldiers, he ordered a massacre; many thousands of the city's people were killed in a single day, though the numbers given in the sources vary widely.

Nader left Delhi with the treasure of the Mughals, including the jewelled تخت طاووس Takht-e Tāvus Peacock Throne and the diamonds known as the کوه نور Kuh-e Nur Koh-i-Noor and the دریای نور Daryā-ye Nur Darya-ye Noor. The booty was so great that he cancelled Iran's taxes for three years. The original throne was broken up after his death; the Darya-ye Noor is now in the national jewel collection in Tehran, and the Koh-i-Noor in the British Crown Jewels.

The end

Nader went on to campaign in Central Asia, the Caucasus and against the Ottomans, and he even built a fleet in the Persian Gulf. But the wars never stopped, and neither did the مالیات māliyāt taxes that paid for them. After an attempt on his life in 1741 he suspected his own son Reza Qoli and had him blinded. In his last years he grew ever more suspicious and cruel, and revolts broke out across the country.

In June 1747, at a camp near Quchan in Khorasan, a group of his own officers entered his tent at night and killed him. His empire fell apart almost at once.

How he is remembered

Iranians remember Nader in two ways at once: as the saviour who rescued the country from foreign occupation, and as a ruler whose endless wars and cruelty exhausted it. European writers called him 'the Napoleon of Persia'. His modern mausoleum in مشهد Mashhad Mashhad, built in the 1950s, shows him on horseback with his soldiers, and the name نادر Nāder Nader – which means 'rare' – is still given to Iranian boys. In the shrine of Imam Reza in the same city, the golden iwan of the old courtyard was gilded on his orders.

نادر در خانواده‌ای فقیر در خراسان به دنیا آمد.

Nāder dar khānevāde-i faghir dar Khorāsān be donyā āmad.

Nader was born into a poor family in Khorasan.

می‌گویند پدرش چوپان بود.

mi-guyand pedar-ash chupān bud.

They say his father was a shepherd.

در آن زمان افغان‌ها اصفهان را گرفته بودند.

dar ān zamān afghān-hā Esfahān rā gerefte budand.

At that time the Afghans had taken Isfahan.

نادر افغان‌ها را از ایران بیرون کرد.

Nāder afghān-hā rā az Irān birun kard.

Nader drove the Afghans out of Iran.

او در سال ۱۷۳۶ در دشت مغان شاه شد.

u dar sāl-e hezār-o-haftsad-o-si-o-shesh dar Dasht-e Moghān shāh shod.

He became shah on the Moghan plain in 1736.

نادر فرمانده‌ای بسیار باهوش بود.

Nāder farmānde-i besyār bā-hush bud.

Nader was a very clever commander.

سپاه نادر در سال ۱۷۳۹ وارد دهلی شد.

sepāh-e Nāder dar sāl-e hezār-o-haftsad-o-si-o-noh vāred-e Dehli shod.

Nader's army entered Delhi in 1739.

نادر تخت طاووس و الماس‌های معروف را به ایران آورد.

Nāder Takht-e Tāvus va almās-hā-ye ma'ruf rā be Irān āvard.

Nader brought the Peacock Throne and the famous diamonds to Iran.

الماس دریای نور امروز در تهران است.

almās-e Daryā-ye Nur emruz dar Tehrān ast.

The Darya-ye Noor diamond is in Tehran today.

مالیات‌های سنگین مردم را خسته کرده بود.

māliyāt-hā-ye sangin mardom rā khaste karde bud.

Heavy taxes had worn the people out.

در سال‌های آخر عمرش بسیار بدگمان و خشن شد.

dar sāl-hā-ye ākhar-e 'omr-ash besyār badgamān va khashen shod.

In the last years of his life he became very suspicious and cruel.

نادرشاه در سال ۱۷۴۷ به دست افسرانش کشته شد.

Nāder-shāh dar sāl-e hezār-o-haftsad-o-chehel-o-haft be dast-e afsarān-ash koshte shod.

Nader Shah was killed by his own officers in 1747.
