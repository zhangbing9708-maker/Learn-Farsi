Culture / Arts

سینمای ایرانIranian cinema

sinamā-ye Irān

From popular melodramas to the New Wave, from Kiarostami's winding roads to Farhadi's family dramas, Iranian cinema has become one of the most admired in the world – at home and, more and more, abroad.

Facts

  • The first film shot by an Iranian was made in 1900, when Mirza Ebrahim Khan Akkas-bashi, photographer to Mozaffar ad-Din Shah, filmed a flower festival in Ostend, Belgium.
  • The first Persian sound film, The Lor Girl (1933), was made in Bombay by Abdolhossein Sepanta and Ardeshir Irani.
  • Kiarostami's Taste of Cherry shared the 1997 Palme d'Or with Shohei Imamura's The Eel.
  • Majid Majidi's Children of Heaven was the first Iranian film to be nominated for an Oscar.
  • A Separation won both the Golden Bear in Berlin (2011) and the Oscar for best foreign-language film (2012).
  • The Fajr Film Festival, Iran's main film festival, is held in Tehran every February.

A century of cinema

Cinema reached Iran in 1900, when the shah's court photographer bought a camera in Europe and filmed a flower parade in Belgium. The first Persian talking picture, The Lor Girl, followed in 1933. By the 1960s Tehran's cinemas were full of popular melodramas with songs, dances and tough-guy heroes – a genre critics called, with a touch of scorn, فیلم‌فارسی film-fārsi 'Persian film'.

The New Wave

Then a few artists began to film real life. In 1962 the poet Forugh Farrokhzad made The House Is Black, a short, deeply humane documentary about a leper colony. In 1969 Dariush Mehrjui's The Cow – the story of a villager who loses his beloved cow and comes to believe he has become it – announced the موج نو mowj-e now New Wave. Bahram Beyzai, Sohrab Shahid-Saless and others followed, and a state institute for children, the Kanun, gave a young graphic designer called Abbas Kiarostami his first chance to direct.

Children, villages and journeys

After the revolution of 1979 cinema survived, but under strict rules about what could be shown. Many directors turned to subjects that were safe and universal: children, villages, journeys. In Kiarostami's Where Is the Friend's House? (1987) a boy searches a neighbouring village to return his friend's notebook; in Majid Majidi's Children of Heaven (1997) a brother and sister share one pair of shoes; in Jafar Panahi's The White Balloon (1995) a girl loses the money for her New Year goldfish. Simple stories like these won prizes all over the world.

Abbas Kiarostami

عباس کیارستمی 'Abbās Kiyārostami Abbas Kiarostami (1940–2016) became the best known of all Iranian directors. His films are quiet and open-ended: long takes, cars winding up zigzag roads through the hills, and non-professional actors – a نابازیگر nā-bāzigar non-actor – playing versions of themselves. In Close-Up (1990) a real man who had pretended to be a famous director re-enacts his own story. Taste of Cherry, about a man driving around the edge of Tehran with a desperate plan, won the Palme d'Or at Cannes in 1997 – a first for Iran.

Makhmalbaf and Panahi

محسن مخملباف Mohsen Makhmalbāf Mohsen Makhmalbaf, a self-taught former revolutionary, made colourful, poetic films such as Gabbeh and Kandahar and turned his family into a film school: his daughter Samira directed her first feature at seventeen. He has lived abroad since the 2000s.

جعفر پناهی Ja'far Panāhi Jafar Panahi, once Kiarostami's assistant, made films about women and the city, such as The Circle and Offside, about girls who dress as boys to get into a football match. Since 2010 he has been banned from filmmaking and jailed more than once, yet he keeps making films, often in secret; in 2025 It Was Just an Accident won him the Palme d'Or.

Farhadi and Iranian films abroad

اصغر فرهادی Asghar Farhādi Asghar Farhadi (born 1972) makes tense moral dramas about middle-class families, built like thrillers, in which every character has a good reason. A Separation (2011), about a couple's divorce and a quarrel that spirals out of control, won Iran's first Oscar, and The Salesman a second in 2017.

Today Iranian cinema lives both at home and abroad. Films made in Iran still fill festival programmes, while Mohammad Rasoulof, Marjane Satrapi – whose animated Persepolis told the story of her childhood in Tehran – and many younger directors of the diaspora tell Iranian stories from Europe and America.

Say it in Persian

Show

سینما در سال ۱۹۰۰ به ایران آمد.

sinamā dar sāl-e hezār-o-nohsad be Irān āmad.

Cinema came to Iran in 1900.

«گاو» یکی از اولین فیلم‌های موج نو بود.

«Gāv» yek-i az avvalin film-hā-ye mowj-e now bud.

'The Cow' was one of the first films of the New Wave.

کیارستمی بیشتر با نابازیگرها کار می‌کرد.

Kiyārostami bishtar bā nā-bāzigar-hā kār mi-kard.

Kiarostami mostly worked with non-professional actors.

در فیلم‌های او ماشین و جاده زیاد دیده می‌شود.

dar film-hā-ye u māshin va jādde ziyād dide mi-shavad.

Cars and roads appear a lot in his films.

«طعم گیلاس» در سال ۱۹۹۷ نخل طلای کن را برد.

«Ta'm-e gilās» dar sāl-e hezār-o-nohsad-o-navad-o-haft nakhl-e talā-ye Kan rā bord.

'Taste of Cherry' won the Palme d'Or at Cannes in 1997.

Literally: won the golden palm of Cannes

بچه‌ها قهرمان بسیاری از فیلم‌های ایرانی هستند.

bacche-hā ghahremān-e besyāri az film-hā-ye irāni hastand.

Children are the heroes of many Iranian films.

پناهی سال‌ها اجازه‌ی فیلم ساختن نداشت.

Panāhi sāl-hā ejāze-ye film sākhtan na-dāsht.

For years Panahi was not allowed to make films.

«جدایی نادر از سیمین» اولین اسکار را برای ایران برد.

«Jodāyi-ye Nāder az Simin» avvalin Oskār rā barā-ye Irān bord.

'A Separation' won Iran's first Oscar.

Literally: 'the separation of Nader from Simin' won the first Oscar for Iran

فرهادی درباره‌ی خانواده‌های طبقه‌ی متوسط فیلم می‌سازد.

Farhādi darbāre-ye khānevāde-hā-ye tabaghe-ye motavasset film mi-sāzad.

Farhadi makes films about middle-class families.

جشنواره‌ی فیلم فجر هر سال در تهران برگزار می‌شود.

jashnvāre-ye film-e Fajr har sāl dar Tehrān bargozār mi-shavad.

The Fajr Film Festival is held in Tehran every year.

بسیاری از فیلم‌سازان ایرانی امروز خارج از ایران کار می‌کنند.

besyāri az film-sāzān-e irāni emruz khārej az Irān kār mi-konand.

Many Iranian filmmakers work outside Iran today.

ایرانی‌ها سینما را خیلی دوست دارند.

irāni-hā sinamā rā kheyli dust dārand.

Iranians love the cinema.
