Culture / Food

میوه و آجیلFruit and ajil

mive o ājil

Iran is a garden of fruit: pomegranates from Saveh, pistachios from Kerman, summer melons, sour green plums, fruit leather and the bowls of nuts and seeds that are set before every guest.

Facts

  • The pomegranate is native to the region from Iran to northern India.
  • Iran and the United States are the world's two largest pistachio producers; Iran's pistachios come mainly from Kerman province.
  • 'Laughing' pistachios (peste-ye khandan) are those whose shells split open naturally.
  • In Iran cucumbers go in the fruit bowl and are eaten with salt.
  • Lavashak, Iranian fruit leather, is cooked fruit purée – often sour plum – spread thin and dried.
  • Ajil is a must for Nowruz visits and for Yalda night.

A fruit bowl for every guest

In an Iranian home a guest is offered میوه mive fruit almost as soon as tea. The host brings a heaped bowl and gives each person a small plate and a knife – or peels and slices the fruit for you. Don't be surprised to find cucumbers (خیار khiyār cucumber) among the apples and oranges: in Iran cucumbers count as fruit and are eaten with a pinch of salt. Iran's climates, from the wet Caspian coast to the hot southern deserts, grow almost everything – apples, apricots, grapes, cherries, figs and quinces, oranges in the north and dates in the south.

Pomegranates

The انار anār pomegranate is native to the region that stretches from Iran to northern India and has been grown here for thousands of years. Saveh, south-west of Tehran, is the most famous pomegranate town, and the fruit comes in شیرین shirin sweet, ترش torsh sour and ملس malas sweet-sour varieties. Iranians eat the seeds sprinkled with salt or گلپر golpar golpar, squeeze the fruit for juice, or boil the juice down into رب انار rob-e anār pomegranate molasses for fesenjan. Some still eat it the old way: roll it between your hands until the seeds burst, then bite a small hole and drink the juice straight from the fruit.

Pistachios and other nuts

The پسته peste pistachio is Iran's best-known nut, grown mainly in Kerman province around رفسنجان Rafsanjān Rafsanjan; Iran and the United States are the world's two largest producers. The best pistachios are 'laughing': their shells split open by themselves – پسته‌ی خندان peste-ye khandān 'laughing' pistachio. Iran also grows almonds (بادام bādām almond), walnuts and hazelnuts (فندق fandogh hazelnut), and roasted watermelon, pumpkin and sunflower seeds (تخمه tokhme roasted seeds) are the snack of every long journey and football match. Cracking them with the front teeth is a skill every Iranian child learns.

Ajil

آجیل ājil mixed nuts and dried fruit is a mix of roasted nuts and seeds with dried fruit: pistachios, almonds, hazelnuts, roasted chickpeas (نخودچی nokhodchi roasted chickpeas), raisins (کشمش keshmesh raisins), dried mulberries and figs. It is the snack of celebration. Every house keeps a dish ready for Nowruz visits, and on شب یلدا shab-e yaldā Yalda night ajil sits beside the pomegranates and watermelon. A sweet mix called آجیل مشکل‌گشا ājil-e moshkel-goshā 'problem-solving' ajil is eaten on Chaharshanbe Suri, the eve of the year's last Wednesday, and shared with friends in the hope that a wish comes true.

Melons, sour plums and lavashak

Summer is the season of melons: هندوانه hendevāne watermelon, the sweet orange طالبی tālebi cantaloupe and the long, pale خربزه kharboze musk melon, for which Khorasan is famous. Spring brings the sour fruit that Iranians love: little green plums (گوجه‌سبز gowje-sabz green sour plums) eaten with salt, unripe almonds and, a little later, sour cherries (آلبالو ālbālu sour cherry). All year round there is لواشک lavāshak fruit leather: sheets of cooked fruit purée – sour plum, apricot, pomegranate – dried thin and sold in rolls. Markets such as Tehran's Tajrish bazaar pile it all up in glowing pyramids.

Say it in Persian

Show

بفرمایید میوه؛ تعارف نکنید.

befarmāyid mive; ta'ārof na-konid.

Please have some fruit – don't be shy.

Spoken بفرمایین میوه؛ تعارف نکنین. befarmāyin mive; ta'ārof na-konin.

در ایران خیار هم جزو میوه است.

dar Irān khiyār ham jozv-e mive ast.

In Iran cucumber counts as a fruit too.

انار ساوه خیلی معروف است.

anār-e Sāve kheyli ma'ruf ast.

Saveh pomegranates are very famous.

این انار شیرین است یا ترش؟

in anār shirin ast yā torsh?

Is this pomegranate sweet or sour?

یک کیلو پسته‌ی خندان می‌خواهم.

yek kilu peste-ye khandān mi-khāham.

I'd like a kilo of open pistachios.

Spoken یه کیلو پسته‌ی خندون می‌خوام. ye kilu peste-ye khandun mi-khām.

آجیل شب یلدا را خریدی؟

ājil-e shab-e yaldā rā kharidi?

Did you buy the ajil for Yalda night?

در سفر همیشه تخمه می‌شکنیم.

dar safar hamishe tokhme mi-shekanim.

On trips we always crack seeds.

گوجه‌سبز را با نمک می‌خورند.

gowje-sabz rā bā namak mi-khorand.

Green plums are eaten with salt.

بچه‌ها عاشق لواشک‌اند.

bacche-hā āshegh-e lavāshak-and.

Children love fruit leather.

هندوانه را خنک کن، بعد قاچ کن.

hendevāne rā khonak kon, ba'd ghāch kon.

Chill the watermelon, then cut it into slices.

این خربزه مثل عسل شیرین است.

in kharboze mesl-e asal shirin ast.

This melon is as sweet as honey.

پوست میوه را برایتان بکنم؟

pust-e mive rā barā-yetān be-kanam?

Shall I peel the fruit for you?

Spoken پوست میوه رو براتون بکنم؟ pust-e mive ro barā-tun be-kanam?
