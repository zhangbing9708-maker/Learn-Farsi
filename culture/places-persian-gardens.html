باغ‌های ایرانیThe Persian gardens

bāgh-hā-ye irāni

Walls against the desert, water from the mountains, cypresses and a pavilion: the Persian garden gave the world the word 'paradise'. The idea behind it, and the nine gardens on UNESCO's list.

Facts

  • UNESCO inscribed nine Iranian gardens together as 'The Persian Garden' in 2011.
  • The English word 'paradise' comes, through Greek, from an Old Iranian word for a walled garden.
  • The garden of Cyrus the Great at Pasargadae (6th century BC) is the earliest known chahar bagh.
  • The Fin Garden in Kashan, completed in 1590, is the oldest surviving garden in Iran.
  • Chehel Sotoun's twenty wooden columns are reflected in its pool – twenty and twenty make 'forty'.
  • The windcatcher of the Dowlat Abad Garden in Yazd is 33.8 metres tall.
  • The gardens of the Taj Mahal follow the Persian chahar bagh plan.

Paradise on Earth

The word paradise comes from Iran. Old Iranian had a word for a walled garden, pairidaēza, 'walled around', which passed into Greek as paradeisos and from there into the languages of Europe; modern Persian uses it as پردیس pardis paradise; campus.

For three thousand years Iranians have answered the desert by building gardens: a high wall to keep out dust and wind, water brought from the mountains, shade trees, flowers and fruit, and a کوشک kushk pavilion from which to enjoy it all.

The fourfold garden

The classic plan is the چهارباغ chahār-bāgh fourfold garden: two water channels cross at the centre and divide the garden into four parts, often with a حوض howz pool where they meet and the pavilion on the main axis.

Water is the soul of the design. It arrives through a قنات ghanāt qanat, runs along narrow stone channels (جوی juy channel, stream), leaps up in fountains (فواره favvāre fountain) and rests in long pools that reflect the buildings. Rows of tall سرو sarv cypress trees and broad چنار chenār plane trees give shade. The idea travelled with Persian culture: the gardens of the Taj Mahal in India follow the same plan.

Nine gardens on the UNESCO list

In 2011 UNESCO inscribed nine gardens together as 'The Persian Garden':

  • پاسارگاد Pāsārgād Pasargadae, the garden of Cyrus the Great, 6th century BC – the earliest known chahar bagh;
  • باغ ارم Bāgh-e Eram Eram in Shiraz, with its tall cypresses;
  • چهل‌ستون Chehel-sotun Chehel Sotoun in Isfahan, a Safavid pavilion behind a long pool;
  • باغ فین Bāgh-e Fin Fin in Kashan, completed in 1590;
  • عباس‌آباد Abbās-ābād Abbasabad at Behshahr, a Safavid garden beside an artificial lake in the forests of Mazandaran;
  • باغ شازده Bāgh-e Shāzde Shazdeh at Mahan near Kerman, a Qajar garden of terraced fountains;
  • دولت‌آباد Dowlat-ābād Dowlat Abad in Yazd, with its great windcatcher;
  • پهلوان‌پور Pahlavān-pur Pahlevanpour at Mehriz near Yazd, a Qajar garden fed by a qanat;
  • اکبریه Akbariye Akbarieh in Birjand, South Khorasan, a Qajar garden of the local governors.

Chehel Sotoun and Fin

Two of the nine lie on the classic route through Iran. In Isfahan, چهل‌ستون Chehel-sotun Chehel Sotoun, 'forty columns', has twenty slender wooden columns on its veranda, which are doubled by their reflection in the pool – and in Persian chehel, forty, can also simply mean 'many'. Inside, Safavid wall paintings show feasts, battles and the reception of foreign kings.

In Kashan, water runs so fast through the turquoise channels of the باغ فین Bāgh-e Fin Fin Garden that it bubbles up in the fountains, which work without pumps, by the pressure of the water alone.

Gardens in the desert

The most moving gardens are in the desert, where every tree depends on water carried for kilometres underground. In Yazd, the باغ دولت‌آباد Bāgh-e Dowlat-ābād Dowlat Abad Garden, laid out in the mid-18th century, cools its pavilion with a windcatcher 33.8 metres tall. Near Mahan, the Shazdeh Garden steps down its slope in a cascade of pools, a green ribbon against the bare brown mountains.

Visiting a Persian garden

Go in spring, when the fruit trees are in blossom and the roses are out, or in the soft light of an autumn afternoon. For Iranians a garden is a place for a family outing: they come to stroll, to take photos by the pools and, in public parks, to spread a cloth on the grass for a picnic. Two words to know: the person who looks after it all is the باغبان bāghbān gardener, and the little garden in a courtyard is a باغچه bāghche small garden, flower bed.

Say it in Persian

Show

واژه‌ی «پردیس» به معنی باغ است.

vāzhe-ye «pardis» be ma'ni-ye bāgh ast.

The word 'pardis' means garden.

باغ ایرانی دیوار بلندی دارد.

bāgh-e irāni divār-e boland-i dārad.

A Persian garden has a high wall.

آب از قنات به باغ می‌آید.

āb az ghanāt be bāgh mi-āyad.

The water comes into the garden from a qanat.

دو جوی آب باغ را به چهار قسمت تقسیم می‌کنند.

do juy-e āb bāgh rā be chahār ghesmat taghsim mi-konand.

Two water channels divide the garden into four parts.

فواره‌های باغ فین بدون پمپ کار می‌کنند.

favvāre-hā-ye Bāgh-e Fin bedun-e pomp kār mi-konand.

The fountains of the Fin Garden work without pumps.

چهل‌ستون بیست ستون دارد، نه چهل ستون!

Chehel-sotun bist sotun dārad, na chehel sotun!

Chehel Sotoun has twenty columns, not forty!

عکس ستون‌ها در حوض افتاده است.

aks-e sotun-hā dar howz oftāde ast.

The columns are reflected in the pool.

زیر سایه‌ی درخت‌های چنار بنشینیم.

zir-e sāye-ye derakht-hā-ye chenār be-neshinim.

Let's sit in the shade of the plane trees.

باغبان هر روز به گل‌ها آب می‌دهد.

bāghbān har ruz be gol-hā āb mi-dahad.

The gardener waters the flowers every day.

در بهار درخت‌های باغ پر از شکوفه‌اند.

dar bahār derakht-hā-ye bāgh por az shokufe-and.

In spring the trees of the garden are full of blossom.

پیک‌نیک در این باغ ممنوع است.

pik-nik dar in bāgh mamnu' ast.

Picnics are not allowed in this garden.

باغ دولت‌آباد بلندترین بادگیر ایران را دارد.

Bāgh-e Dowlat-ābād boland-tarin bādgir-e Irān rā dārad.

The Dowlat Abad Garden has the tallest windcatcher in Iran.
