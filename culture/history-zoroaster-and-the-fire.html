زرتشت و آتشZoroaster and the fire

Zartosht o ātash

Long before Islam, Iranians followed the teachings of Zarathustra: one wise god, a cosmic struggle between truth and the lie, and fire as the sign of purity. The religion is small today, but its festivals, words and symbols are everywhere in Iranian life.

Facts

  • The Gathas, seventeen hymns attributed to Zarathustra himself, are the oldest part of the Avesta.
  • The Magi of the Christmas story bear the title of Zoroastrian priests – Old Persian magu-, modern Persian mogh.
  • The fire of the Atash Behram in Yazd is said to have burned since about AD 470; its present temple was built in 1934.
  • All twelve months of the Iranian solar calendar are named after Zoroastrian divine beings and ideas.
  • Zoroastrians and Jews each have one reserved seat in Iran's parliament, and Armenian Christians have two.
  • Freddie Mercury (born Farrokh Bulsara, 1946) grew up in a Parsi Zoroastrian family.
  • English 'paradise' goes back to an Old Iranian word for a walled garden, the ancestor of Persian pardis.

The prophet

زرتشت Zartosht Zarathustra – Zoroaster to the Greeks – is one of the most mysterious founders of a religion. He lived somewhere in eastern Iran or Central Asia, but nobody knows exactly when: the language of his hymns is so old that many scholars place him between about 1500 and 1000 BC, while a later tradition puts him in the 6th century BC. His seventeen hymns, the گاهان Gāhān Gathas, are the oldest part of the اوستا Avestā Avesta, the holy book of the Zoroastrians. For many centuries the Avesta was passed on by memory; it was written down only in Sasanian times, in an alphabet invented for the purpose.

Good thoughts, good words, good deeds

Zarathustra taught that there is one wise creator, اهورامزدا Ahurāmazdā Ahura Mazda, 'the Wise Lord', and that the world is a battlefield between truth and order, اشه asha, and the lie, دروغ dorugh, whose champion is the hostile spirit اهریمن Ahriman. Every person must choose a side, and the choice is made in daily life, as the best-known Zoroastrian motto says: پندار نیک، گفتار نیک، کردار نیک pendār-e nik, goftār-e nik, kerdār-e nik good thoughts, good words, good deeds.

After death, the soul crosses a bridge that is wide for the righteous and as narrow as a blade for the wicked. Ideas like these – heaven and hell, a last judgement, a saviour at the end of time – are why many historians believe Zoroastrianism influenced Judaism, Christianity and Islam.

Fire temples and towers of silence

Fire, clean and full of light, is the symbol of truth, and Zoroastrians pray facing a source of light – the sun, a lamp or a sacred fire. They do not worship the fire itself. The Achaemenid kings called on Ahura Mazda in their inscriptions, and the Sasanians made the faith the religion of the state, with great fires burning in temples such as Adur Gushnasp at Takht-e Soleyman. In the آتشکده āteshkade fire temple of Yazd a flame said to have burned since about AD 470 is still tended by priests, موبد mobad, who cover their mouths so that their breath does not pollute it.

The same respect for the pure elements meant that the dead could be neither buried nor burned. Bodies were laid out in دخمه dakhme towers of silence on hilltops, to be cleaned by vultures. In Iran the custom ended in the 1970s, and the towers outside Yazd now stand empty.

After Islam

After the Arab conquest most Iranians slowly became Muslims, but Zoroastrian communities survived, especially around Yazd and Kerman. Between the 8th and 10th centuries some groups sailed to Gujarat in India, where their descendants, the پارسی Pārsi Parsis, became famous merchants and industrialists; the singer Freddie Mercury was born into a Parsi family. In Iran today Zoroastrians are a small, officially recognised minority with a reserved seat in parliament, and most of them live in Tehran. Every June pilgrims climb to چک‌چک Chak-chak, a mountain shrine near Yazd where water drips from the rock.

A living heritage

You do not have to be a Zoroastrian to live with Zoroastrian traditions. نوروز Nowruz at the spring equinox, jumping over bonfires on چهارشنبه‌سوری Chahārshanbe-suri, the long night of یلدا Yaldā and the midwinter fire festival of سده Sade all go back to pre-Islamic Iran. The months of the Iranian calendar are named after Zoroastrian divine beings – مهر Mehr is Mithra, آذر Āzar is fire, آبان Ābān the waters. Persian بهشت behesht paradise comes from an Avestan word meaning 'best', and دیو div demon from the old word for false gods. Even the winged figure of the فروهر foruhar Faravahar, once a royal emblem of the Achaemenids, is now worn on necklaces all over Iran as a sign of Iranian identity.

زرتشت پیامبر ایران باستان بود.

Zartosht payāmbar-e Irān-e bāstān bud.

Zoroaster was the prophet of ancient Iran.

کتاب دینی زرتشتیان «اوستا» نام دارد.

ketāb-e dini-ye zartoshtiyān «Avestā» nām dārad.

The holy book of the Zoroastrians is called the Avesta.

زرتشتیان به اهورامزدا، خدای دانا، باور دارند.

zartoshtiyān be Ahurāmazdā, khodā-ye dānā, bāvar dārand.

Zoroastrians believe in Ahura Mazda, the wise god.

شعار زرتشتیان «پندار نیک، گفتار نیک، کردار نیک» است.

sho'ār-e zartoshtiyān «pendār-e nik, goftār-e nik, kerdār-e nik» ast.

The Zoroastrian motto is 'good thoughts, good words, good deeds'.

آتش برای زرتشتیان نماد پاکی و روشنایی است.

ātash barā-ye zartoshtiyān namād-e pāki va roshanāyi ast.

For Zoroastrians, fire is a symbol of purity and light.

زرتشتیان آتش را نمی‌پرستند؛ آن‌ها رو به نور دعا می‌کنند.

zartoshtiyān ātash rā nemi-parastand; ān-hā ru be nur do'ā mi-konand.

Zoroastrians do not worship fire; they pray facing the light.

می‌گویند آتش آتشکده‌ی یزد بیش از هزار و پانصد سال است که می‌سوزد.

mi-guyand ātash-e āteshkade-ye Yazd bish az hezār o pānsad sāl ast ke mi-suzad.

They say the fire in the Yazd fire temple has been burning for more than 1,500 years.

زرتشتیان امروز بیشتر در تهران، یزد و کرمان زندگی می‌کنند.

zartoshtiyān emruz bishtar dar Tehrān, Yazd o Kermān zendegi mi-konand.

Today Zoroastrians live mostly in Tehran, Yazd and Kerman.

جشن سده در زمستان با آتشی بزرگ برگزار می‌شود.

jashn-e Sade dar zemestān bā ātash-i bozorg bargozār mi-shavad.

The Sadeh festival is held in winter with a big fire.

نام ماه‌های تقویم ایرانی از اوستا آمده است.

nām-e māh-hā-ye taghvim-e irāni az Avestā āmade ast.

The names of the months of the Iranian calendar come from the Avesta.

واژه‌ی «بهشت» از زبان اوستایی آمده است.

vāzhe-ye «behesht» az zabān-e Avestāyi āmade ast.

The word behesht (paradise) comes from the Avestan language.
