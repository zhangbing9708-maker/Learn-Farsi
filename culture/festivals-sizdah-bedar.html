Culture / Festivals

سیزده‌به‌درSizdah Bedar, the day out of doors

sizdah-be-dar

On the thirteenth day of the new year the whole country goes on a picnic. Iranians leave the bad luck of the number thirteen outside, throw the New Year sabze into running water and tie knots in it for wishes.

Facts

  • Sizdah Bedar falls on 13 Farvardin, usually 1 or 2 April, and ends the Nowruz holidays.
  • In the official Iranian calendar the day is called Ruz-e Tabi'at, 'Nature Day', and it is a public holiday.
  • Sizdah means 'thirteen'; be dar means 'out of doors'.
  • The sabze grown for the haft-sin is thrown into running water to carry away the year's bad luck.
  • Young unmarried women knot blades of sabze and wish to be married by the next Sizdah Bedar.
  • Dorugh-e sizdah, 'the lie of the thirteenth', is a day for pranks, close in spirit and in date to April Fool's Day.

The thirteenth day

The Nowruz holidays end on the thirteenth day of the year, 13 فروردین Farvardin – usually 1 or 2 April – and on that day almost no one stays at home. سیزده‌به‌در sizdah-be-dar Sizdah Bedar means roughly 'thirteen out of doors'. Families pack the car with rugs, pots and a samovar and head for parks, orchards, riversides and mountain valleys. In the official calendar the day is called روز طبیعت ruz-e tabi'at Nature Day, and it is a public holiday.

Why go out?

Nobody knows exactly how the custom began. The usual explanation is that thirteen was an unlucky number, نحس nahs ill-omened, so families spent the day outside and left the bad luck there. Others see in it an old spring festival for rain and green fields. Either way, the mood is anything but gloomy: it is the last party of the holidays before work and school begin again.

A day in the park

Wise families leave early: by mid-morning every patch of shade is taken and the roads out of town are jammed. A زیرانداز zir-andāz picnic rug is spread under a tree, the tea goes on, and the day settles into a rhythm of eating, chatting, napping and games – volleyball, football, cards, a rope swing hung from a branch, and طناب‌کشی tanāb-keshi tug-of-war between the cousins. By the afternoon someone has brought out a drum or a speaker, and there is music and dancing between the rugs.

Picnic food

Lunch is big. The classic is آش رشته āsh-e reshte herb and noodle soup, cooked at home and carried in a huge pot, then kababs grilled on the spot, or کوکو kuku herb omelette with bread. Crisp کاهو kāhu lettuce leaves are dipped in سکنجبین sekanjabin vinegar-and-mint syrup, and there is fruit, ajil and endless tea.

Knotting the sabze

Before going home, the family does the most important thing of the day. The سبزه sabze sprouted greens that stood on the haft-sin for two weeks are thrown into آب روان āb-e ravān running water, taking the illness and bad luck of the year with them. First, young unmarried women tie a گره gereh knot in the blades and make a wish, reciting a rhyme: by next Sizdah Bedar, may I be in my husband's house with a baby in my arms! The day also has its own pranks, دروغ سیزده dorugh-e sizdah the lie of the thirteenth, rather like April Fool's Day.

If you are there

Join in – Iranians love to share a picnic with strangers, and you will probably be offered tea and food. Bring your own rug, expect heavy traffic, and take your rubbish home: every year campaigns ask picnickers to leave the parks clean. The greeting of the day is simply سیزده‌به‌در مبارک sizdah-be-dar mobārak happy Sizdah Bedar.

Say it in Persian

Show

سیزده‌به‌در مبارک!

sizdah-be-dar mobārak!

Happy Sizdah Bedar!

امروز همه از خانه بیرون می‌روند.

emruz hame az khāne birun mi-ravand.

Today everyone goes out of the house.

روز سیزده را نباید در خانه ماند.

ruz-e sizdah rā na-bāyad dar khāne mānd.

You mustn't stay at home on the thirteenth.

صبح زود برویم تا جای خوبی پیدا کنیم.

sobh-e zud be-ravim tā jā-ye khub-i peydā konim.

Let's go early in the morning so we can find a good spot.

زیرانداز را زیر سایه‌ی درخت پهن کن.

zir-andāz rā zir-e sāye-ye derakht pahn kon.

Spread the rug out in the shade of the tree.

مادرم یک دیگ بزرگ آش رشته پخته است.

mādar-am yek dig-e bozorg āsh-e reshte pokhte ast.

My mother has cooked a huge pot of ash reshteh.

بیایید طناب‌کشی بازی کنیم!

biyāyid tanāb-keshi bāzi konim!

Let's play tug-of-war!

سبزه را به آب روان می‌اندازیم.

sabze rā be āb-e ravān mi-andāzim.

We throw the sabze into running water.

دخترها سبزه گره می‌زنند و آرزو می‌کنند.

dokhtar-hā sabze gereh mi-zanand va ārezu mi-konand.

The girls tie knots in the sabze and make a wish.

سیزده‌به‌در، سال دیگر، خانه‌ی شوهر، بچه بغل!

sizdah-be-dar, sāl-e digar, khāne-ye showhar, bacche baghal!

Next Sizdah Bedar – in my husband's house, with a baby in my arms!

Literally: Sizdah Bedar, next year, husband's house, baby in arms

Spoken سیزده‌به‌در، سال دیگه، خونه‌ی شوهر، بچه بغل! sizdah-be-dar, sāl-e dige, khune-ye showhar, bacche baghal!

The rhyme young women say as they knot the sabze.

مواظب باش، امروز روز دروغ سیزده است!

movāzeb bāsh, emruz ruz-e dorugh-e sizdah ast!

Careful – today is the day of Sizdah pranks!

لطفاً زباله‌هایتان را با خودتان ببرید.

lotfan zobāle-hā-yetān rā bā khod-etān be-barid.

Please take your rubbish home with you.
