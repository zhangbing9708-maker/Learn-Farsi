Culture / Language

زبان‌های ایرانThe languages of Iran

zabān-hā-ye Irān

Persian is the language everyone in Iran shares, but it is the mother tongue of only around half of Iranians. Persian's own accents, and the other languages of Iran – Azerbaijani, Kurdish, Gilaki, Mazandarani, Lori, Baluchi, Arabic, Armenian, Turkmen and more.

Facts

  • Article 15 of Iran's constitution makes Persian the official language and allows regional languages in the press and media and for teaching their literature in schools.
  • Persian and Lori are south-western Iranian languages; Kurdish, Gilaki, Mazandarani, Baluchi and Talysh belong to the north-western branch.
  • Azerbaijani, Turkmen and Qashqai are Turkic languages, related to Turkish rather than to Persian.
  • Shah Abbas I moved Armenians from Julfa on the Aras river to New Julfa in Isfahan in 1604–05.
  • Iran's parliament reserves two seats for Armenians and one for Assyrians and Chaldeans.
  • Tehrani Persian turns ān into un in many everyday words: nān becomes nun and khāne becomes khune.
  • Georgian is still spoken in Fereydunshahr, by descendants of Georgians settled there under Shah Abbas.

A country of bilinguals

Persian is the language of school, government and national television, and nearly every Iranian speaks it. But it is the mother tongue of only around half of Iran's roughly 90 million people. The others grow up with another language at home and learn Persian as well, so Iran is a country of دوزبانه dozabāne bilingual people. Article 15 of the constitution makes Persian the official language and allows regional languages in the press and media and for teaching their literature in schools.

Persian accents

Persian itself sounds different from city to city. The standard spoken form is the speech of Tehran, where نان nān bread becomes nun and خانه khāne house becomes khune. Iranians love to imitate the sing-song intonation of Isfahan, and the accents of Shiraz, Mashhad, Yazd and Kerman are recognised at once. An accent is a لهجه lahje, and where's your accent from? is a friendly question, not a rude one.

Iranian cousins of Persian

Many of Iran's other languages belong to the same Iranian family as Persian – but they are cousins, not dialects: a Tehrani cannot follow them without learning them. کردی kordi Kurdish is spoken in the west, in Kurdistan, Kermanshah, Ilam and West Azerbaijan, in several varieties such as Sorani and Kurmanji. Along the Caspian, گیلکی gilaki Gilaki and مازندرانی māzandarāni Mazandarani are the home languages of Gilan and Mazandaran. لری lori Lori, spoken in Lorestan and, as Bakhtiari, in the central Zagros, is the closest relative of Persian. In the south-east, بلوچی baluchi Baluchi is spoken in Sistan and Baluchestan and across the border in Pakistan. Smaller languages such as Talysh and Tati live on in the north.

Turkic languages

The largest minority language is not Iranian at all but Turkic. ترکی آذربایجانی torki-ye āzarbāyjāni Azerbaijani Turkish – Iranians usually just say ترکی torki – is spoken across the north-west, in Tabriz, Ardabil, Urmia and Zanjan, and by many people in Tehran. Estimates vary, but somewhere between a sixth and a quarter of Iranians speak it as their mother tongue. It is close to the language of the Republic of Azerbaijan and related to Turkish. ترکمنی torkamani Turkmen is spoken in Golestan province in the north-east, and the Qashqai nomads of Fars speak another Turkic language.

Arabic, Armenian and more

In Khuzestan, near the border with Iraq, many people speak عربی arabi Arabic at home, a variety close to the Arabic of southern Iraq. ارمنی armani Armenian has been spoken in Iran for centuries: Shah Abbas I settled Armenians in New Julfa, Isfahan, in 1604–05, and Tehran, Isfahan and Tabriz still have Armenian schools and churches. Assyrians around Urmia speak Neo-Aramaic, and in Fereydunshahr, west of Isfahan, the descendants of Georgians settled there four centuries ago still speak Georgian.

Talking about languages

A friendly question is زبان مادری شما چیست؟ zabān-e mādari-ye shomā chist? what is your mother tongue?. Be careful with گویش guyesh dialect: Persian speakers sometimes call any regional language a dialect, but Kurdish, Azerbaijani and Baluchi speakers rightly see theirs as languages with their own poetry, songs and history. Showing interest – and learning to say hello in them – is always welcome.

Say it in Persian

Show

در ایران به زبان‌های زیادی صحبت می‌کنند.

dar Irān be zabān-hā-ye ziyād-i sohbat mi-konand.

Many languages are spoken in Iran.

Spoken تو ایران به زبون‌های زیادی حرف می‌زنن. tu Irān be zabun-hā-ye ziyād-i harf mi-zanan.

زبان مادری من ترکی است.

zabān-e mādari-ye man torki ast.

My mother tongue is Azerbaijani Turkish.

Spoken زبون مادری من ترکیه. zabun-e mādari-ye man torki-ye.

مادربزرگم فقط کردی صحبت می‌کند.

mādar-bozorg-am faghat kordi sohbat mi-konad.

My grandmother only speaks Kurdish.

Spoken مادربزرگم فقط کردی حرف می‌زنه. mādar-bozorg-am faghat kordi harf mi-zane.

در رشت مردم گیلکی حرف می‌زنند.

dar Rasht mardom gilaki harf mi-zanand.

In Rasht people speak Gilaki.

Spoken تو رشت مردم گیلکی حرف می‌زنن. tu Rasht mardom gilaki harf mi-zanan.

در تبریز بیشتر مردم ترکی و فارسی می‌دانند.

dar Tabriz bishtar-e mardom torki va fārsi mi-dānand.

In Tabriz most people know Azerbaijani and Persian.

Spoken تو تبریز بیشتر مردم ترکی و فارسی بلدن. tu Tabriz bishtar-e mardom torki o fārsi balad-an.

لری از همه‌ی زبان‌ها به فارسی نزدیک‌تر است.

lori az hame-ye zabān-hā be fārsi nazdik-tar ast.

Lori is closer to Persian than any other language.

Spoken لری از همه‌ی زبونا به فارسی نزدیک‌تره. lori az hame-ye zabun-ā be fārsi nazdik-tar-e.

زبان بلوچی در دو طرف مرز ایران و پاکستان صحبت می‌شود.

zabān-e baluchi dar do taraf-e marz-e Irān va Pākestān sohbat mi-shavad.

Baluchi is spoken on both sides of the border between Iran and Pakistan.

ارمنی‌های ایران مدرسه‌ها و کلیساهای خودشان را دارند.

armani-hā-ye Irān madrese-hā va kelisā-hā-ye khod-eshān rā dārand.

Iran's Armenians have their own schools and churches.

خیلی از ایرانی‌ها دوزبانه هستند.

kheyli az irāni-hā dozabāne hastand.

Many Iranians are bilingual.

Spoken خیلی از ایرانی‌ها دوزبونه‌ان. kheyli az irāni-hā dozabune-an.

لهجه‌ی اصفهانی خیلی معروف است.

lahje-ye esfahāni kheyli ma'ruf ast.

The Isfahan accent is very famous.

Spoken لهجه‌ی اصفهانی خیلی معروفه. lahje-ye esfahāni kheyli ma'ruf-e.

شما اهل کجا هستید؟

shomā ahl-e kojā hastid?

Where are you from?

Spoken شما اهل کجایین؟ shomā ahl-e kojā-yin?

ببخشید، من کردی بلد نیستم.

bebakhshid, man kordi balad nistam.

Sorry, I don't know Kurdish.
