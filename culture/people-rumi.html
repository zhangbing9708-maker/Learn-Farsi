Culture / People

مولاناRumi

Mowlānā

A preacher's son who fled the Mongols with his family, became a respected scholar in Konya – and then met a wandering dervish who turned him into one of the great poets of the world. Rumi, Shams, the Masnavi and the whirling dervishes.

Facts

  • Rumi was born in 1207 and died in Konya on 17 December 1273.
  • 'Rumi' means 'from Rum' – Anatolia, once part of the Eastern Roman Empire.
  • The Masnavi has six books and some 25,000 couplets.
  • The Divan-e Shams contains more than 3,000 ghazals, many signed with the name of Shams instead of Rumi's own.
  • UNESCO declared 2007, the 800th anniversary of his birth, the International Year of Rumi.
  • The night of his death, the 'wedding night' (Şeb-i Arus in Turkish), is commemorated in Konya every December.
  • His tomb under the Green Dome in Konya is now the Mevlana Museum.

From Balkh to Konya

Jalal al-Din Mohammad was born in 1207 in the region of بلخ Balkh, in today's northern Afghanistan – perhaps in the small town of Vakhsh, now in Tajikistan. His father, Baha al-Din Valad, was a well-known preacher. Some years before the Mongols destroyed Balkh in 1220, the family set out westwards. They passed through Nishapur (where, legend says, the old poet Attar blessed the boy), Baghdad and Mecca, and around 1228 settled in Konya, capital of the Seljuk sultans of Anatolia.

Anatolia was then called روم Rum 'Rome', the lands of the Eastern Roman Empire, and so the boy from Balkh became known in the West as Rumi. Iranians call him مولانا Mowlānā our master or مولوی Mowlavi; Afghans often call him Balkhi. After his father's death in 1231 he took over his teaching post, and by his thirties he was a respected scholar with many students.

Shams of Tabriz

In 1244 a wandering dervish arrived in Konya: شمس تبریزی Shams-e Tabrizi Shams of Tabriz, a man of about sixty with a sharp tongue and no patience for book learning. The meeting turned Rumi's life upside down. For months the two were inseparable, and the sober scholar began to compose poems, listen to music and dance. His students were jealous. Shams left, was brought back from Damascus, and around 1247 vanished for good; whether he simply left or was killed has never been known.

From this loss came the دیوان شمس Divān-e Shams, more than three thousand غزل ghazal love lyrics of longing and joy, many of them signed not with Rumi's name but with Shams's.

The Masnavi

In his last years Rumi dictated his greatest work, the مثنوی معنوی Masnavi-ye ma'navi Spiritual Couplets, to his disciple Hesam al-Din Chalabi. Its six دفتر daftar books hold about 25,000 couplets of stories, jokes, fables and teaching, flowing one into another like a conversation. It opens with the lament of the reed flute, cut from its reed bed and longing to return – an image of the soul separated from God: بشنو این نی چون شکایت می‌کند beshno in ney chon shekāyat mi-konad listen to this reed, how it complains. Later Persian writers called the Masnavi 'the Quran in Persian'.

The whirling dervishes

Rumi died in Konya on 17 December 1273. His followers call that night his شب عروس shab-e 'arus wedding night – his union with God – and still mark it every year. His son Sultan Valad organised the disciples into the Mevlevi order, famous for its سماع samā' Sufi ritual of music and dance. In tall felt hats and white skirts, the dervishes turn slowly and then faster, right palm raised to receive and left palm lowered to give, while the ney and drums play. Turkey closed the Sufi lodges in 1925, but since the 1950s the ceremony has again been performed in Konya, and UNESCO lists it as intangible heritage.

A poet for many countries

Rumi wrote almost all his poetry in Persian, though he lived among Turkish and Greek speakers. Iran, Afghanistan, Tajikistan and Turkey all claim him, and in 2007, the 800th anniversary of his birth, UNESCO declared an International Year of Rumi. His tomb under the Green Dome in Konya is now a museum visited by people of every faith. In loose English versions he has become one of the best-selling poets in the United States – but to hear his music, you need the Persian.

خانواده‌ی مولانا اهل بلخ بود.

khānevāde-ye Mowlānā ahl-e Balkh bud.

Rumi's family came from Balkh.

بلخ امروز در شمال افغانستان است.

Balkh emruz dar shomāl-e Afghānestān ast.

Balkh is in northern Afghanistan today.

خانواده‌ی او پیش از حمله‌ی مغول از بلخ کوچ کردند.

khānevāde-ye u pish az hamle-ye Moghol az Balkh kuch kardand.

His family left Balkh before the Mongol invasion.

آن‌ها سرانجام در قونیه ساکن شدند.

ān-hā saranjām dar Ghuniye sāken shodand.

In the end they settled in Konya.

قونیه امروز شهری در ترکیه است.

Ghuniye emruz shahr-i dar Torkiye ast.

Konya is a city in Turkey today.

مولانا در قونیه با شمس تبریزی آشنا شد.

Mowlānā dar Ghuniye bā Shams-e Tabrizi āshnā shod.

In Konya, Rumi met Shams of Tabriz.

دوستی با شمس زندگی مولانا را تغییر داد.

dusti bā Shams zendegi-ye Mowlānā rā taghyir dād.

His friendship with Shams changed Rumi's life.

مثنوی معنوی شش دفتر دارد.

Masnavi-ye ma'navi shesh daftar dārad.

The Masnavi has six books.

مثنوی با داستان نی شروع می‌شود.

Masnavi bā dāstān-e ney shoru' mi-shavad.

The Masnavi begins with the story of the reed.

مولانا بیشتر شعرهایش را به فارسی سرود.

Mowlānā bish-tar-e she'r-hā-yash rā be fārsi sorud.

Rumi composed most of his poems in Persian.

پیروان او در مراسم سماع می‌چرخند.

peyrovān-e u dar marāsem-e samā' mi-charkhand.

His followers whirl in the sama ceremony.

هر سال هزاران نفر به آرامگاه مولانا در قونیه می‌روند.

har sāl hezārān nafar be ārāmgāh-e Mowlānā dar Ghuniye mi-ravand.

Every year thousands of people visit Rumi's tomb in Konya.
