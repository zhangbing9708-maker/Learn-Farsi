Culture / Everyday life

یک روز در تهرانA day in Tehran

yek ruz dar Tehrān

From the bread queue at dawn to dinner close to midnight: how a working day unfolds in Iran's capital – the metro and the bus lanes, the Thursday–Friday weekend, the famous traffic, and evenings in Tajrish or on Tabiat Bridge.

Facts

  • Tehran's first metro service, the line to Karaj, began in March 1999; on every train the carriages at each end are reserved for women.
  • Valiasr Street runs for about 18 kilometres, from the railway station in the south to Tajrish Square in the north, under rows of old plane trees.
  • Tabiat Bridge, 270 metres long, opened in 2014 and won the Aga Khan Award for Architecture in 2016.
  • The Persian week begins on Saturday: Sunday is yekshanbe, 'one-shanbe', and the count goes up to panjshanbe, 'five-shanbe', Thursday.
  • Thursday evening is called shab-e jom'e, 'Friday night', because a night is counted with the day that follows it.
  • Sangak bread is baked on hot pebbles – its name means 'little stone'.
  • Cars need a permit to enter the traffic-restricted zone, the tarh-e terāfik, in central Tehran.

Bread at dawn

A Tehran morning often begins in a queue. Before work someone from the family walks to the neighbourhood نانوایی nānvāyi bakery and waits in the صف saf queue for bread straight from the oven: the long, dimpled سنگک sangak sangak, baked on a bed of hot pebbles, the thick golden بربری barbari barbari, or paper-thin لواش lavāsh lavash. Customers lay the hot sheets on a rack for a minute to let the steam out, then fold them and hurry home.

صبحانه sobhāne Breakfast is simple: bread with white پنیر panir cheese, walnuts or fresh herbs, butter and jam, and a glass of چای chāy tea. On cold winter mornings some people stop at a stall instead for a bowl of حلیم halim wheat-and-meat porridge, topped with cinnamon and sugar.

Underground and in the bus lane

To cross a city of almost nine million people, many Tehranis go underground. The مترو metro metro began with a single line in 1999 and now has several, and at rush hour the carriages are packed. The carriages at each end of the train are reserved for women, though women may ride in the others too. Listen for the ایستگاه بعدی istgāh-e ba'di next station announcement and the polite ببخشید be-bakhshid excuse me of people squeezing towards the doors.

Above ground, the اتوبوس تندرو otobus-e tondrow rapid-transit (BRT) bus runs in its own lane along the big avenues, so it beats the traffic. Passengers tap a rechargeable card or pay with a phone app, and on city buses men usually sit at the front and women at the back.

The working week

The Iranian week begins on شنبه shanbe Saturday, and the next days are counted from it: یکشنبه yekshanbe Sunday is literally 'one-Saturday', دوشنبه doshanbe Monday 'two-Saturday', and so on up to پنجشنبه panjshanbe Thursday. The weekly day off is جمعه jom'e Friday. Many offices also close on Thursday or work only in the morning, so by Thursday afternoon the weekend has begun. Thursday evening is called شب جمعه shab-e jom'e 'Friday night' – in the Persian way of counting, a night belongs to the day that follows – and it is the favourite time for parties and family visits.

Offices tend to start early, at seven or eight, and empty in the afternoon. Shops open mid-morning and stay open well into the evening.

Traffic

Tehran's ترافیک terāfik traffic is legendary. At rush hour the motorways are jammed, drivers invent their own lanes, and the quickest way across town is often the back seat of a motorbike taxi weaving between the cars. To keep the old centre breathable, cars need a permit to enter the طرح ترافیک tarh-e terāfik traffic zone, and the city has also tried an زوج و فرد zowj o fard odd–even scheme that lets cars in on alternate days according to their number plates.

The backbone of the city is خیابان ولیعصر khiyābān-e Vali'asr Valiasr Street, some 18 kilometres of shops and old plane trees running from the railway station in the south up to Tajrish in the north. In winter, still air can trap smog over the city, and on the worst days of آلودگی هوا āludegi-ye havā air pollution schools may close.

Evening in Tajrish and on Tabiat Bridge

As the heat goes out of the day, the city comes outside. Up in the north, میدان تجریش meydān-e Tajrish Tajrish Square is busy until late, with the covered بازار تجریش bāzār-e Tajrish Tajrish bazaar piled high with fruit, pickles and herbs, the shrine of امامزاده صالح Emāmzāde Sāleh Imamzadeh Saleh, and the road up to the tea houses of دربند Darband Darband.

Further south, پل طبیعت Pol-e Tabi'at Tabiat ('Nature') Bridge carries walkers over the Modarres motorway between two parks. Designed by the young architect Leila Araghian and opened in 2014, it has three levels with benches, cafés and views of the mountains. On summer evenings it is full of families and friends eating بستنی bastani ice cream.

Dinner at ten

Tehranis eat late. ناهار nāhār Lunch, around one or two o'clock, is the main meal of the day; شام shām dinner rarely comes before nine, and with guests or in summer it can be close to midnight. Evenings are also the time for visiting relatives: you will be offered tea, fruit and sweets, and if you stay, dinner. When you finally leave, your hosts will probably walk you all the way to the car.

هر روز صبح برای خرید نان به نانوایی می‌روم.

har ruz sobh barā-ye kharid-e nān be nānvāyi mi-ravam.

Every morning I go to the bakery to buy bread.

Spoken هر روز صبح برای خرید نون می‌رم نونوایی. har ruz sobh barā-ye kharid-e nun mi-ram nunvāyi.

صف نانوایی امروز خیلی طولانی بود.

saf-e nānvāyi emruz kheyli tulāni bud.

The queue at the bakery was very long today.

Spoken صف نونوایی امروز خیلی طولانی بود. saf-e nunvāyi emruz kheyli tulāni bud.

با مترو به سر کار می‌روم، چون از ماشین سریع‌تر است.

bā metro be sar-e kār mi-ravam, chon az māshin sari'-tar ast.

I go to work by metro, because it's faster than going by car.

Spoken با مترو می‌رم سر کار، چون از ماشین سریع‌تره. bā metro mi-ram sar-e kār, chon az māshin sari'-tar-e.

ببخشید، ایستگاه بعدی تجریش است؟

be-bakhshid, istgāh-e ba'di Tajrish ast?

Excuse me, is the next station Tajrish?

Spoken ببخشید، ایستگاه بعدی تجریشه؟ be-bakhshid, istgāh-e ba'di Tajrish-e?

پنجشنبه‌ها اداره‌ی ما فقط تا ظهر کار می‌کند.

panjshanbe-hā edāre-ye mā faghat tā zohr kār mi-konad.

On Thursdays our office only works until noon.

Spoken پنجشنبه‌ها اداره‌ی ما فقط تا ظهر کار می‌کنه. panjshanbe-hā edāre-ye mā faghat tā zohr kār mi-kone.

جمعه تعطیل است و همه دیر از خواب بیدار می‌شوند.

jom'e ta'til ast va hame dir az khāb bidār mi-shavand.

Friday is a holiday, and everyone wakes up late.

Spoken جمعه تعطیله و همه دیر از خواب پا می‌شن. jom'e ta'til-e o hame dir az khāb pā mi-shan.

ساعت شش عصر ترافیک خیابان‌ها وحشتناک است.

sā'at-e shesh-e asr terāfik-e khiyābān-hā vahshatnāk ast.

At six in the evening the traffic in the streets is terrible.

Spoken ساعت شیش عصر ترافیک خیابونا وحشتناکه. sā'at-e shish-e asr terāfik-e khiyābun-ā vahshatnāk-e.

شب‌ها با دوستانم روی پل طبیعت قدم می‌زنیم.

shab-hā bā dustān-am ru-ye Pol-e Tabi'at ghadam mi-zanim.

In the evenings my friends and I go for a walk on Tabiat Bridge.

Spoken شبا با دوستام روی پل طبیعت قدم می‌زنیم. shab-ā bā dustām ru-ye Pol-e Tabi'at ghadam mi-zanim.

در تهران مردم معمولاً دیر شام می‌خورند.

dar Tehrān mardom ma'mulan dir shām mi-khorand.

In Tehran people usually have dinner late.

Spoken تو تهران مردم معمولاً دیر شام می‌خورن. tu Tehrān mardom ma'mulan dir shām mi-khoran.

از تجریش تا راه‌آهن با اتوبوس چقدر طول می‌کشد؟

az Tajrish tā rāh-āhan bā otobus cheghadr tul mi-keshad?

How long does it take by bus from Tajrish to the railway station?

Spoken از تجریش تا راه‌آهن با اتوبوس چقدر طول می‌کشه؟ az Tajrish tā rāh-āhan bā otobus cheghadr tul mi-keshe?

تاکسی بگیرم یا با مترو بروم؟

tāksi be-giram yā bā metro be-ravam?

Should I take a taxi or go by metro?

Spoken تاکسی بگیرم یا با مترو برم؟ tāksi be-giram yā bā metro be-ram?
