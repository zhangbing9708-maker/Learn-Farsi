Culture / Language

واژه‌های فارسی در انگلیسیPersian words in English

vāzhe-hā-ye fārsi dar engelisi

Bazaar, caravan, paradise, pyjamas, checkmate, algorithm: you already know more Persian than you think. Where these words came from, and the long roads they took to reach English.

Facts

  • Van, as in delivery van, is a 19th-century shortening of caravan.
  • Xenophon, a Greek soldier who served in Persia around 400 BC, used parádeisos for the parks of Persian kings and nobles.
  • The botanical name of the peach, Prunus persica, still means 'Persian'.
  • Khaki uniforms were first worn in the 1840s by a regiment of the British Indian army.
  • In Persian chess the king is still the شاه; 'check' is کیش and 'checkmate' کیش و مات.
  • The 'wise men from the East' of the Gospel of Matthew are magoi in the Greek original: the word for Persian priests.
  • Al-Khwarizmi also gave English the word algebra, from al-jabr in the title of his book on equations.

Words that travelled

English has been borrowing Persian words for some eight hundred years, though few came directly. Most travelled the way Persian goods did: through Greek and Latin, Arabic, Turkish, Italian and French, or later through the languages of British India.

Trade words led the way. بازار bāzār market reached English in the 16th century, probably through Italian or Turkish. کاروان kārvān caravan, a group travelling together through the desert, came by way of French; the inns where caravans stopped were کاروانسرا kārvānsarā caravanserai, 'caravan palace'. In the 19th century van, a shortened caravan, gave English its word for a delivery vehicle.

From a walled garden to paradise

The finest journey belongs to paradise. In Old Iranian, pairi-daēza meant 'a walled enclosure'. Greek writers such as Xenophon used parádeisos for the parks of Persian nobles, the Greek translators of the Bible used it for the Garden of Eden, and through Latin and French it reached English as heaven itself. In Persian the word lives on as پردیس pardis – today, less heavenly, a university campus – and, through Arabic, as فردوس ferdows paradise, probably the source of the poet Ferdowsi's pen name.

Kitchen and garden

Food and flowers followed. اسفناج esfenāj spinach passed through Arabic and medieval Latin to become spinach. یاسمن yāsaman jasmine reached French and English as jasmine, and lilac probably goes back to Persian نیلک nilak bluish, from نیل nil indigo. The peach is a different case: its name is Latin, malum persicum, 'the Persian apple', because the Romans thought it came from Persia. In fact it came from China, and in Persian a peach is هلو holu.

Pyjamas, shawls and khaki

British India added a wardrobe. پایجامه pāy-jāme leg garment, loose trousers, reached English through Hindi-Urdu as pyjamas – and Iranians later borrowed the word back from French as پیژامه pizhāme. شال shāl became shawl. خاکی khāki dust-coloured, from خاک khāk dust, earth, named the uniforms the British army began wearing in India in the 1840s.

Kings, priests and sofas

Chess came from India to Persia and on to Europe through the Arabs, and its words came with it. Check goes back to شاه shāh king, and checkmate to شاه مات shāh māt. Most scholars read māt as Persian 'stunned, helpless' – the king is at a loss – though some prefer Arabic māta, 'he died'.

Magic comes from Old Persian magush, a Zoroastrian priest – one of the Magi – in modern Persian مغ mogh. Kiosk is کوشک kushk pavilion, via Turkish and French. And دیوان divān, a register or government office, became in Turkish a council room lined with cushions: hence the English divan, a sofa.

An algorithm with a birthplace

The most modern-sounding word of all is a man's name. Muhammad ibn Musa خوارزمی Khārazmi al-Khwarizmi, from Khwarazm, south of the Aral Sea, worked in Baghdad in the 9th century. His book on Indian numerals was translated into Latin as Algoritmi de numero Indorum, and Algoritmi – his name – became algorithm. The title of another of his books, written in Arabic, gave English algebra.

«بازار» یک واژه‌ی فارسی است که در انگلیسی هم هست.

«bāzār» yek vāzhe-ye fārsi ast ke dar engelisi ham hast.

Bazaar is a Persian word that exists in English too.

Spoken «بازار» یه کلمه‌ی فارسیه که تو انگلیسی هم هست. «bāzār» ye kalame-ye fārsi-ye ke tu engelisi ham hast.

«کاروان» یعنی گروهی از مسافران که با هم سفر می‌کنند.

«kārvān» ya'ni goruh-i az mosāferān ke bā ham safar mi-konand.

A caravan is a group of travellers who travel together.

امروز به محوطه‌ی دانشگاه «پردیس» می‌گویند.

emruz be mohavvate-ye dāneshgāh «pardis» mi-guyand.

Today a university campus is called a pardis.

Spoken الان به محوطه‌ی دانشگاه می‌گن «پردیس». alān be mohavvate-ye dāneshgāh mi-gan «pardis».

اسفناج یک واژه‌ی فارسی است که از راه عربی به اروپا رسید.

esfenāj yek vāzhe-ye fārsi ast ke az rāh-e arabi be Orupā resid.

Spinach is a Persian word that reached Europe by way of Arabic.

«یاسمن» هم اسم گل است و هم اسم دختر.

«yāsaman» ham esm-e gol ast va ham esm-e dokhtar.

Yasaman is both the name of a flower and a girl's name.

Spoken «یاسمن» هم اسم گله هم اسم دختر. «yāsaman» ham esm-e gol-e ham esm-e dokhtar.

هلو در لاتین «سیب ایرانی» نام داشت.

holu dar lātin «sib-e irāni» nām dāsht.

In Latin the peach was called 'the Persian apple'.

ایرانی‌ها امروز به پایجامه «پیژامه» می‌گویند.

irāni-hā emruz be pāy-jāme «pizhāme» mi-guyand.

Today Iranians call pyjamas pizhāme.

Spoken ایرانی‌ها الان به پایجامه می‌گن «پیژامه». irāni-hā alān be pāy-jāme mi-gan «pizhāme».

«خاکی» یعنی به رنگ خاک.

«khāki» ya'ni be rang-e khāk.

Khāki means 'the colour of dust'.

ایرانی‌ها در شطرنج می‌گویند «کیش و مات».

irāni-hā dar shatranj mi-guyand «kish o māt».

In chess Iranians say 'kish o māt' – check and mate.

Spoken ایرانی‌ها تو شطرنج می‌گن «کیش و مات». irāni-hā tu shatranj mi-gan «kish o māt».

خوارزمی دانشمند بزرگ ایرانی در قرن نهم بود.

Khārazmi dāneshmand-e bozorg-e irāni dar gharn-e nohom bud.

Khwarizmi was a great Iranian scholar of the 9th century.

واژه‌ی «الگوریتم» از نام خوارزمی می‌آید.

vāzhe-ye «algoritm» az nām-e Khārazmi mi-āyad.

The word algorithm comes from the name of al-Khwarizmi.

Spoken کلمه‌ی «الگوریتم» از اسم خوارزمی میاد. kalame-ye «algoritm» az esm-e Khārazmi mi-yād.

شما چند واژه‌ی فارسی در انگلیسی می‌شناسید؟

shomā chand vāzhe-ye fārsi dar engelisi mi-shenāsid?

How many Persian words in English do you know?

Spoken شما چند تا کلمه‌ی فارسی تو انگلیسی می‌شناسین؟ shomā chand tā kalame-ye fārsi tu engelisi mi-shenāsin?
