Culture / History

جاده‌ی ابریشمThe Silk Road

jāde-ye abrisham

For some 1,500 years caravans carried silk, paper, spices and ideas between China and the Mediterranean – and almost every route ran through Iran. Iranians were the road's middlemen, and Persian was one of its languages.

Facts

  • The term 'Silk Road' (German Seidenstraße) was coined by Ferdinand von Richthofen in 1877.
  • English 'caravan' and 'bazaar' both come from Persian: kārvān and bāzār.
  • In 2023 UNESCO inscribed 54 historic caravanserais across Iran as a single World Heritage site, 'The Persian Caravanserai'.
  • The Historic Bazaar Complex of Tabriz has been a UNESCO World Heritage site since 2010.
  • Caravanserais were usually built about a day's journey apart – roughly 30 to 40 kilometres.
  • Byzantine historians say that around AD 552 monks smuggled silkworm eggs to Constantinople, ending China's monopoly on silk.
  • Sogdian, an Iranian language of Samarkand, was the main trade language of the Silk Road between the 4th and 8th centuries.

Not one road but many

The name was invented in 1877 by the German geographer Ferdinand von Richthofen. In reality the جاده‌ی ابریشم jāde-ye abrisham Silk Road was a web of caravan routes linking China with India, Iran and the Mediterranean, from the 2nd century BC, when the Chinese envoy Zhang Qian travelled west to Central Asia, until about the 15th century.

Iran sat in the middle. From Kashgar and Samarkand the main road ran through مرو Marv Merv, نیشابور Neyshābur Nishapur and ری Rey, just south of today's Tehran, and on to Hamadan, Tabriz or Baghdad. Whoever held the Iranian plateau controlled the trade: the Parthians and Sasanians grew rich as middlemen between Rome and China, and Roman writers complained about the gold that flowed east to pay for silk.

Merchants and languages

Few merchants travelled the whole way. Goods passed from hand to hand and from city to city, and between the 4th and 8th centuries the most successful middlemen were the Sogdians, an Iranian people of Samarkand and Bukhara whose language was spoken in trading posts all the way to China. Later, Persian itself became a common language of the eastern Silk Road: many of the names in Marco Polo's account of his journey to Mongol China in the 13th century are given in their Persian forms.

In the 6th century the Byzantines broke the Persian hold on silk by obtaining silkworm eggs from Central Asia – smuggled, so the story goes, inside the hollow walking sticks of two monks.

Caravans and caravanserais

English has two Silk Road words straight from Persian: کاروان kārvān caravan and بازار bāzār bazaar. A camel caravan covered perhaps 30 or 40 kilometres a day, and along the main roads, about a day's journey apart, stood کاروانسرا kārvānsarā caravanserais: fortified inns built around a courtyard, with rooms for travellers, stables for animals and a single gate that was locked at night. Many were built by the Seljuks and the Safavids; legend says Shah Abbas built 999 of them. In 2023 UNESCO listed 54 Persian caravanserais together as one World Heritage site.

At the end of the road waited the bazaar, a covered city of shops, workshops, mosques, bathhouses and yet more caravanserais. The bazaar of Tabriz, a great Silk Road market since the 13th century, is still busy today.

What travelled

From China came silk, porcelain, tea and paper – tradition says papermaking reached Samarkand with Chinese prisoners taken in a battle in 751, and spread from there to Baghdad and Europe. In the other direction went horses, silver, glass, carpets, saffron and fruit: one old Chinese name for the pomegranate, انار anār in Persian, is usually explained as 'the pomegranate of Anxi', meaning Parthia. Polo, Persian چوگان chowgān, became a passion at the court of Tang China.

Ideas and faiths travelled too: Buddhism went from India to China, Nestorian Christianity and the religion of Mani spread east, and Islam followed the caravans into Central Asia. So, in the 14th century, did the Black Death.

The end of the road

The overland trade declined after the 15th century, when Portuguese and Dutch ships opened sea routes around Africa and the old empires along the road broke apart. But the Silk Road shaped Iran's cities, its food, its art and its view of itself as the crossroads of the world – and the old name is back today, on tourist posters and on the freight trains that now run between China and Iran.

جاده‌ی ابریشم چین را به دریای مدیترانه وصل می‌کرد.

jāde-ye abrisham Chin rā be daryā-ye Mediterāne vasl mi-kard.

The Silk Road connected China with the Mediterranean Sea.

این جاده در واقع چند راه بود، نه یک راه.

in jāde dar vāghe' chand rāh bud, na yek rāh.

This road was really several routes, not one.

ایران در وسط جاده‌ی ابریشم بود.

Irān dar vasat-e jāde-ye abrisham bud.

Iran lay in the middle of the Silk Road.

بازرگانان با کاروان از شهری به شهر دیگر می‌رفتند.

bāzargānān bā kārvān az shahr-i be shahr-e digar mi-raftand.

Merchants travelled by caravan from one city to the next.

کاروان‌ها شب‌ها در کاروانسرا می‌ماندند.

kārvān-hā shab-hā dar kārvānsarā mi-māndand.

At night the caravans stayed in caravanserais.

فاصله‌ی کاروانسراها معمولاً یک روز راه بود.

fāsele-ye kārvānsarā-hā ma'mulan yek ruz rāh bud.

The caravanserais were usually a day's journey apart.

Literally: the distance of the caravanserais was usually one day of road

ابریشم و کاغذ از چین به غرب می‌رفت.

abrisham va kāghaz az Chin be gharb mi-raft.

Silk and paper went from China to the West.

انار و بازی چوگان از ایران به چین رسیدند.

anār va bāzi-ye chowgān az Irān be Chin residand.

The pomegranate and the game of polo reached China from Iran.

سغدی‌ها بازرگانان بزرگ جاده‌ی ابریشم بودند.

soghdi-hā bāzargānān-e bozorg-e jāde-ye abrisham budand.

The Sogdians were the great merchants of the Silk Road.

واژه‌ی انگلیسی «کاروان» از فارسی آمده است.

vāzhe-ye engelisi-ye «kārvān» az fārsi āmade ast.

The English word 'caravan' comes from Persian.

بازار تبریز یکی از بزرگ‌ترین بازارهای سرپوشیده‌ی جهان است.

bāzār-e Tabriz yek-i az bozorg-tarin bāzār-hā-ye sarpushide-ye jahān ast.

The Tabriz bazaar is one of the largest covered bazaars in the world.
