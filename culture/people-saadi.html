Culture / People

سعدیSaadi

Sa'di

The great traveller of Persian literature came home to Shiraz and wrote two books every Iranian knows – the Bustan and the Golestan. Why his prose still sounds modern, and where his famous lines on humankind come from.

Facts

  • Saadi was born in Shiraz around 1210 and died there around 1291.
  • He finished the Bustan in 1257 and the Golestan in 1258 – the year the Mongols sacked Baghdad.
  • The Golestan has eight chapters and the Bustan ten.
  • His pen-name is thought to come from his patrons, Salghurid rulers of Fars named Sa'd.
  • The 'children of Adam' lines come from the first chapter of the Golestan, on the conduct of kings.
  • His present mausoleum in Shiraz was completed in 1952.
  • 1 Ordibehesht (21 April) is Saadi Day in Iran.

A Shirazi in Baghdad

Mosleh al-Din Saadi was born in شیراز Shirāz Shiraz around 1210, when Fars was ruled by the Salghurids, a local Turkish dynasty. His pen-name, سعدی Sa'di, probably comes from his patrons, Salghurid princes named Sa'd. As a young man he went to Baghdad, capital of the Abbasid caliphs, to study at the نظامیه Nezāmiyye Nizamiyya college, the most famous school of the Islamic world.

Years on the road

Then, for many years, Saadi travelled. In his books he tells of journeys through Syria, Arabia, Anatolia and Central Asia; of being captured by the Crusaders and made to dig ditches at Tripoli until an acquaintance from Aleppo paid his ransom; of a clever boy he met in Kashgar. How much of this is autobiography and how much good storytelling is still debated. What is certain is that he came home around 1256, a man who had seen the world – shortly before the Mongols sacked Baghdad in 1258.

The Bustan and the Golestan

Back in Shiraz he wrote his two masterpieces in quick succession. The بوستان Bustān the Orchard (1257) is a long poem in ten chapters on justice, kindness, love, humility and contentment, told through short stories. The گلستان Golestān the Rose Garden (1258) is a book of prose anecdotes in eight chapters – on kings, dervishes, love and youth, old age, education and good conduct – each sprinkled with verses.

The Golestan contains the most quoted lines in Persian: بنی‌آدم اعضای یکدیگرند bani-ādam a'zā-ye yek-digar-and the children of Adam are limbs of one another, که در آفرینش ز یک گوهرند ke dar āfarinesh ze yek gowhar-and for in creation they are of one essence. They are often cited as an early message of human solidarity.

Saadi's prose

Saadi's prose is famous for its balance. He writes short, rhymed phrases that sound like proverbs, and many of them became proverbs. In the very first story of the Golestan a minister tells a white lie to save a prisoner's life, and the king concludes: دروغی مصلحت‌آمیز به که راستی فتنه‌انگیز dorugh-i maslahat-āmiz beh ke rāsti-ye fetne-angiz a well-meant lie is better than a truth that stirs up trouble.

This clear, polished style shaped Persian prose for centuries, and his language is so close to today's that schoolchildren still read him with little help. Iranians call him استاد سخن ostād-e sokhan the master of speech. He was also a great writer of غزل ghazal love lyrics, the master of the form before Hafez.

The Sa'diyeh

Saadi died in Shiraz around 1291 and was buried at the edge of the city, beside the lodge where he lived in his last years. The present tomb, the سعدیه Sa'diyye Sa'diyeh, was completed in 1952: a tiled dome behind a columned porch, set in a garden. Steps lead down to a small pool fed by an underground channel, where visitors toss coins and watch the fish. 1 Ordibehesht (21 April), a date mentioned in the preface of the Golestan, is celebrated in Iran as Saadi Day.

Say it in Persian

Show

سعدی در شیراز به دنیا آمد.

Sa'di dar Shirāz be donyā āmad.

Saadi was born in Shiraz.

او برای درس خواندن به بغداد رفت.

u barā-ye dars khāndan be Baghdād raft.

He went to Baghdad to study.

سعدی سال‌ها در سرزمین‌های دیگر سفر کرد.

Sa'di sāl-hā dar sarzamin-hā-ye digar safar kard.

Saadi travelled in other lands for years.

او پس از سفرهایش به شیراز برگشت.

u pas az safar-hā-yash be Shirāz bargasht.

After his travels he returned to Shiraz.

بوستان به شعر است و گلستان به نثر و شعر.

Bustān be she'r ast va Golestān be nasr o she'r.

The Bustan is in verse, and the Golestan in prose and verse.

گلستان هشت باب دارد.

Golestān hasht bāb dārad.

The Golestan has eight chapters.

سعدی گلستان را در سال ۱۲۵۸ نوشت.

Sa'di Golestān rā dar sāl-e hezār-o-devist-o-panjāh-o-hasht nevesht.

Saadi wrote the Golestan in 1258.

نثر سعدی ساده و زیباست.

nasr-e Sa'di sāde va zibā-st.

Saadi's prose is simple and beautiful.

بسیاری از ضرب‌المثل‌های فارسی از سعدی است.

besyāri az zarb-ol-masal-hā-ye fārsi az Sa'di ast.

Many Persian proverbs come from Saadi.

شعر «بنی‌آدم» از گلستان سعدی است.

she'r-e «Bani-ādam» az Golestān-e Sa'di ast.

The poem 'Children of Adam' is from Saadi's Golestan.

دانش‌آموزان ایرانی گلستان را در مدرسه می‌خوانند.

dānesh-āmuzān-e irāni Golestān rā dar madrese mi-khānand.

Iranian schoolchildren read the Golestan at school.

آرامگاه سعدی را «سعدیه» می‌گویند.

ārāmgāh-e Sa'di rā «Sa'diyye» mi-guyand.

Saadi's tomb is called the Sa'diyeh.
