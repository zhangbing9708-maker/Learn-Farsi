صفویان و اصفهانThe Safavids and Isfahan

Safaviyān o Esfahān

In 1501 a teenage Sufi leader made himself shah and Shi'ism the faith of Iran. A century later Shah Abbas the Great built a capital at Isfahan so splendid that people called it 'half the world'.

Facts

  • Naqsh-e Jahan Square measures about 560 by 160 metres and was one of Iran's first three UNESCO World Heritage sites (1979).
  • Shah Ismail was only fourteen when he took Tabriz and proclaimed himself shah in 1501.
  • Si-o-se Pol, 'the thirty-three bridge', is named after its 33 arches.
  • Chehel Sotun, 'Forty Columns', has twenty wooden columns – their reflection in the long pool makes forty.
  • The Ardabil Carpet (1539–40), now in London's Victoria and Albert Museum, is one of the oldest dated carpets in the world.
  • Shah Abbas the Great is buried in Kashan.
  • In 1739 Nader Shah carried off the Peacock Throne and the Koh-i-Noor diamond from Delhi.

From Ardabil to the throne

The Safavids took their name from شیخ صفی Sheykh Safi Sheikh Safi (1252–1334), a Sufi master of Ardabil in Azerbaijan, whose shrine is still one of the jewels of Iranian architecture. Over two centuries his order became a militant movement, backed by Turkmen tribesmen called قزلباش ghezelbāsh 'red heads' after their red caps.

In 1501 its fourteen-year-old leader, شاه اسماعیل Shāh Esmā'il Shah Ismail, took Tabriz, crowned himself shah and declared Twelver Shi'ism the religion of the state. Most Iranians were then Sunni; over the following two centuries, through teaching, pressure and persecution, Iran became the mainly Shi'a country it is today. The new faith also set Iran apart from its Sunni rivals, the Ottomans to the west and the Uzbeks to the east. In 1514 Ottoman cannon crushed Ismail's cavalry at Chaldiran, and the capital later moved east, first to Qazvin and then to Isfahan.

Shah Abbas the Great

The greatest Safavid, شاه عباس Shāh Abbās Shah Abbas I (ruled 1588–1629), came to the throne at seventeen in a kingdom falling apart. He rebuilt the army around soldiers loyal only to him – many of them Georgian, Armenian and Circassian converts – armed with muskets and cannon, won back Tabriz and Baghdad from the Ottomans and, with the help of English ships, drove the Portuguese from the island of Hormuz in 1622.

Abbas could be ruthless – he had his eldest son killed and two others blinded – but he was also a shrewd planner. He made the silk trade a royal monopoly, built roads and caravanserais, and in 1604 moved thousands of Armenians from Julfa on the Aras river to a new suburb of Isfahan, جلفا Jolfā New Julfa, where they became the merchants of Iran's silk trade with Europe.

Half the world

In 1598 Abbas made اصفهان Esfahān Isfahan his capital and rebuilt it around an immense square, نقش جهان Naghsh-e Jahān 'Image of the World', 560 metres long – a royal polo ground and marketplace in one. Around it rose the Shah Mosque with its great blue-tiled dome; the small and perfect mosque of شیخ لطف‌الله Sheykh Lotfollāh Sheikh Lotfollah, built for the royal household; the Ali Qapu palace, from whose balcony the shah watched polo; and the gateway to the great bazaar.

An avenue of plane trees, the چهارباغ Chahārbāgh, led down to the Zayandeh river, crossed by the 33 arches of سی‌وسه‌پل Si-o-se-pol and, later, by the Khaju bridge. European visitors compared Isfahan with London and Paris, and Iranians made up a rhyme that is still repeated today: اصفهان نصف جهان Esfahān nesf-e jahān Isfahan is half the world.

Art, faith and trade

Safavid Iran was one of the great artistic powers of its time. Royal workshops produced carpets – the Ardabil Carpet of 1539–40, now in the Victoria and Albert Museum in London, is one of the oldest dated carpets in the world – as well as silk textiles, tiles and illustrated manuscripts such as the Shahnameh of Shah Tahmasp, with its 258 paintings. At the same time Shi'a scholars, some invited from Lebanon and Iraq, built up the religious institutions whose influence is still felt today, and the mourning rituals of محرم Moharram Muharram became a central part of Iranian life.

The fall of Isfahan

After Abbas the dynasty slowly weakened. In 1722 an Afghan army under Mahmud Hotak besieged Isfahan for months, until the starving city surrendered and Shah Soltan Hossein handed over his crown. Order was restored by a soldier of genius, نادر شاه Nāder Shāh, who drove out the Afghans, ended the Safavid line in 1736 and in 1739 sacked Delhi, carrying home the Mughals' Peacock Throne and the Koh-i-Noor diamond. But the Safavid legacy outlasted them all: many historians see their state as the beginning of modern Iran.

Say it in Persian

Show

شاه اسماعیل در سال ۱۵۰۱ در تبریز تاج‌گذاری کرد.

Shāh Esmā'il dar sāl-e hezār-o-pānsad-o-yek dar Tabriz tāj-gozāri kard.

Shah Ismail was crowned in Tabriz in 1501.

صفویان مذهب شیعه را مذهب رسمی ایران کردند.

Safaviyān mazhab-e shi'e rā mazhab-e rasmi-ye Irān kardand.

The Safavids made Shi'ism the official religion of Iran.

شاه عباس پایتخت را به اصفهان برد.

Shāh Abbās pāytakht rā be Esfahān bord.

Shah Abbas moved the capital to Isfahan.

میدان نقش جهان یکی از بزرگ‌ترین میدان‌های جهان است.

meydān-e Naghsh-e Jahān yek-i az bozorg-tarin meydān-hā-ye jahān ast.

Naqsh-e Jahan Square is one of the largest squares in the world.

در اطراف میدان، مسجد، کاخ و بازار ساخته شد.

dar atrāf-e meydān, masjed, kākh va bāzār sākhte shod.

Mosques, a palace and a bazaar were built around the square.

شاه از ایوان عالی‌قاپو چوگان تماشا می‌کرد.

shāh az eyvān-e Āli-ghāpu chowgān tamāshā mi-kard.

The shah watched polo from the balcony of Ali Qapu.

مردم می‌گفتند: «اصفهان نصف جهان است.»

mardom mi-goftand: «Esfahān nesf-e jahān ast.»

People used to say: 'Isfahan is half the world.'

سی‌وسه‌پل سی و سه دهانه دارد.

Si-o-se-pol si o se dahāne dārad.

Si-o-se Pol has thirty-three arches.

ارمنی‌ها در محله‌ی جلفا کلیسای وانک را ساختند.

armani-hā dar mahalle-ye Jolfā kelisā-ye Vānk rā sākhtand.

The Armenians built the Vank Cathedral in the Julfa quarter.

فرش‌های دوره‌ی صفوی در موزه‌های بزرگ جهان هستند.

farsh-hā-ye dowre-ye Safavi dar muze-hā-ye bozorg-e jahān hastand.

Carpets from the Safavid period are in the great museums of the world.

در سال ۱۷۲۲ افغان‌ها اصفهان را گرفتند.

dar sāl-e hezār-o-haftsad-o-bist-o-do afghān-hā Esfahān rā gereftand.

In 1722 the Afghans took Isfahan.

نادرشاه دوباره ایران را متحد کرد.

Nāder-shāh dobāre Irān rā mottahed kard.

Nader Shah reunited Iran.
