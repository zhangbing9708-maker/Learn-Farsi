Culture / Food

قیمه و فسنجانGheymeh and fesenjan

gheyme o fesenjān

Two stews every Iranian knows: gheymeh, with split peas, tomato and a crown of crisp potato straws, and fesenjan, dark and glossy with ground walnuts and pomegranate molasses.

Facts

  • Gheymeh is the classic nazri of Muharram, cooked in huge pots and given away on Tasua and Ashura.
  • The crisp potato straws go on gheymeh only just before serving, so that they stay crunchy.
  • Rob-e anar, pomegranate molasses, is pomegranate juice boiled down until it is thick and dark.
  • In the hot–cold food system walnuts are 'hot' and pomegranates 'cold', so fesenjan is considered a balanced dish.
  • In Gilan fesenjan is often made with duck and tends to be more sour.
  • Qazvin's festive gheymeh nesar is rice with small pieces of lamb, barberries, pistachios and almonds.
  • Tehranis say fesenjun: in spoken Persian -ān often becomes -un, as nān 'bread' becomes nun.

Two classics

After ghormeh sabzi, the most famous Iranian stews are قیمه gheyme gheymeh and فسنجان fesenjān fesenjan – in Tehrani speech fesenjun. They could hardly be more different. Gheymeh is everyday comfort food: small cubes of meat with yellow split peas in a tomato sauce, sharpened with dried lime and crowned with crisp potato straws. Fesenjan is rich and festive: chicken or duck in a thick, dark, sweet-and-sour sauce of ground walnuts and pomegranate molasses, cooked for guests and for the cold months, when pomegranates are in season.

Gheymeh

Gheyme means finely chopped meat, and the lamb or beef is cut into small cubes. The other ingredients are لپه lape yellow split peas, onion, turmeric, رب گوجه‌فرنگی rob-e gowje-farangi tomato paste and dried limes; many cooks add a pinch of cinnamon, and some a little saffron at the end.

  1. Fry the onion and turmeric, add the meat and brown it.
  2. Stir in the tomato paste and fry it for a minute until it darkens.
  3. Add water and the pierced dried limes and simmer for about an hour and a half, adding the split peas for the last forty minutes – some cooks boil them separately so they keep their shape.
  4. Cut potatoes into thin matchsticks and fry them until crisp (سیب‌زمینی سرخ‌کرده sib-zamini-ye sorkh-karde fried potatoes).
  5. Serve with rice, and pile the potato straws on top just before eating so they stay crunchy.

A favourite variation is قیمه بادمجان gheyme bādemjān gheymeh with aubergine, topped with fried aubergine instead of, or as well as, potatoes.

Gheymeh as nazri

Gheymeh has a special place in religious life. In the first ten days of the month of محرم moharram Muharram, and above all on تاسوعا tāsu'ā Tasua and عاشورا āshurā Ashura, families, mosques and neighbourhood groups cook enormous pots (دیگ dig large cooking pot) of gheymeh and rice as نذری nazri votive food – food promised in a vow and given away to anyone who comes. People queue with their own bowls, and helping to stir the pot is itself thought to bring blessing.

Fesenjan

Fesenjan's sauce has two main ingredients: گردو gerdu walnuts, ground fine, and رب انار rob-e anār pomegranate molasses, the thick, sour syrup made by boiling down pomegranate juice. In the traditional hot–cold system walnuts are 'hot' and pomegranate 'cold', so the dish balances itself.

  1. Toast the ground walnuts in a dry pot for a few minutes, stirring.
  2. Add water and simmer gently for an hour or more, stirring now and then, until the walnut oil rises to the surface.
  3. Stir in pomegranate molasses, salt and – depending on your taste and your region – some sugar.
  4. Add browned pieces of chicken (or duck, or small meatballs) and simmer for another hour, until the sauce is thick and almost black.
  5. Taste, and balance ترش torsh sour against شیرین shirin sweet.

Sweet or sour?

That last step starts friendly arguments. Roughly speaking, the Caspian north likes its fesenjan sour – in گیلان Gilān Gilan it is often made with duck from the wetlands – while many cooks elsewhere add sugar for a ملس malas gently sweet-and-sour taste. Gheymeh has its regional stars too: Qazvin is famous for قیمه‌نثار gheyme-nesār gheymeh nesar, a festive rice dish with small pieces of lamb, barberries, pistachios and almonds.

Say it in Persian

Show

قیمه را با لپه و رب گوجه‌فرنگی درست می‌کنند.

gheyme rā bā lape va rob-e gowje-farangi dorost mi-konand.

Gheymeh is made with split peas and tomato paste.

رب را کمی تفت بده تا رنگش باز شود.

rob rā kami taft be-de tā rang-ash bāz shavad.

Fry the tomato paste briefly to bring out its colour.

Literally: sauté the paste a little so its colour opens

سیب‌زمینی را خلالی خرد کن و سرخ کن.

sib-zamini rā khalāli khord kon va sorkh kon.

Cut the potatoes into thin sticks and fry them.

سیب‌زمینی سرخ‌کرده را آخر کار روی خورش بریز.

sib-zamini-ye sorkh-karde rā ākhar-e kār ru-ye khoresh be-riz.

Put the fried potatoes on the stew at the very end.

در محرم خیلی‌ها قیمه‌ی نذری می‌پزند.

dar moharram kheyli-hā gheyme-ye nazri mi-pazand.

In Muharram many people cook gheymeh to give away.

فسنجان را با گردو و رب انار می‌پزند.

fesenjān rā bā gerdu va rob-e anār mi-pazand.

Fesenjan is cooked with walnuts and pomegranate molasses.

گردو باید آن‌قدر بپزد که روغن بیندازد.

gerdu bāyad ān-ghadr be-pazad ke rowghan bi-yandāzad.

The walnuts must cook until they release their oil.

شما فسنجان شیرین دوست دارید یا ترش؟

shomā fesenjān-e shirin dust dārid yā torsh?

Do you like your fesenjan sweet or sour?

Spoken شما فسنجون شیرین دوست دارین یا ترش؟ shomā fesenjun-e shirin dust dārin yā torsh?

در گیلان فسنجان را با اردک هم درست می‌کنند.

dar Gilān fesenjān rā bā ordak ham dorost mi-konand.

In Gilan they also make fesenjan with duck.

این فسنجان واقعاً عالی شده است!

in fesenjān vāghe'an āli shode ast!

This fesenjan has turned out really wonderful!

Spoken این فسنجون واقعاً عالی شده! in fesenjun vāghe'an āli shode!

آخر هفته فسنجان می‌پزم؛ برای ناهار تشریف بیاورید.

ākhar-e hafte fesenjān mi-pazam; barā-ye nāhār tashrif bi-yāvarid.

I'm making fesenjan at the weekend – please come for lunch.
