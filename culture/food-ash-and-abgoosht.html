آش و آبگوشتAsh and abgoosht

āsh o ābgusht

Thick soups of herbs, beans and noodles, and a slow-cooked lamb broth that is eaten in two courses: ash and abgoosht are the hearty, old-fashioned heart of Iranian home cooking.

Facts

  • The Persian words āshpaz 'cook' and āshpazkhāne 'kitchen' are built on āsh.
  • Ash-e posht-e pa is cooked a few days after a traveller leaves, to wish them a safe return.
  • Dizi takes its name from the stone or clay pot it is cooked and served in.
  • Kashk is made from yoghurt or buttermilk that is drained and dried; it is sold as hard balls or as a thick liquid.
  • Abgoosht is eaten in two stages: first the broth with bread (terid), then the pounded meat and beans (gusht-kubideh).
  • The idiom nokhod-e har ashi, 'the chickpea in every ash', describes a busybody.

The soup that named the cook

آش āsh thick soup is so central to Iranian cooking that it named the cook and the kitchen: a cook is an آشپز āshpaz 'ash-cooker' and a kitchen an آشپزخانه āshpazkhāne 'ash-cooker's house'. An ash is thicker than a soup and thinner than a stew – a meal in a bowl of beans, grains and herbs, often with noodles or meat. There are dozens: barley آش جو āsh-e jow barley ash, sour آش انار āsh-e anār pomegranate ash, the yoghurt آش دوغ āsh-e dugh doogh ash of Azerbaijan and many more.

Ash has given Persian many sayings. A busybody is نخود هر آشی nokhod-e har āshi the chickpea in every ash; someone blamed for a pleasure they never had has آش نخورده و دهان سوخته āsh na-khorde va dahān sukhte eaten no ash but burnt their mouth; and when nothing has changed, it is همان آش و همان کاسه hamān āsh o hamān kāse the same ash and the same bowl.

Ash reshteh

The best-loved ash is آش رشته āsh-e reshte noodle ash. It is the dish of cold evenings and of Nowruz, when its noodles (رشته reshte noodles; thread) stand for the threads of fortune you take hold of in the new year. It is also آش پشت پا āsh-e posht-e pā 'behind-the-feet' ash, cooked a few days after someone sets off on a long journey, to wish them a safe return.

  1. Soak chickpeas (نخود nokhod chickpeas), kidney beans and lentils (عدس adas lentils) overnight, then cook them until tender.
  2. Add plenty of chopped herbs – spinach, parsley, coriander, dill and leek or chives – and simmer for half an hour.
  3. Add the flat noodles and cook until they are soft and the ash is thick.
  4. Stir in some diluted کشک kashk kashk, fermented whey.
  5. Top each bowl with more kashk, fried onions (پیازداغ piyāz-dāgh fried onions), fried garlic and dried mint fried in oil (نعناداغ na'nā-dāgh fried mint).

Ash as nazri

Like gheymeh and sholeh zard, ash is a favourite نذری nazri votive food. A family that has made a vow cooks a huge pot in the courtyard or the street, neighbours take turns to stir it and make a wish, and the ash is ladled into the bowls of friends and passers-by. Sharing the work is part of the point: it is a neighbourhood occasion as much as a religious one.

Abgoosht and dizi

آبگوشت ābgusht abgoosht means 'meat water': lamb on the bone with chickpeas, white beans, potatoes, tomatoes, onion, turmeric and a dried lime, cooked for hours until the meat falls apart. Traditionally a piece of tail fat (دنبه dombe sheep's tail fat) goes in for richness. It is often cooked and served in individual stone or clay pots called دیزی dizi dizi, so the dish itself is called dizi too. Once the lunch of bazaar workers and labourers, it is now served in دیزی‌سرا dizi-sarā dizi restaurants and traditional teahouses, and almost every region has its own version.

How to eat dizi

Dizi is eaten in two courses, and there is a method:

  1. Tear bread – سنگک sangak sangak is best – into small pieces in a bowl.
  2. Pour the broth from the pot over the bread and eat this first; it is called ترید terid bread soaked in broth.
  3. Tip the solids – meat, chickpeas, beans, potato and fat – into a second bowl and pound them with a pestle (گوشت‌کوب gusht-kub meat pounder) into a thick paste, گوشت‌کوبیده gusht-kubide pounded meat.
  4. Eat the paste with more bread, raw onion, fresh herbs, pickles and a glass of doogh. A long nap afterwards is traditional too.

مادرم برای نوروز آش رشته می‌پزد.

mādar-am barā-ye nowruz āsh-e reshte mi-pazad.

My mother cooks ash reshteh for Nowruz.

روی آش کشک و پیازداغ و نعناداغ بریز.

ru-ye āsh kashk o piyāz-dāgh o na'nā-dāgh be-riz.

Top the ash with kashk, fried onions and fried mint.

رشته را آخر از همه به آش اضافه کن.

reshte rā ākhar az hame be āsh ezāfe kon.

Add the noodles to the ash last of all.

وقتی کسی به سفر می‌رود، برایش آش پشت پا می‌پزند.

vaghti kasi be safar mi-ravad, barā-yash āsh-e posht-e pā mi-pazand.

When someone goes on a journey, people cook 'posht-e pa' ash for them.

یک کاسه آش داغ در زمستان خیلی می‌چسبد.

yek kāse āsh-e dāgh dar zemestān kheyli mi-chasbad.

A bowl of hot ash really hits the spot in winter.

Literally: a bowl of hot ash sticks very much in winter

آبگوشت را باید ساعت‌ها روی آتش ملایم پخت.

ābgusht rā bāyad sā'at-hā ru-ye ātash-e molāyem pokht.

Abgoosht has to cook for hours over a low flame.

اول نان را در آب دیزی ترید کن.

avval nān rā dar āb-e dizi terid kon.

First soak the bread in the dizi broth.

بعد گوشت و نخود را با گوشت‌کوب بکوب.

ba'd gusht o nokhod rā bā gusht-kub be-kub.

Then pound the meat and chickpeas with the pestle.

آبگوشت را با پیاز خام و سبزی و ترشی می‌خوریم.

ābgusht rā bā piyāz-e khām va sabzi va torshi mi-khorim.

We eat abgoosht with raw onion, herbs and pickles.

بیا به دیزی‌سرا برویم؛ من مهمانت می‌کنم.

bi-yā be dizi-sarā be-ravim; man mehmān-at mi-konam.

Let's go to a dizi house – my treat.

Literally: come, let's go to the dizi house; I'll make you my guest

Spoken بیا بریم دیزی‌سرا؛ من مهمونت می‌کنم. bi-yā be-rim dizi-sarā; man mehmun-et mi-konam.

نخود هر آشی نباش!

nokhod-e har āshi na-bāsh!

Don't poke your nose into everything!

Literally: don't be the chickpea of every ash
