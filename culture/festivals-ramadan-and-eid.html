Culture / Festivals

ماه رمضان و عید فطرRamadan and Eid al-Fitr

māh-e Ramezān va eyd-e fetr

During Ramadan many Iranians fast from dawn to sunset, rising in the dark for sahari and breaking the fast at dusk with dates, soup and syrupy zoolbia and bamieh. The month ends with the new moon and the feast of Eid al-Fitr.

Facts

  • Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic lunar calendar and moves about eleven days earlier each year.
  • The fast runs from dawn to sunset; children, the sick, travellers and the very old are excused.
  • Zoolbia and bamieh, fried dough soaked in saffron and rose-water syrup, are the classic sweets of Ramadan.
  • The 21st of Ramadan, the anniversary of Imam Ali's death, is a public holiday in Iran.
  • Eid al-Fitr falls on 1 Shavvāl, once the new crescent moon has been sighted.
  • Fetriyeh, the Eid charity, is paid for every member of the household before the Eid prayer.
  • Mohammad-Reza Shajarian's recording of the Rabbana prayer has been part of Ramadan evenings in Iran for decades.

The month of fasting

رمضان Ramezān Ramadan, the ninth month of the Islamic lunar calendar, is the month of fasting, روزه ruze the fast. From dawn until sunset those who fast take no food, no drink – not even water – and no cigarettes. Children, the sick, travellers, pregnant women and the very old are excused. Like Muharram, Ramadan moves through the seasons, about eleven days earlier each year, so a summer Ramadan with its long, hot days is far harder than a winter one. Life slows by day and wakes up after dark: offices close early, and the evenings are full of visits, prayers and television series made for the month.

Before dawn: sahari

The day begins in the dark. An hour or so before dawn, families get up for سحری sahari the pre-dawn meal: rice and stew left over from the evening, bread, cheese, dates and plenty of tea and water. Radio and television broadcast prayers and Quran recitation, and when the morning اذان azān call to prayer sounds, eating stops. Many people pray and then go back to bed for a few hours before work.

Sunset: iftar

As sunset nears, the streets fill with people hurrying home, and bakeries and sweet shops have queues. For decades Iranians have waited for this moment listening to the singer Mohammad-Reza Shajarian's recording of the prayer ربنا Rabbanā 'Our Lord'. At the sunset call to prayer the fast is broken, افطار eftār iftar, usually with dates and warm water or tea, then نان و پنیر و سبزی nān o panir o sabzi bread, cheese and herbs, a bowl of آش āsh thick soup or حلیم halim wheat-and-meat porridge, and the sweets of the month: زولبیا و بامیه zulbiyā va bāmiye zoolbia and bamieh, fried dough soaked in saffron and rose-water syrup. Inviting friends to iftar, افطاری دادن eftāri dādan giving an iftar, is a great kindness, and mosques and shrines offer free iftars to all.

The Nights of Qadr

In the last third of the month come the شب‌های قدر shab-hā-ye ghadr Nights of Qadr – the 19th, 21st and 23rd – when, believers hold, the Quran was first revealed and the destiny of the coming year is decided. Mosques stay full until dawn with prayers and readings, and at the high point worshippers hold a copy of the Quran on their heads. The 19th and 21st also mark the wounding and death of Imam Ali, so these nights mix devotion with mourning; the 21st is a public holiday.

Eid al-Fitr

The month ends when the new crescent moon is sighted, and the next day, 1 شوال Shavvāl Shawwal, is عید فطر eyd-e fetr Eid al-Fitr. Because it depends on the moon, people often learn only the evening before whether tomorrow is Eid. In the morning crowds gather for the Eid prayer in large mosques and prayer grounds, and every family pays فطریه fetriye the Eid charity – the price of about three kilos of wheat or rice for each member of the household – to the poor. Then come visits to relatives, sweets and a long lunch. The greetings are عید فطر مبارک eyd-e fetr mobārak happy Eid al-Fitr and طاعات قبول tā'āt ghabul may your devotions be accepted.

If you are visiting

Visitors are not expected to fast, but in Iran eating, drinking or smoking in public during fasting hours is against the law as well as impolite. Hotels and some restaurants serve travellers discreetly; otherwise eat in private. If you are invited to an iftar, go – it is one of the warmest meals of the year – and arrive before sunset.

Say it in Persian

Show

عید فطر مبارک!

eyd-e fetr mobārak!

Happy Eid al-Fitr!

طاعات قبول!

tā'āt ghabul!

May your prayers and fasting be accepted!

A greeting for the end of Ramadan and for Eid.

روزه‌هایتان قبول باشد.

ruze-hā-yetān ghabul bāshad.

May your fasts be accepted.

Spoken روزه‌هاتون قبول باشه. ruze-hā-tun ghabul bāshe.

ببخشید، من روزه‌ام.

bebakhshid, man ruze-am.

Sorry, I'm fasting.

A polite way to turn down food or drink during the day.

ساعت چهار برای سحری بیدار شدیم.

sā'at-e chahār barā-ye sahari bidār shodim.

We got up at four for the pre-dawn meal.

اذان را گفتند؛ بفرمایید افطار!

azān rā goftand; befarmāyid eftār!

There's the call to prayer – come and break your fast!

روزه‌مان را با خرما و چای باز می‌کنیم.

ruze-mān rā bā khormā va chāy bāz mi-konim.

We break our fast with dates and tea.

سر سفره‌ی افطار زولبیا و بامیه هم داریم.

sar-e sofre-ye eftār zulbiyā va bāmiye ham dārim.

We have zoolbia and bamieh on the iftar table too.

امشب ما را برای افطار دعوت کرده‌اند.

emshab mā rā barā-ye eftār da'vat karde-and.

We've been invited to an iftar tonight.

در شب‌های قدر مردم تا صبح دعا می‌خوانند.

dar shab-hā-ye ghadr mardom tā sobh do'ā mi-khānand.

On the Nights of Qadr people pray until morning.

ماه را دیده‌اند؛ فردا عید است.

māh rā dide-and; fardā eyd ast.

The new moon has been seen – tomorrow is Eid.

فطریه را قبل از نماز عید می‌دهیم.

fetriye rā ghabl az namāz-e eyd mi-dahim.

We pay the fetriyeh before the Eid prayer.
