# Syllabus – what each lesson teaches

Every lesson follows `CONTENT_GUIDE.md`. Use only grammar that has been taught in earlier lessons (or teach it
in the lesson). Recycle vocabulary from earlier lessons. Names to reuse across the course: Mina (مینا), Reza
(رضا), Sara (سارا), Amir (امیر), Mr Karimi (آقای کریمی), Ms Ahmadi (خانم احمدی), Tom (تام, an Australian
student in Tehran), Leila (لیلا), Dariush (داریوش), Mahsa (مهسا). Places: Tehran, Isfahan, Shiraz, Yazd.

## Script (reading and writing) – ids `script-01` … `script-08`

Audio for letters and example words already exists in `data/alphabets.json`; the `letters` block shows them.
Each lesson: a `text` block on what the letters sound like and how they join, a `letters` block, a `vocab`
block of 10–16 real words spelled only with letters taught so far (with meaning), `tips`, then exercises:
`mcq` (which word is this? / which letter?), `listen` (hear a word, choose the spelling), `match`.

1. **How Persian is written** – right to left, 32 letters, four shapes, no capitals, short vowels usually
   unwritten (a e o), long vowels written (ā u i), the ZWNJ, Persian vs Arabic letters, the three dots.
   No `letters` block; a `vocab` block of 8 international words readers can already guess (تاکسی, هتل, تلفن…) written out for them.
2. **ا ب پ ت ث** + the vowels: آ/ا, the marks fatha/kasra/damma (shown but explained as learning aids), words: آب، بابا، تب، باب…
3. **ج چ ح خ د ذ** – words with these.
4. **ر ز ژ س ش**
5. **ص ض ط ظ ع غ** – the "Arabic" letters, same sounds as س ز ت ز ' گه (gh), why Persian keeps them, ع and the glottal stop.
6. **ف ق ک گ ل م** – ق vs غ both gh; ک گ final shapes.
7. **ن و ه ی** – و as v/u/o/ow; ه as h and final silent e, the two-eyed ه in the middle; ی as y/i/ey; hamza seats (ئ أ ؤ); ezafe writing (خانه‌ی).
8. **Numbers and reading practice** – Persian digits ۰–۹ (written left to right!), punctuation ، ؛ ؟ «», reading 20 real signs and words, handwriting notes, typing Persian (keyboard layout), Nastaliq vs Naskh fonts.

## B1 – ids `b1-01` … `b1-14`

1. **Passive and impersonal** – شدن passive (ساخته شد), impersonal با -ند (می‌گویند که), آدم/یکی, when Persian avoids the passive.
2. **Making things happen** – causatives (-ān-: خوراندن، رساندن، نشاندن), دادن + infinitive, گذاشتن (let).
3. **He said that** – reported speech (no tense shift), که, گفت که می‌آید, indirect questions, پرسید که آیا.
4. **The formal future** – خواهم + short infinitive, when it is used (news, writing), vs spoken future; می‌رود که.
5. **If only** – unreal conditions: اگر … می‌رفتم, past subjunctive (رفته باشم), کاش, باید + past (باید می‌رفتی).
6. **Building words** – suffixes -ی -گر -گار -بان -چی -ستان -دان -زار -گاه -مند -ناک -آمیز -انه, prefixes نا- بی- هم- باز- پیش- پس-.
7. **Arabic in Persian** – Arabic roots and patterns learners meet (مفعول، تفعیل، استفعال…), broken plurals (کتب، علما), تنوین adverbs (لطفاً، مثلاً), ة → ت/ه.
8. **Formal and informal** – registers: book Persian vs spoken; honorifics (جناب، سرکار); plural of respect (ایشان، آمدند for one person); letter openings.
9. **Proverbs and idioms** – 25 proverbs with stories and usage.
10. **Reading the news** – headline grammar (dropped verbs, nominal style), news verbs (اعلام کرد، افزود، تأکید کرد، گزارش داد), numbers and dates in news, two sample news items with gloss.
11. **Writing well** – paragraph structure, connectors (بنابراین، با این حال، علاوه بر این، از یک سو…), punctuation, a sample essay.
12. **First steps in poetry** – how to read a ghazal: بیت, مصراع, قافیه, ردیف, تخلص, metre basics; Saadi's بنی‌آدم line by line.
13. **Telling a story** – narrative tenses together, یکی بود یکی نبود, time connectors, a model story to retell.
14. **Debate** – arguing, conceding, interrupting politely; a model debate on city vs village.

## B2 – ids `b2-01` … `b2-09`

1. **Hafez** – a full ghazal (الا یا ایها الساقی) with word-by-word gloss, imagery (ساقی، می، زلف), how Iranians use Hafez (فال حافظ).
2. **Rumi and the reed** – نی‌نامه opening 18 lines, Sufi vocabulary, masnavi form.
3. **Saadi and Ferdowsi** – Golestan prose style; Shahnameh epic style; توانا بود هر که دانا بود.
4. **Literary Persian** – literary word order, verb at the end variations, archaic forms (بُوَد، همی، -ا), literary vocabulary vs spoken.
5. **Persian through the ages** – Old Persian (cuneiform of Darius), Middle Persian (Pahlavi), New Persian; sample words then and now; Zoroastrian texts.
6. **Dari and Tajik** – differences in pronunciation (majhul vowels), vocabulary, Cyrillic Tajik alphabet with examples, sample sentences in all three.
7. **Dialects and accents** – Tehrani, Isfahani, Shirazi, Mashhadi, Yazdi features; Gilaki/Mazandarani/Kurdish/Lori/Azeri as other languages of Iran.
8. **Advanced idioms** – 30 idioms with body parts (دل، دست، سر، چشم), their literal images, registers.
9. **Humour and wordplay** – jokes, Mullah Nasreddin, Obeyd Zakani (public domain), puns (جناس), satire vocabulary.

## C1 – ids `c1-01` … `c1-05`

1. **Academic Persian** – abstracts, citation phrases, nominalisation, passive avoidance, Persian academic vocabulary vs Arabic loans, a sample abstract.
2. **Translation workshop** – translating 10 sentences each way with commentary on choices; untranslatable words (تعارف، دلتنگی، غیرت…).
3. **Rhetoric and style** – figures of speech: تشبیه، استعاره، کنایه، مبالغه، تضاد; speeches; how to give a toast.
4. **Persian words in English** – 40 words (bazaar, paradise, pyjamas, caravan, kiosk, checkmate, lemon, orange, spinach, tulip, jasmine, candy, sugar, khaki, shawl, divan, lilac, pistachio, saffron, taffeta, turban, jackal, magic, peach, scarlet, rook, spinach, julep, musk, mummy, sherbet, algorithm (via Khwarazmi), azure, lapis…) with Persian originals and routes.
5. **Calligraphy and the written word** – scripts (کوفی، نسخ، ثلث، نستعلیق، شکسته), reading Nastaliq, siyāh-mashq, how to hold the reed pen, famous calligraphers (Mir Emad), reading a decorative inscription.
