در شهر: تاکسی، مترو و مسیرGetting around: taxi, metro and directions

dar shahr: tāksi, metro o masir

Tehran is a city of twelve million people, shared taxis, a clean metro and legendary traffic. In this lesson you learn to take a taxi and the metro, ask the way and understand the answer – and you meet the imperative, the verb form for telling people what to do: go, turn, stop.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • take a taxi and say where you want to go
  • ask for and understand simple directions (right, left, straight on)
  • use the metro and buses
  • give instructions with the imperative: برو، بپیچ، بایستید

In a taxi

Tom stops a taxi in Tehran to go to Tajrish bazaar in the north of the city.

Tom

سلام، آقا. دربست تجریش؟

salām, āghā. darbast Tajrish?

Hello, sir. Private taxi to Tajrish?

Driver

بفرمایید، بنشینید. تجریش کجا؟

befarmāyid, beneshinid. Tajrish kojā?

Please, sit down. Where in Tajrish?

Spoken بفرمایید، بشینید. تجریش کجا؟ befarmāyid, beshinid. Tajrish kojā?

Tom

بازار تجریش، لطفاً. چقدر می‌شود؟

bāzār-e Tajrish, lotfan. cheghadr mi-shavad?

Tajrish bazaar, please. How much will it be?

Spoken بازار تجریش، لطفاً. چقدر می‌شه؟ bāzār-e Tajrish, lotfan. cheghadr mi-she?

Driver

صد و پنجاه تومان.

sad o panjāh tumān.

A hundred and fifty (thousand) toman.

Tom

خیلی گران است! صد تومان خوب است؟

kheyli gerān ast! sad tumān khub ast?

That's very expensive! Is a hundred okay?

Spoken خیلی گرونه! صد تومن خوبه؟ kheyli gerune! sad toman khube?

Driver

باشد، صد و بیست. ترافیک زیاد است.

bāshad, sad o bist. terāfik ziyād ast.

Fine, a hundred and twenty. There's a lot of traffic.

Spoken باشه، صد و بیست. ترافیک زیاده. bāshe, sad o bist. terāfik ziyāde.

Tom

باشد. از خیابان ولیعصر بروید، لطفاً.

bāshad. az khiyābān-e Vali'asr beravid, lotfan.

Okay. Go along Valiasr Street, please.

Driver

چشم. شما ایرانی نیستید؟ فارسی خوب صحبت می‌کنید!

chashm. shomā irāni nistid? fārsi khub sohbat mi-konid!

Certainly. You're not Iranian? You speak Persian well!

Tom

نه، استرالیایی هستم. ممنون! این‌جا نگه دارید، لطفاً.

na, ostorāliyāyi hastam. mamnun! in-jā negah dārid, lotfan.

No, I'm Australian. Thanks! Stop here, please.

Driver

بفرمایید. خدا حافظ!

befarmāyid. khodā hāfez!

Here you are. Goodbye!

Taxis: darbast or shared?

Iranian cities run on taxis, and there are two kinds. A دربست darbast private taxi (literally door-closed) takes only you, to wherever you say; you agree the price first – or use an app such as Snapp, where the price is fixed. A shared taxi, تاکسی خطی tāksi-ye khatti line taxi, runs along a fixed route and picks up anyone going the same way: you stand at the roadside and call out your destination – آزادی! Āzādi! – and the driver stops if he is going there.

To stop a taxi, hold out your hand and say where you are going. To get out, say این‌جا نگه دارید. in-jā negah dārid. Stop here. or همین‌جا پیاده می‌شوم. hamin-jā piyāde mi-shavam. I'll get out right here.

The metro, مترو metro, is the fastest way across Tehran: seven lines, cheap tickets, and the first and last carriages reserved for women. The word for station is ایستگاه istgāh – from the verb to stand, so a station is literally a standing-place.

The imperative: telling someone what to do

To give an instruction, Persian takes the present stem of the verb (the part after می in the present tense) and puts be- (بـ) in front of it. For one friend, that is the whole word; for a stranger or several people, add the polite ending -id.

So from می‌روید mi-ravid you go you get بروید! beravid! Go!, and from می‌پیچید mi-pichid you turn you get بپیچید! bepichid! Turn!.

Two things to notice. First, be- becomes bi- in front of a stem that starts with the long vowel ā: بیایید biyāyid come, بیاورید biyāvarid bring. Second, with compound verbs such as نگه داشتن negah dāshtan the be- is usually dropped: نگه دارید negah dārid stop, صحبت کنید sohbat konid speak.

To tell someone not to do something, replace be- with na-: نروید! na-ravid! Don't go!, نپیچید na-pichid don't turn. Always add لطفاً lotfan – an imperative without it sounds like an order.

Asking the way, and 'by taxi'

You know کجاست؟ kojāst? where is? from lesson 8. For directions, add how to get there: چطور بروم؟ chetor beravam? how do I get there? – literally how should I go. (That beravam is the same be- form as the imperative, with the I ending; you will meet it properly in the next lesson.)

For by taxi, by bus, by metro, Persian uses با bā with: با تاکسی bā tāksi by taxi, با مترو bā metro by metro. On foot is پیاده piyāde.

Distance words: دور dur far, نزدیک nazdik near, and the useful answers پنج دقیقه پیاده panj daghighe piyāde five minutes on foot and ایستگاه بعد istgāh-e ba'd the next station.

ببخشید، ایستگاه مترو کجاست؟

bebakhshid, istgāh-e metro kojāst?

Excuse me, where is the metro station?

مستقیم بروید، سر چهارراه بپیچید به راست.

mostaghim beravid, sar-e chahār-rāh bepichid be rāst.

Go straight on, at the crossroads turn right.

دور است؟ نه، پنج دقیقه پیاده است.

dur ast? na, panj daghighe piyāde ast.

Is it far? No, it's five minutes on foot.

من هر روز با مترو به سر کار می‌روم.

man har ruz bā metro be sar-e kār mi-ravam.

I go to work by metro every day.

در ایستگاه بعد پیاده شوید.

dar istgāh-e ba'd piyāde shavid.

Get off at the next station.

Tom finds the museum

Tom wants to visit the National Museum. Read how he gets there.

تام می‌خواهد به موزه‌ی ملی برود.

Tām mi-khāhad be muze-ye melli beravad.

Tom wants to go to the National Museum.

او از یک خانم می‌پرسد: «ببخشید، موزه کجاست؟»

u az yek khānom mi-porsad: «bebakhshid, muze kojāst?»

He asks a lady: "Excuse me, where is the museum?"

خانم می‌گوید: «با مترو بروید. در ایستگاه امام خمینی پیاده شوید.»

khānom mi-guyad: «bā metro beravid. dar istgāh-e Emām Khomeyni piyāde shavid.»

The lady says: "Go by metro. Get off at Imam Khomeini station."

«بعد مستقیم بروید و سر چهارراه بپیچید به چپ.»

«ba'd mostaghim beravid o sar-e chahār-rāh bepichid be chap.»

"Then go straight on and at the crossroads turn left."

تام یک بلیت می‌خرد و سوار مترو می‌شود.

Tām yek belit mi-kharad o savār-e metro mi-shavad.

Tom buys a ticket and gets on the metro.

مترو پر است، اما سریع است.

metro por ast, ammā sari' ast.

The metro is full, but it is fast.

بعد از ده دقیقه پیاده، تام جلوی موزه است.

ba'd az dah daghighe piyāde, Tām jelo-ye muze ast.

After ten minutes on foot, Tom is in front of the museum.

او می‌گوید: «خیلی ممنون، خانم!»

u mi-guyad: «kheyli mamnun, khānom!»

He says: "Thank you very much, madam!"

Tehran traffic and the art of the shared taxi

Tehran's traffic is a national joke and a daily reality: a trip that takes twenty minutes at night can take two hours at six in the evening. That is why the metro, opened in 1999, is loved – and why the تاکسی خطی tāksi-ye khatti shared taxi survives. On the main avenues a stream of orange and yellow cars crawls along the kerb while would-be passengers shout destinations: Vanak! Tajrish! Enghelāb! Four strangers share the back seat and the front, pay a few thousand tomans each, and nobody speaks unless the driver has an opinion about the traffic, which he always does.

The road that matters most is خیابان ولیعصر khiyābān-e Vali'asr, which runs almost 18 km from the railway station in the south to Tajrish in the north under a canopy of plane trees – the longest street in the Middle East. Going up (بالا) means north and richer; going down (پایین) means south and older. Tehranis give directions in those terms, so learn them: بالاتر bālātar further up, پایین‌تر pāyin-tar further down.

Tips

  • Taxi prices are quoted in tomans, and drivers drop the thousands: sad tumān in a Tehran taxi means 100,000 tomans. Agree the price before you get in, or use an app.
  • chashm (literally eye) is how a driver, waiter or shopkeeper says 'certainly'. Answering a request with chashm is very polite; you can use it too.
  • In spoken Persian the polite imperative ending -id becomes -in: beravid → berin, bepichid → bepichin, negah dārid → negah dārin. The friendly form boro stays boro.
  • Place names are addresses: Tehranis say meydān-e Āzādi, Enghelāb, Vanak and expect you to know them. Learn the metro map and you have learned the city.
