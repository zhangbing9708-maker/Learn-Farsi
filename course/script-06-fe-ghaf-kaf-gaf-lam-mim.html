ف، ق، ک، گ، ل، مFe, ghāf, kāf, gāf, lām, mim

fe, ghāf, kāf, gāf, lām, mim

Six letters, and with them some of the first words anyone learns: salām, ketāb, kelās. You meet the second way of writing gh, the slanted letters k and g, and lām-alef, the one place where two letters melt into a single shape.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • read and write ف ق and tell them apart by their dots and end shapes
  • know that ق and غ are both pronounced gh
  • read and write ک گ ل م in all their shapes
  • recognise lām-alef لا as two letters
  • read words such as salām, ketāb, ghalam and kelās

Fe and ghāf – loops with dots

ف fe is a small round loop on a short stem, with one dot above. ق ghāf is the same loop with two dots. At the start or in the middle of a word only the dots tell them apart: فـ ـفـ, قـ ـقـ. Alone or at the end they part ways: fe ends in a long flat stroke along the line (ف), while ghāf drops into a deep round bowl below it (ق).

Fe is f as in fish: برف barf snow, فرش farsh carpet. Ghāf is the gh you learned with غ gheyn in lesson 5: in standard Persian the two letters sound exactly the same. So قرمز ghermez red begins with the same sound that ends مرغ morgh hen, written two different ways. Ghāf turns up mostly in words from Arabic and Turkish, such as قلم ghalam pen and اتاق otāgh room. Some regional accents, and the Dari Persian of Afghanistan, still say a q for ق.

Kāf and gāf – the slanted strokes

ک kāf is a tall stroke slanting up to the left from a flat base. Alone or at the end of a word the base is long: ک, ـک. At the start or in the middle it shrinks: کـ, ـکـ. گ gāf is the same letter with a second slanted stroke on top – one of the four letters Persian added to the Arabic alphabet: گ, گـ, ـگـ, ـگ.

Kāf is k: کتاب ketāb book. Gāf is always a hard g, as in go, never as in gem: گل gol flower, بزرگ bozorg big. Count the strokes the way you count dots – one is k, two is g.

Arabic writes kāf differently at the end of a word, with a little squiggle inside instead of the slanted stroke. On a computer the two are different characters, so make sure you always use the Persian ک – lesson 8 shows you how.

Lām, mim – and lām-alef

ل lām is a tall upright stroke, like alef, but it joins on both sides. At the start and in the middle it is just the tall stroke: لـ, ـلـ. Alone or at the end it curves into a deep hook below the line: ل, ـل. Lām is l.

م mim is a small round loop. At the start of a word it sits on the line (مـ), in the middle it hangs just below it (ـمـ), and alone or at the end it grows a tail straight down: م, ـم. Mim is m: مادر mādar mother.

One pair of letters has a shape of its own. When lām is followed by alef, the two melt into lām-alef, a single V-shaped sign: لا lā. You see it in the most useful word of all, سلام salām hello – s, lā, m – and in کلاس kelās class. Lām-alef is still two letters, l + ā.

Reading tips

  • ق and غ sound the same. When you hear gh, you cannot know the letter – learn it with the word.
  • One slanted stroke is ک (k), two are گ (g). Like the dots, the extra stroke is usually added after the rest of the word is written.
  • لا lā is two letters, lām + alef – not a new letter.
  • At the end of a word, ف lies flat on the line and ق drops into a round bowl.
  • Practise writing سلام salām until it flows: sin's teeth, lām-alef, then mim with its tail. It is the first word you will write to an Iranian friend.
