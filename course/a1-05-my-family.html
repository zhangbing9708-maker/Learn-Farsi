Course / Beginner (A1) / Lesson 5

خانواده‌ی منMy family

khānevāde-ye man

Get out your phone and show someone a photo of your family. In this lesson you learn the words for the people in your family – including four different uncles and aunts – how to say 'my', 'your' and 'his' with short endings, how to say whose someone is, and how to use 'to have' for brothers, sisters and children.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • name the people in your family
  • say my, your, his / her… with the endings -am, -at, -ash, -emān, -etān, -eshān
  • say whose someone is with the ezafe: mādar-e Sārā
  • use dāshtan in the present: dāram, dāri, dārad…
  • say how old the people in your family are

A family photo

After class, Sara shows Tom a photo of her family on her phone.

Tom

این عکس خانواده‌ی توست؟

in aks-e khānevāde-ye to-st?

Is this a photo of your family?

Sara

بله، این خانواده‌ام است. این پدرم است و این مادرم.

bale, in khānevāde-am ast. in pedar-am ast o in mādar-am.

Yes, this is my family. This is my father and this is my mother.

Spoken آره، این خانواده‌مه. این بابامه، این هم مامانم. āre, in khānevāde-m-e. in bābā-m-e, in ham māmān-am.

Tom

پدرت چند سال دارد؟

pedar-at chand sāl dārad?

How old is your father?

Spoken بابات چند سالشه؟ bābā-t chand sāl-esh-e?

Sara

پنجاه و هشت سال. مادرم پنجاه و دو سال دارد.

panjāh o hasht sāl. mādar-am panjāh o do sāl dārad.

Fifty-eight. My mother is fifty-two.

Tom

تو خواهر و برادر داری؟

to khāhar o barādar dāri?

Do you have brothers and sisters?

Sara

بله، یک برادر و دو خواهر دارم.

bale, yek barādar o do khāhar dāram.

Yes, I have one brother and two sisters.

Spoken آره، یه برادر و دو تا خواهر دارم. āre, ye barādar o do tā khāhar dāram.

Tom

اسم برادرت چیست؟

esm-e barādar-at chist?

What's your brother's name?

Spoken اسم برادرت چیه؟ esm-e barādar-et chi-ye?

Sara

اسمش داریوش است. این پسر کوچک هم پسرش است.

esm-ash Dāriyush ast. in pesar-e kuchak ham pesar-ash ast.

His name is Dariush. And this little boy is his son.

Tom

و این خانم کیست؟

va in khānom kist?

And who is this lady?

Sara

مادربزرگم است. او هشتاد و یک سال دارد!

mādar-bozorg-am ast. u hashtād o yek sāl dārad!

That's my grandmother. She's eighty-one!

Tom

خانواده‌ات خیلی بزرگ است!

khānevāde-at kheyli bozorg ast!

Your family is very big!

Spoken خانواده‌ت خیلی بزرگه! khānevāde-t kheyli bozorg-e!

Sara

خانواده‌ی تو چطور؟ شما چند نفر هستید؟

khānevāde-ye to chetor? shomā chand nafar hastid?

What about your family? How many of you are there?

Tom

ما چهار نفر هستیم: پدرم، مادرم، خواهرم و من.

mā chahār nafar hastim: pedar-am, mādar-am, khāhar-am o man.

There are four of us: my father, my mother, my sister and me.

Pedar, mādar, barādar

Family words are another place where Persian and English show that they are relatives: پدر pedar father (Latin pater), مادر mādar mother, برادر barādar brother and دختر dokhtar daughter. At home, children say بابا bābā dad and مامان māmān mum.

To say my father you can use the ezafe from lesson 3, پدر من pedar-e man, or – much more often – a short ending glued to the word: پدرم pedar-am. Sara uses these endings all through the dialogue: pedar-am (my father), barādar-at (your brother), esm-ash (his name), pesar-ash (his son).

Notice too how Tom says how many people are in his family: ما چهار نفر هستیم mā chahār nafar hastim we are four people – with the nafar from lesson 4.

My, your, his: the possessive endings

Persian has two ways to say my. You already know the ezafe with a pronoun: کتاب من ketāb-e man my book. The other way – the one you will hear most – is a short ending glued to the noun: کتابم ketāb-am my book. Both mean the same; the ezafe is a little more emphatic (ketāb-e MAN, MY book). The endings are never stressed: pe-DAR-am, not pedar-AM.

After a final silent -e (خانه، خانواده) the first three endings are written with a separate ا after a ZWNJ: خانه‌ام khāne-am my house, خانه‌ات khāne-at, خانه‌اش khāne-ash; the plural ones lose their e: خانه‌مان khāne-mān our house. After -ā or -u a y slips in: بابایم bābā-yam my dad, عمویش amu-yash his uncle.

If the noun has an adjective, the ending goes at the very end: برادر بزرگم barādar-e bozorg-am my older brother.

In speech -at and -ash become -et and -esh, and -emān -etān -eshān become -emun -etun -eshun: pedar-et, esm-esh, khāne-mun.

Whose? The ezafe with names

To say Sara's mother, Persian says the mother of Sara: the person comes first and the owner second, linked by the ezafe – مادر سارا mādar-e Sārā Sara's mother, پسر آقای کریمی pesar-e āghā-ye Karimi Mr Karimi's son.

Remember the spelling rule from lesson 3: after a vowel the ezafe sounds -ye. It is written ی after a silent -e (with a ZWNJ) and after -ā or -u, but after -i you only say it: خاله‌ی مینا khāle-ye Minā, عموی امیر amu-ye Amir, دایی لیلا dāyi-ye Leylā.

Links can chain, and an ending can join in at the end: پسر برادرم pesar-e barādar-am my brother's son. Cousins are named exactly this way – there is no everyday word for just cousin: پسرعمو pesar-amu son of your father's brother, دخترخاله dokhtar-khāle daughter of your mother's sister.

dāshtan: I have, you have

داشتن dāshtan to have is one of the very few verbs with no mi- in the present: just the stem dār- and the personal endings. They are the endings you know from khub-am, khub-i…, except that he / she gets -ad: دارد dārad. You used these forms for ages in lesson 4; now use them for people and things.

The thing you have comes just before the verb, and after a number it stays singular: دو خواهر دارم do khāhar dāram I have two sisters. For a question, simply raise your voice: بچه دارید؟ bacche dārid? do you have children?

To say you don't have something, Persian puts na- in front: ندارم na-dāram I don't have. You will practise that in lesson 9.

Mina's family

Mina writes a few lines about her family. Read, then answer the questions.

اسم من میناست. من بیست و شش سال دارم.

esm-e man Minā-st. man bist o shesh sāl dāram.

My name is Mina. I am twenty-six.

خانواده‌ی ما پنج نفر است: پدرم، مادرم، خواهرم، برادرم و من.

khānevāde-ye mā panj nafar ast: pedar-am, mādar-am, khāhar-am, barādar-am o man.

There are five of us in my family: my father, my mother, my sister, my brother and me.

پدرم شصت و یک سال دارد و مادرم پنجاه و هفت سال.

pedar-am shast o yek sāl dārad o mādar-am panjāh o haft sāl.

My father is sixty-one and my mother fifty-seven.

خواهر بزرگم مهسا سی و دو سال دارد.

khāhar-e bozorg-am Mahsā si o do sāl dārad.

My older sister, Mahsa, is thirty-two.

او شوهر و دو بچه دارد: یک پسر و یک دختر.

u showhar o do bacche dārad: yek pesar o yek dokhtar.

She has a husband and two children: a boy and a girl.

My younger brother is a student in Isfahan.

Four uncles, four aunts

Where English has one uncle and one aunt, Persian has four words, and every Iranian child learns them early: عمو amu is your father's brother and عمه amme his sister; دایی dāyi is your mother's brother and خاله khāle her sister. Cousins are the son of your amu, the daughter of your khāle and so on: پسرعمو pesar-amu, دخترخاله dokhtar-khāle.

The words travel far beyond the family. Children call any friendly older man amu and their mother's friends khāle; a shopkeeper may call a small boy amu jān, and young men call each other داداش dādāsh brother.

Family life is close. Fridays often mean a long lunch at the grandparents' house, and on the great family occasions – شب یلدا shab-e yaldā, the longest night of the year, or the visits of نوروز nowruz the Persian New Year, which begin at the homes of the oldest relatives – the whole clan gathers around one table.

Tips

  • The possessive endings never take the stress: pe-DAR-am, khā-HAR-at, ESM-ash.
  • khāhar (sister) is written with a silent و after خ: خواهر. The same silent و appears in خواندن khāndan 'to read' and خواب khāb 'sleep'.
  • With brothers and sisters, bozorg and kuchak usually mean older and younger: barādar-e bozorg-am is 'my older brother'.
  • zan is both 'woman' and 'wife': zan-am is 'my wife'. The polite word for a husband or a wife is hamsar.
  • Learn the endings in pairs with their spoken forms – you will read pedar-at but hear pedar-et.
