اصطلاحات دل و دست و سر و چشمAdvanced idioms: heart, hand, head and eye

estelāhāt-e del o dast o sar o cheshm

Persian feels with the heart and the liver, works with the hands and feet, and judges with the head, eyes, face, ears and tongue. Iranians use body idioms all day long – to say they miss you, that they doubt something, that someone conned them or that they are too shy to ask. This lesson teaches 30 of the most useful, each with its literal picture, its meaning, its register and an example, and a long conversation where they come thick and fast.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • understand and use 30 everyday idioms built on دل، جگر، دست، پا، سر، چشم، رو، گوش and زبان
  • picture the literal image behind each idiom and remember it through that image
  • attach the possessive ending to the body part, not the verb: دلم تنگ شده
  • choose an idiom that fits the register – affectionate, everyday, informal or literary

A letter from home

Leila writes to her brother Dariush, who has been studying in Germany for two years. Family news in Persian is hard to tell without body idioms – count how many you find.

داریوش عزیزم، سلام.

Dāriush-e aziz-am, salām.

Dearest Dariush, hello.

نمی‌دانی چقدر دلمان برایت تنگ شده است.

nemi-dāni cheghadr del-emān barā-yat tang shode ast.

You can't imagine how much we miss you.

Literally: you don't know how much our heart has become tight for you

مامان هر شب گوش به زنگ است که زنگ بزنی.

Māmān har shab gush be zang ast ke zang bezani.

Every evening Mum is on the alert, waiting for you to call.

Literally: Mum is ear-to-the-bell every night

هفته‌ی پیش عکست را دید که لاغر شده‌ای و جگرش کباب شد.

hafte-ye pish aks-at rā did ke lāghar shode-i o jegar-ash kabāb shod.

Last week she saw in your photo that you've got thin, and her heart bled for you.

Literally: her liver became kebab

من هم سرم خیلی شلوغ است، ولی هر روز به یادت هستم.

man ham sar-am kheyli sholugh ast, vali har ruz be yād-at hastam.

I'm swamped too, but I think of you every day.

Literally: my head is very crowded

خبر بزرگ این است که مهسا آخر دلش را به دریا زد و کافه‌اش را باز کرد.

khabar-e bozorg in ast ke Mahsā ākhar del-ash rā be daryā zad o kāfe-ash rā bāz kard.

The big news is that Mahsa finally took the plunge and opened her café.

Literally: she threw her heart into the sea

ماه‌ها دل‌دل می‌کرد، اما حالا پشیمان نیست.

māh-hā del-del mi-kard, ammā hālā pashimān nist.

She dithered for months, but now she has no regrets.

من و امیر هم پا به پایش کار کردیم تا مغازه آماده شد.

man o Amir ham pā be pā-yash kār kardim tā maghāze āmāde shod.

Amir and I worked alongside her, step for step, until the place was ready.

بابا اول گفت چشمش آب نمی‌خورد که این کار بگیرد.

Bābā avval goft cheshm-ash āb nemi-khorad ke in kār begirad.

At first Dad said he doubted the business would take off.

Literally: his eye doesn't drink water

ولی حالا هر روز آن‌جا می‌نشیند و با مشتری‌ها سر به سر می‌گذارد.

vali hālā har ruz ān-jā mi-neshinad o bā moshtari-hā sar be sar mi-gozārad.

But now he sits there every day and teases the customers.

یکی از همسایه‌ها می‌خواست سر مهسا کلاه بگذارد و اجاره را دو برابر کند.

yek-i az hamsāye-hā mi-khāst sar-e Mahsā kolāh begozārad o ejāre rā do barābar konad.

One of the neighbours tried to con Mahsa and double the rent.

Literally: to put a hat on Mahsa's head

اما وکیلمان دستش را رو کرد و او دیگر جرئت نکرد.

ammā vakil-emān dast-ash rā ru kard o u digar jor'at na-kard.

But our lawyer exposed his game, and he didn't dare try again.

Literally: turned his hand face up

مامان می‌گوید زیادی به او رو داده بودیم.

Māmān mi-guyad ziyādi be u ru dāde budim.

Mum says we had given him far too much leeway.

Literally: we had given him face

من زبان به دهان گرفتم و چیزی نگفتم، چون نمی‌خواستم دل کسی را بشکنم.

man zabān be dahān gereftam o chiz-i na-goftam, chon nemi-khāstam del-e kas-i rā beshkanam.

I held my tongue and said nothing, because I didn't want to hurt anyone's feelings.

زود بیا که همه چشم به راهت هستیم.

zud biyā ke hame cheshm be rāh-at hastim.

Come home soon – we're all waiting for you.

Literally: we are all eye-on-your-road

قربانت، لیلا

ghorbān-at, Leylā

With love, Leila

Your body is a dictionary

Most Persian body idioms are compound verbs: a body part plus a common verb – شدن، کردن، دادن، گذاشتن، زدن، گرفتن. Three things make them work.

1. The body part is often the grammatical subject. In دلم تنگ شده del-am tang shode I miss (it/you) the subject is 'my heart', not 'I'. So the possessive ending goes on the body part and the verb stays in the third person singular: del-am tang shode, del-et tang shode, del-esh tang shode – never del tang shode-am.

2. Another person enters with the ezafe or a preposition. سر کسی کلاه گذاشتن sar-e kas-i kolāh gozāshtan – put a hat on someone's head; دلم برایت تنگ شده del-am barā-yat tang shode – my heart is tight for you.

3. The literal picture is your memory aid. A tight heart is a homesick heart; an eye that 'drinks no water' doubts; a liver roasted like kebab is grief for someone else's pain. In older Persian the liver (جگر) was a seat of feeling as much as the heart, which is why it turns up in the most emotional idioms.

Register matters. Some idioms are affectionate (جگرگوشه), many are everyday (سرم شلوغ است), some are informal – fine with friends, too familiar for a job interview (سر کسی کلاه گذاشتن) – and a few sound literary (زبان به دهان گرفتن). The tables give a register for each.

Idioms to learn

  • دلم برایت تنگ شده del-am barā-yat tang shode

    I miss you phrase

    everyday, affectionate; spoken: دلم برات تنگ شده (del-am barāt tang shode)

    Literally: my heart has become tight for you

    دلم برای غذای مادرم تنگ شده است.

    del-am barā-ye ghazā-ye mādar-am tang shode ast.

    I miss my mother's cooking.

    Spoken دلم واسه غذای مامانم تنگ شده. del-am vāse ghazā-ye māmān-am tang shode.

  • دلم گرفته del-am gerefte

    I feel down, I'm in low spirits phrase

    everyday

    Literally: my heart has been seized

    امروز هوا ابری است و دلم گرفته.

    emruz havā abri ast o del-am gerefte.

    It's cloudy today and I feel low.

    Spoken امروز هوا ابریه، دلم گرفته. emruz havā abri-ye, del-am gerefte.

  • به دل گرفتن be del gereftan

    to take something to heart, be hurt by it v

    everyday; very common in the negative imperative

    Literally: to take into the heart

    شوخی کردم؛ به دل نگیر.

    shukhi kardam; be del na-gir.

    I was joking – don't take it to heart.

  • دل کسی را شکستن del-e kas-i rā shekastan

    to hurt someone's feelings v

    neutral; weaker than English 'break someone's heart'

    Literally: to break someone's heart

    با یک حرف ساده دلش را شکستی.

    bā yek harf-e sāde del-ash rā shekasti.

    You hurt her feelings with one careless word.

  • دل را به دریا زدن del rā be daryā zadan

    to take the plunge, risk it v

    everyday

    Literally: to throw one's heart into the sea

    دلم را به دریا زدم و استعفا دادم.

    del-am rā be daryā zadam o este'fā dādam.

    I took the plunge and resigned.

  • دلم به حالش سوخت del-am be hāl-ash sukht

    I felt sorry for him/her phrase

    everyday

    Literally: my heart burned for his state

    وقتی دیدمش، دلم به حالش سوخت.

    vaghti didam-ash, del-am be hāl-ash sukht.

    When I saw him, I felt sorry for him.

  • جگرم کباب شد jegar-am kabāb shod

    my heart bled (for someone), I was heartbroken phrase

    emotional, everyday; for pity and grief, not romance

    Literally: my liver was roasted like kebab

    جگرم کباب شد وقتی خبر زلزله را شنیدم.

    jegar-am kabāb shod vaghti khabar-e zelzele rā shenidam.

    My heart bled when I heard the news of the earthquake.

  • دست کسی را رو کردن dast-e kas-i rā ru kardan

    to expose someone's game or trick v

    informal; passive: دستش رو شد 'his game was up'

    Literally: to turn someone's hand face up (like cards)

    دستش رو شد؛ همه فهمیدند دروغ می‌گفت.

    dast-ash ru shod; hame fahmidand dorugh mi-goft.

    His game was up – everyone realised he had been lying.

  • دست به دست هم دادن dast be dast-e ham dādan

    to join forces v

    neutral, positive

    Literally: to give hand to each other's hand

    همسایه‌ها دست به دست هم دادند و کوچه را تمیز کردند.

    hamsāye-hā dast be dast-e ham dādand o kuche rā tamiz kardand.

    The neighbours joined forces and cleaned up the street.

  • کسی را دست به سر کردن kas-i rā dast be sar kardan

    to fob someone off, get rid of someone with excuses v

    informal

    Literally: to put a hand to someone's head

    هر بار می‌پرسم، مرا با یک بهانه دست به سر می‌کند.

    har bār mi-porsam, marā bā yek bahāne dast be sar mi-konad.

    Every time I ask, he fobs me off with an excuse.

    Spoken هر دفعه می‌پرسم، منو با یه بهونه دست به سر می‌کنه. har daf'e mi-porsam, mano bā ye bahune dast be sar mi-kone.

  • دست از سر کسی برداشتن dast az sar-e kas-i bardāshtan

    to leave someone alone v

    informal; دست از سرم بردار! can be rude

    Literally: to lift one's hand off someone's head

    دست از سرم بردار؛ کار دارم!

    dast az sar-am bardār; kār dāram!

    Leave me alone – I'm busy!

  • پا به پای کسی pā be pā-ye kas-i

    alongside someone, keeping pace with them phrase

    neutral

    Literally: foot to someone's foot

    پدرم در همه‌ی سختی‌ها پا به پای ما آمد.

    pedar-am dar hame-ye sakhti-hā pā be pā-ye mā āmad.

    My father stood by us through all the hard times.

  • پای حرف خود ایستادن pā-ye harf-e khod istādan

    to stand by one's word v

    neutral

    Literally: to stand at the foot of one's words

    او پای حرفش ایستاد و پول را پس داد.

    u pā-ye harf-ash istād o pul rā pas dād.

    He kept his word and paid the money back.

  • سر به سر کسی گذاشتن sar be sar-e kas-i gozāshtan

    to tease someone v

    informal, friendly

    Literally: to put head to someone's head

    داری سر به سرم می‌گذاری؟

    dāri sar be sar-am mi-gozāri?

    Are you teasing me?

    Spoken داری سر به سرم می‌ذاری؟ dāri sar be sar-am mi-zāri?

  • سرم شلوغ است sar-am sholugh ast

    I'm swamped, very busy phrase

    everyday; spoken: سرم شلوغه (sar-am sholughe)

    Literally: my head is crowded

    این هفته سرم خیلی شلوغ است.

    in hafte sar-am kheyli sholugh ast.

    I'm really busy this week.

    Spoken این هفته سرم خیلی شلوغه. in hafte sar-am kheyli sholughe.

  • سر کسی کلاه گذاشتن sar-e kas-i kolāh gozāshtan

    to con, cheat someone v

    informal

    Literally: to put a hat on someone's head

    فروشنده سرمان کلاه گذاشت؛ این گوشی دست‌دوم بود.

    forushande sar-emān kolāh gozāsht; in gushi dast-e-dovvom bud.

    The seller conned us – this phone was second-hand.

  • چشمم آب نمی‌خورد cheshm-am āb nemi-khorad

    I doubt it, I don't believe it will happen phrase

    informal

    Literally: my eye doesn't drink water

    چشمم آب نمی‌خورد که او سر وقت بیاید.

    cheshm-am āb nemi-khorad ke u sar-e vaght biyāyad.

    I doubt he'll come on time.

  • چشم به راه بودن cheshm be rāh budan

    to wait anxiously for someone v

    neutral to literary

    Literally: to have one's eye on the road

    خانواده‌اش سال‌ها چشم به راه او بودند.

    khānevāde-ash sāl-hā cheshm be rāh-e u budand.

    His family waited for him for years.

  • چشمت روشن cheshm-at roshan

    congratulations (on a new baby, a return, good news) phrase

    everyday; also ironic: 'well, isn't that great!'

    Literally: may your eye be bright

    چشمت روشن! شنیدم نوه‌دار شدی.

    cheshm-at roshan! shenidam nave-dār shodi.

    Congratulations! I hear you've become a grandmother.

  • رو دادن ru dādan

    to give someone too much leeway, spoil them v

    informal

    Literally: to give face

    زیادی به بچه‌ها رو می‌دهی.

    ziyādi be bacche-hā ru mi-dahi.

    You let the children get away with too much.

    Spoken زیادی به بچه‌ها رو می‌دی. ziyādi be bacche-hā ru mi-di.

  • رویم نمی‌شود ru-yam nemi-shavad

    I'm too embarrassed / shy to phrase

    everyday; spoken: روم نمی‌شه (ru-m nemi-she)

    Literally: my face doesn't become (able)

    رویم نمی‌شود دوباره از او پول قرض بگیرم.

    ru-yam nemi-shavad dobāre az u pul gharz begiram.

    I'm too embarrassed to borrow money from him again.

    Spoken روم نمی‌شه دوباره ازش پول قرض بگیرم. ru-m nemi-she dobāre az-ash pul gharz begiram.

  • گوش به زنگ بودن gush be zang budan

    to be on the alert, waiting for news v

    neutral

    Literally: to have one's ear to the bell

    منتظر نتیجه‌ی آزمایش هستم و گوش به زنگم.

    montazer-e natije-ye āzmāyesh hastam o gush be zang-am.

    I'm waiting for the test results and on the alert.

  • گوشش بدهکار نیست gush-ash bedehkār nist

    he/she won't listen phrase

    informal

    Literally: his ear is not in debt

    گوشش بدهکار نیست؛ هر چه می‌گویم کار خودش را می‌کند.

    gush-ash bedehkār nist; har che mi-guyam kār-e khod-ash rā mi-konad.

    He won't listen – whatever I say, he does as he pleases.

  • زبان به دهان گرفتن zabān be dahān gereftan

    to hold one's tongue v

    neutral to literary; زبان به دهان بگیر! 'hold your tongue!' is sharp

    Literally: to take one's tongue into one's mouth

    بهتر است فعلاً زبان به دهان بگیری.

    behtar ast fe'lan zabān be dahān begiri.

    You'd better keep quiet for now.

Heart and liver: feelings

With دل del the person who feels is shown by the possessive ending on دل, and the verb stays third person singular: دلم گرفت del-am gereft I felt low, دلت گرفت del-et gereft you felt low, دلشان گرفت del-eshān gereft they felt low. The thing or person you feel for comes with برای barā-ye or به حال be hāl-e.

جگر jegar liver belongs to the strongest feelings: compassion, grief, and the love of parents for children. Iranians also say جیگر jigar in slang to mean 'darling' or 'gorgeous' – affectionate between friends and family, but too familiar to say to strangers.

I miss the streets of Tehran.

Spoken دلم واسه کوچه‌های تهرون تنگ شده. del-am vāse kuche-hā-ye Tehrun tang shode.

دلش گرفته بود و با کسی حرف نمی‌زد.

del-ash gerefte bud o bā kas-i harf nemi-zad.

She was feeling low and wasn't talking to anyone.

از حرفم به دل گرفت و دو روز جواب تلفنم را نداد.

az harf-am be del gereft o do ruz javāb-e telefon-am rā na-dād.

He took what I said to heart and didn't answer my calls for two days.

خیلی دل‌دل کرد، ولی آخر بلیت را خرید.

kheyli del-del kard, vali ākhar belit rā kharid.

She hesitated a long time, but in the end she bought the ticket.

Spoken کلی دل‌دل کرد، ولی آخرش بلیتو خرید. kolli del-del kard, vali ākhar-esh belit-o kharid.

این بچه جگرگوشه‌ی من است.

in bacche jegar-gushe-ye man ast.

This child is my darling.

Literally 'a corner of my liver' – what parents and grandparents say of their children.

وقتی پیرمرد را تنها در باران دیدم، جگرم کباب شد.

vaghti pir-mard rā tanhā dar bārān didam, jegar-am kabāb shod.

When I saw the old man alone in the rain, my heart bled for him.

دلم به حال گربه‌ی گرسنه سوخت و به او غذا دادم.

del-am be hāl-e gorbe-ye gorosne sukht o be u ghazā dādam.

I felt sorry for the hungry cat and fed it.

Hands and feet: action and loyalty

دست dast hand idioms are about doing – helping, cheating, giving up, being generous. پا pā foot idioms are about standing somewhere: beside a friend, between two people who have quarrelled, behind your own word.

Notice the small words. به joins two body parts: دست به دست dast be dast hand to hand, پا به پا pā be pā foot to foot. از means 'off': دست از سر کسی برداشتن dast az sar-e kas-i bardāshtan to take one's hand off someone's head. And in دستش رو شد dast-ash ru shod his game was up the active رو کردن becomes رو شدن, just as کردن becomes شدن in any passive.

My uncle is a generous man.

مادرم پادرمیانی کرد و دو برادر آشتی کردند.

mādar-am pā-dar-miyāni kard o do barādar āshti kardand.

My mother stepped in, and the two brothers made up.

اگر دست به دست هم بدهیم، این کار یک‌روزه تمام می‌شود.

agar dast be dast-e ham bedahim, in kār yek-ruze tamām mi-shavad.

If we join forces, the job will be done in a day.

سرانجام دست شرکت را رو کردند: قیمت‌ها را عمداً بالا برده بود.

saranjām dast-e sherkat rā ru kardand: gheymat-hā rā amdan bālā borde bud.

In the end they exposed the company: it had pushed prices up on purpose.

سه ماه است که صاحبخانه ما را دست به سر می‌کند.

se māh ast ke sāheb-khāne mā rā dast be sar mi-konad.

For three months the landlord has been fobbing us off.

Spoken سه ماهه صاحبخونه ما رو دست به سر می‌کنه. se māh-e sāheb-khune mā ro dast be sar mi-kone.

دوستانش تا آخر شب پا به پایش رقصیدند.

dustān-ash tā ākhar-e shab pā be pā-yash raghsidand.

His friends danced with him until the end of the night.

قول داده بود و پای حرفش ایستاد.

ghowl dāde bud o pā-ye harf-ash istād.

He had promised, and he kept his word.

Head, eyes, face, ears and tongue

The head (سر sar) thinks, gets busy and gets tricked; the eye (چشم cheshm) waits, doubts and rejoices; the face (رو ru) is your honour and your shyness; the ear (گوش gush) listens or refuses to; the tongue (زبان zabān) is best kept still.

Two patterns are worth noticing. With رو the idea is face as in 'losing face': رویم نمی‌شود ru-yam nemi-shavad is 'my face can't manage it' – I'm too embarrassed. With چشم many idioms are blessings: چشمت روشن cheshm-at roshan 'may your eyes be bright' is what you say to someone whose son has come home or whose grandchild has just been born.

I'm not listening – until you apologise, I won't forgive you.

در جلسه زبان به دهان گرفتم، ولی بعد همه چیز را به مدیر گفتم.

The night of the exam, everyone was on the alert for the results to be announced.

رویش نشد بگوید که پول ندارد.

He was too embarrassed to say he had no money.

The report that never comes

Sara and Amir work in the same office. Their colleague Reza promised to finish a report days ago. Listen for the idioms – there are more than a dozen.

Amir

سارا، رضا گزارش را تمام کرد؟

Sārā, Rezā gozāresh rā tamām kard?

Sara, did Reza finish the report?

Spoken سارا، رضا گزارشو تموم کرد؟ Sārā, Rezā gozāresh-o tamum kard?

Sara

نه! سه روز است مرا دست به سر می‌کند: «فردا، فردا».

na! se ruz ast marā dast be sar mi-konad: «fardā, fardā».

No! For three days he's been fobbing me off: 'Tomorrow, tomorrow.'

Spoken نه! سه روزه منو دست به سر می‌کنه: «فردا، فردا». na! se ruz-e mano dast be sar mi-kone: «fardā, fardā».

Amir

چشمم آب نمی‌خورد که تا پنجشنبه تمامش کند.

cheshm-am āb nemi-khorad ke tā panjshanbe tamām-ash konad.

I doubt he'll finish it by Thursday.

Spoken چشمم آب نمی‌خوره تا پنجشنبه تمومش کنه. cheshm-am āb nemi-khore tā panjshanbe tamum-esh kone.

Sara

تقصیر خودمان است؛ زیادی به او رو دادیم.

taghsir-e khod-emān ast; ziyādi be u ru dādim.

It's our own fault – we've given him far too much leeway.

Spoken تقصیر خودمونه؛ زیادی بهش رو دادیم. taghsir-e khod-emun-e; ziyādi beh-esh ru dādim.

Amir

بیا دست به دست هم بدهیم و خودمان تمامش کنیم.

biyā dast be dast-e ham bedahim o khod-emān tamām-ash konim.

Let's join forces and finish it ourselves.

Spoken بیا دست به دست هم بدیم، خودمون تمومش کنیم. biyā dast be dast-e ham bedim, khod-emun tamum-esh konim.

Sara

باشد، ولی رویم نمی‌شود به خانم احمدی بگویم که رضا کار نکرده.

bāshad, vali ru-yam nemi-shavad be khānom-e Ahmadi beguyam ke Rezā kār na-karde.

All right, but I'm too embarrassed to tell Ms Ahmadi that Reza hasn't done the work.

Spoken باشه، ولی روم نمی‌شه به خانم احمدی بگم رضا کار نکرده. bāshe, vali ru-m nemi-she be khānom-e Ahmadi begam Rezā kār na-karde.

Amir

لازم نیست. زبان به دهان بگیر؛ خودش دستش رو می‌شود.

lāzem nist. zabān be dahān begir; khod-ash dast-ash ru mi-shavad.

No need. Keep quiet – his game will be up on its own.

Spoken لازم نیست. زبون به دهن بگیر؛ خودش دستش رو می‌شه. lāzem nist. zabun be dahan begir; khod-ash dast-ash ru mi-she.

تو هم همیشه سر به سر من می‌گذاری! جدی می‌گویی؟

You're always teasing me! Are you serious?

Spoken تو هم همیشه سر به سر من می‌ذاری! جدی می‌گی؟ to ham hamishe sar be sar-e man mi-zāri! jeddi mi-gi?

Amir

این بار جدی‌ام. خانم احمدی گوشش از این حرف‌ها پر است.

This time I'm serious. Ms Ahmadi has heard it all before.

Spoken این دفعه جدی‌ام. خانم احمدی گوشش از این حرفا پره. in daf'e jeddi-am. khānom-e Ahmadi gush-ash az in harf-ā pore.

قول نمی‌دهم. من دلم نازک است!

ghowl nemi-daham. man del-am nāzok ast!

I can't promise. I'm soft-hearted!

Spoken قول نمی‌دم. من دلم نازکه! ghowl nemi-dam. man del-am nāzoke!

Politeness lives in the body too

Many of the polite formulas you learned in A1 are body idioms in disguise. دستت درد نکند dast-at dard na-konad thank you wishes that the hand that worked for you may not hurt; the answer سرت درد نکند sar-at dard na-konad wishes the same for your head. چشم cheshm yes, of course means 'on my eye' – I will do it as willingly as I would protect my own eyes – and a guest is told قدمتان روی چشم ghadam-etān ru-ye cheshm you are most welcome, 'your step is on my eye'.

At a funeral or when someone has lost a relative, Iranians say سرتان سلامت باشد sar-etān salāmat bāshad my condolences – 'may your head be safe' – and the mourner answers سر شما هم سلامت باشد sar-e shomā ham salāmat bāshad. You will also hear چشمت روشن cheshm-at roshan at the other end of life, when a baby is born or a traveller comes home.

The liver deserves a word. In classical poetry lovers and mystics drink خون جگر khun-e jegar the blood of the liver – their suffering – and جگرسوز jegar-suz liver-burning describes anything heart-rending. So when a modern Iranian says that their liver has turned to kebab, they are speaking a very old language of feeling.

Tips

  • Put the ending on the body part, not on the verb: دلم تنگ شده (I miss…), دلت تنگ شده (you miss…), never *دل تنگ شده‌ام.
  • Don't translate body idioms word for word into English – or English ones into Persian. 'To pull someone's leg' is سر به سر کسی گذاشتن, not پای کسی را کشیدن, which sounds bizarre.
  • Watch the register. سر کسی کلاه گذاشتن and دست از سرم بردار are fine with friends but too blunt for a formal letter; there you would write فریب دادن 'to deceive' or مزاحم نشوید 'please do not disturb'.
  • When you have said something a hundred times and nobody listens, Iranians say زبانم مو درآورد zabān-am mu dar-āvard I'm blue in the face – literally 'my tongue grew hair'.
  • A proverb to go with this lesson: زبان سرخ سر سبز می‌دهد بر باد zabān-e sorkh sar-e sabz mi-dahad bar bād – 'the red tongue gives the green head to the wind': careless talk can cost you dearly.
  • Learn idioms in little scenes, not lists: imagine the hat being put on someone's head, the eye that refuses to drink, the liver on a skewer. The sillier the picture, the better you will remember it.
