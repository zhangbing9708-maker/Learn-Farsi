Course / Elementary (A2) / Lesson 8

اگر هوا خوب باشد، می‌رویم.If: plans and conditions

agar havā khub bāshad, mi-ravim.

Plans depend on things: the weather, your mother, the traffic. In this lesson you learn to say 'if' with اگر – with the subjunctive for things that may happen, with the present for things that are true now, and with the past for 'once this has happened' – plus 'otherwise' (وگرنه), 'unless' and 'even if'. You also get a first look at imaginary conditions. The setting: a picnic on Sizdah Bedar, the last day of the New Year holidays.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • make conditions about the future with اگر + subjunctive: اگر باران بیاید
  • use اگر with the present for facts: اگر خسته‌ای
  • use اگر with the past for 'once, when': اگر رسیدی، زنگ بزن
  • add a warning with وگرنه 'or else'
  • say 'unless', 'even if' and 'as long as'
  • recognise an imaginary condition: اگر پول داشتم…

Plans for Sizdah Bedar

It is the twelfth day of Nowruz. Mina phones Reza about tomorrow, Sizdah Bedar, when every family goes out for a picnic.

Mina

رضا، فردا سیزده‌به‌در است. برنامه‌ات چیست؟

Rezā, fardā sizdah-be-dar ast. barnāme-at chist?

Reza, tomorrow is Sizdah Bedar. What are your plans?

Spoken رضا، فردا سیزده‌به‌دره. برنامه‌ت چیه؟ Rezā, fardā sizdah-be-dar-e. barnāme-t chi-ye?

Reza

اگر هوا خوب باشد، با خانواده به باغ عمویم در کرج می‌رویم.

agar havā khub bāshad, bā khānevāde be bāgh-e amu-yam dar Karaj mi-ravim.

If the weather's good, we're going with the family to my uncle's garden in Karaj.

Spoken اگه هوا خوب باشه، با خانواده می‌ریم باغ عموم تو کرج. age havā khub bāshe, bā khānevāde mi-rim bāgh-e amu-m tu Karaj.

Mina

چه خوب! ولی اخبار می‌گوید شاید باران بیاید.

che khub! vali akhbār mi-guyad shāyad bārān biyāyad.

How lovely! But the news says it might rain.

Spoken چه خوب! ولی اخبار می‌گه شاید بارون بیاد. che khub! vali akhbār mi-ge shāyad bārun biyād.

Reza

حتی اگر باران بیاید، می‌رویم! سیزده‌به‌در نباید در خانه بمانیم.

hattā agar bārān biyāyad, mi-ravim! sizdah-be-dar na-bāyad dar khāne bemānim.

Even if it rains, we're going! On Sizdah Bedar we mustn't stay indoors.

Spoken حتی اگه بارون بیاد، می‌ریم! سیزده‌به‌در نباید تو خونه بمونیم. hattā age bārun biyād, mi-rim! sizdah-be-dar na-bāyad tu khune bemunim.

Mina

راست می‌گویی. ما هم اگر ماشین داشته باشیم، به پارک چیتگر می‌رویم.

rāst mi-guyi. mā ham agar māshin dāshte bāshim, be pārk-e Chitgar mi-ravim.

That's true. As for us, if we have the car, we'll go to Chitgar Park.

Spoken راست می‌گی. ما هم اگه ماشین داشته باشیم، می‌ریم پارک چیتگر. rāst mi-gi. mā ham age māshin dāshte bāshim, mi-rim pārk-e Chitgar.

Reza

چرا با ما نمی‌آیید؟ اگر بیایید، عمویم خیلی خوشحال می‌شود.

cherā bā mā nemi-āyid? agar biyāyid, amu-yam kheyli khosh-hāl mi-shavad.

Why don't you come with us? If you come, my uncle will be really pleased.

Spoken چرا با ما نمیاین؟ اگه بیاین، عموم خیلی خوشحال می‌شه. cherā bā mā nemiyāyn? age biyāyn, amu-m kheyli khosh-hāl mi-she.

Mina

اگر مادرم اجازه بدهد، حتماً می‌آییم! باید از او بپرسم.

agar mādar-am ejāze bedahad, hatman mi-āyim! bāyad az u beporsam.

If my mother agrees, we'll definitely come! I have to ask her.

Spoken اگه مامانم اجازه بده، حتماً میایم! باید ازش بپرسم. age māmān-am ejāze bede, hatman miyāym! bāyad az-ash beporsam.

Reza

باشد، ولی تا ساعت نه به من خبر بده، وگرنه جا نداریم!

bāshad, vali tā sā'at-e noh be man khabar bedeh, vagarna jā na-dārim!

OK, but let me know by nine, or we won't have room!

Spoken باشه، ولی تا ساعت نه بهم خبر بده، وگرنه جا نداریم! bāshe, vali tā sā'at-e noh beh-am khabar bede, vagarna jā na-dārim!

Mina

چشم. راستی، سبزه‌مان را هم می‌آورم. باید آن را در آب بیندازیم، نه؟

chashm. rāsti, sabze-mān rā ham mi-āvaram. bāyad ān rā dar āb biyandāzim, na?

Will do. Oh, I'll bring our sabzeh too. We have to throw it into the water, don't we?

Spoken چشم. راستی، سبزه‌مونم میارم. باید بندازیمش تو آب، نه؟ chashm. rāsti, sabze-mun-am miyāram. bāyad bendāzim-esh tu āb, na?

Reza

بله. و می‌گویند اگر یک دختر مجرد سبزه را گره بزند، سال بعد عروس می‌شود!

bale. va mi-guyand agar yek dokhtar-e mojarrad sabze rā gereh bezanad, sāl-e ba'd arus mi-shavad!

Yes. And they say that if a single girl ties a knot in the sabzeh, she'll be a bride next year!

Spoken آره. می‌گن اگه یه دختر مجرد سبزه رو گره بزنه، سال بعد عروس می‌شه! āre. mi-gan age ye dokhtar-e mojarrad sabze ro gereh bezane, sāl-e ba'd arus mi-she!

Mina

پس حتماً گره می‌زنم! اگر کار کرد، تو را به عروسی دعوت می‌کنم.

pas hatman gereh mi-zanam! agar kār kard, to rā be arusi da'vat mi-konam.

Then I'll definitely tie one! If it works, I'll invite you to the wedding.

Spoken پس حتماً گره می‌زنم! اگه جواب داد، تو رو عروسی دعوت می‌کنم. pas hatman gereh mi-zanam! age javāb dād, to ro arusi da'vat mi-konam.

Reza

قبول! فقط فردا دیر نکن، وگرنه بدون تو می‌رویم!

ghabul! faghat fardā dir na-kon, vagarna bedun-e to mi-ravim!

Deal! Just don't be late tomorrow, or we'll go without you!

Spoken قبول! فقط فردا دیر نکن، وگرنه بی تو می‌ریم! ghabul! faghat fardā dir na-kon, vagarna bi to mi-rim!

If…: اگر

Every plan in the dialogue hangs on an اگر agar if (spoken اگه age): اگر هوا خوب باشد agar havā khub bāshad if the weather is good, اگر باران بیاید agar bārān biyāyad if it rains, اگر مادرم اجازه بدهد agar mādar-am ejāze bedahad if my mother agrees.

A Persian condition has two halves, usually in this order and separated by a comma: the if-clause with اگر agar, then the result. The surprise for English speakers is the verb after agar. For something that may or may not happen, Persian uses the subjunctive you learned in lesson 4 – literally if the weather be good – never the future. The result is in the present (which, as you know, also covers the future) or in the imperative:

اگر باران بیاید، در خانه می‌مانیم. agar bārān biyāyad, dar khāne mi-mānim. If it rains, we'll stay at home.

There is also a little word for the opposite case, and if not: وگرنه vagarna otherwise, or else. You hear it after almost every warning: زود بیا، وگرنه بدون تو می‌رویم! zud biyā, vagarna bedun-e to mi-ravim! come early, or we'll go without you!

If it happens: اگر + subjunctive

For a condition that may or may not come true, use اگر agar + subjunctive. The result is in the present (with a future meaning), the imperative, or with می‌توانم mi-tavānam or باید bāyad:

  • اگر بیایی، خوشحال می‌شوم. agar biyāyi, khosh-hāl mi-shavam. If you come, I'll be glad.
  • اگر خسته باشی، می‌توانی بخوابی. agar khaste bāshi, mi-tavāni bekhābi. If you're tired, you can sleep.

The negative is the negative subjunctive: اگر نیایی agar na-yāyi if you don't come, اگر وقت نداشته باشم agar vaght na-dāshte bāsham if I don't have time. Never use the future tense after agar. In speech agar is age and the verbs shrink as usual: اگه بیای age biyāy if you come.

If it's true, once it's done: اگر with the present and the past

Two other tenses are common after اگر agar.

The present – when the condition is a fact now, something you can see or have just heard: اگر خسته‌ای، برو بخواب. agar khaste-i, borow bekhāb. If you're tired (and you are), go to bed. اگر می‌خواهی، با ما بیا. agar mi-khāhi, bā mā biyā. If you want, come with us.

The simple past – for a future event that must be finished before the result: اگر رسیدی، به من زنگ بزن. agar residi, be man zang bezan. When you get there, ring me. Literally if you arrived – think once you've arrived. It is very common in notices: اگر سؤالی داشتید، بپرسید. agar so'āl-i dāshtid, beporsid. If you have any questions, ask. The simple past also works for real conditions about the past: اگر دیروز آمد، چرا مرا ندید؟ agar diruz āmad, cherā marā na-did? If he came yesterday, why didn't he see me?

Unlikely or imaginary conditions move further into the past: اگر پولدار بودم، در شمال خانه می‌خریدم. agar puldār budam, dar shomāl khāne mi-kharidam. If I were rich, I'd buy a house in the north. Just recognise this for now; you will learn it properly at B1.

Or else, unless, even if: وگرنه، مگر این‌که، حتی اگر

A few more words build conditions:

  • وگرنه vagarna otherwise, or else – short for va agar na, 'and if not'. It follows a command, a must or a plan and gives the bad result: عجله کن، وگرنه به قطار نمی‌رسیم. ajale kon, vagarna be ghatār nemi-resim. Hurry up, or we'll miss the train.
  • اگر نه agar na if not – after a question or an offer.
  • مگر این‌که magar in-ke unless + subjunctive.
  • حتی اگر hattā agar even if + subjunctive; in everyday speech also agar … ham: اگر باران هم بیاید agar bārān ham biyāyad even if it rains.
  • به شرطی که be shart-i ke as long as, on condition that + subjunctive.

A message from Ms Ahmadi

Ms Ahmadi, Tom's Persian teacher, sends her class a message about next week's trip to Isfahan. Count the conditions – and notice which tense comes after each اگر.

دانشجویان عزیز، سلام.

dāneshjuyān-e aziz, salām.

Dear students, hello.

پنجشنبه‌ی آینده با هم به اصفهان می‌رویم.

panjshanbe-ye āyande bā ham be Esfahān mi-ravim.

Next Thursday we're going to Isfahan together.

اگر می‌خواهید بیایید، تا یکشنبه اسمتان را به من بدهید.

agar mi-khāhid biyāyid, tā yekshanbe esm-etān rā be man bedahid.

If you want to come, give me your name by Sunday.

اتوبوس ساعت شش صبح از جلوی دانشگاه حرکت می‌کند.

otobus sā'at-e shesh-e sobh az jelo-ye dāneshgāh harekat mi-konad.

The bus leaves from in front of the university at six in the morning.

اگر دیر کنید، اتوبوس منتظر نمی‌ماند!

agar dir konid, otobus montazer nemi-mānad!

If you're late, the bus won't wait!

اگر هوا خوب باشد، عصر کنار زاینده‌رود قدم می‌زنیم.

agar havā khub bāshad, asr kenār-e Zāyande-rud ghadam mi-zanim.

If the weather is good, in the afternoon we'll walk along the Zayandeh River.

اگر باران بیاید، به جای آن به موزه می‌رویم.

agar bārān biyāyad, be jā-ye ān be muze mi-ravim.

If it rains, we'll go to a museum instead.

کفش راحت بپوشید، وگرنه پاهایتان درد می‌گیرد.

kafsh-e rāhat bepushid, vagarna pā-hā-yetān dard mi-girad.

Wear comfortable shoes, or your feet will hurt.

کسی که کارت دانشجویی داشته باشد، بلیت موزه را نصف قیمت می‌خرد.

kas-i ke kārt-e dāneshjuyi dāshte bāshad, belit-e muze rā nesf-e gheymat mi-kharad.

Anyone who has a student card pays half price for the museum ticket.

اگر سؤالی داشتید، به من پیام بدهید.

agar so'āl-i dāshtid, be man payām bedahid.

If you have any questions, send me a message.

به امید دیدار، خانم احمدی

be omid-e didār, khānom Ahmadi

Hoping to see you all, Ms Ahmadi

Sizdah Bedar: thirteen out of doors

The New Year holidays end on سیزده‌به‌در sizdah-be-dar, the thirteenth day of Farvardin (1 or 2 April). Thirteen is thought to be an unlucky number, and the cure is to spend the day out of doors – sizdah be dar means thirteen out of the door. So on that day the cities empty: families take carpets, samovars and pots of food to parks, gardens, riverbanks and even the roadside, and spend the day eating, playing and talking. اگر باران هم بیاید agar bārān ham biyāyad even if it rains, people still go out.

Everyone brings the سبزه sabze – the sprouted wheat or lentils from the haft-sin table – which is said to have soaked up the bad luck of the year. At the end of the day it is thrown into running water. Before that, unmarried girls tie a knot in a few blades of grass and make a wish, sometimes chanting سال دیگر، خانه‌ی شوهر، بچه بغل sāl-e digar, khāne-ye showhar, bacche baghal next year, in a husband's house, with a baby in my arms. The thirteenth is also a day for tricks and jokes, a little like April Fool's Day: the دروغ سیزده dorugh-e sizdah the lie of the thirteenth.

Tips

  • اگر is age in speech. In writing, the two halves of a condition are separated by a comma.
  • Never use the future after اگر. 'If it rains tomorrow' is agar fardā bārān biyāyad – the subjunctive does the job.
  • وگرنه is va agar na 'and if not'. Think of it as 'or else': it always follows a command, a 'must' or a plan.
  • The past after اگر sounds odd to English ears, but it is everyday Persian: agar residi, zang bezan 'ring me when you get there' – literally 'if you arrived'.
  • In conversation you will also hear اگه نه and اگه نشد 'if not, if it doesn't work out': age na-shod, fardā miyām 'if it doesn't work out, I'll come tomorrow'.
