سفر به اصفهانTravelling: the station, the airport and the hotel

safar be Esfahān

Iran is a big country, and sooner or later you will take a plane, a night train or a long bus ride. This lesson gets you through the station, the airport and the hotel desk – booking, checking in, asking about breakfast and check-out – and introduces the real Persian future tense, خواهم رفت. You will mostly read it on tickets, screens and in the news; in conversation people simply use the present.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • understand travel announcements, tickets and timetables
  • form and recognise the formal future: خواهم رفت
  • talk about plans the everyday way, with the present and قرار است
  • check in at a hotel and ask the questions travellers need
Show

At the hotel desk in Isfahan

Tom and Amir have come to Isfahan by train for the Nowruz holidays. It is evening when they reach a small hotel near Naqsh-e Jahan Square.

Receptionist

Hello, welcome! How can I help?

Amir

سلام. ما یک اتاق دوتخته برای سه شب رزرو کرده‌ایم.

salām. mā yek otāgh-e do-takhte barā-ye se shab rezerv karde-im.

Hello. We've booked a twin room for three nights.

Spoken سلام. ما یه اتاق دوتخته برای سه شب رزرو کردیم. salām. mā ye otāgh-e do-takhte barā-ye se shab rezerv kardim.

Receptionist

بله، اتاق شما آماده است. لطفاً کارت ملی یا گذرنامه‌تان را بدهید.

Spoken بله، اتاقتون آماده‌ست. لطفاً کارت ملی یا پاسپورتتونو بدین. bale, otāgh-etun āmāde-st. lotfan kārt-e melli yā pāsport-etun-o bedin.

Spoken بفرمایید، اینم پاسپورت من. صبحونه هم داره؟ befarmāyid, in-am pāsport-e man. sobhune ham dāre?

Receptionist

Yes, breakfast is from seven to ten, on the first floor.

Amir

ببخشید، هتل وای‌فای دارد؟

Excuse me, does the hotel have wifi?

Spoken ببخشید، هتل وای‌فای داره؟ bebakhshid, hotel vāy-fāy dāre?

Receptionist

بله، رمزش روی این کارت است. این هم کلید شما: اتاق ۲۰۴، طبقه‌ی دوم.

Spoken بله، رمزش رو این کارته. اینم کلیدتون: اتاق ۲۰۴، طبقه‌ی دوم. bale, ramz-esh ru in kārt-e. in-am kelid-etun: otāgh-e devist-o-chahār, tabaghe-ye dovvom.

Spoken ممنون. فردا می‌خوایم میدون نقش جهان رو ببینیم. از این‌جا دوره؟ mamnun. fardā mi-khāym meydun-e Naghsh-e Jahān ro bebinim. az in-jā dur-e?

No, it's ten minutes on foot.

Spoken یه سؤال دیگه: روز آخر ساعت چند باید اتاقو تحویل بدیم؟ ye so'āl-e dige: ruz-e ākhar sā'at-e chand bāyad otāgh-o tahvil bedim?

Spoken تا ساعت دوازده. اگه قطارتون عصر حرکت می‌کنه، می‌تونین چمدونا رو این‌جا بذارین. tā sā'at-e davāzdah. age ghatār-etun asr harekat mi-kone, mi-tunin chamedun-ā ro in-jā bezārin.

Tom

عالی است! قطار ما ساعت پنج حرکت می‌کند.

Great! Our train leaves at five.

Spoken عالیه! قطارمون ساعت پنج حرکت می‌کنه. āli-ye! ghatār-emun sā'at-e panj harekat mi-kone.

Receptionist

پس امیدوارم سفر خوبی داشته باشید. شب به خیر!

pas omidvār-am safar-e khub-i dāshte bāshid. shab be kheyr!

Then I hope you have a lovely trip. Good night!

Will: three ways to talk about the future

English has I'll go, I'm going to go and I'm going. Persian has three ways too, but they differ in style rather than meaning:

  • The formal future – خواهم رفت khāham raft I will go. You read it on tickets and departure boards, hear it in the news and in station announcements, and use it in writing and in solemn promises.
  • The present – فردا می‌روم fardā mi-ravam I'm going tomorrow. This is what people actually say: a time word shows that it is the future. In Tehrani speech, فردا می‌رم fardā mi-ram.
  • قرار است + subjunctive – قرار است فردا برویم gharār ast fardā beravim we're due to go tomorrow, for anything arranged or timetabled.

In the dialogue nobody uses the formal future – it would sound like a news bulletin. But on the way to Isfahan, Tom and Amir heard it on the station loudspeaker: قطار تهران به اصفهان ساعت هفت حرکت خواهد کرد. ghatār-e Tehrān be Esfahān sā'at-e haft harekat khāhad kard. The Tehran–Isfahan train will depart at seven.

The formal future: خواهم رفت

Persian has a true future tense, but you will mostly read it rather than hear it. Take the present of خواستن khāstan to want without می mi – خواهم khāham, خواهی khāhi, خواهد khāhad … – and add the past stem of the main verb, which is the infinitive without -an: رفتن → رفت raft. Only khāh- changes for the person; the main verb stays the same.

Negative: ن na goes on khāh-: نخواهم رفت na-khāham raft I will not go.

Compound verbs: the future goes between the two parts: حرکت خواهد کرد harekat khāhad kard it will depart, تماس خواهیم گرفت tamās khāhim gereft we will be in touch.

Don't confuse it with want: می‌خواهم بروم mi-khāham beravam I want to go has mi- and the subjunctive; the future has no mi- and the past stem.

What people actually say: the present for the future

In conversation Iranians hardly ever say khāham raft. They use the ordinary present tense you learned in A1, and a time word does the rest: فردا می‌روم fardā mi-ravam I'm going tomorrow, I'll go tomorrow. In Tehrani speech that is فردا می‌رم fardā mi-ram, and the destination often moves to the end: فردا می‌رم اصفهان fardā mi-ram Esfahān.

For something fixed in advance – a timetable, an appointment, a plan made with other people – use قرار است gharār ast it is arranged + the subjunctive: قرار است ساعت هشت حرکت کنیم gharār ast sā'at-e hasht harekat konim we're due to leave at eight. In speech: قراره gharāre.

Questions for the hotel and the station

Travel questions are built from words you know, with the verb at the end. Three things make them polite: start with ببخشید bebakhshid excuse me; use the plural شما shomā forms; and for requests ask is it possible…? – می‌شود…؟ mi-shavad…? or the more formal ممکن است…؟ momken ast…? – with the subjunctive.

In writing and in very formal speech a yes/no question can begin with آیا āyā. It adds no meaning – it is simply a question mark you can hear: آیا اتاق خالی دارید؟ āyā otāgh-e khāli dārid? Do you have a free room?

Three days in Isfahan: a tour programme

A travel agency's programme, written the way such leaflets are – in the formal future. Find every خواهد and خواهید, then try saying the same thing the everyday way.

قطار تهران به اصفهان روز پنج‌شنبه ساعت هفت صبح حرکت خواهد کرد.

ghatār-e Tehrān be Esfahān ruz-e panj-shanbe sā'at-e haft-e sobh harekat khāhad kard.

مسافران حدود ساعت یک بعد از ظهر به اصفهان خواهند رسید.

Night trains, Nowruz and water behind the traveller

The راه‌آهن سراسری rāh-āhan-e sarāsari Trans-Iranian Railway, built between 1927 and 1938, climbs from the Caspian Sea over the Alborz mountains and runs all the way down to the Persian Gulf; since 2021 it has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Trains are cheap and comfortable, and the overnight train to مشهد Mashhad is a small society of its own: strangers in a کوپه kupe compartment share tea from a flask, fruit and life stories, and by morning they have usually swapped phone numbers.

The busiest travel time of the year is نوروز nowruz, the Iranian New Year in late March, when schools close for two weeks and millions of families take to the roads. Mashhad, with the shrine of Imam Reza, receives more visitors than any other city in Iran.

Setting off on a journey has its own rituals. The family holds a Qur'an up for the traveller to pass under, and after the car has gone someone pours a bowl of water on the ground behind it – آب پشت سر مسافر āb posht-e sar-e mosāfer water behind the traveller – so that the journey goes as smoothly as water and the traveller comes back. As people leave you say سفر به خیر! safar be kheyr! have a good trip!, and when they return, رسیدن به خیر! residan be kheyr! welcome back!

Tips

  • Want or will? می‌خواهم بروم = I want to go; خواهم رفت = I will go. The future has no می and takes the short past stem.
  • On screens and tickets: حرکت = departure, ورود = arrival, سکو = platform, تأخیر = delay, لغو شد = cancelled.
  • بلیت or بلیط? You will see both spellings; بلیت is the one the Academy of Persian Language recommends.
  • Use the formal future in conversation and you will sound like a newsreader. For your own plans, say فردا می‌روم – or, Tehrani style, فردا می‌رم.
  • To a traveller: سفر به خیر! (have a good trip) or خوش بگذرد! (have a great time – spoken خوش بگذره!).
