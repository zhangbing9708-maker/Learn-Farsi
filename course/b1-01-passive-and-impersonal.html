Course / Intermediate (B1) / Lesson 1

ساخته شد، می‌گویندPassive and impersonal: 'it was built', 'they say'

sākhte shod, mi-guyand

Persian can say that something was done without saying who did it – but it does so far less often than English. In this lesson you learn how the passive is built with شدن, when Iranians actually use it, and the three everyday ways of being impersonal: 'they say', 'one can', and 'a person'.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • form the passive of any verb with شدن in every tense
  • understand why Persian prefers 'they built it' to 'it was built'
  • use می‌گویند، می‌شود and آدم to speak impersonally
  • read a short history text written in the formal passive style

The bridge of thirty-three arches

A short text in the style of a guidebook. Notice how many verbs end in شد or شده است – the passive is at home in writing about history.

سی‌وسه‌پل یکی از زیباترین پل‌های ایران است.

Si-o-se-pol yek-i az zibā-tarin pol-hā-ye Irān ast.

Si-o-se-pol is one of the most beautiful bridges in Iran.

این پل در دوره‌ی صفوی در شهر اصفهان ساخته شد.

in pol dar dowre-ye Safavi dar shahr-e Esfahān sākhte shod.

This bridge was built in the Safavid period in the city of Isfahan.

کار ساختن آن در سال ۱۶۰۲ میلادی به پایان رسید.

kār-e sākhtan-e ān dar sāl-e hezār-o-sheshsad-o-do-ye milādi be pāyān resid.

The work of building it was finished in the year 1602 AD.

پل به دستور شاه عباس ساخته شد و نام فرمانده‌ی سپاه او، الله‌وردی‌خان، بر آن گذاشته شد.

pol be dastur-e Shāh Abbās sākhte shod o nām-e farmānde-ye sepāh-e u, Allāhverdi-khān, bar ān gozāshte shod.

The bridge was built on the orders of Shah Abbas, and the name of his army commander, Allahverdi Khan, was given to it.

در این پل سی و سه طاق دیده می‌شود.

dar in pol si o se tāgh dide mi-shavad.

Thirty-three arches can be seen in this bridge.

به همین دلیل به آن «سی‌وسه‌پل» گفته می‌شود.

be hamin dalil be ān «Si-o-se-pol» gofte mi-shavad.

For this reason it is called "Si-o-se-pol".

پل از آجر و سنگ ساخته شده است و حدود سیصد متر طول دارد.

pol az ājor o sang sākhte shode ast o hodud-e sisad metr tul dārad.

The bridge has been built of brick and stone and is about three hundred metres long.

در گذشته از پل برای عبور کاروان‌ها استفاده می‌شد.

dar gozashte az pol barā-ye obur-e kārvān-hā estefāde mi-shod.

In the past the bridge was used for the passage of caravans.

امروز پل هر شب روشن می‌شود و مردم اصفهان روی آن قدم می‌زنند.

emruz pol har shab roshan mi-shavad o mardom-e Esfahān ru-ye ān ghadam mi-zanand.

Today the bridge is lit up every night and the people of Isfahan stroll on it.

گفته می‌شود که در شب‌های تابستان، صدای آواز مردم از زیر طاق‌ها شنیده می‌شود.

gofte mi-shavad ke dar shab-hā-ye tābestān, sedā-ye āvāz-e mardom az zir-e tāgh-hā shenide mi-shavad.

It is said that on summer nights, the sound of people singing is heard from under the arches.

متأسفانه رودخانه‌ی زاینده‌رود در بیشتر سال خشک است.

mota'assefāne rudkhāne-ye Zāyande-rud dar bishtar-e sāl khoshk ast.

Unfortunately the Zayandeh-rud river is dry for most of the year.

پل در سال ۱۳۱۰ در فهرست آثار ملی ایران ثبت شد.

pol dar sāl-e hezār-o-sisad-o-dah dar fehrest-e āsār-e melli-ye Irān sabt shod.

The bridge was registered in the list of Iran's national monuments in the year 1310 (1931).

A language that would rather say who did it

English loves the passive: the bridge was built, the letter was sent, mistakes were made. Persian has a passive too, but in everyday speech Iranians usually prefer an active sentence with a vague subject: پل را ساختند pol rā sākhtand they built the bridge rather than پل ساخته شد pol sākhte shod the bridge was built. Both are correct; the second simply sounds more formal and written.

So you will meet the passive constantly in newspapers, history books, signs and official notices – and much less in conversation. Learn to form it so you can read; learn the impersonal 'they' so you can speak.

The passive is built from two pieces you already know: the past participle (the past stem plus ـه -e: ساخته sākhte built, دیده dide seen, گفته gofte said) and the verb شدن shodan to become, which carries the tense. Literally, the bridge became built.

Building the passive: past participle + شدن

Take the past stem, add ـه -e to get the past participle, then conjugate شدن shodan in whatever tense you need. The participle never changes; شدن does all the work. Here is ساختن sākhtan to build in every tense you have met:

TensePersianReadingEnglish
simple pastساخته شد sākhte shodsākhte shodit was built
presentساخته می‌شود sākhte mi-shavadsākhte mi-shavadit is (being) built
past continuousساخته می‌شد sākhte mi-shodsākhte mi-shodit was being built, used to be built
perfectساخته شده است sākhte shode astsākhte shode astit has been built
past perfectساخته شده بود sākhte shode budsākhte shode budit had been built
futureساخته خواهد شد sākhte khāhad shodsākhte khāhad shodit will be built
subjunctiveساخته شود sākhte shavadsākhte shavad(that) it be built
negativeساخته نشد sākhte na-shodsākhte na-shodit was not built

این نامه دیروز فرستاده شد.

in nāme diruz ferestāde shod.

This letter was sent yesterday.

در ایران هر سال میلیون‌ها تن گندم تولید می‌شود.

dar Irān har sāl milyun-hā ton gandom towlid mi-shavad.

Millions of tonnes of wheat are produced in Iran every year.

کتاب‌های او به بیست زبان ترجمه شده‌اند.

ketāb-hā-ye u be bist zabān tarjome shode-and.

His books have been translated into twenty languages.

وقتی رسیدیم، در بسته شده بود.

vaghti residim, dar baste shode bud.

When we arrived, the door had been closed.

نتیجه‌ی امتحان فردا اعلام خواهد شد.

natije-ye emtehān fardā e'lām khāhad shod.

The exam result will be announced tomorrow.

باید این مشکل زودتر حل شود.

bāyad in moshkel zud-tar hal shavad.

This problem must be solved sooner.

این خانه هنوز فروخته نشده است.

in khāne hanuz forukhte na-shode ast.

This house has not been sold yet.

Spoken این خونه هنوز فروخته نشده. in khune hanuz forukhte na-shode.

Compound verbs: just swap کردن for شدن

Most Persian verbs are compounds – a noun or adjective plus کردن kardan to do. Their passive is wonderfully simple: replace کردن with شدن. No participle is needed, because the noun part stays as it is.

The same trick turns an action into a change of state: باز کردن bāz kardan to open (something) → باز شدن bāz shodan to open, to be opened; خراب کردن kharāb kardan to break (something) → خراب شدن kharāb shodan to break down.

By whom? Persian rarely says it. If you must, use به دست be dast-e by the hand of or توسط tavassot-e by (formal): این پل به دست شاه عباس ساخته شد in pol be dast-e Shāh Abbās sākhte shod. In speech people simply switch to the active.

Active (کردن)Passive (شدن)English
تمام کردن tamām kardanتمام شدن tamām shodanto finish → to be finished, run out
شروع کردن shoru' kardanشروع شدن shoru' shodanto start → to begin
پیدا کردن peydā kardanپیدا شدن peydā shodanto find → to be found
تعمیر کردن ta'mir kardanتعمیر شدن ta'mir shodanto repair → to be repaired
آماده کردن āmāde kardanآماده شدن āmāde shodanto prepare → to get ready
گم کردن gom kardanگم شدن gom shodanto lose → to get lost
برگزار کردن bargozār kardanبرگزار شدن bargozār shodanto hold (an event) → to be held

کلاس ساعت نه شروع می‌شود.

kelās sā'at-e noh shoru' mi-shavad.

The class starts at nine.

کلیدم پیدا شد!

kelid-am peydā shod!

My key has been found! (My key turned up!)

ماشین هنوز تعمیر نشده است.

māshin hanuz ta'mir na-shode ast.

The car has not been repaired yet.

Spoken ماشین هنوز تعمیر نشده. māshin hanuz ta'mir na-shode.

جشنواره هر سال در شهریور برگزار می‌شود.

jashnvāre har sāl dar Shahrivar bargozār mi-shavad.

The festival is held every year in Shahrivar.

در بازار اصفهان گم شدیم.

dar bāzār-e Esfahān gom shodim.

We got lost in the Isfahan bazaar.

شام آماده شد؟ بله، همه چیز آماده است.

shām āmāde shod? bale, hame chiz āmāde ast.

Is dinner ready? Yes, everything is ready.

Three ways to be impersonal

1. They say… – the third person plural. When the doer is 'people in general', Persian uses the verb in the they form with no subject at all: می‌گویند که… mi-guyand ke… they say that…, پل را ساختند pol rā sākhtand they built the bridge. This is the conversational substitute for the passive.

2. One can… – می‌شود / می‌توان. می‌شود mi-shavad (spoken mishe) plus a verb means it is possible to, one can: این‌جا می‌شود پارک کرد؟ in-jā mi-shavad pārk kard? can one park here? – note the short infinitive (past stem) after it in formal style. In writing you also see می‌توان mi-tavān: می‌توان گفت که… mi-tavān goft ke… one can say that…

3. A person… – آدم / یکی / کسی. آدم ādam means a human being and works like English you in you never know: آدم نمی‌داند چه بگوید ādam nemi-dānad che beguyad one doesn't know what to say.

می‌گویند این غذا را اول در شیراز پختند.

mi-guyand in ghazā rā avval dar Shirāz pokhtand.

They say this dish was first cooked in Shiraz.

Spoken می‌گن این غذا رو اول شیراز پختن. mi-gan in ghazā ro avval Shirāz pokhtan.

در این خیابان نمی‌شود پارک کرد.

dar in khiyābān nemi-shavad pārk kard.

One can't park on this street.

Spoken این خیابون نمی‌شه پارک کرد. in khiyābun nemi-she pārk kard.

می‌توان گفت که زبان فارسی تغییر کمی کرده است.

mi-tavān goft ke zabān-e fārsi taghyir-e kam-i karde ast.

One can say that the Persian language has changed little.

آدم وقتی خسته است، زود عصبانی می‌شود.

ādam vaghti khaste ast, zud asabāni mi-shavad.

When one is tired, one gets angry quickly.

یکی زنگ زد و اسم شما را پرسید.

yek-i zang zad o esm-e shomā rā porsid.

Someone called and asked your name.

این‌جا را تمیز کرده‌اند.

in-jā rā tamiz karde-and.

This place has been cleaned. (lit. they have cleaned here)

At the garage

Reza's car broke down. He talks to the mechanic, Mr Karimi. Listen to how often شدن appears in normal talk about things that happen to things.

Reza

سلام آقای کریمی. ماشینم دیروز وسط خیابان خراب شد.

salām āghā-ye Karimi. māshin-am diruz vasat-e khiyābān kharāb shod.

Hello Mr Karimi. My car broke down in the middle of the street yesterday.

Spoken سلام آقای کریمی. ماشینم دیروز وسط خیابون خراب شد. salām āghā-ye Karimi. māshin-am diruz vasat-e khiyābun kharāb shod.

Mr Karimi

چه شد؟ یک‌دفعه خاموش شد؟

che shod? yek-daf'e khāmush shod?

What happened? Did it suddenly switch off?

Spoken چی شد؟ یه‌دفعه خاموش شد؟ chi shod? ye-daf'e khāmush shod?

Reza

بله. و دیگر روشن نشد.

bale. o digar roshan na-shod.

Yes. And it wouldn't start again.

Spoken آره. دیگه روشن نشد. āre. dige roshan na-shod.

Mr Karimi

باتری چک شده است؟

bātri chek shode ast?

Has the battery been checked?

Spoken باتری چک شده؟ bātri chek shode?

Reza

بله، پارسال تعویض شد.

bale, pārsāl ta'viz shod.

Yes, it was replaced last year.

Mr Karimi

خب، باید ماشین به تعمیرگاه آورده شود. فردا صبح آماده می‌شود.

khob, bāyad māshin be ta'mirgāh āvarde shavad. fardā sobh āmāde mi-shavad.

Well, the car must be brought to the workshop. It will be ready tomorrow morning.

Spoken خب، ماشین باید بیاد تعمیرگاه. فردا صبح آماده می‌شه. khob, māshin bāyad biyād ta'mirgāh. fardā sobh āmāde mi-she.

Reza

هزینه‌اش چقدر می‌شود؟

hazine-ash cheghadr mi-shavad?

How much will it cost?

Spoken هزینه‌ش چقدر می‌شه؟ hazine-sh cheghadr mi-she?

Mr Karimi

هنوز نمی‌شود گفت. اول باید دیده شود.

hanuz nemi-shavad goft. avval bāyad dide shavad.

One can't say yet. First it has to be looked at.

Spoken هنوز نمی‌شه گفت. اول باید دیده شه. hanuz nemi-she goft. avval bāyad dide she.

Reza

می‌گویند شما بهترین مکانیک محله هستید.

mi-guyand shomā behtarin mekānik-e mahalle hastid.

They say you are the best mechanic in the neighbourhood.

Spoken می‌گن شما بهترین مکانیک محله‌این. mi-gan shomā behtarin mekānik-e mahalle-in.

Mr Karimi

لطف دارید. نگران نباشید، درست می‌شود.

lotf dārid. negarān na-bāshid, dorost mi-shavad.

You're kind. Don't worry, it will be fixed.

Spoken لطف دارین. نگران نباشین، درست می‌شه. lotf dārin. negarān na-bāshin, dorost mi-she.

Reading the signs

Once you know the passive, Iranian streets become readable. Shop windows and official notices use it everywhere, usually in the present tense with می‌شود:

  • این‌جا کلید ساخته می‌شود in-jā kelid sākhte mi-shavad keys cut here (literally here key is made)
  • به قیمت مناسب فروخته می‌شود be gheymat-e monāseb forukhte mi-shavad sold at a reasonable price
  • خواهشمند است از کشیدن سیگار خودداری شود khāheshmand ast az keshidan-e sigār khoddāri shavad please refrain from smoking – the most formal register of all, with a subjunctive passive.
  • ورود ممنوع vorud mamnu' no entry – no verb at all; Arabic participles like ممنوع (forbidden) do the passive's job.

In Isfahan, Si-o-se-pol is where the city goes for its evening walk. Under the arches, people sing: the vaulted brick makes a natural echo chamber, and on summer nights you can hear old songs and poems of Hafez sung by strangers – a tradition that is, as the reading says, گفته می‌شود, 'said to be' as old as the bridge.

Tips

  • If you are speaking, not writing, try the they form first: کتاب را چاپ کردند sounds more natural than کتاب چاپ شد.
  • Not every شدن is a passive. In خسته شدم ('I got tired') or بزرگ شد ('he grew up'), شدن just means 'to become'. Ask yourself: could I add 'by someone'? If not, it is a change of state, not a passive.
  • Intransitive verbs have no passive: you cannot say was gone in Persian any more than in English. Verbs like آمدن، رفتن، خوابیدن never take شدن.
  • After می‌شود / نمی‌شود in the sense 'one can / one can't', the formal written style uses the short infinitive (the past stem): نمی‌شود گفت. In speech you will also hear the subjunctive: نمی‌شه بگیم.
  • Spoken Persian keeps شد as it is but shortens شده است to just شده: فروخته شده 'it's been sold'.
