Course / The Script / Lesson 7

ن، و، ه، یNun, vāv, he, ye – and the hamza

nun, vāv, he, ye

The last four letters – and the busiest. Nun completes the tooth family; vāv and ye are both consonants and long vowels; he is either an h or a silent final e. You also meet the hamza, a small sign for the glottal stop, and learn how the ezafe is written after a silent e. By the end of this lesson you know all 32 letters.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • read and write ن و ه ی in all their shapes
  • read و as v, u, o or ow, and ی as y, i or ey
  • tell the consonant h from a silent final e
  • recognise the hamza on its seats ئ أ ؤ
  • write the ezafe after a silent e, as in خانه‌ی من

Nun and ye – the tooth family is complete

ن nun is n. At the start or in the middle of a word it is a short tooth with one dot above: نـ, ـنـ. Alone or at the end it becomes a deep round bowl with the dot over it: ن, ـن. In نان nān bread you see both: a tooth at the start, alef, and the full round nun at the end.

ی ye also starts as a tooth, with two dots below: یـ, ـیـ. Alone or at the end it swings into a wide curve, and there it has no dots at all: ی, ـی. Arabic dots its final ye; Persian never does – a quick way to tell the two languages apart.

So the tooth family is complete, and in the middle of a word only the dots tell its members apart:

  • one dot below – b (ب be)
  • three dots below – p (پ pe)
  • two dots above – t (ت te)
  • three dots above – s (ث se)
  • one dot above – n (ن nun)
  • two dots below – y (ی ye)

Vāv and ye – consonants that are also vowels

و vāv is a small round head with a tail curving below the line. It is a non-joiner – the last of the seven: ا د ذ ر ز ژ و – so it has just two shapes, و and ـو.

Vāv and ye each do several jobs. Vāv can be:

  • the consonant v: وقت vaght time
  • the long vowel u, as in moon: دوست dust friend, روز ruz day
  • a short o, in a few small words: تو to you, دو do two, and the word for and, و va, which is usually said o
  • the diphthong ow, as in low: نوروز nowruz New Year

Ye can be:

  • the consonant y: یک yek one
  • the long vowel i, as in see: سیب sib apple
  • the diphthong ey, as in day: خیلی kheyli very – where the first ye is ey and the second i

The spelling does not tell you which job a letter is doing; the word does. As a rule of thumb, both are consonants at the start of a word and vowels inside it. A long vowel at the very start of a word gets an alef in front as a seat: ایران Irān Iran, او u he, she.

One oddity: after خ khe, vāv is sometimes not pronounced at all. خواهر khāhar sister is spelled kh-v-ā-h-r but said khāhar, and خواب khāb sleep is khāb. This silent vāv is a fossil of an older pronunciation, khw-.

He – h or a silent e

ه he is the second h of Persian – the first was ح he-ye jimi in lesson 3 – and much the more common one. Its four shapes look surprisingly different:

  • alone: ه, a small loop
  • at the start: هـ, a rounder shape with a loop inside
  • in the middle: ـهـ, the 'two-eyed he', two little loops one above the other
  • at the end: ـه, a small loop hanging off the line

At the start or in the middle of a word, he is always the consonant h: هوا havā air; weather, شهر shahr city. At the end it may be h, as in ماه māh moon; month, but very often it is silent and stands for the vowel e: خانه khāne house. Nothing in the spelling tells you which – you learn it with the word. ده dah ten and ده deh village are spelled alike, and so are نه na no and نه noh nine.

When something is added after a silent final he, Persian keeps the two apart with a ZWNJ: خانه‌ها khāne-hā houses.

The hamza and its seats

The hamza (ء) is not one of the 32 letters but a small sign for the glottal stop – the same catch as ع eyn, and written with the same apostrophe in this course. It comes mostly in words from Arabic. Now and then it stands on its own, as at the end of جزء joz' part, but usually it sits on a seat, a letter that only carries it and is not pronounced itself:

  • on a dotless ye-tooth, ئ: مسئول mas'ul responsible, in charge, رئیس ra'is head, boss
  • on alef, أ: تأثیر ta'sir effect, influence
  • on vāv, ؤ: سؤال so'āl question

Read the hamza as a small catch – in quick speech it often disappears altogether. Spellings vary, so you will also meet سئوال for so'āl and مسؤول for mas'ul.

One more place for a little hamza: older books and some newspapers write the ezafe after a silent he as a tiny hamza on top, خانهٔ. This course writes it the modern way, خانه‌ی khāne-ye – see below.

Writing the ezafe

Persian links a noun to a word that describes it, or to its owner, with a short vowel called the ezafe (اضافه ezāfe): -e after a consonant, -ye after a vowel. کتاب من ketāb-e man my book is literally 'book-of me'. You will use it all the time from A1 on; here is just how it is written, which depends on how the first word ends:

  • after a consonant, it is not written at all: کتاب من ketāb-e man
  • after a silent final he, it is a ZWNJ and a ye: خانه‌ی من khāne-ye man my house. The ZWNJ stops the ye from joining the he.
  • after a long ā or u (alef or vāv), a ye is added: پای چپ pā-ye chap the left foot, دانشجوی ایرانی dāneshju-ye irāni the Iranian student
  • after a final i (ye), nothing is added – you just say -ye: صندلی من sandali-ye man my chair

Reading tips

  • A tooth in the middle of a word: one dot above is n, two dots below is y. Check both the number of dots and their side.
  • Vāv and ye at the start of a word are consonants (v, y); inside a word they are usually the vowels u and i.
  • A final he is usually a silent e – but not in ماه māh, راه rāh road or کوه kuh mountain. These you learn as you go.
  • The final ye has no dots. If you see two dots under a final ye on a screen, the text was typed on an Arabic keyboard (see lesson 8).
  • Read every hamza seat – ئ أ ؤ – as a small catch, like eyn. The seat letter itself is silent.
