Course / Intermediate (B1) / Lesson 2

خواباندن، رساندن، گذاشتنMaking things happen: 'put to sleep', 'drop off', 'let'

khābāndan, resāndan, gozāshtan

English needs extra words to make something happen: make someone laugh, put a child to sleep, give a friend a lift, let someone go. Persian often does it inside the verb. Add -ān- to خندیدن 'to laugh' and you get خنداندن 'to make laugh'; add it to رسیدن 'to arrive' and you get رساندن 'to get someone somewhere'. In this lesson you learn these causative verbs, the everyday alternatives with کردن and دادن, how to say 'I had my car repaired', and the verb گذاشتن in its second meaning, 'to let'.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • turn verbs like خوابیدن and ترسیدن into causatives: خواباندن، ترساندن
  • use the most common causatives: رساندن، نشاندن، خوراندن، گذراندن، فهماندن
  • make things happen with کردن and دادن: بیدار کردن، یاد دادن، مجبور کردن
  • say 'I had something done' with دادن: ماشین را دادم تعمیر کنند
  • say 'let me', 'don't let', 'he didn't let me' with گذاشتن

A sleepless father

Reza arrives at the office looking terrible, and his colleague Sara wants to know why. Listen for verbs that make someone else do something – put to sleep, give medicine, drop off – and for نگذاشت 'didn't let'.

Sara

رضا، چه شده؟ خیلی خسته به نظر می‌رسی.

Rezā, che shode? kheyli khaste be nazar mi-resi.

Reza, what's happened? You look really tired.

Spoken رضا، چی شده؟ خیلی خسته به نظر می‌رسی. Rezā, chi shode? kheyli khaste be nazar mi-resi.

Reza

دیشب پسرم نگذاشت بخوابیم. تا ساعت سه گریه کرد.

dishab pesar-am nagozāsht bekhābim. tā sā'at-e se gerye kard.

Last night my son didn't let us sleep. He cried until three o'clock.

Spoken دیشب پسرم نذاشت بخوابیم. تا ساعت سه گریه کرد. dishab pesar-am nazāsht bekhābim. tā sā'at-e se gerye kard.

Sara

مگر او را نمی‌خوابانید؟

magar u rā nemi-khābānid?

Don't you put him to sleep?

Spoken مگه نمی‌خوابونینش؟ mage nemi-khābunin-esh?

Reza

چرا، مینا برایش لالایی می‌خواند و او را می‌خواباند، اما نیم ساعت بعد بیدار می‌شود.

cherā, Minā barāy-ash lālāyi mi-khānad o u rā mi-khābānad, ammā nim sā'at ba'd bidār mi-shavad.

Of course we do – Mina sings him a lullaby and puts him to sleep, but half an hour later he wakes up.

Spoken چرا، مینا براش لالایی می‌خونه و می‌خوابونه، ولی نیم ساعت بعد بیدار می‌شه. cherā, Minā barā-sh lālāyi mi-khune o mi-khābune, vali nim sā'at ba'd bidār mi-she.

Sara

شاید گرسنه باشد. قبل از خواب به او شیر می‌دهید؟

shāyad gorosne bāshad. ghabl az khāb be u shir mi-dahid?

Maybe he's hungry. Do you give him milk before bed?

Spoken شاید گشنه باشه. قبل از خواب بهش شیر می‌دین؟ shāyad goshne bāshe. ghabl az khāb behesh shir mi-din?

Reza

بله. دکتر هم گفت که هر شب یک قاشق شربت به او بخورانیم.

bale. doktor ham goft ke har shab yek ghāshogh sharbat be u bekhorānim.

Yes. And the doctor said we should give him a spoonful of syrup every night.

Spoken آره. دکتر هم گفت هر شب یه قاشق شربت بهش بخورونیم. āre. doktor ham goft har shab ye ghāshogh sharbat behesh bekhorunim.

Sara

بیچاره! با این خستگی که نمی‌توانی رانندگی کنی. چطور آمدی؟

bichāre! bā in khastegi ke nemi-tavāni rānandegi koni. chetowr āmadi?

Poor thing! You can't drive in this state. How did you get here?

Spoken بیچاره! با این خستگی که نمی‌تونی رانندگی کنی. چطوری اومدی؟ bichāre! bā in khastegi ke nemi-tuni rānandegi koni. chetori umadi?

Reza

برادرم مرا با ماشین رساند.

barādar-am marā bā māshin resānd.

My brother gave me a lift.

Spoken داداشم منو رسوند. dādāsh-am mano resund.

Sara

عصر من تو را می‌رسانم. خانه‌ی ما نزدیک شماست.

asr man to rā mi-resānam. khāne-ye mā nazdik-e shomāst.

I'll drop you off this evening. Our house is near yours.

Spoken عصری من می‌رسونمت. خونه‌ی ما نزدیک شماست. asri man mi-resunam-et. khune-ye mā nazdik-e shomāst.

Reza

دستت درد نکند، زحمتت می‌شود.

dast-at dard nakonad, zahmat-at mi-shavad.

Thank you – but it'll be trouble for you.

Spoken دستت درد نکنه، زحمتت می‌شه. dast-at dard nakone, zahmat-at mi-she.

Sara

چه زحمتی! بگذار یک چای هم برایت بیاورم.

che zahmat-i! begozār yek chāy ham barāy-at biyāvaram.

No trouble at all! Let me bring you a tea as well.

Spoken چه زحمتی! بذار یه چایی هم برات بیارم. che zahmat-i! bezār ye chāyi ham barā-t biyāram.

Reza

ممنون. فقط نگذار رئیس مرا پشت میز خواب ببیند!

mamnun. faghat nagozār ra'is marā posht-e miz khāb bebinad!

Thanks. Just don't let the boss see me asleep at my desk!

Spoken مرسی. فقط نذار رئیس منو پشت میز خواب ببینه! mersi. faghat nazār ra'is mano posht-e miz khāb bebine!

Verbs that make things happen

In English you make someone laugh, put a baby to sleep, give someone a lift. Persian packs the 'make' into the verb itself with a small piece, ـان -ān-, added to the present stem: خندیدن khandidan to laugh → خنداندن khandāndan to make laugh, خوابیدن khābidan to sleep → خواباندن khābāndan to put to sleep. Grammar books call these causative verbs (فعل سببی fe'l-e sababi).

You heard four of them in the dialogue: Mina می‌خواباند mi-khābānad puts (him) to sleep, the doctor wants them to بخورانیم bekhorānim give (him) syrup, Reza's brother رساند resānd got him here, and Sara will می‌رسانم mi-resānam drop (him) off. In speech the ا ā of -ān- becomes و u: می‌رسونم mi-resunam, خوابوند khābund.

Not every verb has a causative of this kind. For the rest, Persian uses a compound with کردن kardan or دادن dādan – and for 'let', the verb گذاشتن gozāshtan, which you already know as 'to put'.

-ān-: the causative

To make a causative, take the present stem of a verb and add ـاندن -āndan. The result is an ordinary regular verb: its past stem ends in ـاند -ānd and its present stem in ـان -ān.

  • خواب khāb (stem of خوابیدن) → خواباندن khābāndan: خواباندم khābāndam I put (him) to sleep, می‌خوابانم mi-khābānam I put (him) to sleep, now, بخوابان! bekhābān! put him to sleep!
  • رس res (stem of رسیدن) → رساندن resāndan: رساندم resāndam, می‌رسانم mi-resānam, برسان! beresān!

Three things to know:

  1. Watch the stem. A few causatives are built on a short or old stem: نشستن neshastan → نشاندن neshāndan (not *نشیناندن); گذشتن gozashtan (stem گذر gozar) → گذراندن gozarāndan; سوختن sukhtan (stem سوز suz) → سوزاندن suzāndan.
  2. The causative takes an object. Someone is put to sleep, frightened, delivered – so a definite object gets را rā: بچه را خواباند bacche rā khābānd she put the child to sleep.
  3. Two spellings, one verb. Formal texts also use an older ending ـانیدن -ānidan: رسانیدن resānidan = رساندن resāndan. In speech -ān- becomes -un-: رسوندن resundan, ترسوندی! tarsundi! you scared me!

Making and having things done: کردن and دادن

Most verbs have no -ān- form. Persian then uses a pair of compounds: the 'it happens' verb takes شدن shodan, گرفتن gereftan or خوردن khordan; the 'make it happen' verb takes کردن kardan, دادن dādan or زدن zadan. Learn them in pairs, as in the table.

Having something done. To say that you got someone else to do a job – I had my car repaired, I had a suit made – use دادن dādan followed by a clause in the subjunctive: ماشین را دادم تعمیر کنند māshin rā dādam ta'mir konand I had the car repaired (literally I gave the car [so that] they repair it). In speech the second verb is often in the past: ماشینو دادم تعمیر کردن māshin-o dādam ta'mir kardan.

Making someone do something. For force, make (someone) do, use مجبور کردن majbur kardan or the more formal وادار کردن vādār kardan with که ke and a subjunctive: مرا مجبور کرد که بروم marā majbur kard ke beravam he made me go. In newspapers you will also see به be + a noun: او را وادار به استعفا کردند u rā vādār be este'fā kardand they forced him to resign.

Let: گذاشتن and اجازه دادن

گذاشتن gozāshtan means to put – کتاب را روی میز گذاشت ketāb rā ru-ye miz gozāsht he put the book on the table – but followed by a subjunctive it means to let: نگذاشت بروم nagozāsht beravam he didn't let me go. The person who is (not) allowed is simply the subject of the second verb; you need no را, and که ke is optional.

The imperative بگذار begozār (spoken بذار bezār) is the everyday way to say let me…: بگذار فکر کنم begozār fekr konam let me think, بذار ببینم bezār bebinam let me see. To several people, or politely: بگذارید begozārid, spoken بذارین bezārin.

For permission in the official sense, use اجازه دادن ejāze dādan: پدرم اجازه نداد که تنها سفر کنم pedar-am ejāze nadād ke tanhā safar konam my father didn't allow me to travel alone. And to ask for it: اجازه می‌دهید؟ ejāze mi-dahid? may I?

Spoken نذار بچه‌ها به اجاق دست بزنن. nazār bacche-hā be ojāgh dast bezanan.

پلیس اجازه نداد که ماشین‌ها از پل رد شوند.

polis ejāze nadād ke māshin-hā az pol rad shavand.

The police didn't let cars cross the bridge.

بگذارید ببینم، فردا ساعت ده وقت دارم.

begozārid bebinam, fardā sā'at-e dah vaght dāram.

Let me see – tomorrow at ten I'm free.

Spoken بذارین ببینم، فردا ساعت ده وقت دارم. bezārin bebinam, fardā sā'at-e dah vaght dāram.

ببخشید، اجازه می‌دهید این‌جا بنشینم؟

bebakhshid, ejāze mi-dahid in-jā beneshinam?

Excuse me, may I sit here?

Spoken ببخشید، اجازه هست این‌جا بشینم؟ bebakhshid, ejāze hast in-jā beshinam?

Night shift

Leila is a nurse on a children's ward. Her job is full of causatives: she gives medicine, calms, seats and puts to sleep. Look out for the verbs with -ān-.

لیلا پرستار بخش کودکان یکی از بیمارستان‌های تهران است.

Leylā parastār-e bakhsh-e kudakān-e yek-i az bimārestān-hā-ye Tehrān ast.

Leila is a nurse on the children's ward of one of Tehran's hospitals.

او بیشتر شب‌ها کار می‌کند.

u bishtar-e shab-hā kār mi-konad.

She works most nights.

شیفت او ساعت هشت شب شروع می‌شود و ساعت هشت صبح تمام می‌شود.

shift-e u sā'at-e hasht-e shab shoru' mi-shavad o sā'at-e hasht-e sobh tamām mi-shavad.

Her shift starts at eight in the evening and ends at eight in the morning.

اول داروهای بچه‌ها را به آن‌ها می‌خوراند.

avval dāru-hā-ye bacche-hā rā be ān-hā mi-khorānad.

First she gives the children their medicines.

بعضی از بچه‌ها از آمپول می‌ترسند و گریه می‌کنند.

ba'zi az bacche-hā az āmpul mi-tarsand o gerye mi-konand.

Some of the children are afraid of injections and cry.

لیلا با قصه و شوخی آن‌ها را می‌خنداند و آرامشان می‌کند.

Leylā bā ghesse o shukhi ān-hā rā mi-khandānad o ārām-eshān mi-konad.

Leila makes them laugh with stories and jokes and calms them down.

دیشب پسر کوچکی را آوردند که خیلی ترسیده بود.

dishab pesar-e kuchak-i rā āvardand ke kheyli tarside bud.

Last night they brought in a little boy who was very frightened.

پدرش او را روی تخت نشاند، اما پسر مدام گریه می‌کرد.

pedar-ash u rā ru-ye takht neshānd, ammā pesar modām gerye mi-kard.

His father sat him on the bed, but the boy wouldn't stop crying.

لیلا کنار تخت نشست و برایش لالایی خواند تا او را بخواباند.

Leylā kenār-e takht neshast o barāy-ash lālāyi khānd tā u rā bekhābānad.

Leila sat by the bed and sang him a lullaby to get him to sleep.

ساعت دو بالاخره بچه خوابید و پدرش هم روی صندلی خوابش برد.

sā'at-e do bel-akhare bacche khābid o pedar-ash ham ru-ye sandali khāb-ash bord.

At two the child finally fell asleep, and his father dozed off on a chair too.

صبح، شوهر لیلا او را با ماشین به خانه رساند.

sobh, showhar-e Leylā u rā bā māshin be khāne resānd.

In the morning, Leila's husband drove her home.

لیلا می‌گوید: «کار سختی است، اما هیچ‌وقت نمی‌گذارم یک بچه تنها گریه کند.»

Leylā mi-guyad: «kār-e sakht-i ast, ammā hich-vaght nemi-gozāram yek bacche tanhā gerye konad.»

Leila says: 'It's hard work, but I never let a child cry alone.'

Choose the right answer.

Shall I drop you off?

In Iran a guest almost never walks home alone. At the end of an evening, whoever has a car will offer: برسونمتون؟ beresunam-etun? shall I drop you off? – and the guest, as تعارف ta'ārof demands, will refuse at least once: نه، مزاحم نمی‌شم na, mozāhem nemi-sham no, I don't want to impose. The driver insists – چه مزاحمتی! سر راهمه che mozāhemat-i! sar-e rāh-ame no trouble at all, it's on my way – even when it clearly is not on the way. Two refusals and one insistence later, everyone gets in the car.

The same verb, رساندن resāndan, carries greetings. As you leave someone's house you will hear به خانواده سلام برسانید be khānevāde salām beresānid give my regards to your family – literally deliver my greeting – and you answer چشم، حتماً cheshm, hatman I will, of course. When you pass the greeting on, you say مینا سلام رساند Minā salām resānd Mina sends her regards.

And at bedtime, Iranian mothers and grandmothers still sing the old lullaby that begins لالا لالا گل پونه lālā lālā gol-e pune hush, hush, little mint flower – the gentlest causative of all, a song to make a child sleep.

Tips

  • In speech -ān- becomes -un-: رسوندن، خوابوندن، ترسوندن. You will hear برسونمت؟ 'shall I drop you?' and منو ترسوندی! 'you scared me!' many times a day.
  • Not every verb has an -ān- causative. If you cannot find one in the dictionary, use the compound with کردن or دادن: بیدار کردن 'to wake someone', never *بیداراندن.
  • رساندن is one of the most useful verbs in Persian: give a lift (شما را می‌رسانم), deliver (نامه را رساند), pass on greetings (سلام برسانید), and in formal style 'bring to': به پایان رساندن 'to complete', به اطلاع رساندن 'to inform'.
  • گذاشتن means 'put' with an object and a place (کیف را روی صندلی گذاشت), but 'let' when a subjunctive follows (نگذاشت بنشینم). Its negative نگذاشت is spoken نذاشت.
  • خوراندن is for medicine, or for feeding someone who cannot feed themselves. For simply giving someone food, say غذا دادن.
  • The older forms in -ānidan (رسانیدن، خوابانیدن) mean exactly the same; you will meet them in books and official letters.
