Course / Elementary (A2) / Lesson 9

این کار را بکن!Do this! Instructions and a recipe

in kār rā bekon!

Recipes, directions, a teacher's commands and a mother's 'don't touch that!' all use the imperative. In this lesson you learn the plain and the polite imperative, how to say 'don't', and how to give the steps of a task in order – and you cook a pot of āsh-e reshte, the noodle soup that Iranians eat when someone sets off on a journey.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • give instructions and commands to one person or to several
  • say 'don't …' with the negative imperative
  • soften a command with لطفاً and بفرمایید
  • put the steps of a task in order with اول، بعد، سپس، در آخر
  • follow and give a simple recipe
Show

In Aunt Parvin's kitchen

Sara is learning to cook āsh-e reshte with her aunt Parvin. Tom, who is visiting, wants to help.

Parvin

سارا جان، لوبیاها را بیاور و توی قابلمه بریز.

Sārā jān, lubiyā-hā rā biyāvar o tu-ye ghāblame beriz.

Sara dear, bring the beans and pour them into the pot.

Spoken سارا جان، لوبیاها رو بیار و تو قابلمه بریز. Sārā jān, lubiyā-hā ro biyār o tu ghāblame beriz.

Sara

چشم، خاله. آب هم بریزم؟

chashm, khāle. āb ham berizam?

Yes, auntie. Shall I pour in water too?

Parvin

بله، دو لیوان آب بریز و زیرش را روشن کن.

bale, do livān āb beriz o zir-ash rā roshan kon.

Yes, pour in two glasses of water and turn on the heat under it.

Tom

من چه کار کنم؟

man che kār konam?

What shall I do?

Spoken من چی کار کنم؟ man chi kār konam?

Parvin

تام جان، پیازها را خرد کن. اما گریه نکن!

Tām jān, piyāz-hā rā khord kon. ammā gerye na-kon!

Tom dear, chop the onions. But don't cry!

Tom

سعی می‌کنم! کارد کجاست؟

sa'y mi-konam! kārd kojā-st?

I'll try! Where is the knife?

Spoken سعی می‌کنم! کارد کجاست؟ sa'y mi-konam! kārd kojā-st?

Parvin

توی کشو است. مواظب دستت باش.

tu-ye keshow ast. movāzeb-e dast-at bāsh.

It's in the drawer. Mind your hand.

Spoken تو کشوئه. مواظب دستت باش. tu keshow-e. movāzeb-e dast-et bāsh.

Sara

خاله، سبزی‌ها را حالا بریزم یا بعد؟

khāle, sabzi-hā rā hālā berizam yā ba'd?

Auntie, shall I put the herbs in now or later?

Parvin

نه، هنوز نه. اول بگذار لوبیا نرم شود.

na, hanuz na. avval begozār lubiyā narm shavad.

No, not yet. First let the beans get soft.

Spoken نه، هنوز نه. اول بذار لوبیا نرم بشه. na, hanuz na. avval bezār lubiyā narm beshe.

Tom

پیازها تمام شد. حالا چی؟

piyāz-hā tamām shod. hālā chi?

The onions are done. Now what?

Parvin

آفرین! حالا بنشینید و یک چای بخورید. آش یک ساعت دیگر آماده می‌شود.

āfarin! hālā beneshinid o yek chāy bokhorid. āsh yek sā'at-e digar āmāde mi-shavad.

Well done! Now sit down and have a tea. The soup will be ready in an hour.

Spoken آفرین! حالا بنشینین و یه چای بخورین. آش یه ساعت دیگه آماده می‌شه. āfarin! hālā beneshinin o ye chāy bokhorin. āsh ye sā'at-e dige āmāde mi-she.

Sara

خاله، تو هم بنشین! ما بقیه‌اش را درست می‌کنیم.

khāle, to ham beneshin! mā baghiyye-ash rā dorost mi-konim.

Auntie, you sit down too! We'll do the rest.

Telling people what to do

Persian commands are built on the present stem – the same stem you already use after می: می‌روم mi-ravam I go has the stem رو rav. Put بـ be- in front of the stem and you have the command for one person: برو! borow! go!. Add ـید -id for several people, or for one person politely: بروید! beravid! go!.

That is the whole system. The only things to watch are a few stems that change shape, and the compound verbs, where be- attaches to the verb part: روشن کن roshan kon turn on, خرد کن khord kon chop – here the plain kon without be- is normal.

To say don't, replace be- with نـ na-: نرو! na-row! don't go!, گریه نکن! gerye na-kon! don't cry!.

The imperative: be- + stem

Take the present stem, add بـ be- in front. For one person (a friend, a child) the stem alone is the command; for several people or for politeness add ـید -id.

The prefix is normally be-, but before a stem that begins with o or u it becomes bo- (بخور bokhor eat, بکن bokon do), and before a stem beginning with ā or a it is written with an extra ی: بیاور biyāvar bring, بیا biyā come.

بیا این‌جا و بنشین.

Come here and sit down.

Spoken بیا این‌جا و بشین. biyā in-jā o beshin.

لطفاً در را ببندید.

lotfan dar rā bebandid.

Please close the door.

esm-at rā in-jā benevis.

Write your name here.

به من بگو کجا بودی.

be man begu kojā budi.

Tell me where you were.

آرام باشید، همه چیز خوب است.

ārām bāshid, hame chiz khub ast.

Stay calm, everything is fine.

Don't! The negative imperative

For don't, the prefix نـ na- replaces be-: نرو na-row don't go, نخور na-khor don't eat, نگو na-gu don't say. Before ā, na- is written with a ی, just like be-: نیا na-yā don't come, نیاور na-yāvar don't bring.

With compound verbs the plain verb part takes na-: گریه نکن gerye na-kon don't cry, نگران نباش negarān na-bāsh don't worry, فراموش نکنید farāmush na-konid don't forget.

In speech you will constantly hear two softeners: لطفاً lotfan please before the command, and the all-purpose polite verb بفرمایید befarmāyid, which replaces come in, sit down, go ahead, here you are, help yourself. A polite request can also avoid the imperative entirely: می‌شود در را ببندید؟ mi-shavad dar rā bebandid? Could you close the door? – literally does it become that you close the door?

نگران نباش، من هستم.

negarān na-bāsh, man hastam.

Don't worry, I'm here.

این را به کسی نگو.

in rā be kas-i na-gu.

Don't tell anyone this.

Spoken اینو به کسی نگو. in-o be kas-i na-gu.

دیر نیایید؛ غذا سرد می‌شود.

dir na-yāyid; ghazā sard mi-shavad.

Don't come late; the food will get cold.

کلیدها را فراموش نکن.

kelid-hā rā farāmush na-kon.

Don't forget the keys.

بفرمایید تو، خانه‌ی خودتان است.

befarmāyid tu, khāne-ye khod-etān ast.

Please come in – treat it as your own house.

Spoken بفرمایین تو، خونه‌ی خودتونه. befarmāyin tu, khune-ye khod-etun-e.

می‌شود کمی آرام‌تر صحبت کنید؟

mi-shavad kam-i ārām-tar sohbat konid?

Could you speak a little more slowly?

First, then, finally: putting steps in order

Instructions need order words. The everyday set is اول avval first, بعد ba'd then, سپس sepas then (more formal), در آخر dar ākhar finally. After that is بعد از آن ba'd az ān, before that قبل از آن ghabl az ān, and while is وقتی که vaghti ke or, in cooking, تا tā until: بپز تا نرم شود bepaz tā narm shavad cook until it is soft.

Notice until it becomes soft: after تا tā and after بگذار begozār let the next verb is in the subjunctive you met in lesson 4 – شود shavad, not می‌شود mi-shavad.

اول پیاز را سرخ کن، بعد لوبیا را بریز.

avval piyāz rā sorkh kon, ba'd lubiyā rā beriz.

First fry the onion, then add the beans.

سپس دو لیتر آب اضافه کنید.

sepas do litr āb ezāfe konid.

Then add two litres of water.

بگذار یک ساعت بپزد.

begozār yek sā'at bepazad.

Let it cook for an hour.

در آخر کشک و نعنای داغ را روی آش بریزید.

dar ākhar kashk o na'nā-ye dāgh rā ru-ye āsh berizid.

Finally pour the kashk and the hot mint over the soup.

Recipe: āsh-e reshte for six

A real recipe written the way Iranian cookbooks write them – in the polite plural imperative. Read it, listen to each step, then try to retell the steps with اول، بعد، سپس، در آخر.

یک لیوان نخود و یک لیوان لوبیا را از شب قبل خیس کنید.

yek livān nokhod o yek livān lubiyā rā az shab-e ghabl khis konid.

Soak a glass of chickpeas and a glass of beans from the night before.

دو پیاز بزرگ را خرد کنید و در روغن سرخ کنید.

do piyāz-e bozorg rā khord konid o dar rowghan sorkh konid.

Chop two large onions and fry them in oil.

نصف پیازها را برای روی آش کنار بگذارید.

nesf-e piyāz-hā rā barā-ye ru-ye āsh kenār begozārid.

Set aside half of the onions for the top of the soup.

نخود، لوبیا و یک لیوان عدس را با شش لیوان آب در قابلمه بریزید.

nokhod, lubiyā o yek livān adas rā bā shesh livān āb dar ghāblame berizid.

Put the chickpeas, beans and a glass of lentils in the pot with six glasses of water.

بگذارید یک ساعت بپزد تا نرم شود.

begozārid yek sā'at bepazad tā narm shavad.

Let it cook for an hour until it is soft.

سپس سبزی خرد شده را اضافه کنید: تره، جعفری، گشنیز و اسفناج.

sepas sabzi-ye khord shode rā ezāfe konid: tare, ja'fari, geshniz o esfenāj.

Then add the chopped herbs: leek, parsley, coriander and spinach.

بعد از نیم ساعت، رشته‌ها را بشکنید و در آش بریزید.

ba'd az nim sā'at, reshte-hā rā beshkanid o dar āsh berizid.

After half an hour, break the noodles and put them in the soup.

نمک، فلفل و زردچوبه بزنید و خوب هم بزنید.

namak, felfel o zard-chube bezanid o khub ham bezanid.

Add salt, pepper and turmeric and stir well.

در آخر، کشک، نعنای داغ و پیاز سرخ شده را روی آش بریزید.

dar ākhar, kashk, na'nā-ye dāgh o piyāz-e sorkh shode rā ru-ye āsh berizid.

Finally, pour the kashk, fried mint and fried onion over the soup.

نوش جان!

nush-e jān!

Enjoy!

Soup for the traveller

آش رشته āsh-e reshte is more than lunch. Iranians cook it on چهارشنبه‌سوری chahārshanbe-suri, the fire festival before Nowruz, and when someone is about to travel: the long noodles stand for the threads of life and the road ahead, and the family eats a bowl together پشت پای مسافر posht-e pā-ye mosāfer behind the traveller's foot – after the traveller has left – to wish them a safe return.

The word آش āsh is old; it gave Persian its words for cook, آشپز āshpaz (literally soup-cooker), and kitchen, آشپزخانه āshpaz-khāne. Thick soups like this one, with legumes, herbs and sour کشک kashk, are the everyday food of Iranian homes, far more than the kebabs tourists know.

Tips

  • You already know several imperatives without realising it: بفرمایید, ببخشید (excuse me = forgive!), بگو in بگو ببینم ('tell me, let's see') and برو in برو بابا ('get away with you!').
  • In speech, the polite -id ending becomes -in: بنشینید → بنشینین, بخورید → بخورین; and bo-khor often loses its vowel: بخور sounds like bokhor or bkhor.
  • The stem of a verb is in the dictionary section of this site under every verb; learn the stem together with the infinitive, as a pair: رفتن / رو, گفتن / گو.
  • A command to a stranger is softer with می‌شود … ؟ ('could you …?') or لطفاً. To a waiter or taxi driver, a plain polite plural is normal: لطفاً این‌جا نگه دارید 'please stop here'.
