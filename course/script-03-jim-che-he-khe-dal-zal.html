ج، چ، ح، خ، د، ذJim, che, he, khe, dāl, zāl

jim, che, he, khe, dāl, zāl

Two new families today: the four 'hook' letters jim, che, he and khe, which share one shape and differ by their dots, and the pair dāl and zāl, which never join the letter after them. You also meet kh, the sound English does not have.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • read and write ج چ ح خ in all their shapes
  • read and write د ذ and know they are non-joiners
  • pronounce kh (خ) correctly
  • read words such as khodā, bād and tāj
Show

Jim, che, he, khe – the hook family

These four letters are one shape with different dots, exactly like the dish family of the last lesson. Alone or at the end of a word the letter is a big open hook that dips below the line; at the start or in the middle it shrinks to a small roof over the baseline.

  • ج jim – one dot inside the hook – j as in jam
  • چ che – three dots inside – ch as in church (a Persian letter)
  • ح he-ye jimi – no dot – h. It is called he-ye jimi, 'the he shaped like jim', to tell it from the other h you will meet in lesson 7.
  • خ khe – one dot above – kh

All four join on both sides. Look at تاج tāj crown: te, alef, then jim at the end with its full hook. In چاپ chāp print the che at the start is just a small roof with three dots, joined to nothing because the next letter is alef.

The sound kh

خ khe is the sound in Scottish loch and German Bach: a rough h made at the back of the mouth, as if clearing your throat gently. It is one of the most common sounds in Persian – in good (خوب khub), God (خدا khodā), house (خانه khāne) – so it is worth getting right from the start. Say k, then let the air hiss through instead of stopping it.

In this course it is always written kh in the transliteration.

Dāl and zāl – the non-joiners

د dāl d and ذ zāl z are a small angular hook sitting on the line, zāl with one dot above. Like alef, they join the letter before them but never the letter after them: the pen lifts and the next letter starts fresh. They have only two shapes – alone (د) and joined to the previous letter (ـد).

That is why داد dād gave; justice is three separate pieces: d, ā, d, each refusing to hold hands with the next. And بد bad bad is a be that joins forward into a dāl that stops the chain.

Zāl appears only in words from Arabic, such as ذات zāt essence. It sounds exactly like the ordinary z you will meet in the next lesson.

Reading tips

  • A small roof in the middle of a word with a dot inside is j or ch; with a dot above it is kh; with no dot it is h.
  • Whenever you see د or ذ, the word breaks there: the next letter starts a new piece.
  • Place names ending in -ābād (Abadan, Hyderabad, Islamabad) all contain آباد ābād – 'made to flourish'.
  • Practise kh with خدا khodā and بخت bakht; do not let it slide into a plain k or h.
