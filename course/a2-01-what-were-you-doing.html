Course / Elementary (A2) / Lesson 1

چه کار می‌کردی؟What were you doing? The past continuous

che kār mi-kardi?

At A1 you learned to say what happened (رفتم – I went). Now you learn to paint the background: what was going on, what you used to do as a child, and what you were in the middle of doing when something interrupted. Persian does this with one small prefix you already know – می – stuck onto the past.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • describe what was happening in the past (می‌رفتم)
  • say what you used to do as a child
  • say you were in the middle of doing something (داشتم می‌رفتم)
  • join two past events with وقتی که

A phone call that came too late

Reza rings Mina in the evening. She did not answer his first call.

Reza

سلام مینا. ساعت پنج زنگ زدم، جواب ندادی.

salām Minā. sā'at-e panj zang zadam, javāb na-dādi.

Hi Mina. I rang at five, you didn't answer.

Spoken سلام مینا. ساعت پنج زنگ زدم، جواب ندادی. salām Minā. sā'at-e panj zang zadam, javāb na-dādi.

Mina

ببخشید! ساعت پنج داشتم شام می‌پختم.

bebakhshid! sā'at-e panj dāshtam shām mi-pokhtam.

Sorry! At five I was cooking dinner.

Spoken ببخشید! ساعت پنج داشتم شام می‌پختم. bebakhshid! sā'at-e panj dāshtam shām mi-pokhtam.

Reza

چه کار می‌کردی؟ موبایلت کجا بود؟

che kār mi-kardi? mobāyl-at kojā bud?

What were you doing? Where was your phone?

Spoken چی کار می‌کردی؟ موبایلت کجا بود؟ chi kār mi-kardi? mobāyl-et kojā bud?

Mina

در اتاق بود. من در آشپزخانه بودم و آهنگ گوش می‌کردم.

dar otāgh bud. man dar āshpaz-khāne budam o āhang gush mi-kardam.

It was in the room. I was in the kitchen listening to music.

Spoken تو اتاق بود. من تو آشپزخونه بودم و آهنگ گوش می‌کردم. tu otāgh bud. man tu āshpaz-khune budam o āhang gush mi-kardam.

Reza

آهان. وقتی که زنگ زدم، چیزی نشنیدی؟

āhān. vaghti ke zang zadam, chiz-i na-shenidi?

Ah. When I rang, you didn't hear anything?

Spoken آهان. وقتی زنگ زدم، چیزی نشنیدی؟ āhān. vaghti zang zadam, chizi na-shenidi?

Mina

نه. سارا هم این‌جا بود. با هم حرف می‌زدیم و می‌خندیدیم.

na. Sārā ham in-jā bud. bā ham harf mi-zadim o mi-khandidim.

No. Sara was here too. We were talking together and laughing.

Reza

خوش به حالتان! من تا ساعت هفت کار می‌کردم.

khosh be hāl-etān! man tā sā'at-e haft kār mi-kardam.

Lucky you! I was working until seven.

Spoken خوش به حالتون! من تا ساعت هفت کار می‌کردم. khosh be hāl-etun! man tā sā'at-e haft kār mi-kardam.

Mina

چرا زنگ زدی؟ چیزی شده بود؟

cherā zang zadi? chiz-i shode bud?

Why did you ring? Had something happened?

Reza

نه، فقط می‌خواستم بگویم فردا جلسه ساعت نه است، نه ساعت ده.

na, faghat mi-khāstam beguyam fardā jalase sā'at-e noh ast, na sā'at-e dah.

No, I just wanted to say the meeting tomorrow is at nine, not at ten.

Spoken نه، فقط می‌خواستم بگم فردا جلسه ساعت نهه، نه ساعت ده. na, faghat mi-khāstam begam fardā jalase sā'at-e nohe, na sā'at-e dah.

Mina

خوب شد گفتی! ممنون.

khub shod gofti! mamnun.

Good thing you said! Thanks.

One prefix, two tenses

You know that می mi in front of the present stem makes the present tense: می‌روم mi-ravam I go / I am going. Put the same می mi in front of the past form and you get the past continuous: می‌رفتم mi-raftam I was going / I used to go.

English needs two different tenses for I was going and I used to go; Persian uses one. The context tells you which is meant:

  • هر روز به مدرسه می‌رفتم. har ruz be madrese mi-raftam. Every day I went (used to go) to school. – a habit in the past.
  • ساعت پنج شام می‌پختم. sā'at-e panj shām mi-pokhtam. At five I was cooking dinner. – an action in progress.

To stress that you were right in the middle of something, Persian adds the past of داشتن dāshtan to have in front: داشتم شام می‌پختم dāshtam shām mi-pokhtam I was (busy) cooking dinner. Both verbs carry the same ending. This is exactly like the present دارم می‌روم dāram mi-ravam I am going (right now) you may have heard in speech.

The past continuous: می + past

Take the simple past you know (رفتم raftam, رفتی rafti, رفت raft …) and put می mi in front, joined with a ZWNJ, never a space. The endings do not change. The one difference from the simple past: the third person singular has no extra ending in either tense (رفت raft → می‌رفت mi-raft).

Negative: ن na goes before می mi and both are joined: نمی‌رفتم nemi-raftam I wasn't going / I didn't use to go. Notice the vowel: na + mi becomes nemi.

Simple pastPast continuousEnglish
رفتم raftamمی‌رفتم mi-raftamI was going / used to go
رفتی raftiمی‌رفتی mi-raftiyou were going
رفت raftمی‌رفت mi-rafthe / she was going
رفتیم raftimمی‌رفتیم mi-raftimwe were going
رفتید raftidمی‌رفتید mi-raftidyou were going
رفتند raftandمی‌رفتند mi-raftandthey were going
نرفتم na-raftamنمی‌رفتم nemi-raftamI wasn't going

در بچگی هر تابستان به شمال می‌رفتیم.

dar bacchegi har tābestān be shomāl mi-raftim.

In childhood we went to the north (the Caspian coast) every summer.

a past habit

پدرم همیشه روزنامه می‌خواند.

pedar-am hamishe ruznāme mi-khānd.

My father always used to read the newspaper.

آن وقت‌ها موبایل نداشتیم.

ān vaght-hā mobāyl na-dāshtim.

In those days we didn't have mobile phones.

داشتن and بودن rarely take می in the past: نداشتیم, not نمی‌داشتیم

ساعت هشت هنوز کار می‌کردند.

sā'at-e hasht hanuz kār mi-kardand.

At eight they were still working.

بچه‌ها گریه نمی‌کردند، می‌خندیدند.

bacche-hā gerye nemi-kardand, mi-khandidand.

The children weren't crying, they were laughing.

In the middle of it: داشتم می‌رفتم

When you want to say you were right in the middle of an action – usually one that gets interrupted – add the simple past of داشتن dāshtan in front of the past continuous. Both verbs agree with the subject:

داشتم می‌رفتم dāshtam mi-raftam I was (just) going, داشتی می‌رفتی dāshti mi-rafti, داشت می‌رفت dāsht mi-raft, داشتیم می‌رفتیم dāshtim mi-raftim …

The object goes between the two verbs: داشتم شام می‌پختم dāshtam shām mi-pokhtam I was cooking dinner. This form has no negative – for I wasn't doing use the plain past continuous.

داشتم می‌خوابیدم که تلفن زنگ زد.

dāshtam mi-khābidam ke telefon zang zad.

I was just falling asleep when the phone rang.

داشتیم غذا می‌خوردیم که مهمان آمد.

dāshtim ghazā mi-khordim ke mehmān āmad.

We were eating when a guest arrived.

تو داشتی چه کار می‌کردی؟

to dāshti che kār mi-kardi?

What were you doing (just then)?

Spoken تو داشتی چی کار می‌کردی؟ to dāshti chi kār mi-kardi?

سارا داشت درس می‌خواند.

Sārā dāsht dars mi-khānd.

Sara was studying.

When one thing cut into another: وقتی که

وقتی که vaghti ke when (literally the time that) opens the background clause. The background is in the past continuous, the sudden event in the simple past:

وقتی که زنگ زدی، شام می‌پختم. vaghti ke zang zadi, shām mi-pokhtam. When you rang, I was cooking dinner.

You can also put the background first with که ke joining the interruption, as in the examples above: dāshtam mi-khābidam ke telefon zang zad. In speech که ke is often dropped after وقتی vaghti.

وقتی که به خانه رسیدم، همه خوابیده بودند.

vaghti ke be khāne residam, hame khābide budand.

When I got home, everyone had gone to sleep.

khābide budand: the past perfect, 'had slept' – you will meet it properly in lesson 2

وقتی که بچه بودم، در شیراز زندگی می‌کردیم.

vaghti ke bacche budam, dar Shirāz zendegi mi-kardim.

When I was a child, we lived in Shiraz.

وقتی باران می‌آمد، در خانه بازی می‌کردیم.

vaghti bārān mi-āmad, dar khāne bāzi mi-kardim.

When it rained, we played indoors.

Tom remembers his first week in Tehran

Tom, an Australian student, writes about his first days in Iran. Watch for the two past tenses side by side.

سال گذشته برای درس خواندن به تهران آمدم.

sāl-e gozashte barā-ye dars khāndan be Tehrān āmadam.

Last year I came to Tehran to study.

هفته‌ی اول خیلی سخت بود.

hafte-ye avval kheyli sakht bud.

The first week was very hard.

هیچ‌کس را نمی‌شناختم و فارسی کم بلد بودم.

hich-kas rā nemi-shenākhtam o fārsi kam balad budam.

I didn't know anyone and I knew little Persian.

هر روز صبح با مترو به دانشگاه می‌رفتم.

har ruz sobh bā metro be dāneshgāh mi-raftam.

Every morning I went to the university by metro.

در مترو مردم با هم حرف می‌زدند و من هیچ چیز نمی‌فهمیدم.

dar metro mardom bā ham harf mi-zadand o man hich chiz nemi-fahmidam.

On the metro people talked to each other and I didn't understand anything.

یک روز داشتم نقشه را نگاه می‌کردم که پیرمردی آمد.

yek ruz dāshtam naghshe rā negāh mi-kardam ke pir-mard-i āmad.

One day I was looking at the map when an old man came up.

او آهسته فارسی حرف می‌زد و راه را به من نشان داد.

u āheste fārsi harf mi-zad o rāh rā be man neshān dād.

He spoke Persian slowly and showed me the way.

بعد از آن، هر روز با کسی حرف می‌زدم.

ba'd az ān, har ruz bā kas-i harf mi-zadam.

After that, I talked with someone every day.

حالا تهران مثل خانه‌ی خودم است.

hālā Tehrān mesl-e khāne-ye khod-am ast.

Now Tehran is like my own home.

Memories of 'the north'

Ask any Tehrani about childhood and you will hear about شمال shomāl the north: the green, rainy Caspian provinces of Gilan and Mazandaran, a few hours over the Alborz mountains. Families drive up on long weekends, and هر تابستان به شمال می‌رفتیم har tābestān be shomāl mi-raftim every summer we went to the north is a sentence half the country can say. The road through the Chalus valley, the rice fields, the smell of the sea and sweetcorn roasted on the beach – these are the stock images of Iranian nostalgia, and the past continuous is the tense they come in.

Watch out

  • می is always joined to the verb with a ZWNJ (می‌رفتم), never with a space – and the stress stays on mi.
  • بودن and داشتن almost never take می in the past: bachche budam (I was a child), mobāyl nadāshtim (we had no phone).
  • Persian has no separate 'used to' – just use the past continuous and a time word: ghablan (before), ān vaght-hā (in those days), dar bachchegi (in childhood).
  • In speech, mi-raftam stays mi-raftam, but mi-khāndam often becomes mi-khundam and mi-raft → mi-raft; the third person singular of می‌رود becomes mi-re only in the present, not the past.
