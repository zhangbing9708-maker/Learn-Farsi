الف، ب، پ، ت، ثAlef, be, pe, te, se – and the vowels

alef, be, pe, te, se

Your first five letters: the tall stroke alef and the four 'dish' letters that differ only in their dots. With them, and with the three vowel marks, you can already read and write real words – water, father, foot, fever.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • read and write ا ب پ ت ث in all their shapes
  • read the vowel marks a, e, o and know when alef stands for a vowel
  • sound out your first fifteen Persian words
Show

Alef – the tall one

ا alef is a single vertical stroke, the simplest letter of all, and it does two jobs.

Inside or at the end of a word it is the long vowel ā – the deep a of father: با bā with, پا pā foot.

At the start of a word it is a seat for a vowel. If the vowel is long ā, alef wears a little wavy hat, آ ā (called madde): آب āb water. If the vowel is short, the plain alef takes a vowel mark – or, in normal writing, no mark at all, and you know the vowel from the word: اَبا abā cloak.

Alef is a non-joiner: it connects to the letter before it but never to the one after. Its two shapes are the same stroke, with or without a little tail joining it to the previous letter: ا and ـا.

Be, pe, te, se – one dish, different dots

Four letters share one shape, a shallow dish, and are told apart only by their dots:

  • ب be – one dot below – b
  • پ pe – three dots below – p (a Persian addition)
  • ت te – two dots above – t
  • ث se – three dots above – s (only in words from Arabic)

All four are joiners. At the start or in the middle of a word the dish shrinks to a short tooth with its dots: بـ ـبـ, پـ ـپـ, تـ ـتـ, ثـ ـثـ. Only when the letter stands alone or ends a word do you see the full dish: ب, ـب.

So بابا bābā dad is: a tooth with a dot below (b), alef (ā), another tooth with a dot (b), alef (ā). Read it from the right: b-ā-b-ā. Notice that each alef breaks the chain, so the second be starts fresh.

The three vowel marks

The short vowels are written, when they are written at all, as small marks:

  • بَ ba – a short stroke above the letter, called zebar or fathe: the vowel a as in cat.
  • بِ be – a short stroke below, called zir or kasre: the vowel e as in bed.
  • بُ bo – a small hook above, called pish or zamme: the vowel o as in soft.

The mark sits on the consonant that comes before the vowel. تَب tab fever is te with a zebar, then be: t-a-b. بُت bot idol is be with a pish, then te: b-o-t.

In this course every new word is shown with its marks the first time, and the audio reads them. Out in the world, تب tab and بت bot are written bare, and you recognise them as you recognise tab and bot in English – by knowing the word.

Reading tips

  • Count the dots first, then read. One below = b, three below = p, two above = t, three above = s.
  • A tooth in the middle of a word is one of these letters (or a few others you will meet later). Its dots tell you which.
  • آ at the start is always ā; ا at the start is a short vowel – a, e or o – that you learn with the word.
  • ا inside a word is always ā, never a short vowel.
  • Write the dish letters as one movement from right to left, then add the dots. Iranians dot after finishing the word.
