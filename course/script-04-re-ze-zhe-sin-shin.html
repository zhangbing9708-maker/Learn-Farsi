ر، ز، ژ، س، شRe, ze, zhe, sin, shin

re, ze, zhe, sin, shin

Five letters that open up a lot of everyday words: re, ze and zhe, three little slides that never join the letter after them, and sin and shin, the letters with three teeth. By the end of this lesson you can read hand, night, brother and bazaar – and you will have met zhe, one of the rarest letters in the alphabet.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • read and write ر ز ژ and know that they never join the letter after them
  • read and write س ش in all their shapes
  • tell re from dāl at a glance
  • read words such as dast, shab, barādar and bāzār
Show

Re, ze, zhe – the slides

ر re is a small curved stroke that starts on the line and slides down below it. ز ze is the same stroke with one dot above, and ژ zhe has three dots above.

All three are non-joiners, like alef, dāl and zāl: they join the letter before them but never the letter after, so they have only two shapes – alone (ر) and joined to the previous letter (ـر). Don't confuse re with dāl: dāl is an angle that sits on the line, re is a curve that dips below it.

With this many non-joiners in play, words often fall into several pieces. درد dard pain is three separate letters, d-r-d, none of them joined. برادر barādar brother is four pieces: be joined to re, then alef, dāl and re, each standing alone. A gap inside a word is not a space – look for where the word really ends.

Three sounds: r, z and zh

ر re is a tapped r, as in Spanish or Italian: the tip of the tongue flicks once against the ridge behind your top teeth. It is never silent, so سرد sard cold has a clear r in the middle, and so does the end of بازار bāzār bazaar.

ز ze is plain z as in zoo. You met ذ zāl in lesson 3, which sounds the same, but ze is the everyday spelling of z: زرد zard yellow, سبز sabz green.

ژ zhe is zh, the s in pleasure. It is one of the four letters Persian added to the Arabic alphabet, and one of the rarest. You find it in a few old Persian words, like دژ dezh fortress and ژاله zhāle dew, and in words from French, like ژاکت zhāket jacket and ژست zhest pose.

Sin and shin – three teeth

س sin is three small teeth on the line. Alone or at the end of a word the teeth are followed by a wide bowl that dips below the line: س, ـس. At the start or in the middle only the teeth remain: سـ, ـسـ. ش shin is the same letter with three dots above: ش, شـ, ـشـ, ـش. Both join on both sides.

Sin is the everyday s. You met ث se in lesson 2 and will meet one more s in the next lesson, but most words use sin: سر sar head, دست dast hand. Shin is sh as in shop: شب shab night, آتش ātash fire.

In the middle of a word, count the teeth. Three in a row are sin or shin; a single tooth is one of the dish letters from lesson 2. In دست dast you can see dāl standing alone, then the three teeth of sin running straight into te.

Reading tips

  • Re, ze and zhe dip below the line; dāl and zāl sit on it. That is the quickest way to tell ر re from د dāl.
  • Dots again: ر has none, ز one, ژ three.
  • Three teeth in a row mean sin (س) or shin (ش). In handwriting the teeth are often smoothed into a flat line, and shin's three dots become a little caret: ^.
  • Tap every r. Persian never drops it the way British English does in car or park.
  • You now have two ways to write z (ذ ز) and two to write s (ث س). When you are unsure, ز and س are the everyday spellings.
