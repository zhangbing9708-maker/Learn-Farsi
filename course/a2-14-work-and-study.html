کار و تحصیلWork and study: a job interview and university life

kār o tahsil

Say what you studied and where you have worked, read the headings of a Persian CV, get through a job interview and talk about university life. On the way you learn two of the most useful word-building tricks in Persian – the endings -esh and -ande, which turn verbs into nouns like دانش (knowledge) and نویسنده (writer) – and the polite, formal Persian you use with a manager, a professor or anyone you do not know well.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • say what you studied and where you have worked
  • read the headings of a Persian CV and answer interview questions
  • make nouns from verbs with -esh (آموزش) and -ande (نویسنده)
  • speak formally: شما with a plural verb, آقای / خانم + family name, بفرمایید, ایشان
  • talk about university life: رشته، ترم، امتحان، کنکور
Show

The interview

Mina has finished her degree and has a job interview at a publishing house in Tehran. Mr Karimi, the manager, asks the questions. Listen for شما and the plural verbs – and notice that even in a formal interview people use the spoken forms.

Mr Karimi

سلام، خوش آمدید. بفرمایید بنشینید.

salām, khosh āmadid. befarmāyid beneshinid.

Hello, welcome. Please have a seat.

Spoken سلام، خوش اومدین. بفرمایین بشینین. salām, khosh umadin. befarmāyin beshinin.

Mina

سلام آقای کریمی. خیلی ممنون که وقت گذاشتید.

salām āghā-ye Karimi. kheyli mamnun ke vaght gozāshtid.

Hello, Mr Karimi. Thank you very much for your time.

Spoken سلام آقای کریمی. خیلی ممنون که وقت گذاشتین. salām āghā-ye Karimi. kheyli mamnun ke vaght gozāshtin.

Mr Karimi

خواهش می‌کنم. رزومه‌ی شما را خواندم. شما زبان انگلیسی خوانده‌اید، درست است؟

khāhesh mi-konam. rezume-ye shomā rā khāndam. shomā zabān-e engelisi khānde-id, dorost ast?

Not at all. I've read your CV. You studied English, is that right?

Spoken خواهش می‌کنم. رزومه‌تون رو خوندم. شما زبان انگلیسی خوندین، درسته؟ khāhesh mi-konam. rezume-tun ro khundam. shomā zabān-e engelisi khundin, doroste?

Mina

بله. لیسانسم را از دانشگاه تهران گرفتم. بعد دو سال در یک شرکت کار کردم.

bale. lisāns-am rā az dāneshgāh-e Tehrān gereftam. ba'd do sāl dar yek sherkat kār kardam.

Yes. I got my bachelor's degree from the University of Tehran. Then I worked for two years at a company.

Spoken بله. لیسانسمو از دانشگاه تهران گرفتم. بعد دو سال تو یه شرکت کار کردم. bale. lisāns-am-o az dāneshgāh-e Tehrān gereftam. ba'd do sāl tu ye sherkat kār kardam.

Mr Karimi

در آن شرکت چه کار می‌کردید؟

dar ān sherkat che kār mi-kardid?

What did you do at that company?

Spoken تو اون شرکت چی کار می‌کردین؟ tu un sherkat chi kār mi-kardin?

Mina

مترجم بودم. نامه‌ها و قراردادهای شرکت را ترجمه می‌کردم.

motarjem budam. nāme-hā o gharārdād-hā-ye sherkat rā tarjome mi-kardam.

I was a translator. I translated the company's letters and contracts.

Spoken مترجم بودم. نامه‌ها و قراردادای شرکتو ترجمه می‌کردم. motarjem budam. nāme-hā o gharārdād-ā-ye sherkat-o tarjome mi-kardam.

Mr Karimi

چرا می‌خواهید در انتشارات ما کار کنید؟

cherā mi-khāhid dar enteshārāt-e mā kār konid?

Why do you want to work at our publishing house?

Spoken چرا می‌خواین تو انتشارات ما کار کنین؟ cherā mi-khāyn tu enteshārāt-e mā kār konin?

Mina

چون عاشق کتاب هستم. ترجمه‌ی کتاب همیشه آرزوی من بوده است.

chon āshegh-e ketāb hastam. tarjome-ye ketāb hamishe ārezu-ye man bude ast.

Because I love books. Translating books has always been my dream.

Spoken چون عاشق کتابم. ترجمه‌ی کتاب همیشه آرزوم بوده. chon āshegh-e ketāb-am. tarjome-ye ketāb hamishe ārezu-m bude.

Mr Karimi

بسیار خوب. نقطه‌ی قوت و نقطه‌ی ضعف شما چیست؟

besyār khub. noghte-ye ghovvat o noghte-ye za'f-e shomā chist?

Very good. What are your strengths and weaknesses?

Literally: your point of strength and point of weakness

Spoken خیلی خب. نقطه‌ی قوت و ضعفتون چیه؟ kheyli khob. noghte-ye ghovvat o za'f-etun chiye?

Mina

نقطه‌ی قوتم دقت است. نقطه‌ی ضعفم؟ شاید این‌که گاهی خیلی زیاد کار می‌کنم!

noghte-ye ghovvat-am deghghat ast. noghte-ye za'f-am? shāyad in-ke gāhi kheyli ziyād kār mi-konam!

My strength is precision. My weakness? Maybe that I sometimes work far too much!

Spoken نقطه‌ی قوتم دقته. نقطه‌ی ضعفم؟ شاید این‌که گاهی خیلی زیاد کار می‌کنم! noghte-ye ghovvat-am deghghat-e. noghte-ye za'f-am? shāyad in-ke gāhi kheyli ziyād kār mi-konam!

Mr Karimi

این که ضعف نیست! کی می‌توانید شروع کنید؟

in ke za'f nist! key mi-tavānid shoru' konid?

That's not a weakness! When can you start?

Spoken این که ضعف نیست! کی می‌تونین شروع کنین؟ in ke za'f nist! key mi-tunin shoru' konin?

Mina

از اول ماه بعد. البته اگر شما بخواهید.

az avval-e māh-e ba'd. albatte agar shomā bekhāhid.

From the beginning of next month. If that suits you, of course.

Spoken از اول ماه بعد. البته اگه شما بخواین. az avval-e māh-e ba'd. albatte age shomā bekhāyn.

Mr Karimi

خیلی خوب. تا آخر هفته با شما تماس می‌گیریم. موفق باشید!

kheyli khub. tā ākhar-e hafte bā shomā tamās mi-girim. movaffagh bāshid!

Very good. We'll contact you by the end of the week. Good luck!

Spoken خیلی خب. تا آخر هفته باهاتون تماس می‌گیریم. موفق باشین! kheyli khob. tā ākhar-e hafte bāhā-tun tamās mi-girim. movaffagh bāshin!

Mina

خیلی ممنونم. خدانگهدار.

kheyli mamnun-am. khodā-negahdār.

Thank you very much. Goodbye.

خدانگهدار, 'God keep you', is a more formal goodbye than خداحافظ

Formal – but still spoken

A job interview is one of the most formal conversations you will have, and Persian marks it clearly:

  • Everyone says شما shomā you, never تو to, and the verb is plural even for one person: می‌توانید mi-tavānid you can, بخواهید bekhāhid you want.
  • People are addressed by title and family name: آقای کریمی āghā-ye Karimi Mr Karimi, خانم احمدی khānom-e Ahmadi Ms Ahmadi.
  • Every offer comes with بفرمایید befarmāyid, and every thank-you is answered with خواهش می‌کنم khāhesh mi-konam not at all.

But formal is not the same as written. Look at the spoken lines: Mr Karimi says بفرمایین بشینین befarmāyin beshinin and می‌تونین mi-tunin, with everyday pronunciation. Politeness lives in the words you choose – شما, بفرمایید, the plural verb – not in a bookish accent. Lesson 16 sets out all the spoken changes.

The rest of this lesson is about where people learn their trade: the university, its exams and its words – many of them built from verbs you already know with two endings, -esh and -ande.

-esh: nouns of action from the present stem

Take the present stem of a verb – the part that follows می mi – and add ـش -esh: you get a noun for the action or its result. You have been using one since your first week: خواهش می‌کنم khāhesh mi-konam is literally I make a request, and خواهش khāhesh request is خواه khāh want + -esh.

After a stem that ends in a vowel, a ی y slips in: نما namā show → نمایش namāyesh a show, a play, گو gu say → گویش guyesh dialect.

These words are the backbone of the Persian of schools and offices. The Ministry of Education is called آموزش و پرورش āmuzesh o parvaresh – teaching and upbringing – and the front desk of a university, a hospital or a hotel is the پذیرش paziresh admission, 'acceptance'.

Spoken پذیرش دانشگاه از ساعت هشت بازه. paziresh-e dāneshgāh az sā'at-e hasht bāz-e.

Spoken دیشب یه نمایش خیلی خوب دیدیم. dishab ye namāyesh-e kheyli khub didim.

-ande: the person who does it

Add ـنده -ande to the same present stem and you get the doer: نویس nevis write → نویسنده nevisande writer, ران rān drive → راننده rānande driver. Many job words are made this way. After a vowel a ی y appears again: آ ā come → آینده āyande the future – 'the one that is coming'.

With a compound verb, -ande goes on کن kon, the stem of کردن: شرکت کردن sherkat kardan to take part → شرکت‌کننده sherkat-konande participant, خسته کردن khaste kardan to tire → خسته‌کننده khaste-konande tiring, boring – the word for a long lecture.

In speech the plural is ـها -hā; in writing it is often ـگان -gān, and the silent ه turns into گ: نویسنده nevisande → نویسندگان nevisandegān writers.

Formal address: شما, titles and polite verbs

With a manager, a professor, an official or any adult you do not know, use شما shomā and a plural verb, even for one person – the polite -id imperative of lesson 9 is part of the same system. With friends, family and children, تو to and the singular are normal; شما to a close friend sounds cold, or like a joke.

Formally, use title + family name: آقای کریمی āghā-ye Karimi, خانم احمدی khānom-e Ahmadi, or a professional title such as دکتر doktor (lesson 7 of A1). A warmer form that is still respectful puts the title next to the first name: آقا رضا āghā Rezā, مینا خانم Minā khānom – note that خانم comes after the name.

To talk about a respected person, use ایشان ishān and a plural verb: ایشان آمدند ishān āmadand he / she has come. And polite Persian has a few special verbs. You don't need to use them yet, but you will hear them every day:

A Persian CV

A رزومه rezume in Iran has the same parts as anywhere else. These are the headings you will see:

  • مشخصات فردی moshakhkhasāt-e fardi personal details: نام nām first name, نام خانوادگی nām-e khānevādegi family name, تاریخ تولد tārikh-e tavallod date of birth, تلفن telefon phone, ایمیل imeyl email
  • سوابق تحصیلی savābegh-e tahsili education: the degree, the رشته reshte subject, the دانشگاه dāneshgāh university and the year
  • سوابق کاری savābegh-e kāri work experience: the job, the شرکت sherkat company and the years
  • مهارت‌ها mahārat-hā skills and زبان‌ها zabān-hā languages

Years are in the Iranian calendar, so Mina's first line reads کارشناسی زبان انگلیسی، دانشگاه تهران، ۱۴۰۱ kārshenāsi-ye zabān-e engelisi, dāneshgāh-e Tehrān, hezār-o-chahārsad-o-yek BA in English, University of Tehran, 1401 (2022). A CV uses the official کارشناسی kārshenāsi for a bachelor's degree; in conversation people say لیسانس lisāns.

Tom at the University of Tehran

Tom describes a semester at the University of Tehran. Look for the -esh and -ande words and the university vocabulary.

تام دانشجوی زبان و ادبیات فارسی در دانشگاه تهران است.

Tām dāneshju-ye zabān o adabiyyāt-e fārsi dar dāneshgāh-e Tehrān ast.

Tom is a student of Persian language and literature at the University of Tehran.

دانشگاه تهران یکی از قدیمی‌ترین و بزرگ‌ترین دانشگاه‌های ایران است.

dāneshgāh-e Tehrān yek-i az ghadimi-tarin o bozorg-tarin dāneshgāh-hā-ye Irān ast.

The University of Tehran is one of the oldest and largest universities in Iran.

دانشجوهای ایرانی برای ورود به دانشگاه باید در کنکور قبول شوند.

dāneshju-hā-ye irāni barā-ye vorud be dāneshgāh bāyad dar konkur ghabul shavand.

Iranian students have to pass the konkur to get into university.

تام کنکور نداده است، اما یک امتحان زبان داده است.

Tām konkur na-dāde ast, ammā yek emtehān-e zabān dāde ast.

Tom hasn't taken the konkur, but he has taken a language exam.

او هر ترم شش درس دارد و بیشتر کلاس‌ها صبح هستند.

u har term shesh dars dārad o bishtar-e kelās-hā sobh hastand.

He has six courses each semester, and most of the classes are in the morning.

استادها به فارسی درس می‌دهند و تام همه چیز را در دفترش می‌نویسد.

ostād-hā be fārsi dars mi-dahand o Tām hame chiz rā dar daftar-ash mi-nevisad.

The professors teach in Persian, and Tom writes everything down in his notebook.

سخت‌ترین قسمت برای او فهمیدن جزوه‌های دست‌نویس هم‌کلاسی‌هایش است.

sakht-tarin ghesmat barā-ye u fahmidan-e jozve-hā-ye dast-nevis-e ham-kelāsi-hā-yash ast.

The hardest part for him is understanding his classmates' handwritten notes.

جزوه: lecture notes, which students copy and pass round before exams

ظهر ناهار را در سلف دانشگاه می‌خورد، چون ارزان و خوشمزه است.

zohr nāhār rā dar self-e dāneshgāh mi-khorad, chon arzān o khoshmaze ast.

At noon he has lunch in the university canteen, because it's cheap and tasty.

سلف, from English 'self-service', is what every Iranian student calls the canteen

بعد از کلاس، با دوستانش به کتابخانه‌ی مرکزی می‌رود.

ba'd az kelās, bā dustān-ash be ketāb-khāne-ye markazi mi-ravad.

After class he goes to the central library with his friends.

آخر ترم، کتابخانه پر از دانشجوهای خسته است.

ākhar-e term, ketāb-khāne por az dāneshju-hā-ye khaste ast.

At the end of the semester the library is full of tired students.

تام می‌گوید: «در آینده می‌خواهم مترجم یا نویسنده شوم.»

Tām mi-guyad: «dar āyande mi-khāham motarjem yā nevisande shavam.»

Tom says: 'In the future I want to become a translator or a writer.'

The street of books

Opposite the main gate of the University of Tehran, خیابان انقلاب khiyābān-e Enghelāb Enghelab Street is lined for well over a kilometre with bookshops and publishers' showrooms. This is where students buy their textbooks, dictionaries and photocopied جزوه jozve lecture notes, where publishers like the one Mina hopes to work for have their windows, and where you can find a Persian translation of almost any famous novel – translation is a large and respected profession in Iran.

Every spring the city also holds the نمایشگاه بین‌المللی کتاب تهران namāyeshgāh-e beyn-ol-melali-ye ketāb-e Tehrān Tehran International Book Fair, one of the largest in Asia. For about ten days whole families come to browse, and students carry home bags of discounted books. Look at the word for exhibition: نمایشگاه namāyeshgāh is نمایش namāyesh show + گاه gāh place – the -esh of this lesson at work, inside a word you will see on posters all over Iran.

Tips

  • Learn every verb with its present stem. One stem gives you the verb (می‌نویسم), the command (بنویس), the doer (نویسنده) and often an -esh noun – four words for the price of one.
  • دانشجو is a university student, دانش‌آموز a school pupil; استاد teaches at a university, معلم at a school.
  • Some -esh and -ande words come from verbs that are now rare or literary: ارزش (value, from ارزیدن 'to be worth'), آرامش (calm, from آرامیدن 'to rest'), پرنده (bird, from پریدن). Use the pattern to understand new words rather than to invent them.
  • At an interview, answer a compliment modestly – نظر لطف شماست ('that's kind of you', literally 'it is your kind view') – and leave with ممنون از وقتی که گذاشتید ('thank you for your time').
  • Both endings are stressed: nevisande, verzesh – like almost every Persian noun, on the last syllable.
