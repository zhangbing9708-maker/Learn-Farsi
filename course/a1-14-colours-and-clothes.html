رنگ‌ها و لباس‌هاColours and clothes: describing things and people

rang-hā o lebās-hā

A white shirt, black shoes, a turquoise scarf: in this lesson you learn the colours and the clothes, and the most important pattern for describing anything in Persian – the adjective comes after the noun, joined by the ezafe -e: pirāhan-e sefid. You also learn that adjectives never change, how to say 'very' and 'this one, that one', and how to shop for clothes in your size.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • name the colours and the main clothes
  • describe things with noun + -e + adjective: kafsh-e meshki
  • tell 'the white shirt' from 'the shirt is white'
  • use kheyli 'very' and in / ān 'this / that' with nouns
  • ask for another colour or size in a shop
Show

A manteau on Valiasr Street

Mahsa and her friend Leila are in a clothes shop on Valiasr Street in Tehran. Mahsa needs a new manteau – the light coat many Iranian women wear over their clothes.

Seller

سلام، خوش آمدید. چه رنگی دوست دارید؟

salām, khosh āmadid. che rang-i dust dārid?

Hello, welcome. What colour would you like?

Mahsa

سلام. یک مانتوی ساده می‌خواهم، مشکی یا سرمه‌ای.

salām. yek mānto-ye sāde mi-khāham, meshki yā sorme-i.

Hello. I want a simple manteau, black or navy blue.

Seller

این مانتوی مشکی خیلی قشنگ است. سایز شما چند است؟

in mānto-ye meshki kheyli ghashang ast. sāyz-e shomā chand ast?

This black manteau is very nice. What's your size?

Spoken این مانتوی مشکی خیلی قشنگه. سایزتون چنده؟ in mānto-ye meshki kheyli ghashange. sāyz-etun chande?

Mahsa

سی و هشت.

si o hasht.

Thirty-eight.

Leila

مهسا، این مانتو برای تو کمی بلند است.

Mahsā, in mānto barā-ye to kami boland ast.

Mahsa, this manteau is a bit long for you.

Mahsa

راست می‌گویی. کوتاه‌تر هم دارید؟

rāst mi-guyi. kutāh-tar ham dārid?

You're right. Do you have a shorter one too?

Spoken راست می‌گی. کوتاه‌تر هم دارین؟ rāst mi-gi. kutāh-tar ham dārin?

Seller

بله، این یکی کوتاه‌تر است، اما سرمه‌ای است.

bale, in yeki kutāh-tar ast, ammā sorme-i ast.

Yes, this one is shorter, but it's navy blue.

Mahsa

خوب است! سرمه‌ای هم دوست دارم.

khub ast! sorme-i ham dust dāram.

That's fine! I like navy too.

Spoken خوبه! سرمه‌ای هم دوست دارم. khube! sorme-i ham dust dāram.

Leila

این روسری فیروزه‌ای با مانتوی سرمه‌ای خیلی قشنگ است.

in rusari-ye firuze-i bā mānto-ye sorme-i kheyli ghashang ast.

This turquoise scarf looks really pretty with the navy manteau.

Mahsa

بله، قشنگ است. روسری چند است؟

bale, ghashang ast. rusari chand ast?

Yes, it's pretty. How much is the scarf?

Seller

روسری چهارصد هزار تومان، مانتو دو میلیون.

rusari chahārsad hezār tumān, mānto do milyun.

The scarf is four hundred thousand tomans, the manteau two million.

Mahsa

هر دو را می‌خرم. ممنون!

har do rā mi-kharam. mamnun!

I'll take both. Thank you!

Seller

مبارک باشد!

mobārak bāshad!

Enjoy them!

Spoken مبارک باشه! mobārak bāshe!

What colour?

The question for colour is چه رنگی؟ che rang-i? what colour? – literally what a colour? You can ask it about anything: ماشینت چه رنگی است؟ māshin-at che rang-i ast? What colour is your car?

Notice how the seller says the black manteau: مانتوی مشکی mānto-ye meshki – the noun first, then the colour, joined by the little ezafe you have used since lesson 3. Every adjective in Persian works this way, and it is the main point of this lesson.

Persian has two words for black. سیاه siyāh is the basic colour word (a black cat, black hair), but for clothes, shoes and cars Iranians usually say مشکی meshki, literally musk-coloured. Leila's turquoise, فیروزه‌ای firuze-i, is فیروزه firuze turquoise, the stone + -i – many Persian colours are made this way, as you will see in the culture note.

The white shirt: noun + -e + adjective

In Persian the adjective comes after the noun, and the two are joined by the ezafe -e: پیراهن سفید pirāhan-e sefid a white shirt, کفش مشکی kafsh-e meshki black shoes, کلاه قرمز kolāh-e ghermez a red hat. After a consonant the -e is not written – you just have to say it.

After a vowel the ezafe becomes -ye, and it shows in the spelling: after ā, o or u it is written ی -ye – مانتوی سبز mānto-ye sabz a green manteau; after a silent final -e it is written ‌ی -ye with a ZWNJ – خانه‌ی بزرگ khāne-ye bozorg a big house; after i nothing is added – روسری زرد rusari-ye zard a yellow scarf.

You can line up several adjectives, each with its own -e: پیراهن سفید قشنگ pirāhan-e sefid-e ghashang a pretty white shirt.

'The white shirt' or 'the shirt is white'? Adjectives never change

Compare: پیراهن سفید pirāhan-e sefid the white shirt is a phrase, with the ezafe; پیراهن سفید است. pirāhan sefid ast. The shirt is white. is a sentence, with no ezafe and a verb. The little -e is the whole difference – so say it clearly.

Persian adjectives never change. There is no masculine or feminine and no plural adjective: the plural ending goes on the noun, before the ezafe – کفش‌های مشکی kafsh-hā-ye meshki black shoes (shoes-of black), لباس‌های قشنگ lebās-hā-ye ghashang beautiful clothes. With plural things, the verb usually stays singular: کفش‌ها مشکی است. kafsh-hā meshki ast. The shoes are black.

Very is خیلی kheyli, before the adjective: خیلی قشنگ kheyli ghashang very pretty, خیلی گران kheyli gerān very expensive. A bit is کمی kami: کمی بلند kami boland a bit long.

This one, that one: in and ān with nouns

این in this and آن ān that go before the noun, with no ezafe: این پیراهن in pirāhan this shirt, آن کفش ān kafsh that shoe. Like adjectives, they never take a plural, even when the noun does: این کفش‌ها in kafsh-hā these shoes, آن لباس‌ها ān lebās-hā those clothes.

When the noun is obvious, use این یکی in yeki this one and آن یکی ān yeki that one – the seller's in yeki kutāh-tar ast. And to compare sizes, use -tar from lesson 13: بزرگ‌تر دارید؟ bozorg-tar dārid? do you have a bigger one?

Put it all together and you can describe anything: این مانتوی سرمه‌ای خیلی قشنگ است. in mānto-ye sorme-i kheyli ghashang ast. This navy manteau is very pretty.

Colours that come from things

Persian has a handful of basic colour words – سفید sefid white, سیاه siyāh black, سبز sabz green, زرد zard yellow, سرخ sorkh red – and then a whole paintbox made from things, with the ending -i, of or like:

  • آبی ābi blue – from آب āb water
  • نارنجی nārenji orange – from نارنج nārenj bitter orange, the tree of Shiraz's courtyards
  • صورتی surati pink – from صورت surat face: the colour of a cheek
  • قهوه‌ای ghahve-i brown – from coffee; خاکستری khākestari grey – from ash
  • سرمه‌ای sorme-i navy blue – from sorme, kohl, the dark eye make-up
  • فیروزه‌ای firuze-i turquoise – from the stone mined near Nishapur for thousands of years
  • لاجوردی lājevardi lapis blue – from lapis lazuli; the word travelled through Arabic and Latin into English as azure

You can see both blues on the great mosques of Iran, where turquoise and lapis tiles were the glory of Persian architecture. Turquoise is a lucky colour too: a turquoise ring or a blue bead is said to keep away the evil eye.

Clothes in Iran are as varied as the country. Since 1979 women have been expected to wear a مانتو mānto and a روسری rusari, or a چادر chādor, in public; how strictly this is followed has changed a lot in recent years, and in Tehran you will see every style. In the villages and among the nomads, such as the Qashqai and the Bakhtiari, women's dresses are layered in brilliant reds, greens and golds.

Tips

  • Say the ezafe! pirāhan-e sefid 'the white shirt' and pirāhan sefid ast 'the shirt is white' differ only by that little -e.
  • kolāh 'hat' ends in a real h, so its ezafe is a plain -e: kolāh-e ghermez. Words ending in a silent -e, like khāne, take -ye: khāne-ye sefid.
  • meshki is the everyday black for things you wear or drive; siyāh is the general word – and the colour of mourning, worn in the month of Muharram.
  • Clothes sizes: women's clothes usually use European numbers (si o hasht, 38); men's shirts and T-shirts often use small, medium, large, said the English way.
  • In speech, point and name the colour with -esh: sabz-esh! 'the green one!' – every shopkeeper understands.
