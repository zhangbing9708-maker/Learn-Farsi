خوب نوشتنWriting well: paragraphs, connectors and punctuation

khub neveshtan

At B1 you can write correct sentences; now it is time to write good paragraphs. In this lesson you learn how a Persian paragraph is built, the connectors that hold an argument together (بنابراین، با این حال، علاوه بر این، از یک سو … از سوی دیگر…), and how Persian punctuation works – including where the comma goes and where it doesn't. You study a model three-paragraph essay on learning languages, then the same ideas written badly, sentence by sentence, and corrected.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • build a paragraph with a topic sentence, support and a conclusion
  • link ideas with بنابراین، با این حال، علاوه بر این، از یک سو … از سوی دیگر، در نتیجه، به عبارت دیگر and در حالی که
  • use Persian punctuation – ، ؛ « » ؟ – and put commas in the right places
  • spot and fix the commonest mistakes in a learner's essay

Model essay: why learn a foreign language?

A short essay in three paragraphs, of the kind Iranian students write at school. After the title, paragraph 1 (sentences 2–4) introduces the topic, paragraph 2 (5–10) gives the arguments, and paragraph 3 (11–14) concludes. The notes show what each sentence does.

چرا باید زبان خارجی یاد بگیریم؟

cherā bāyad zabān-e khāreji yād begirim?

Why should we learn a foreign language?

The title: a question that the essay will answer.

امروزه یادگیری زبان خارجی دیگر یک سرگرمی نیست، بلکه یک ضرورت است.

emruze yādgiri-ye zabān-e khāreji digar yek sargarmi nist, balke yek zarurat ast.

Today learning a foreign language is no longer a hobby but a necessity.

Paragraph 1. The topic sentence: it states the main idea of the whole essay. … نیست، بلکه … – 'not …, but rather …'.

در جهانی که مرزها روزبه‌روز کم‌رنگ‌تر می‌شوند، زبان پلی میان انسان‌ها و فرهنگ‌هاست.

dar jahān-i ke marz-hā ruz-be-ruz kam-rang-tar mi-shavand, zabān pol-i miyān-e ensān-hā va farhang-hā-st.

In a world where borders grow fainter every day, language is a bridge between people and cultures.

Background: why the topic matters. Notice the comma after the long opening clause.

در این نوشته، به سه دلیل مهم برای یادگیری زبان خارجی اشاره می‌کنم.

dar in neveshte, be se dalil-e mohem barā-ye yādgiri-ye zabān-e khāreji eshāre mi-konam.

In this essay I point to three important reasons for learning a foreign language.

The plan: it tells the reader what is coming.

نخست اینکه زبان خارجی درهای تازه‌ای به روی ما باز می‌کند.

nokhost inke zabān-e khāreji dar-hā-ye tāze-i be ru-ye mā bāz mi-konad.

First, a foreign language opens new doors for us.

Paragraph 2. The first argument, announced by نخست اینکه – the written equivalent of اول اینکه.

برای مثال، بسیاری از شرکت‌ها به کارمندانی نیاز دارند که با مشتریان خارجی ارتباط برقرار کنند.

barā-ye mesāl, besyār-i az sherkat-hā be kārmandān-i niyāz dārand ke bā moshtariyān-e khāreji ertebāt bar-gharār konand.

For example, many companies need employees who can communicate with foreign customers.

Support: an example for the first argument.

علاوه بر این، یادگیری زبان ذهن را فعال نگه می‌دارد.

alāve bar in, yādgiri-ye zabān zehn rā fa'āl negah mi-dārad.

Moreover, learning a language keeps the mind active.

The second argument, introduced by علاوه بر این – 'in addition, moreover'.

به گفته‌ی برخی پژوهشگران، یادگیری زبان دوم حافظه و تمرکز را تقویت می‌کند.

be gofte-ye barkhi pazhuheshgarān, yādgiri-ye zabān-e dovvom hāfeze va tamarkoz rā taghviyat mi-konad.

According to some researchers, learning a second language strengthens memory and concentration.

Evidence for the second argument, with its source.

مهم‌تر از همه، زبان ما را با فرهنگ دیگران آشنا می‌کند.

mohem-tar az hame, zabān mā rā bā farhang-e digarān āshnā mi-konad.

Most important of all, a language introduces us to other people's culture.

The third and strongest argument is kept for last.

کسی که شعر حافظ را به فارسی می‌خواند، چیزی را می‌فهمد که در هیچ ترجمه‌ای پیدا نمی‌شود.

kas-i ke she'r-e Hāfez rā be fārsi mi-khānad, chiz-i rā mi-fahmad ke dar hich tarjome-i peydā nemi-shavad.

Someone who reads Hafez in Persian understands something that cannot be found in any translation.

An example for the third argument. The comma after a subject that contains a whole clause helps the reader find the verb.

البته یادگیری زبان کار آسانی نیست و به صبر و تمرین نیاز دارد.

albatte yādgiri-ye zabān kār-e āsān-i nist va be sabr va tamrin niyāz dārad.

Of course, learning a language is not easy, and it takes patience and practice.

Paragraph 3. A concession: admit the other side before you conclude.

با این حال، فایده‌های آن بسیار بیشتر از سختی‌هایش است.

bā in hāl, fāyede-hā-ye ān besyār bish-tar az sakhti-hā-yash ast.

Nevertheless, its benefits far outweigh its difficulties.

با این حال turns back to the main idea after the concession.

بنابراین، هر کس که فرصت دارد، باید دست‌کم یک زبان خارجی یاد بگیرد.

banābar-in, har kas ke forsat dārad, bāyad dast-e-kam yek zabān-e khāreji yād begirad.

Therefore, everyone who has the chance should learn at least one foreign language.

The conclusion, introduced by بنابراین.

به عبارت دیگر، یادگیری زبان سرمایه‌گذاری برای آینده است.

be ebārat-e digar, yādgiri-ye zabān sarmāye-gozāri barā-ye āyande ast.

In other words, learning a language is an investment in the future.

A last sentence that restates the main idea in a new, memorable form.

What makes Persian prose good

Good Persian writing – نثر nasr prose – values the same things as good English writing: one idea per paragraph, clear links between sentences, no padding. But a few habits are particular to Persian:

  • The paragraph (بند band or پاراگراف pārāgrāf) opens with a topic sentence (جمله‌ی اصلی jomle-ye asli) that states its idea; the following sentences explain it or give examples and evidence; the last one rounds it off or leads on to the next.
  • Keep the verb in sight. Persian puts the verb last, so a long sentence keeps the reader waiting. If a sentence runs past two lines, split it.
  • Don't repeat. Persian style dislikes the same word – above all the same verb – twice in a row. Vary with a synonym, a different construction, or by joining two sentences with a connector.
  • Choose one register. An essay is written in book Persian from start to finish: no خوبه khub-e, no می‌خوام mi-khām (lesson 8).
  • Connectors are the joints of an argument. Learn a dozen and your writing will at once read as more mature.

Iranian editors also warn against two over-formal habits: using می‌باشد mi-bāshad where است ast will do, and the passive with توسط tavassot-e by (کتاب توسط او نوشته شد) where a simple active sentence is clearer: او کتاب را نوشت u ketāb rā nevesht he wrote the book.

Connectors: the joints of an argument

A connector tells the reader how the next sentence relates to the last one: more of the same, the opposite, the result, an example. In writing, a connector at the start of a sentence is followed by a comma. Speech has its own, simpler set – the third column – which does not belong in an essay.

Two of them need a note. از یک سو az yek su always waits for its partner از سوی دیگر az su-ye digar, like on the one hand … on the other. And در حالی که dar hāl-i ke means both while (two things at the same time) and whereas (a contrast); it joins two clauses inside one sentence instead of starting a new one.

Mina was studying while her brother was watching television.

در شمال ایران باران زیاد می‌بارد، در حالی که جنوب کشور خشک است.

dar shomāl-e Irān bārān ziyād mi-bārad, dar hāl-i ke jonub-e keshvar khoshk ast.

In the north of Iran it rains a lot, whereas the south of the country is dry.

Persian punctuation – and where the comma goes

Persian borrowed its punctuation from European languages about a century ago, and turned some marks round to suit right-to-left writing: the comma (،) and the semicolon (؛) are upside down, the question mark (؟) is mirrored, and quotation marks are the guillemets « ». The full stop, the colon and the exclamation mark are the same.

Where the comma goes:

  1. after an introductory phrase or a connector: بنابراین، banābar-in, به نظر من، be nazar-e man,
  2. after a long clause that comes first: وقتی به خانه رسیدم، باران شروع شد. vaghti be khāne residam, bārān shoru' shod.
  3. between the items of a list – but not before the last و va: سیب، پرتقال و انار sib, porteghāl va anār
  4. around an inserted phrase: حافظ، شاعر بزرگ شیراز، در قرن هشتم زندگی می‌کرد. Hāfez, shā'er-e bozorg-e Shirāz, dar gharn-e hashtom zendegi mi-kard.
  5. after the name of the person you are addressing: سارا، بیا این‌جا! Sārā, biyā in-jā!

Where it does not go: between a simple subject and its verb, and before که ke when it introduces a short clause (می‌دانم که می‌آید mi-dānam ke mi-āyad). After a subject that contains a whole clause, though – کسی که شعر حافظ را می‌خواند،… kas-i ke she'r-e Hāfez rā mi-khānad,… – a comma helps the reader find the verb.

The semicolon joins two complete sentences that belong together, and Persian uses it more than English does. Spacing: no space before a mark, one space after it – and inside words use the zero-width non-joiner, which Iranians call نیم‌فاصله nim-fāsele half-space: می‌روم mi-ravam, not میروم or می روم.

Building a paragraph

A good Persian paragraph has the same skeleton as a good English one. The topic sentence says what the paragraph is about; the supporting sentences explain it with reasons, examples or facts; the closing sentence sums up or leads on to the next paragraph. Each slot has its typical openings – learn two or three for each. The examples below build one complete paragraph, sentence by sentence.

One of the best ways of getting around Tehran is the metro.

نخست اینکه مترو از ترافیک سنگین خیابان‌ها سریع‌تر است.

nokhost inke metro az terāfik-e sangin-e khiyābān-hā sari'-tar ast.

First, the metro is faster than the heavy traffic in the streets.

The first supporting point.

علاوه بر این، بلیت آن ارزان است و آلودگی هوا را کم می‌کند.

alāve bar in, belit-e ān arzān ast va āludegi-ye havā rā kam mi-konad.

Moreover, its tickets are cheap and it reduces air pollution.

The second point.

برای مثال، سفر از تجریش تا میدان آزادی با ماشین گاهی دو ساعت طول می‌کشد، در حالی که با مترو کمتر از یک ساعت است.

barā-ye mesāl, safar az Tajrish tā meydān-e Āzādi bā māshin gāhi do sā'at tul mi-keshad, dar hāl-i ke bā metro kam-tar az yek sā'at ast.

For example, the trip from Tajrish to Azadi Square sometimes takes two hours by car, whereas by metro it is under an hour.

An example, with a contrast introduced by در حالی که.

البته مترو در ساعت‌های شلوغ بسیار پرجمعیت است.

albatte metro dar sā'at-hā-ye sholugh besyār por-jam'iyyat ast.

Of course, the metro is very crowded at rush hour.

A concession.

با این حال، برای بیشتر تهرانی‌ها مترو سریع‌ترین و ارزان‌ترین گزینه است.

bā in hāl, barā-ye bishtar-e Tehrāni-hā metro sari'-tarin va arzān-tarin gozine ast.

Nevertheless, for most Tehranis the metro is the fastest and cheapest option.

The closing sentence takes us back to the main idea.

The same ideas, written badly – and corrected

Tom wrote a first draft of the same essay. His Persian is correct word by word, but the writing is weak: spoken forms, endless sentences, no connectors, English punctuation, missing half-spaces and repetition. Each draft sentence (Draft) is followed by the version his teacher Ms Ahmadi suggested (Corrected); read only the corrected sentences and you have a good paragraph.

زبان خارجی خیلی خوبه و آدم باید یاد بگیره و من هم دارم فارسی یاد میگیرم و خیلی دوستش دارم.

zabān-e khāreji kheyli khub-e va ādam bāyad yād begire va man ham dāram fārsi yād migiram va kheyli dust-esh dāram.

Foreign languages are really good and you should learn them and I'm learning Persian too and I really like it.

Draft. Spoken forms (خوبه، بگیره) in a written text; four clauses chained with و; میگیرم has no half-space; and no clear main idea.

یادگیری زبان خارجی امروزه یک ضرورت است و من نیز به همین دلیل فارسی می‌آموزم.

yādgiri-ye zabān-e khāreji emruze yek zarurat ast va man niz be hamin dalil fārsi mi-āmuzam.

Learning a foreign language is a necessity today, and that is why I am learning Persian.

Corrected. One clear main idea, all in the written register (است، نیز، می‌آموزم), with the half-space.

شرکت‌ها کارمند زبان‌دان میخواهند.

sherkat-hā kārmand-e zabān-dān mikhāhand.

Companies want staff who know languages.

Draft. A reason with no signpost – the reader can't tell it is the first argument – and no example. میخواهند is missing its half-space.

نخست اینکه بسیاری از شرکت‌ها، برای مثال شرکت‌های گردشگری، به کارمندان زبان‌دان نیاز دارند.

nokhost inke besyār-i az sherkat-hā, barā-ye mesāl sherkat-hā-ye gardeshgari, be kārmandān-e zabān-dān niyāz dārand.

First, many companies – travel companies, for example – need staff who know languages.

Corrected. The argument is announced with نخست اینکه, and an example is set off by commas.

زبان برای مغز خوب است و زبان حافظه را خوب می‌کند و زبان تمرکز را هم خوب می‌کند.

zabān barā-ye maghz khub ast va zabān hāfeze rā khub mi-konad va zabān tamarkoz rā ham khub mi-konad.

Language is good for the brain and language makes memory good and language makes concentration good too.

Draft. Repetition: زبان three times, خوب three times. Persian style dislikes this even more than English does.

علاوه بر این، یادگیری زبان حافظه و تمرکز را تقویت می‌کند.

alāve bar in, yādgiri-ye zabān hāfeze va tamarkoz rā taghviyat mi-konad.

Moreover, learning a language strengthens memory and concentration.

Corrected. One clause instead of three, a precise verb (تقویت کردن) instead of خوب کردن, and a connector that links it to the first point.

بنابراین فرهنگ هم مهم است ، مثلاً حافظ.

banābar-in farhang ham mohem ast , masalan Hāfez.

Therefore culture is also important, for example Hafez.

Draft. The wrong connector – nothing here follows from the sentence before, so بنابراین ('therefore') makes no sense; a space before the comma; and مثلاً حافظ is not a sentence.

از همه مهم‌تر، زبان ما را با فرهنگ دیگران آشنا می‌کند؛ برای مثال، شعر حافظ را فقط به فارسی می‌توان به‌درستی فهمید.

az hame mohem-tar, zabān mā rā bā farhang-e digarān āshnā mi-konad; barā-ye mesāl, she'r-e Hāfez rā faghat be fārsi mi-tavān be-dorosti fahmid.

Most importantly, a language introduces us to other cultures; for example, Hafez can only be properly understood in Persian.

Corrected. A connector that fits (از همه مهم‌تر, 'most importantly'), a semicolon between the claim and the example, and a complete example sentence.

یاد گرفتن زبان سخت است ولی ارزش دارد چون فایده هایش زیاد است?

yād gereftan-e zabān sakht ast vali arzesh dārad chon fāyede hā-yash ziyād ast?

Learning a language is hard but it's worth it because its benefits are many?

Draft. A Latin question mark (?) – on a statement, which needs a full stop; فایده هایش needs a half-space, not a space; and the contrast is buried in the middle of a long sentence.

یادگیری زبان کار سختی است؛ با این حال، فایده‌هایش بسیار بیشتر از سختی‌هایش است.

yādgiri-ye zabān kār-e sakht-i ast; bā in hāl, fāyede-hā-yash besyār bish-tar az sakhti-hā-yash ast.

Learning a language is hard work; nevertheless, its benefits far outweigh its difficulties.

Corrected. The contrast gets its own clause after a semicolon and با این حال; فایده‌هایش has its half-space; the sentence ends with a full stop.

در آخر من فکر میکنم همه باید زبان یاد بگیرند چون "زبان پل است".

dar ākhar man fekr mikonam hame bāyad zabān yād begirand chon "zabān pol ast".

In the end I think everyone should learn languages because "language is a bridge".

Draft. English quotation marks instead of « »; میکنم without the half-space; and a conclusion that just repeats a phrase instead of summing up the argument.

بنابراین، هر کس که فرصت دارد، باید دست‌کم یک زبان خارجی بیاموزد؛ به عبارت دیگر، زبان سرمایه‌ای برای آینده است.

banābar-in, har kas ke forsat dārad, bāyad dast-e-kam yek zabān-e khāreji biyāmuzad; be ebārat-e digar, zabān sarmāye-i barā-ye āyande ast.

Therefore, everyone who has the chance should learn at least one foreign language; in other words, a language is an investment in the future.

Corrected. بنابراین now really draws a conclusion; commas after the connector and after the subject clause هر کس که فرصت دارد; and a last phrase that sums up the whole essay.

Enshā: the Iranian school essay

Every Iranian remembers انشا enshā, the composition class of primary and secondary school, and its famous topics. The most famous of all is علم بهتر است یا ثروت؟ elm behtar ast yā servat? Is knowledge better, or wealth? – a topic so overused that it has become a joke for anything predictable. Others are تابستان خود را چگونه گذراندید؟ tābestān-e khod rā chegune gozarāndid? How did you spend your summer? and descriptions of spring or of 'my mother'.

School essays follow the three-part plan of this lesson – مقدمه moghaddame introduction, بدنه badane body, نتیجه‌گیری natije-giri conclusion – and many teachers like a line of poetry or a proverb at the start or the end. That habit is worth copying, sparingly: a well-placed verse of Saadi is the Persian essay's equivalent of a good quotation.

For adults, the classic guide to good written Persian is غلط ننویسیم ghalat na-nevisim Let's not write incorrectly (1987) by the translator and editor Abolhassan Najafi, which campaigned against needless loan structures – among them می‌باشد mi-bāshad for است and the passive with توسط tavassot-e. The Academy of Persian Language and Literature, فرهنگستان زبان و ادب فارسی farhangestān-e zabān va adab-e fārsi, publishes the official spelling rules, including when to use the half-space.

Tips

  • Write the topic sentence first, then the conclusion, then fill in the middle. It keeps every paragraph on one idea.
  • Read your text aloud. If you run out of breath before the verb, the sentence is too long – split it.
  • Use the half-space (نیم‌فاصله): on most Persian keyboards it is Shift+Space, and on phones it usually sits next to the space bar. می‌روم، کتاب‌ها، خانه‌ی من.
  • Don't carry English punctuation over: no comma before و in a list, « » instead of " ", and the Persian ، ؛ ؟.
  • Vary your connectors, but don't overdo them: one every two or three sentences is plenty.
  • Collect good sentences. When you read a Persian text you like, copy two or three sentences by hand – the old way Iranian students learned to write.
