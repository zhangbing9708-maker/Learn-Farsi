فارسی در گذر زمانPersian through the ages

fārsi dar gozar-e zamān

Persian has a written history of 2,500 years. Darius the Great spoke Old Persian and had it carved in cuneiform on a cliff at Behistun; the Sasanian kings and the Zoroastrian priests wrote Middle Persian in the Pahlavi script; and from the ninth century New Persian, in Arabic letters, became the language of Rudaki, Ferdowsi – and you. In this lesson you follow 25 everyday words from Old to New Persian, see how the sounds and the grammar changed, and learn why an Iranian today can read a poem written eleven centuries ago.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • name the three stages of Persian – Old, Middle and New – with their scripts, dates and best-known texts
  • trace 25 everyday words from Old Persian through Middle Persian to today (pitar → pidar → pedar)
  • explain where the ezafe, the plural -ān, را and the prefix می come from
  • tell how the Arabic alphabet came to Persian, and read the first couplet of Rudaki's «بوی جوی مولیان»

A language three thousand years old

An overview in the style of an encyclopaedia article. Notice the passives (نوشته می‌شد، افزوده شد) and the impersonal «می‌نامند» 'they call' – the usual voice of history writing.

فارسی یکی از کهن‌ترین زبان‌های زنده‌ی جهان است.

fārsi yek-i az kohan-tarin zabān-hā-ye zende-ye jahān ast.

Persian is one of the oldest living languages in the world.

تاریخ این زبان را معمولاً به سه دوره تقسیم می‌کنند: فارسی باستان، فارسی میانه و فارسی نو.

tārikh-e in zabān rā ma'mulan be se dowre taghsim mi-konand: fārsi-ye bāstān, fārsi-ye miyāne va fārsi-ye now.

The history of the language is usually divided into three periods: Old Persian, Middle Persian and New Persian.

فارسی باستان زبان هخامنشیان بود و به خط میخی نوشته می‌شد.

fārsi-ye bāstān zabān-e Hakhāmaneshiyān bud va be khatt-e mikhi neveshte mi-shod.

Old Persian was the language of the Achaemenids and was written in cuneiform.

مهم‌ترین نوشته‌ی فارسی باستان، کتیبه‌ی داریوش بزرگ در بیستون است.

mohemm-tarin neveshte-ye fārsi-ye bāstān, katibe-ye Dāriyush-e bozorg dar Bisotun ast.

The most important Old Persian text is the inscription of Darius the Great at Behistun.

این کتیبه را حدود دو هزار و پانصد سال پیش بر دل کوهی در نزدیکی کرمانشاه کنده‌اند.

in katibe rā hodud-e do hezār o pānsad sāl pish bar del-e kuh-i dar nazdiki-ye Kermānshāh kande-and.

This inscription was carved into a mountainside near Kermanshah about two and a half thousand years ago.

داریوش در آن به سه زبان، یعنی فارسی باستان، ایلامی و بابلی، از پیروزی‌هایش سخن می‌گوید.

Dāriyush dar ān be se zabān, ya'ni fārsi-ye bāstān, ilāmi va bābeli, az piruzi-hā-yash sokhan mi-guyad.

In it Darius speaks of his victories in three languages: Old Persian, Elamite and Babylonian.

پس از هخامنشیان، زبان فارسی کم‌کم ساده‌تر شد و به فارسی میانه رسید.

pas az Hakhāmaneshiyān, zabān-e fārsi kam-kam sāde-tar shod va be fārsi-ye miyāne resid.

After the Achaemenids, the Persian language gradually became simpler and turned into Middle Persian.

فارسی میانه، که آن را پهلوی هم می‌نامند، زبان رسمی ساسانیان بود.

fārsi-ye miyāne, ke ān rā pahlavi ham mi-nāmand, zabān-e rasmi-ye Sāsāniyān bud.

Middle Persian, which is also called Pahlavi, was the official language of the Sasanians.

بسیاری از متن‌های دینی زرتشتیان به این زبان نوشته شده است.

besyār-i az matn-hā-ye dini-ye zartoshtiyān be in zabān neveshte shode ast.

Many of the religious texts of the Zoroastrians are written in this language.

پس از ورود اسلام به ایران، فارسی میانه جای خود را به فارسی نو داد.

pas az vorud-e eslām be Irān, fārsi-ye miyāne jā-ye khod rā be fārsi-ye now dād.

After Islam came to Iran, Middle Persian gave way to New Persian.

فارسی نو از قرن سوم هجری با الفبای عربی نوشته شد و چهار حرف «پ، چ، ژ، گ» به آن افزوده شد.

fārsi-ye now az gharn-e sevvom-e hejri bā alefbā-ye arabi neveshte shod va chahār harf-e «pe, che, zhe, gāf» be ān afzude shod.

From the third century of the Hijra (the ninth century AD) New Persian was written in the Arabic alphabet, with four letters added: پ، چ، ژ، گ.

رودکی، که او را پدر شعر فارسی می‌دانند، در همین روزگار در دربار سامانیان در بخارا شعر می‌گفت.

Rudaki, ke u rā pedar-e she'r-e fārsi mi-dānand, dar hamin ruzgār dar darbār-e Sāmāniyān dar Bokhārā she'r mi-goft.

Rudaki, who is considered the father of Persian poetry, was writing poems at that time at the Samanid court in Bukhara.

شگفت این است که ایرانیان امروز شعر رودکی را پس از هزار و صد سال به آسانی می‌فهمند.

shegeft in ast ke irāniyān-e emruz she'r-e Rudaki rā pas az hezār o sad sāl be āsāni mi-fahmand.

The remarkable thing is that Iranians today understand Rudaki's poetry easily, eleven hundred years later.

یک انگلیسی‌زبان برای خواندن انگلیسی هزار سال پیش به فرهنگ لغت نیاز دارد.

yek engelisi-zabān barā-ye khāndan-e engelisi-ye hezār sāl pish be farhang-e loghat niyāz dārad.

An English speaker needs a dictionary to read the English of a thousand years ago.

Three stages of Persian

Old Persian (about 550–330 BC) is the language of the Achaemenid kings. It survives almost only in royal inscriptions, written in a cuneiform script of some 36 sound signs that was probably created for the purpose under Darius. Its grammar looked like Latin or Sanskrit: three genders, several cases, endings everywhere. The great inscription at Behistun begins:

adam Dārayavauš xšāyaθiya vazraka xšāyaθiya xšāyaθiyānām xšāyaθiya Pārsaiy xšāyaθiya dahyūnām Vištāspahyā puça Aršāmahyā napā Haxāmanišiya

'I am Darius, the great king, king of kings, king in Persia, king of the lands, son of Hystaspes, grandson of Arsames, an Achaemenid.' (In these transliterations x is kh, š is sh, θ is th as in thin.) You can already hear today's words: xšāyaθiya became شاه shāh, vazraka بزرگ bozorg, puça 'son' پسر pesar, and Darius himself, Dārayavauš, is today's داریوش Dāriyush. A sentence later Darius says manā pitā Vištāspa 'my father is Hystaspes' – and manā pitā is, word for word, پدر من pedar-e man: the Persian من man 'I' comes from manā 'my'.

Middle Persian (about 300 BC to AD 900), also called Pahlavi, was the language of the Sasanian empire and of Zoroastrian books such as the Bundahishn, on the creation of the world, and the Dēnkard, an encyclopaedia of the faith. Its cases and genders were gone, and its grammar was already close to today's. It was written in a hard script derived from Aramaic, in which many words were spelt as Aramaic words but read in Persian: a scribe wrote the Aramaic MLKA 'king' and said šāh.

New Persian (from the ninth century) is the Persian you are learning. It grew out of the spoken Middle Persian of the east, took the Arabic alphabet and many Arabic words, and became a language of poetry at the Samanid court in Bukhara. Rudaki, who died in 941, is its first great poet – and his lines are still read in Iran without a dictionary.

Twenty-five words across 2,500 years

Here are 25 everyday words in their three shapes. Old and Middle Persian are given in the scholars' Latin transliteration (x = kh, š = sh, θ = th, ē and ō are long e and o); a star (*) marks a form that is not found in the Old Persian inscriptions but is known from Avestan, the sister language of the Zoroastrian scriptures, or reconstructed.

A handful of sound changes explain most of the table:

  1. Endings fall away: nāman- → nām, dasta- → dast.
  2. t becomes d between vowels: pitar- → pidar, brātar- → brādar.
  3. xš becomes š (sh): xšāyaθiya- → šāh, xšapa- → šab.
  4. θ becomes s: θard- → sāl, aθanga- → sang.
  5. au becomes ō, and ō becomes u in Iran: rauča- → rōz → روز ruz, kaufa- → kōf → کوه kuh.
  6. w becomes b: vāta- → wād → باد bād, vazraka- → wuzurg → بزرگ bozorg.
  7. Two consonants can no longer start a word, so a vowel slips in: brādar → برادر barādar, drō → دروغ dorugh.

پدر و مادرم در شیراز زندگی می‌کنند.

pedar o mādar-am dar Shirāz zendegi mi-konand.

My father and mother live in Shiraz.

Two words that have kept their shape for 2,500 years: Old Persian pitar-, mātar-.

برادرم اسب‌سواری را خیلی دوست دارد.

barādar-am asb-savāri rā kheyli dust dārad.

My brother loves horse riding.

In برادر barādar an a has slipped between b and r. اسب asb comes from the Median aspa-; the Old Persian word was asa-.

شب‌ها از کوه باد سردی می‌آید.

shab-hā az kuh bād-e sard-i mi-āyad.

At night a cold wind comes down from the mountain.

Three sound changes in one sentence: xšapa- → شب shab, kaufa- → کوه kuh, vāta- → باد bād.

دروغ در آیین ایرانیان باستان بزرگ‌ترین گناه بود.

dorugh dar āyin-e irāniyān-e bāstān bozorg-tarin gonāh bud.

In the religion of the ancient Iranians, the lie was the greatest sin.

Drauga, 'the Lie', is the great enemy of the Behistun inscription.

نام این روستا در هزار سال تغییر نکرده است.

nām-e in rustā dar hezār sāl taghyir na-karde ast.

The name of this village has not changed in a thousand years.

دبیر ما ادبیات فارسی درس می‌دهد.

dabir-e mā adabiyāt-e fārsi dars mi-dahad.

Our teacher teaches Persian literature.

Today a دبیر dabir is a secondary-school teacher or a secretary; in Sasanian times a dibīr was a scribe, from Old Persian dipi- 'inscription'.

داریوش و کوروش هنوز نام‌های رایجی در ایران هستند.

Dāriyush o Kurosh hanuz nām-hā-ye rāyej-i dar Irān hastand.

Dariush and Kurosh are still common names in Iran.

Darius is Old Persian Dārayavauš, Cyrus is Kuruš.

Grammar then and now

Between Darius and Rudaki, Persian lost almost all its endings. Old Persian marked gender and case on every noun; Middle Persian had no gender and hardly any cases; New Persian inherited that simple grammar and has kept it for a thousand years. Many little pieces of today's grammar are old words worn smooth:

  • The ezafe began as the Old Persian relative pronoun hya 'which, who': kāra hya manā 'the army which (is) mine' became Middle Persian ī, and today's سپاه من sepāh-e man.
  • The plural -ān is the Old Persian genitive plural -ānām 'of the …s': xšāyaθiya xšāyaθiyānām 'king of kings' lives on as شاه شاهان shāh-e shāhān and شاهنشاه shāhanshāh.
  • را was the Old Persian rādiy 'for the sake of', Middle Persian rāy – which is why old texts still use it for 'for, to': مرا marā 'for me'.
  • The prefix می comes from Middle Persian hamē 'always', which gave the poets' همی hami and then می mi.
  • The verb ending -ad goes back to Old Persian -atiy: baratiy 'he carries', Middle Persian barēd, today می‌برد mi-barad.

شاهان هخامنشی خود را «شاه شاهان» می‌نامیدند.

shāhān-e Hakhāmaneshi khod rā «shāh-e shāhān» mi-nāmidand.

The Achaemenid kings called themselves 'king of kings'.

Old Persian xšāyaθiya xšāyaθiyānām: the genitive plural -ānām survives as the plural ـان -ān.

سپاه من پیروز شد.

sepāh-e man piruz shod.

My army was victorious.

The unwritten -e of سپاه من sepāh-e man is all that is left of kāra hya manā.

دانشجویان کتاب‌ها را به کتابخانه بردند.

dāneshjuyān ketāb-hā rā be ketābkhāne bordand.

The students took the books to the library.

Three old pieces: -ān (Old Persian -ānām), -hā (Middle Persian -īhā) and را (Old Persian rādiy).

او هر روز نامه‌ها را به اداره می‌برد.

u har ruz nāme-hā rā be edāre mi-barad.

He takes the letters to the office every day.

می‌برد mi-barad: mi- from Middle Persian hamē 'always', -ad from Old Persian -atiy.

فارسی امروز جنس دستوری ندارد؛ «او» هم برای مرد است و هم برای زن.

fārsi-ye emruz jens-e dasturi nadārad; «u» ham barā-ye mard ast va ham barā-ye zan.

Persian today has no grammatical gender: «او» is used both for a man and for a woman.

در کتیبه‌ی بیستون، داریوش شورشیان را «دروغگو» می‌نامد.

dar katibe-ye Bisotun, Dāriyush shureshiyān rā «dorugh-gu» mi-nāmad.

In the Behistun inscription, Darius calls the rebels 'liars'.

زبان فارسی در طول زمان ساده‌تر شده است.

zabān-e fārsi dar tul-e zamān sāde-tar shode ast.

The Persian language has become simpler over time.

From Pahlavi to Rudaki

How New Persian was born – and the famous story of a poem that brought a king home. The couplet of Rudaki is quoted as it is commonly printed; note the old همی after the verb.

در قرن هفتم میلادی، عرب‌ها ایران را فتح کردند و ساسانیان از میان رفتند.

dar gharn-e haftom-e milādi, arab-hā Irān rā fath kardand va Sāsāniyān az miyān raftand.

In the seventh century AD the Arabs conquered Iran and the Sasanians came to an end.

زبان عربی زبان دین و دیوان شد، اما مردم در خانه و بازار همچنان فارسی حرف می‌زدند.

zabān-e arabi zabān-e din o divān shod, ammā mardom dar khāne o bāzār hamchenān fārsi harf mi-zadand.

Arabic became the language of religion and government, but at home and in the bazaar people went on speaking Persian.

دیوان divān here means 'the government offices', not a book of poems.

زرتشتیان تا چند قرن کتاب‌های خود را به خط پهلوی می‌نوشتند.

zartoshtiyān tā chand gharn ketāb-hā-ye khod rā be khatt-e pahlavi mi-neveshtand.

For several centuries the Zoroastrians went on writing their books in the Pahlavi script.

اما خط پهلوی دشوار بود و بسیاری از واژه‌ها را به آرامی می‌نوشتند و به فارسی می‌خواندند.

ammā khatt-e pahlavi doshvār bud va besyār-i az vāzhe-hā rā be ārāmi mi-neveshtand va be fārsi mi-khāndand.

But the Pahlavi script was difficult: many words were written in Aramaic and read in Persian.

کم‌کم ایرانیان فارسی را با الفبای عربی نوشتند، که ساده‌تر و آشناتر بود.

kam-kam irāniyān fārsi rā bā alefbā-ye arabi neveshtand, ke sāde-tar o āshnā-tar bud.

Gradually Iranians began to write Persian in the Arabic alphabet, which was simpler and more familiar.

برای صداهایی که در عربی نبود، چهار حرف تازه ساختند: پ، چ، ژ و گ.

barā-ye sedā-hā-yi ke dar arabi nabud, chahār harf-e tāze sākhtand: pe, che, zhe o gāf.

For the sounds that Arabic did not have, they made four new letters: پ، چ، ژ and گ.

واژه‌های عربی بسیاری وارد فارسی شد، اما دستور زبان فارسی تقریباً دست نخورده ماند.

vāzhe-hā-ye arabi-ye besyār-i vāred-e fārsi shod, ammā dastur-e zabān-e fārsi taghriban dast nakhorde mānd.

Many Arabic words entered Persian, but Persian grammar remained almost untouched.

نخستین شاعران بزرگ فارسی نو در خراسان و آسیای میانه پدید آمدند.

nakhostin shā'erān-e bozorg-e fārsi-ye now dar Khorāsān o Āsiyā-ye miyāne padid āmadand.

The first great poets of New Persian appeared in Khorasan and Central Asia.

در آن‌جا امیران سامانی شاعران را تشویق می‌کردند که به فارسی بنویسند.

dar ān-jā amirān-e Sāmāni shā'erān rā tashvigh mi-kardand ke be fārsi benevisand.

There the Samanid princes encouraged poets to write in Persian.

رودکی نابینا بود و چنگ می‌نواخت و شعرهایش را خود می‌خواند.

Rudaki nābinā bud va chang mi-navākht va she'r-hā-yash rā khod mi-khānd.

Rudaki was blind; he played the harp and sang his own poems.

می‌گویند امیر نصر سامانی سال‌ها در هرات مانده بود و نمی‌خواست به بخارا برگردد.

mi-guyand Amir Nasr-e Sāmāni sāl-hā dar Harāt mānde bud va nemi-khāst be Bokhārā bar-gardad.

They say that the Samanid prince Nasr had stayed in Herat for years and did not want to return to Bukhara.

یارانش از رودکی خواستند شعری بگوید تا دل امیر برای بخارا تنگ شود.

yārān-ash az Rudaki khāstand she'r-i beguyad tā del-e amir barā-ye Bokhārā tang shavad.

His companions asked Rudaki to write a poem that would make the prince homesick for Bukhara.

رودکی چنگ برداشت و چنین آغاز کرد:

Rudaki chang bardāsht va chenin āghāz kard:

Rudaki took up his harp and began like this:

بوی جوی مولیان آید همی / یاد یار مهربان آید همی

bu-ye ju-ye Muliyān āyad hami / yād-e yār-e mehrabān āyad hami

The scent of the Muliyan stream is coming to me; the memory of a dear friend is coming to me.

The Muliyan was a stream in Bukhara. آید همی āyad hami = می‌آید mi-āyad: in early New Persian همی could follow the verb.

امیر چنان به هیجان آمد که بی‌کفش بر اسب نشست و راهی بخارا شد.

amir chonān be hayajān āmad ke bi-kafsh bar asb neshast va rāhi-ye Bokhārā shod.

The prince was so moved that he jumped on his horse without his boots and set off for Bukhara.

هنوز هم بیشتر ایرانیان این بیت را می‌شناسند و بدون فرهنگ لغت می‌فهمند.

hanuz ham bishtar-e irāniyān in beyt rā mi-shenāsand va bedun-e farhang-e loghat mi-fahmand.

Most Iranians still know this couplet and understand it without a dictionary.

Old words in living mouths

Behistun. Darius had his inscription carved high up a cliff on the road from Babylon to Ecbatana (today's Hamadan), beside a relief of himself with the defeated rebels, and then had the rock below cut away so that no one could reach it. In the 1830s and 1840s the British officer Henry Rawlinson climbed the cliff, copied the Old Persian text and used it to decipher cuneiform: Behistun did for cuneiform what the Rosetta Stone did for Egyptian hieroglyphs. The site is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The calendar. Every Iranian speaks a little Avestan without knowing it. The months of the Iranian year carry the names of Zoroastrian divine beings: اردیبهشت Ordibehesht comes from Aša Vahišta 'the best truth', خرداد Khordād from Haurvatāt 'wholeness', مرداد Mordād from Amərətāt 'immortality', شهریور Shahrivar from Xšaθra Vairya 'the desired kingdom', بهمن Bahman from Vohu Manah 'good mind' and اسفند Esfand from Spəntā Ārmaiti 'holy devotion'.

The Zoroastrians. Iran's Zoroastrian communities, in Yazd, Kerman and Tehran, still pray in Avestan and keep festivals with Middle Persian names – Mehregan, Sadeh, Tirgan. In Yazd and Kerman some of them also speak their own Iranian language at home, which they call Behdini, 'of the good religion'.

Names. Our friend Dariush carries the name of Darius, Dārayavauš, 'he who holds firm the good'. Kurosh is Cyrus; Bahram, Ardeshir, Mitra and Anahita come from the old religion and the old kings, and Rostam, Sohrab and Tahmineh from the Shahnameh. Ask an Iranian what their name means, and you will often get a short lesson in history.

Tips

  • Old and Middle Persian are written here in Latin letters on purpose: they were never written in the Persian alphabet, and spelling them in it would be guesswork.
  • Persian has changed far less in a thousand years than English. Rudaki and Ferdowsi are harder than a newspaper, but they are not a foreign language – use them for reading practice.
  • The w-to-b change explains many words: Middle Persian wād → باد 'wind', wahār → بهار 'spring', wārān → باران 'rain'.
  • Middle Persian ō and ē became u and i in Iran but stayed o and e in Afghanistan and Tajikistan: Iranian روز ruz, شیر shir against Dari roz, sher (lesson 6).
  • When a word begins bar-, dor-, ser- or kh(o)r-, think of an old double consonant: برادر < brātar-, دروغ < drauga-.
  • Learn the month names with their meanings – they are a small lesson in ancient Iranian religion, and Iranians are often surprised and pleased when a foreigner knows them.
