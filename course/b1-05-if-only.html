کاش می‌دانستمIf only: unreal conditions, wishes and regrets

kāsh mi-dānestam

'If I had known…', 'I wish you were here', 'you should have told me', 'he must have forgotten'. This is the grammar of imagination and regret, and Persian builds almost all of it from forms you already know: the past continuous for anything unreal, the past perfect for the unreal past, and one new form, the past subjunctive رفته باشم. In this lesson you learn unreal conditions with اگر, wishes with کاش, 'should have' with باید, and 'may have' and 'must have' with the past subjunctive.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • make unreal conditions: اگر پول داشتم، می‌خریدم; اگر زودتر آمده بودی، او را می‌دیدی
  • form and use the past subjunctive: رفته باشم، دیده باشد
  • say 'may have', 'must have' and 'I hope … has' with شاید، باید، امیدوارم
  • express wishes and regrets with کاش
  • say 'should have', 'shouldn't have' and 'could have': باید می‌رفتی، نباید می‌گفتی، می‌توانستی بپرسی

Forty

On the night of his fortieth birthday, Amir looks back. The text is full of things that did not happen: notice the past continuous after اگر, the past perfect after کاش, and the past subjunctive after شاید.

امیر دیروز چهل ساله شد.

Amir diruz chehel sāle shod.

Amir turned forty yesterday.

شب، بعد از رفتن مهمان‌ها، تنها در بالکن نشست و به گذشته فکر کرد.

shab, ba'd az raftan-e mehmān-hā, tanhā dar bālkon neshast o be gozashte fekr kard.

That night, after the guests had left, he sat alone on the balcony and thought about the past.

بیست سال پیش، یک شرکت به او پیشنهاد کار در کانادا داده بود، اما او قبول نکرده بود.

bist sāl pish, yek sherkat be u pishnahād-e kār dar Kānādā dāde bud, ammā u ghabul nakarde bud.

Twenty years ago a company had offered him a job in Canada, but he had turned it down.

اگر آن پیشنهاد را قبول کرده بود، حالا زندگی دیگری داشت.

agar ān pishnahād rā ghabul karde bud, hālā zendegi-ye digar-i dāsht.

If he had accepted that offer, he would have a different life now.

شاید پولدارتر بود، اما هرگز با همسرش، مینا، آشنا نمی‌شد.

shāyad puldār-tar bud, ammā hargez bā hamsar-ash, Minā, āshnā nemi-shod.

He might be richer, but he would never have met his wife, Mina.

پدرش می‌خواست که او پزشک شود، اما امیر مهندس شد.

pedar-ash mi-khāst ke u pezeshk shavad, ammā Amir mohandes shod.

His father wanted him to become a doctor, but Amir became an engineer.

پدرش هنوز گاهی می‌گوید: «باید پزشکی می‌خواندی!»

pedar-ash hanuz gāhi mi-guyad: «bāyad pezeshki mi-khāndi!»

His father still says now and then: 'You should have studied medicine!'

اما امیر از کارش راضی است و فکر نمی‌کند اشتباه کرده باشد.

ammā Amir az kār-ash rāzi ast o fekr nemi-konad eshtebāh karde bāshad.

But Amir is happy with his work and doesn't think he made a mistake.

فقط گاهی با خودش می‌گوید: «کاش در جوانی تار زدن را یاد گرفته بودم.»

faghat gāhi bā khod-ash mi-guyad: «kāsh dar javāni tār zadan rā yād gerefte budam.»

Only sometimes he says to himself: 'If only I'd learned to play the tar when I was young.'

اما افسوس واقعی او داریوش است، بهترین دوست دوران دانشگاهش.

ammā afsus-e vāghe'i-ye u Dāriush ast, behtarin dust-e dowrān-e dāneshgāh-ash.

But his real regret is Dariush, his best friend from university.

ده سال پیش سر یک موضوع کوچک با هم دعوا کردند و از آن روز با هم قهرند.

dah sāl pish sar-e yek mowzu'-e kuchak bā ham da'vā kardand o az ān ruz bā ham ghahr-and.

Ten years ago they quarrelled over something small, and they haven't spoken since.

امیر فکر می‌کند: «اگر آن شب ساکت مانده بودم، دوستم را از دست نمی‌دادم.»

Amir fekr mi-konad: «agar ān shab sāket mānde budam, dust-am rā az dast nemi-dādam.»

Amir thinks: 'If I had kept quiet that night, I wouldn't have lost my friend.'

شاید داریوش هم همین فکر را کرده باشد.

shāyad Dāriush ham hamin fekr rā karde bāshad.

Perhaps Dariush has had the same thought.

امیر گوشی‌اش را برداشت و شماره‌ی داریوش را پیدا کرد.

Amir gushi-ash rā bardāsht o shomāre-ye Dāriush rā peydā kard.

Amir picked up his phone and found Dariush's number.

The grammar of things that didn't happen

English has a special word for the unreal: would. Persian doesn't. It takes the past continuous – می‌رفتم mi-raftam I was going, I used to go – and uses it for anything imagined: اگر وقت داشتم، می‌رفتم agar vaght dāshtam, mi-raftam if I had time, I would go. The past is far enough from reality to stand for 'not real'. English does the same in its if-clause: if I had time is a past tense about now.

The same trick gives you wishes – کاش می‌دانستم kāsh mi-dānestam I wish I knew – and, with باید bāyad, should have: باید می‌رفتی bāyad mi-rafti you should have gone. Only one form in this lesson is new: the past subjunctive, رفته باشم rafte bāsham, which you need for may have, must have and I hope … has.

If I had…: unreal conditions

You already know real conditions (A2): اگر وقت داشته باشم، می‌آیم agar vaght dāshte bāsham, mi-āyam if I have time, I'll come. For unreal conditions – things that are not true now, or did not happen – move both halves into the past:

  • Unreal now: the past continuous (or, for بودن and داشتن, the simple past) in the if-clause, and the past continuous in the result: اگر وقت داشتم، می‌آمدم agar vaght dāshtam, mi-āmadam if I had time, I would come.
  • Unreal past: the same sentence can also mean if I had had time, I would have come – context decides. To make the past unmistakable, use the past perfect in the if-clause: اگر زودتر خبر داده بودی، می‌آمدم agar zud-tar khabar dāde budi, mi-āmadam if you had told me earlier, I would have come.

بودن budan and داشتن dāshtan never take می- here: اگر جای تو بودم agar jā-ye to budam if I were you, اگر ماشین داشتیم agar māshin dāshtim if we had a car.

The past subjunctive: رفته باشم

The past subjunctive is the past participle plus the subjunctive of بودن budan: رفته باشم rafte bāsham, دیده باشی dide bāshi, گفته باشد gofte bāshad. It is the subjunctive of the perfect: use it wherever a subjunctive is needed but the action is already complete.

  • after شاید shāyad – may have: شاید رفته باشد shāyad rafte bāshad he may have gone
  • after باید bāyad – must have (a deduction): باید رفته باشد bāyad rafte bāshad he must have gone
  • after امیدوارم omidvār-am – I hope … has: امیدوارم رسیده باشی omidvār-am reside bāshi I hope you've arrived
  • after doubt: فکر نمی‌کنم fekr nemi-konam I don't think, بعید است ba'id ast it's unlikely
  • after اگر agar when you are not sure what happened: اگر نامه را خوانده باشد، می‌داند agar nāme rā khānde bāshad, mi-dānad if he's read the letter, he knows

Don't be misled by داشته باشم dāshte bāsham: that is simply the ordinary subjunctive of داشتن (that I have), not a past form.

Wishes and regrets: کاش and باید می‌…

کاش (ای کاش ey kāsh, spoken کاشکی kāshki) introduces a wish. The tense after it tells you what kind:

  • past continuous (or the simple past of بودن and داشتن) – a wish about now that cannot come true: کاش این‌جا بودی kāsh in-jā budi I wish you were here;
  • past perfect – a regret about the past: کاش نرفته بودم kāsh narafte budam I wish I hadn't gone;
  • present subjunctive – a hope for the future: کاش فردا آفتابی باشد kāsh fardā āftābi bāshad I hope it's sunny tomorrow.

Should have. باید bāyad + past continuous means should have: باید زودتر می‌آمدی bāyad zud-tar mi-āmadi you should have come earlier; نباید nabāyad + past continuous, shouldn't have. In formal writing you also meet می‌بایست mi-bāyest with the same meaning. Could have is می‌توانستم mi-tavānestam + subjunctive: می‌توانستی زنگ بزنی mi-tavānesti zang bezani you could have called.

Careful: باید رفته باشد bāyad rafte bāshad he must have gone is a guess about what happened; باید می‌رفت bāyad mi-raft he should have gone / he had to go is about what was necessary.

کاش فردا باران نیاید؛ می‌خواهیم به کوه برویم.

kāsh fardā bārān nayāyad; mi-khāhim be kuh beravim.

I hope it doesn't rain tomorrow – we want to go to the mountains.

Spoken کاشکی فردا بارون نیاد؛ می‌خوایم بریم کوه. kāshki fardā bārun nayād; mi-khāyim berim kuh.

Amir calls Dariush for the first time in ten years. Listen for کاش, اگر … بودم and باید می‌…

Hi Dariush, it's Amir.

Spoken سلام داریوش، امیرم. salām Dāriush, Amir-am.

Dariush

امیر؟ باورم نمی‌شود! ده سال است که صدایت را نشنیده‌ام.

Amir? bāvar-am nemi-shavad! dah sāl ast ke sedā-yat rā nashenide-am.

Amir? I can't believe it! I haven't heard your voice for ten years.

Spoken امیر؟ باورم نمی‌شه! ده ساله صداتو نشنیدم. Amir? bāvar-am nemi-she! dah sāle sedā-t-o nashenidam.

Amir

می‌دانم. باید خیلی زودتر زنگ می‌زدم.

mi-dānam. bāyad kheyli zud-tar zang mi-zadam.

I know. I should have called much sooner.

Spoken می‌دونم. باید خیلی زودتر زنگ می‌زدم. mi-dunam. bāyad kheyli zud-tar zang mi-zadam.

Dariush

من هم باید زنگ می‌زدم. چند بار شماره‌ات را گرفتم، اما قطع کردم.

man ham bāyad zang mi-zadam. chand bār shomāre-at rā gereftam, ammā ghat' kardam.

So should I. A few times I dialled your number, but then I hung up.

Spoken منم باید زنگ می‌زدم. چند بار شماره‌تو گرفتم، ولی قطع کردم. man-am bāyad zang mi-zadam. chand bār shomāre-t-o gereftam, vali ghat' kardam.

Amir

I wish I hadn't said those things that night.

Spoken کاشکی اون شب اون حرفا رو نزده بودم. kāshki un shab un harfā ro nazade budam.

Dariush

اگر من هم آن‌قدر عصبانی نشده بودم، کار به این‌جا نمی‌کشید.

If I hadn't got so angry either, things would never have come to this.

Spoken اگه منم اون‌قدر عصبانی نشده بودم، کار به این‌جا نمی‌کشید. age man-am un-ghadr asabāni nashode budam, kār be in-jā nemi-keshid.

Amir

دیروز تولدم بود. اگر تو هم آن‌جا بودی، همه چیز کامل بود.

diruz tavallod-am bud. agar to ham ān-jā budi, hame chiz kāmel bud.

It was my birthday yesterday. If you had been there too, everything would have been perfect.

Spoken دیروز تولدم بود. اگه تو هم اون‌جا بودی، همه چی کامل بود. diruz tavallod-am bud. age to ham un-jā budi, hame chi kāmel bud.

Dariush

تولدت مبارک! شاید هدیه‌ات کمی دیر برسد!

tavallod-at mobārak! shāyad hedye-at kam-i dir beresad!

Happy birthday! Your present may arrive a little late!

Spoken تولدت مبارک! شاید کادوت یه کم دیر برسه! tavallod-et mobārak! shāyad kādo-t ye kam dir berese!

Amir

بهترین هدیه همین تلفن است. فردا شب وقت داری؟

behtarin hedye hamin telefon ast. fardā shab vaght dāri?

This call is the best present. Are you free tomorrow night?

Spoken بهترین کادو همین تلفنه. فردا شب وقت داری؟ behtarin kādo hamin telefone. fardā shab vaght dāri?

Dariush

برای تو همیشه وقت دارم. همان قهوه‌خانه‌ی قدیمی؟

barā-ye to hamishe vaght dāram. hamān ghahve-khāne-ye ghadimi?

For you I always have time. The same old tea-house?

Spoken برای تو همیشه وقت دارم. همون قهوه‌خونه‌ی قدیمی؟ barā-ye to hamishe vaght dāram. hamun ghahve-khune-ye ghadimi?

Amir

امیدوارم هنوز بسته نشده باشد!

omidvār-am hanuz baste nashode bāshad!

I hope it hasn't closed down yet!

Spoken امیدوارم هنوز بسته نشده باشه! omidvār-am hanuz baste nashode bāshe!

Qahr and āshti

Few words are as Iranian as قهر ghahr. To be qahr with someone is to stop speaking to them after an offence – sometimes for an afternoon, sometimes, like Amir and Dariush, for years. Children announce it loudly: باهات قهرم! bāhāt ghahr-am! I'm not talking to you! Adults do it in silence: they skip a family gathering, don't answer the phone, send greetings to everyone except one person.

The cure is آشتی āshti reconciliation, and it rarely happens by itself. Usually an elder or a friend acts as go-between, and the making-up is sealed with a visit, a meal or a gift. Nowruz is the traditional moment: during the New Year visits (دید و بازدید did o bāzdid) the younger person calls on the older one, and old quarrels are expected to end with the old year.

The Persian of regret has its own sighs. When it is too late to change anything, people say کار از کار گذشته است kār az kār gozashte ast it's too late now – literally the work has passed beyond the work. Amir picked up the phone before it was.

Tips

  • In unreal conditions use the past continuous: اگر می‌دانستم، می‌گفتم. Only بودن and داشتن use the simple past instead: اگر بودم، اگر داشتم.
  • اگر می‌رفتم can mean 'if I went' or 'if I had gone'. When you need to be clearly in the past, use the past perfect: اگر رفته بودم.
  • باید رفته باشد = 'he must have gone' (a guess). باید می‌رفت = 'he should have gone' or 'he had to go' – context tells you whether he went.
  • In speech کاش is usually کاشکی; ای کاش sounds more emotional, even poetic.
  • داشته باشم is the normal subjunctive of داشتن ('that I have'), not a past subjunctive: امیدوارم روز خوبی داشته باشی 'I hope you have a good day'.
  • When you hear می‌بایست in the news, it means the same as باید: 'should (have)'.
