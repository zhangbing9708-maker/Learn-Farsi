برنامه‌ها و آرزوهاPlans and wishes: the weekend and beyond

barnāme-hā o ārezu-hā

What are you doing this weekend? In this lesson you talk about plans and wishes: 'I want to go', 'I'd like to see', 'let's go!'. You meet the subjunctive – the be- form of the verb that follows mi-khāham – and learn that Persian simply uses the present tense for the future: fardā mi-ravam, 'I'm going tomorrow'. And you go where Tehranis go at the weekend: up to Darband and Tochal, and north to the Caspian.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • say what you want to do with mi-khāham + subjunctive: mi-khāham beravam
  • make the subjunctive: be- + present stem + ending
  • talk about the future with the present tense and time words: fardā, hafte-ye ba'd
  • make suggestions with 'let's' (beravim!) and dust dāram + subjunctive
  • talk about weekend trips: Darband, Tochal, the Caspian

Plans for the weekend

It is Wednesday evening in Tehran. Amir asks Tom what he is doing at the weekend.

Amir

تام، آخر هفته چه کار می‌کنی؟

Tām, ākhar-e hafte che kār mi-koni?

Tom, what are you doing at the weekend?

Spoken تام، آخر هفته چی کار می‌کنی؟ Tām, ākhar-e hafte chi kār mi-koni?

Tom

هنوز نمی‌دانم. می‌خواهم کوه بروم، اما تنها نمی‌خواهم بروم.

hanuz nemi-dānam. mi-khāham kuh beravam, ammā tanhā nemi-khāham beravam.

I don't know yet. I want to go hiking in the mountains, but I don't want to go alone.

Spoken هنوز نمی‌دونم. می‌خوام برم کوه، اما تنها نمی‌خوام برم. hanuz nemi-dunam. mi-khām beram kuh, ammā tanhā nemi-khām beram.

Amir

ما جمعه صبح به توچال می‌رویم. تو هم می‌خواهی بیایی؟

mā jom'e sobh be Tochāl mi-ravim. to ham mi-khāhi biyāyi?

We're going to Tochal on Friday morning. Do you want to come too?

Spoken ما جمعه صبح می‌ریم توچال. تو هم می‌خوای بیای؟ mā jom'e sobh mi-rim Tochāl. to ham mi-khāy biyāy?

Tom

بله، حتماً! ساعت چند می‌روید؟

bale, hatman! sā'at-e chand mi-ravid?

Yes, definitely! What time are you going?

Amir

ساعت شش صبح. با تله‌کابین بالا می‌رویم.

sā'at-e shesh-e sobh. bā tele-kābin bālā mi-ravim.

At six in the morning. We go up by cable car.

Tom

ساعت شش؟ خیلی زود است!

sā'at-e shesh? kheyli zud ast!

At six? That's very early!

Amir

زود است، اما آن بالا هوا عالی است. بعد از ظهر به دربند می‌رویم و ناهار می‌خوریم.

zud ast, ammā ān bālā havā āli ast. ba'd az zohr be Darband mi-ravim va nāhār mi-khorim.

It is early, but the air up there is wonderful. In the afternoon we go to Darband and have lunch.

Tom

عالی است! من دربند را خیلی دوست دارم.

āli ast! man Darband rā kheyli dust dāram.

Great! I love Darband.

Amir

هفته‌ی بعد هم می‌خواهیم به شمال برویم. دوست داری بیایی؟

hafte-ye ba'd ham mi-khāhim be shomāl beravim. dust dāri biyāyi?

Next week we also want to go up north. Would you like to come?

Spoken هفته‌ی بعد هم می‌خوایم بریم شمال. دوست داری بیای؟ hafte-ye ba'd ham mi-khāym berim shomāl. dust dāri biyāy?

Tom

شمال؟ یعنی دریای خزر؟

shomāl? ya'ni daryā-ye Khazar?

The north? You mean the Caspian Sea?

Amir

بله. سه روز در ویلای دایی‌ام می‌مانیم.

bale. se ruz dar vilā-ye dāyi-am mi-mānim.

Yes. We're staying three days at my uncle's villa.

Tom

خیلی دوست دارم بیایم! ممنون، امیر.

kheyli dust dāram biyāyam! mamnun, Amir.

I'd love to come! Thank you, Amir.

Amir

خواهش می‌کنم. پس جمعه ساعت شش، جلوی خانه‌ی ما!

khāhesh mi-konam. pas jom'e sā'at-e shesh, jelo-ye khāne-ye mā!

You're welcome. So: Friday at six, in front of our house!

Talking about plans

Amir and Tom talk about the future without any future tense: ما جمعه به توچال می‌رویم. mā jom'e be Tochāl mi-ravim. We're going to Tochal on Friday. The time word – jom'e, fardā, hafte-ye ba'd – does the work, just as in English we're going on Friday.

When Tom says what he wants to do, the second verb changes shape: می‌خواهم کوه بروم mi-khāham kuh beravam I want to go to the mountains. Beravam is the subjunctive, the main new grammar of this lesson. You met its cousin in lesson 16: the imperative beravid! 'go!'.

کوه رفتن kuh raftan, literally to go mountain, means to go walking or hiking in the mountains – a favourite Friday activity for Tehranis, who have the Alborz range at the end of their streets. Notice too آخر هفته ākhar-e hafte the weekend, literally the end of the week, and شمال shomāl the north, which for a Tehrani means one thing: the Caspian coast.

I want to go: mi-khāham + subjunctive

After want, Persian does not use an infinitive like English to go. Instead both verbs take the person ending: I want I-go – می‌خواهم بروم mi-khāham beravam I want to go, می‌خواهی بروی mi-khāhi beravi you want to go, می‌خواهد برود mi-khāhad beravad she wants to go. The second verb is in the subjunctive (Persian grammar calls it التزامی eltezāmi).

The subjunctive is easy to make: be- + present stem + ending – the same stem and endings as the present tense, with be- in place of mi-: می‌روم mi-ravam I go → بروم beravam (that) I go. Before a stem beginning with ā, be- becomes bi-: بیایم biyāyam (that) I come. Two-word verbs with kardan or shodan usually drop the be-: استراحت کنم esterāhat konam (that) I rest, بیدار شوم bidār shavam (that) I wake up.

To say you don't want to do something, make want negative – the subjunctive stays as it is: نمی‌خواهم بروم nemi-khāham beravam I don't want to go. In speech, mi-khāham beravam is mi-khām beram – perhaps the most useful sentence in Tehran.

Tomorrow I'm going: the present for the future

Persian has a formal future tense, but in everyday life people use the present tense for plans, with a time word to show when: فردا به شمال می‌رویم. fardā be shomāl mi-ravim. Tomorrow we're going north. – هفته‌ی بعد برمی‌گردم. hafte-ye ba'd bar-mi-gardam. I'll be back next week. The written future, khāham raft, comes later in the course.

The time word usually goes at the start of the sentence or right after the subject. These are the ones you will use most:

Let's go! Wishes and suggestions

The subjunctive follows many words of wanting and planning, not only mi-khāham:

  • دوست دارم dust dāram I'd like + subjunctive is softer than I want: دوست دارم اصفهان را ببینم. dust dāram Esfahān rā bebinam. I'd like to see Isfahan.
  • قرار است gharār ast it is planned + subjunctive: قرار است جمعه به شمال برویم. gharār ast jom'e be shomāl beravim. We're supposed to go north on Friday.
  • شاید shāyad maybe + subjunctive: شاید فردا بیایم. shāyad fardā biyāyam. Maybe I'll come tomorrow.
  • آرزو دارم ārezu dāram I wish, my dream is + subjunctive: آرزو دارم ایران را ببینم. ārezu dāram Irān rā bebinam. My dream is to see Iran.

On its own, the we form of the subjunctive means let's: برویم! beravim! let's go! – spoken berim! With a rising voice it becomes a suggestion: به دربند برویم؟ be Darband beravim? shall we go to Darband?

A weekend in Tehran: Darband, Tochal and the road north

Tehran sits at the foot of the البرز Alborz mountains, and on a clear day snowy peaks close off the end of every northern street. At the weekend, especially early on Friday morning, thousands of Tehranis go کوه kuh – hiking in the mountains – with a flask of tea, bread and cheese, and often the whole family.

دربند Darband is where many walks begin. A path climbs beside a mountain stream, lined with small restaurants and teahouses where people sit cross-legged on carpet-covered wooden platforms, تخت takht, above the water, eating kebab or دیزی dizi (a lamb and chickpea stew) and drinking tea. In the evening the lights and the crowds turn it into one long party.

توچال Tochāl, almost 4,000 metres high, is Tehran's own mountain. A long cable car, the تله‌کابین tele-kābin, carries visitors from the edge of the city up towards the summit, where there is skiing for much of the year.

And when a long weekend or a holiday comes, Tehran empties northwards. شمال shomāl the north – the green Caspian coast of Gilan and Mazandaran, with its rice fields, forests, rain and seaside villas – is three or four hours away over the mountains, by the famous Chalus road or the Haraz road. On the way back, everyone meets in the same traffic jam.

Tips

  • mi-khām beram 'I want to go' is the spoken form of mi-khāham beravam: khāham shrinks to khām, beravam to beram. In the same way beravim 'let's go' becomes berim!
  • be- often sounds bo- before a stem with o: bokhoram '(that) I eat', boro! 'go!'. Before ā it becomes bi-: biyāyam '(that) I come'.
  • With kardan and shodan verbs the be- is normally left out: kār konam, bidār shavam. You will also hear bekonam, beshavam (spoken besham) – both are fine.
  • Wish anyone who is going away safar be kheyr! 'have a good trip!' – the same be kheyr as in sobh be kheyr.
  • For Tehranis, shomāl needs no explanation: shomāl mi-ram means 'I'm going to the Caspian'.
