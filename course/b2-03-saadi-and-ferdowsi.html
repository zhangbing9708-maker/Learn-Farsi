سعدی و فردوسیSaadi and Ferdowsi

Sa'di o Ferdowsi

Two styles every Iranian knows by ear. Saadi's Golestan (1258) tells short, wise stories in prose that rhymes; Ferdowsi's Shahnameh, finished around 1010, tells the history of Iran in some 50,000 marching couplets, in a Persian with remarkably few Arabic words. In this lesson you read a complete Golestan story in the original, retell it in today's Persian, learn how rhymed prose works, and meet the epic voice of «توانا بود هر که دانا بود».

In this lesson you will learn to

  • read a complete story from the Golestan in the original and retell it in modern Persian
  • recognise سجع, Saadi's rhymed prose, with its short, balanced, verb-sharing clauses
  • read couplets of the Shahnameh, including «توانا بود هر که دانا بود / ز دانش دل پیر برنا بود»
  • explain why Ferdowsi's Persian has so few Arabic words, and pair his words with their Arabic counterparts

A story from the Golestan: the slave in the boat

The seventh story of the first chapter of the Golestan, «در سیرت پادشاهان» ('On the conduct of kings'), complete. In Saadi's day the boat was a terrifying place: notice how few words he needs to tell a whole story. The story is followed in the book by a short poem on the same theme, which we leave out.

پادشاهی با غلامی عجمی در کشتی نشست و غلام دیگر دریا را ندیده بود و محنت کشتی نیازموده.

pādshāh-i bā gholām-i ajami dar keshti neshast va gholām digar daryā rā nadide bud va mehnat-e keshti nayāzmude.

A king boarded a boat with a Persian slave; the slave had never seen the sea before, nor known the hardships of a ship.

عجمی ajami 'non-Arab, Persian'. دیگر digar here means 'ever before'. In the second half the verb بود bud is left out: نیازموده (بود) nayāzmude (bud) 'had not tried'.

گریه و زاری در نهاد و لرزه بر اندامش اوفتاد.

gerye o zāri dar nahād o larze bar andām-ash uftād.

He began to weep and wail, and a trembling came over his limbs.

در نهادن dar nahādan 'to begin'. اوفتاد uftād is an old spelling of افتاد oftād. Hear the rhyme: نهاد nahād – اوفتاد uftād.

چندان که ملاطفت کردند آرام نمی‌گرفت و عیش ملک ازو منغص بود.

chandān ke molātefat kardand ārām nemi-gereft va eysh-e malek az-u monaghghas bud.

However kindly they treated him, he would not calm down, and he spoiled the king's pleasure.

چندان که chandān ke 'however much'; ملک malek 'king'; ازو azu = از او az u; منغص monaghghas 'spoiled, troubled'.

چاره ندانستند.

chāre nadānestand.

They did not know what to do.

حکیمی در آن کشتی بود.

hakim-i dar ān keshti bud.

There was a wise man on that boat.

ملک را گفت: اگر فرمان دهی، من او را به طریقی خامش گردانم.

malek rā goft: agar farmān dehi, man u rā be tarigh-i khāmosh gardānam.

He said to the king, 'If you command it, I will quieten him by a certain method.'

ملک را گفت malek rā goft = به پادشاه گفت be pādshāh goft: here را means 'to'. خامش khāmosh = خاموش khāmush 'silent'.

گفت: غایت لطف و کرم باشد.

goft: ghāyat-e lotf o karam bāshad.

The king said, 'That would be the height of kindness.'

بفرمود تا غلام به دریا انداختند.

befarmud tā gholām be daryā andākhtand.

He gave the order, and they threw the slave into the sea.

After بفرمود تا befarmud tā 'ordered that', classical Persian can use a plain past where we now use a subjunctive: دستور داد که بیندازند dastur dād ke biyandāzand.

باری چند غوطه خورد.

bār-i chand ghute khord.

He went under a few times.

باری چند bār-i chand = چند بار chand bār 'a few times'.

مویش گرفتند و پیش کشتی آوردند.

mu-yash gereftand o pish-e keshti āvardand.

They grabbed him by the hair and brought him to the boat.

No را after مویش muyash: old prose often leaves out the object marker.

به دو دست در سکان کشتی آویخت.

be do dast dar sokkān-e keshti āvikht.

He clung to the rudder with both hands.

چون برآمد، به گوشه‌ای بنشست و آرام یافت.

chon bar-āmad, be gushe-i benshast o ārām yāft.

When he came out, he sat down in a corner and was calm.

چون chon here is 'when'. بنشست benshast: be- on a past verb adds nothing to the meaning.

ملک را عجب آمد.

malek rā ajab āmad.

The king was amazed.

Literally: to the king amazement came

پرسید: در این چه حکمت بود؟

porsid: dar in che hekmat bud?

He asked, 'What was the wisdom in this?'

گفت: از اول محنت غرقه شدن ناچشیده بود و قدر سلامت کشتی نمی‌دانست.

goft: az avval mehnat-e gharghe shodan nācheshide bud va ghadr-e salāmat-e keshti nemi-dānest.

The wise man said, 'At first he had never tasted the misery of drowning, and so he did not know the value of the safety of the boat.'

همچنین قدر عافیت کسی داند که به مصیبتی گرفتار آید.

hamchenin ghadr-e āfiyat kas-i dānad ke be mosibat-i gereftār āyad.

'In the same way, only someone who has been struck by misfortune knows the value of well-being.'

The moral, still quoted today. داند dānad = می‌داند mi-dānad; گرفتار آید gereftār āyad = گرفتار شود gereftār shavad.

Saadi and the Rose Garden

Saadi (سعدی Sa'di, who died around 1291) left Shiraz as a young man, studied in Baghdad and wandered for many years through the Arab world before coming home. In 1258 he finished the Golestan (گلستان golestān, 'The Rose Garden'), a book of short stories in eight chapters: on kings, on dervishes, on contentment, on silence, on love and youth, on old age, on education and on good company. A year earlier he had written the Bustan (بوستان bustān, 'The Orchard'), a masnavi of moral tales in verse.

The Golestan mixes prose and verse: a few lines of story, a couplet that sums it up, a few more lines, a little poem. Its prose is famous for three things you can see in the story you have just read:

  • Short sentences. چاره ندانستند. chāre nadānestand. 'They did not know what to do.' Two words and a full stop: Saadi tells a story at the speed of speech.
  • Rhymed prose (سجع saj'). A sentence falls into two balanced halves that end in rhyming words: گریه و زاری در نهاد gerye o zāri dar nahād / و لرزه بر اندامش اوفتاد o larze bar andām-ash uftād.
  • A moral at the end, phrased so well that it becomes a proverb: قدر عافیت کسی داند که به مصیبتی گرفتار آید ghadr-e āfiyat kas-i dānad ke be mosibat-i gereftār āyad.

For seven centuries the Golestan was the first book Persian-speaking children read after the Quran, from Istanbul to Delhi. Iranians still study its stories at school, and dozens of its sentences live on as proverbs.

Rhymed prose: سجع

In Saadi's prose a sentence usually falls into two balanced halves, often of about the same length, and the last words of the halves rhyme. This is سجع saj', 'rhymed prose', borrowed from Arabic and perfected in Persian. Three habits go with it:

  1. The verb is shared. The second half leaves out a verb the first half already has: موجب قربت است و … مزید نعمت mujeb-e ghorbat ast va … mazid-e ne'mat – the است ast is understood.
  2. Words come in pairs: گریه و زاری gerye o zāri, لطف و کرم lotf o karam – two near-synonyms for one idea.
  3. The rhyme marks the break. If you hear -ād at a pause, expect -ād at the end of the sentence.

The most famous saj' in Persian is the opening of the Golestan, which every Iranian schoolchild meets; the first three examples come from it. The others are sentences from the stories that have become proverbs.

منت خدای را عز و جل که طاعتش موجب قربت است و به شکر اندرش مزید نعمت.

mennat khodāy rā azza va jall ke tā'at-ash mujeb-e ghorbat ast va be shokr andar-ash mazid-e ne'mat.

Thanks be to God, mighty and glorious: obeying Him brings us near to Him, and thanking Him increases His blessings.

The first sentence of the Golestan. عز و جل azza va jall is Arabic, 'mighty and glorious is He'.

هر نفسی که فرو می‌رود ممد حیات است و چون برمی‌آید مفرح ذات.

har nafas-i ke foru mi-ravad momedd-e hayāt ast va chon bar-mi-āyad mofarreh-e zāt.

Every breath that goes in sustains life, and when it comes out it gladdens the soul.

پس در هر نفسی دو نعمت موجود است و بر هر نعمتی شکری واجب.

pas dar har nafas-i do ne'mat mowjud ast va bar har ne'mat-i shokr-i vājeb.

So in every breath there are two blessings, and for every blessing a thanksgiving is due.

Again no verb in the second half: شکری واجب (است) shokri vājeb (ast).

باران رحمت بی‌حسابش همه را رسیده و خوان نعمت بی‌دریغش همه جا کشیده.

bārān-e rahmat-e bi-hesāb-ash hame rā reside va khān-e ne'mat-e bi-dareygh-ash hame jā keshide.

The rain of His countless mercy has reached everyone, and the table of His unstinting bounty is spread everywhere.

ده درویش در گلیمی بخسبند و دو پادشاه در اقلیمی نگنجند.

dah darvish dar gelim-i bekhosband o do pādshāh dar eghlim-i nagonjand.

Ten dervishes can sleep under one blanket, but two kings cannot fit into one country.

A double rhyme: گلیمی gelimi / اقلیمی eghlimi and بخسبند bekhosband / نگنجند nagonjand.

دروغی مصلحت‌آمیز به که راستی فتنه‌انگیز.

dorugh-i maslahat-āmiz beh ke rāsti-ye fetne-angiz.

A lie that serves a good purpose is better than a truth that stirs up trouble.

From the first story of the Golestan. به beh is 'better' (= بهتر behtar), not the preposition به be.

مشک آن است که خود ببوید، نه آن که عطار بگوید.

moshk ān ast ke khod bebuyad, na ān ke attār beguyad.

Real musk is what smells of itself, not what the perfume-seller says it is.

Quoted daily: true quality speaks for itself.

هر که دست از جان بشوید، هر چه در دل دارد بگوید.

har ke dast az jān beshuyad, har che dar del dārad beguyad.

Whoever has washed his hands of life says whatever is in his heart.

دست شستن از dast shostan az 'to wash one's hands of' works just as in English.

The story in modern Persian

Saadi wrote seven and a half centuries ago, yet an Iranian teenager can follow most of the story. What has changed is mostly small: a few words, the use of را, and the verb forms. Retell the story in today's written Persian and you will meet each change once:

  • را for 'to': ملک را گفت malek rā goft → به پادشاه گفت be pādshāh goft; and ملک را عجب آمد malek rā ajab āmad → پادشاه تعجب کرد pādshāh ta'ajjob kard.
  • The bare present where we now add می- or be-: داند dānad → می‌داند mi-dānad, دهی dehi → بدهی bedahi.
  • Old verbs: در نهادن dar nahādan → شروع کردن shoru' kardan, خامش گردانیدن khāmosh gardānidan → ساکت کردن sāket kardan, آویختن āvikhtan → چسبیدن chasbidan.
  • Word order: باری چند bār-i chand → چند بار chand bār.

پادشاهی با غلامی ایرانی سوار کشتی شد؛ غلام تا آن روز دریا را ندیده بود و سختی سفر با کشتی را تجربه نکرده بود.

pādshāh-i bā gholām-i irāni savār-e keshti shod; gholām tā ān ruz daryā rā nadide bud va sakhti-ye safar bā keshti rā tajrobe na-karde bud.

A king boarded a ship with a Persian slave; until that day the slave had never seen the sea or known the hardship of travelling by ship.

غلام شروع به گریه و زاری کرد و تمام بدنش می‌لرزید.

gholām shoru' be gerye o zāri kard o tamām-e badan-ash mi-larzid.

The slave began to cry and wail, and his whole body was trembling.

هر چه با او مهربانی کردند، آرام نشد و سفر را به پادشاه تلخ کرد.

har che bā u mehrabāni kardand, ārām na-shod va safar rā be pādshāh talkh kard.

However kind they were to him, he did not calm down, and he spoiled the journey for the king.

هیچ‌کس چاره‌ای نمی‌دانست، تا این‌که حکیمی که در کشتی بود گفت که اگر پادشاه اجازه بدهد، او را آرام می‌کند.

hich-kas chāre-i nemi-dānest, tā in-ke hakim-i ke dar keshti bud goft ke agar pādshāh ejāze bedahad, u rā ārām mi-konad.

No one knew what to do, until a wise man on the ship said that if the king allowed him, he would calm the slave down.

پادشاه پذیرفت و به دستور حکیم غلام را به دریا انداختند.

pādshāh pazireft va be dastur-e hakim gholām rā be daryā andākhtand.

The king agreed, and on the wise man's instructions they threw the slave into the sea.

غلام چند بار زیر آب رفت؛ بعد موهایش را گرفتند و او را کنار کشتی آوردند و او با دو دست به سکان کشتی چسبید.

gholām chand bār zir-e āb raft; ba'd mu-hā-yash rā gereftand o u rā kenār-e keshti āvardand va u bā do dast be sokkān-e keshti chasbid.

The slave went under a few times; then they grabbed his hair and brought him to the side of the ship, and he clung to the rudder with both hands.

وقتی بالا آمد، گوشه‌ای نشست و آرام شد، و پادشاه با تعجب پرسید که حکمت این کار چه بود.

vaghti bālā āmad, gushe-i neshast o ārām shod, va pādshāh bā ta'ajjob porsid ke hekmat-e in kār che bud.

When he came up he sat in a corner and was calm, and the king asked in surprise what the wisdom of this had been.

حکیم جواب داد که او سختی غرق شدن را نچشیده بود و قدر امنیت کشتی را نمی‌دانست؛ فقط کسی قدر سلامتی را می‌داند که به مصیبتی گرفتار شده باشد.

hakim javāb dād ke u sakhti-ye ghargh shodan rā na-cheshide bud va ghadr-e amniyat-e keshti rā nemi-dānest; faghat kas-i ghadr-e salāmati rā mi-dānad ke be mosibat-i gereftār shode bāshad.

The wise man answered that the slave had never tasted the hardship of drowning and did not know the value of the ship's safety; only someone who has suffered misfortune knows the value of well-being.

Ferdowsi: the epic voice

Two and a half centuries before Saadi, Ferdowsi wrote the Shahnameh (شاهنامه shāhnāme, 'The Book of Kings'): some 50,000 couplets of myth, legend and history, from the first king of the world to the Arab conquest of Iran. Its metre, motaqāreb, marches – da-DUM-DUM, da-DUM-DUM, da-DUM-DUM, da-DUM: ta-vā-nā / bo-vad har / ke dā-nā / bo-vad. Its sentences are short and its verbs are strong.

Why so few Arabic words? Where later writers say عقل aghl (Arabic) Ferdowsi says خرد kherad; for عدل adl he has داد dād, for علم elm دانش dānesh. Only a few words in every hundred in the Shahnameh are Arabic – far fewer than in Saadi's Golestan. There are several reasons:

  • The subject. The Shahnameh retells the history of pre-Islamic Iran, from older Persian books and the memory of the dehqāns, the landed gentry who kept the old stories alive.
  • The place and time. Ferdowsi lived in tenth-century Khorasan, where Persian had just become a language of literature again and Arabic had not yet flooded it.
  • The style. Epic language prefers old, plain, concrete words.

Scholars warn against seeing a modern 'language purist' in Ferdowsi – he does use Arabic words when he needs them – but his Persian became the model whenever later Iranians wanted to write 'pure Persian'. In the 20th century his vocabulary returned in new words such as دانشگاه dāneshgāh university and دادگاه dādgāh court of law.

توانا بود هر که دانا بود / ز دانش دل پیر برنا بود

tavānā bovad har ke dānā bovad / ze dānesh del-e pir bornā bovad

Whoever is wise is powerful; through knowledge the heart of the old stays young.

بود bovad is the old present 'is' (today است ast); برنا bornā 'young'. The first half is a favourite motto of Iranian schools.

به نام خداوند جان و خرد / کزین برتر اندیشه برنگذرد

be nām-e khodāvand-e jān o kherad / k-az-in bar-tar andishe bar-nagozarad

In the name of the Lord of soul and wisdom, beyond whom no thought can rise.

The first line of the Shahnameh. کزین kazin = که از این ke az in. There is not a single Arabic word in it.

خداوند نام و خداوند جای / خداوند روزی‌ده رهنمای

khodāvand-e nām o khodāvand-e jāy / khodāvand-e ruzi-deh-e rahnamāy

Lord of name and Lord of place, Lord who gives our daily bread and shows the way.

جای jāy and رهنمای rahnamāy are the old forms of جا jā and رهنما rahnamā, kept for the rhyme.

میازار موری که دانه‌کش است / که جان دارد و جان شیرین خوش است

mayāzār mur-i ke dāne-kash ast / ke jān dārad o jān-e shirin khosh ast

Do not hurt an ant that carries grain: it has a life, and sweet life is good.

میازار mayāzār = نیازار nayāzār 'do not hurt', the old negative imperative with مـ. Saadi quotes this couplet in his Bustan, with a blessing on Ferdowsi.

یکی داستان است پر آب چشم / دل نازک از رستم آید به خشم

yeki dāstān ast por āb-e cheshm / del-e nāzok az Rostam āyad be khashm

This is a story full of tears: a tender heart will be angry with Rostam.

The opening of the tragedy of Rostam and Sohrab, in which the hero kills his own son without knowing him. آب چشم āb-e cheshm 'eye-water' is 'tears'.

اگر تندبادی برآید ز کنج / به خاک افکند نارسیده ترنج

agar tondbād-i bar-āyad ze konj / be khāk afkanad nāreside toranj

If a storm wind rises from a corner and throws an unripe citron to the ground…

…should we call it cruel or just? With this question Ferdowsi begins the story of Sohrab, who dies young.

بسی رنج بردم در این سال سی / عجم زنده کردم بدین پارسی

basi ranj bordam dar in sāl-e si / ajam zende kardam bed-in pārsi

I suffered much in these thirty years; I brought the Persians back to life with this Persian.

The best-known couplet attributed to Ferdowsi – though it is missing from the oldest manuscripts and scholars doubt that he wrote it. Ironically, عجم ajam 'the Persians' is an Arabic word. بسی basi = بسیار besyār; بدین bedin = به این be in.

Ferdowsi and the Book of Kings

The poet's life and the legend around it, in modern written Persian. Watch for the impersonal «می‌گویند» 'they say', which marks the legend.

فردوسی، شاعر بزرگ ایران، در حدود سال ۳۲۹ هجری در روستایی نزدیک توس به دنیا آمد.

Ferdowsi, shā'er-e bozorg-e Irān, dar hodud-e sāl-e sisad-o-bist-o-noh-e hejri dar rustā-yi nazdik-e Tus be donyā āmad.

Ferdowsi, the great poet of Iran, was born around the year 329 of the Hijra (940 AD) in a village near Tus.

او دهقان بود؛ یعنی از خانواده‌ای زمین‌دار که تاریخ و افسانه‌های ایران قدیم را نگه داشته بودند.

u dehghān bud; ya'ni az khānevāde-i zamin-dār ke tārikh o afsāne-hā-ye Irān-e ghadim rā negah dāshte budand.

He was a dehqan – from a family of landowners who had kept alive the history and legends of ancient Iran.

فردوسی حدود سی سال از عمرش را صرف سرودن شاهنامه کرد.

Ferdowsi hodud-e si sāl az omr-ash rā sarf-e sorudan-e Shāhnāme kard.

Ferdowsi spent about thirty years of his life composing the Shahnameh.

شاهنامه نزدیک به پنجاه هزار بیت دارد و داستان ایران را از نخستین شاه تا حمله‌ی عرب‌ها روایت می‌کند.

Shāhnāme nazdik be panjāh hezār beyt dārad va dāstān-e Irān rā az nakhostin shāh tā hamle-ye arab-hā revāyat mi-konad.

The Shahnameh has nearly fifty thousand couplets and tells the story of Iran from the first king to the Arab invasion.

داستان‌های رستم، سهراب، سیاوش و اسفندیار از این کتاب است.

dāstān-hā-ye Rostam, Sohrāb, Siyāvash o Esfandiyār az in ketāb ast.

The stories of Rostam, Sohrab, Siyavash and Esfandiyar come from this book.

فردوسی شاهنامه را به سلطان محمود غزنوی پیشکش کرد.

Ferdowsi Shāhnāme rā be Soltān Mahmud-e Ghaznavi pishkesh kard.

Ferdowsi presented the Shahnameh to Sultan Mahmud of Ghazni.

می‌گویند سلطان به جای سکه‌های طلا، سکه‌های نقره برای او فرستاد و شاعر رنجید.

mi-guyand soltān be jā-ye sekke-hā-ye talā, sekke-hā-ye noghre barā-ye u ferestād va shā'er ranjid.

They say the sultan sent him silver coins instead of gold, and the poet was offended.

طبق همین افسانه، وقتی سلطان پشیمان شد و طلا فرستاد، جنازه‌ی فردوسی را از دروازه‌ی دیگر شهر بیرون می‌بردند.

tebgh-e hamin afsāne, vaghti soltān pashimān shod o talā ferestād, jenāze-ye Ferdowsi rā az darvāze-ye digar-e shahr birun mi-bordand.

According to the same legend, when the sultan repented and sent the gold, Ferdowsi's body was being carried out through the other gate of the town.

زبان شاهنامه فارسی‌ای بسیار ناب است و واژه‌های عربی در آن کم است.

zabān-e Shāhnāme fārsi-yi besyār nāb ast va vāzhe-hā-ye arabi dar ān kam ast.

The language of the Shahnameh is a very pure Persian, with few Arabic words.

یکی از دلیل‌هایش این است که منابع فردوسی متن‌های کهن ایرانی بود، نه کتاب‌های عربی.

yek-i az dalil-hā-yash in ast ke manābe'-e Ferdowsi matn-hā-ye kohan-e irāni bud, na ketāb-hā-ye arabi.

One of the reasons is that Ferdowsi's sources were old Iranian texts, not Arabic books.

او در خراسان زندگی می‌کرد، جایی که در آن روزگار نوشتن به فارسی دوباره رونق گرفته بود.

u dar Khorāsān zendegi mi-kard, jā-yi ke dar ān ruzgār neveshtan be fārsi dobāre rownagh gerefte bud.

He lived in Khorasan, where in those days writing in Persian had flourished once again.

فردوسی در حدود سال ۴۱۱ هجری در توس درگذشت.

Ferdowsi dar hodud-e sāl-e chahārsad-o-yāzdah-e hejri dar Tus dargozasht.

Ferdowsi died in Tus around the year 411 of the Hijra (1020 AD).

آرامگاه او در توس، نزدیک مشهد، است و هر سال ۲۵ اردیبهشت روز بزرگداشت اوست.

ārāmgāh-e u dar Tus, nazdik-e Mashhad, ast va har sāl bist-o-panj-e Ordibehesht ruz-e bozorgdāsht-e u-st.

His tomb is in Tus, near Mashhad, and every year the 25th of Ordibehesht is his day of remembrance.

هنوز هم در بعضی قهوه‌خانه‌ها نقال‌ها داستان‌های شاهنامه را برای مردم نقل می‌کنند.

hanuz ham dar ba'zi ghahve-khāne-hā naghghāl-hā dāstān-hā-ye Shāhnāme rā barā-ye mardom naghl mi-konand.

Even today, in some coffee-houses, storytellers perform the stories of the Shahnameh for the public.

Two tombs, two days – and the Shahnameh out loud

Saadi in Shiraz. The سعدیه Sa'diye, Saadi's tomb, stands in a garden on the edge of Shiraz, not far from Hafez. Below it, steps lead down to a spring-fed pool full of fish. The 1st of Ordibehesht (around 21 April) is Saadi Day. His most famous lines, بنی‌آدم اعضای یکدیگرند bani-ādam a'zā-ye yekdigar-and the children of Adam are limbs of one another, are woven into a carpet that hangs at the United Nations in New York.

Ferdowsi in Tus. Ferdowsi's tomb near Mashhad is a white stone monument in the style of the ancient Achaemenid kings, first built in the 1930s for the thousandth anniversary of his birth and surrounded by carved scenes from the Shahnameh. The 25th of Ordibehesht (around 15 May) is Ferdowsi Day.

The Shahnameh out loud. For centuries most people did not read the Shahnameh – they heard it. In the coffee-house a storyteller, the نقال naghghāl, acted out the battles of Rostam in front of a large painted canvas, raising his voice and his stick for the fights and dropping to a whisper for the tragedies. This art, نقالی naghghāli, is on UNESCO's list of heritage in urgent need of protection. And in the traditional gymnasium, the زورخانه zurkhāne, men still swing heavy wooden clubs to the beat of a drum while the master chants couplets of Ferdowsi.

Every Iranian knows the names of the heroes: Rostam with his horse Rakhsh, the white-haired Zal raised by the Simurgh, the tragic Sohrab and Siyavash. Many children are still named after them.

Tips

  • In the Golestan, را often means 'to' or 'for': ملک را گفت 'he said to the king'; ملک را عجب آمد 'the king was amazed' (literally 'to the king amazement came').
  • Read Saadi's sentences in pairs. If the first half ends in -ād, listen for -ād at the end of the second: the rhyme tells you where the sentence breaks.
  • A missing verb in old prose or verse is usually the same as the verb of the previous clause: مزید نعمت = مزید نعمت است.
  • Ferdowsi's بود in a general truth is bovad 'is', not bud 'was': توانا بود هر که دانا بود.
  • Ferdowsi's words are alive in modern formal Persian: دانشگاه 'university', دادگاه 'court', خردمند 'wise', اندیشمند 'thinker'. Learn the old word and you can read the new one.
  • Learn a few Golestan sentences as proverbs – مشک آن است که خود ببوید، نه آن که عطار بگوید – Iranians recognise them at once and will be delighted.
