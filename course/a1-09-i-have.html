Course / Beginner (A1) / Lesson 9

دارم، ندارم، لازم دارمI have, I don't have, I need

dāram, na-dāram, lāzem dāram

One verb opens up a whole world of conversation: dāshtan, 'to have'. In this lesson you learn all its forms, how to say you do not have something, how to ask how many of something someone has, and how to say what you need – at home, in a shop and at a friend's house with a very curious cat.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • say what you have and do not have with dāshtan
  • ask 'do you have…?' and 'how many … do you have?'
  • say what you need with lāzem dāshtan
  • use chand-tā and yek for counting things
Show

Tom visits Amir

Tom, an Australian student in Tehran, visits his classmate Amir at home for the first time. A cat walks in.

Tom

امیر، تو گربه داری؟

Amir, to gorbe dāri?

Amir, do you have a cat?

Amir

بله، دو تا گربه دارم. این پیشی است، آن یکی ملوس.

bale, do tā gorbe dāram. in Pishi ast, ān yeki Malus.

Yes, I have two cats. This one is Pishi, that one is Malus.

Tom

چه قشنگ! من در خانه حیوان ندارم.

che ghashang! man dar khāne heyvān na-dāram.

How pretty! I don't have an animal at home.

Amir

سگ هم نداری؟

sag ham na-dāri?

You don't have a dog either?

Tom

نه، سگ هم ندارم. اما در استرالیا خانواده‌ام یک سگ دارد.

na, sag ham na-dāram. ammā dar Ostorāliyā khānevāde-am yek sag dārad.

No, I don't have a dog either. But in Australia my family has a dog.

Spoken نه، سگ هم ندارم. اما تو استرالیا خانواده‌م یه سگ داره. na, sag ham na-dāram. ammā tu Ostorāliyā khānevāde-m ye sag dāre.

Amir

چای می‌خوری؟ قند داریم، شکر هم داریم.

chāy mi-khori? ghand dārim, shekar ham dārim.

Will you have tea? We have sugar cubes, and we have sugar too.

Tom

بله، ممنون. شیر هم دارید؟

bale, mamnun. shir ham dārid?

Yes, thanks. Do you have milk too?

Amir

متأسفانه شیر نداریم. گربه‌ها همه‌ی شیر را خوردند!

mota'assefāne shir na-dārim. gorbe-hā hame-ye shir rā khordand!

Unfortunately we don't have milk. The cats drank all the milk!

Spoken متأسفانه شیر نداریم. گربه‌ها همه‌ی شیر رو خوردن! mota'assefāne shir na-dārim. gorbe-hā hame-ye shir ro khordan!

Tom

مهم نیست. من چای را بدون شیر دوست دارم.

mohem nist. man chāy rā bedun-e shir dust dāram.

It doesn't matter. I like tea without milk.

Amir

تو برادر یا خواهر داری؟

to barādar yā khāhar dāri?

Do you have a brother or a sister?

Tom

یک خواهر دارم. اسمش امیلی است. چند تا عکس از او دارم. می‌خواهی ببینی؟

yek khāhar dāram. esm-ash Emili ast. chand tā aks az u dāram. mi-khāhi bebini?

I have one sister. Her name is Emily. I have a few photos of her. Do you want to see?

Amir

بله، حتماً!

bale, hatman!

Yes, of course!

The most useful verb after 'to be'

داشتن dāshtan to have is the second verb every learner needs. Iranians use it for far more than owning things: you have a sister, have time, have a cold, have twenty years (that is how you say your age – remember lesson 4), and you have love for something: دوست داشتن dust dāshtan to like, to love is literally to have (as) a friend.

It is also one of the few verbs whose present tense has no mi- in front. Compare می‌روم mi-ravam I go with دارم dāram I have. The endings are the same ones you already know from khub-am, khub-i, khub-ast…

To say you do not have something, put نَـ na- in front: ندارم na-dāram I don't have. The stress jumps onto na-: NA-dāram.

dāshtan – all six forms

The stem is dār-; add the personal endings. There is no mi-. For the negative add na- to the front.

I haveReadingI don't haveReading
دارم dāramdāramندارم na-dāramna-dāram
داری dāridāriنداری na-dārina-dāri
دارد dāraddāradندارد na-dāradna-dārad
داریم dārimdārimنداریم na-dārimna-dārim
دارید dāriddāridندارید na-dāridna-dārid
دارند dāranddārandندارند na-dārandna-dārand

ما یک ماشین داریم.

mā yek māshin dārim.

We have a car.

او دوچرخه ندارد.

u do-charkhe na-dārad.

He / she doesn't have a bicycle.

Spoken او دوچرخه نداره. u do-charkhe na-dāre.

شما کلید دارید؟

shomā kelid dārid?

Do you have a key?

آن‌ها بچه ندارند.

ān-hā bacche na-dārand.

They don't have children.

Counting things: yek, chand tā and the singular

After a number the noun stays singular: دو گربه do gorbe two cats, never do gorbe-hā. In everyday speech Iranians slip the little counting word تا tā between the number and the noun: دو تا گربه do tā gorbe. It is optional, but it makes you sound natural.

To ask how many? use چند تا chand tā (or just چند chand before a noun). The same words mean a few in a statement: چند تا عکس دارم chand tā aks dāram I have a few photos.

For one / a, use یک yek before the noun, or the little ending ـی -i you met in lesson 3: یک خواهر دارم yek khāhar dāram = خواهری دارم khāhar-i dāram. When the thing is not counted at all – milk, money, time – the bare noun is enough: پول ندارم pul na-dāram I have no money.

چند تا برادر داری؟

chand tā barādar dāri?

How many brothers do you have?

سه تا برادر دارم.

se tā barādar dāram.

I have three brothers.

چند تا کتاب فارسی داری؟

chand tā ketāb-e fārsi dāri?

How many Persian books do you have?

فقط یکی دارم.

faghat yeki dāram.

I have only one.

yeki = 'one (of them)' when the noun is not repeated.

Saying what you need

لازم داشتن lāzem dāshtan to need works exactly like dāshtan; the word لازم lāzem necessary sits right before the verb: من چتر لازم دارم man chatr lāzem dāram I need an umbrella. The thing you need comes before lāzem.

A second way, very common in speech, is impersonal: چتر لازم است chatr lāzem ast an umbrella is necessary – no subject at all.

امروز هوا بد است. چتر لازم داریم.

emruz havā bad ast. chatr lāzem dārim.

The weather is bad today. We need an umbrella.

برای این کتاب عینک لازم دارم.

barā-ye in ketāb eynak lāzem dāram.

I need glasses for this book.

چه لازم داری؟

che lāzem dāri?

What do you need?

Spoken چی لازم داری؟ chi lāzem dāri?

هیچ چیز لازم ندارم، ممنون.

hich chiz lāzem na-dāram, mamnun.

I don't need anything, thanks.

hich chiz 'nothing' goes with a negative verb – two negatives are normal in Persian.

Mina's bag

Mina is leaving for university and checks her bag. Read, then answer the questions.

مینا هر روز یک کیف بزرگ دارد.

Minā har ruz yek kif-e bozorg dārad.

Mina has a big bag every day.

در کیفش سه تا کتاب و دو تا دفتر دارد.

dar kif-ash se tā ketāb o do tā daftar dārad.

In her bag she has three books and two notebooks.

یک موبایل و یک عینک هم دارد.

yek mobāyl o yek eynak ham dārad.

She also has a mobile phone and a pair of glasses.

اما امروز کلید ندارد!

ammā emruz kelid na-dārad!

But today she doesn't have her key!

کلید روی میز آشپزخانه است.

kelid ru-ye miz-e āshpaz-khāne ast.

The key is on the kitchen table.

مینا وقت ندارد. مادرش کلید را به او می‌دهد.

Minā vaght na-dārad. mādar-ash kelid rā be u mi-dahad.

Mina has no time. Her mother gives her the key.

«ممنون، مامان! خدا حافظ!»

«mamnun, māmān! khodā hāfez!»

"Thanks, Mum! Goodbye!"

Cats, tea and sugar cubes

The long-haired Persian cat – گربه‌ی ایرانی gorbe-ye irāni – is one of the world's oldest breeds, and street cats are everywhere in Iranian cities; many families leave food out for them. Dogs are less common as pets in cities, so sag dāri? is a rarer question than gorbe dāri?

Offering tea is the first thing a host does, and the answer to chāy mi-khori? is almost always yes. Tea comes in small glasses with a bowl of قند ghand sugar cubes: you put the cube in your mouth and sip the tea through it, rather than stirring sugar in. Milk in tea is a foreign habit – which is why Amir's house had none.

Tips

  • The stress in a negative verb moves to the front: NA-dāram, NA-dāri. Hearing where the stress falls tells you at once whether the answer is yes or no.
  • shir means both 'milk' and 'lion' – and a tap. Context does all the work: shir dāram in a kitchen is about milk.
  • Persians count with the singular: panj ketāb 'five books', dah nafar 'ten people'. The plural -hā is only for uncounted groups: ketāb-hā 'the books'.
  • dust dāram is 'I like' and 'I love' at once. For a person it means love; for tea it means like. Lesson 10 uses it for food.
