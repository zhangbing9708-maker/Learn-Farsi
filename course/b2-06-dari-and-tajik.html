فارسی، دری، تاجیکیDari and Tajik: one language, three standards

fārsi, dari, tājiki

Persian is an official language in three countries: Iran, Afghanistan (where it is called Dari) and Tajikistan (where it is called Tajik and written in Cyrillic). People from Tehran, Kabul and Dushanbe understand each other – but they do not sound the same, and they do not always reach for the same word. In this lesson you learn what the three standards share, the two old vowels ē and ō that Kabul and Dushanbe still pronounce, 25 everyday words that differ, and the Tajik Cyrillic alphabet, so that you can read Hafez in Dushanbe and order lunch in Kabul.

In this lesson you will learn to

  • explain how one language came to have three names and three standards
  • hear and name the old vowels ē and ō: شیر is shēr 'lion' but shīr 'milk' in Kabul
  • recognise 25 everyday words that differ between Tehran, Kabul and Dushanbe
  • read Tajik Cyrillic and turn it back into Persian script

One language, three countries

An article from a student magazine. It is written in standard written Persian, which is almost the same in Tehran and Kabul. Watch the place names: they map the history of all three countries.

زبان فارسی امروز زبان رسمی سه کشور است: ایران، افغانستان و تاجیکستان.

zabān-e fārsi emruz zabān-e rasmi-ye se keshvar ast: Irān, Afghānestān o Tājikestān.

Today Persian is an official language in three countries: Iran, Afghanistan and Tajikistan.

در ایران به آن «فارسی»، در افغانستان «دری» و در تاجیکستان «تاجیکی» می‌گویند.

dar Irān be ān «fārsi», dar Afghānestān «dari» o dar Tājikestān «tājiki» mi-guyand.

In Iran it is called Farsi, in Afghanistan Dari, and in Tajikistan Tajik.

این‌ها سه زبان جدا نیستند، بلکه سه گونه‌ی یک زبان‌اند.

in-hā se zabān-e jodā nistand, balke se gune-ye yek zabān-and.

These are not three separate languages but three varieties of one language.

فارسی نو بیش از هزار سال پیش در خراسان بزرگ و فرارود شکل گرفت.

fārsi-ye now bish az hezār sāl pish dar Khorāsān-e bozorg o Farārud shekl gereft.

New Persian took shape more than a thousand years ago in Greater Khorasan and Transoxiana.

این سرزمین‌ها امروز میان چند کشور تقسیم شده‌اند.

in sarzamin-hā emruz miyān-e chand keshvar taghsim shode-and.

Today these lands are divided among several countries.

رودکی، پدر شعر فارسی، در دربار سامانیان در بخارا شعر می‌گفت.

Rudaki, pedar-e she'r-e fārsi, dar darbār-e Sāmāniyān dar Bokhārā she'r mi-goft.

Rudaki, the father of Persian poetry, recited his poems at the Samanid court in Bukhara.

بخارا امروز در ازبکستان است، اما هنوز بسیاری از مردم آن به تاجیکی حرف می‌زنند.

Bokhārā emruz dar Ozbakestān ast, ammā hanuz besyār-i az mardom-e ān be tājiki harf mi-zanand.

Bukhara is in Uzbekistan today, but many of its people still speak Tajik.

نام «دری» را معمولاً از «دربار» می‌دانند؛ یعنی زبان دربار.

nām-e «dari» rā ma'mulan az «darbār» mi-dānand; ya'ni zabān-e darbār.

The name Dari is usually traced back to darbār, 'the royal court' – the language of the court.

تاجیکی تا حدود صد سال پیش با همین خط فارسی نوشته می‌شد.

tājiki tā hodud-e sad sāl pish bā hamin khatt-e fārsi neveshte mi-shod.

Until about a hundred years ago, Tajik was written in this same Persian script.

در دوره‌ی شوروی خط آن اول به لاتین و بعد، در سال ۱۹۴۰، به سیریلیک تغییر کرد.

dar dowre-ye showravi khatt-e ān avval be lātin o ba'd, dar sāl-e hezār-o-nohsad-o-chehel, be sirilik taghyir kard.

In the Soviet period its script was changed, first to Latin and then, in 1940, to Cyrillic.

به همین دلیل بیشتر تاجیک‌ها حافظ را به خط سیریلیک می‌خوانند.

be hamin dalil bishtar-e tājik-hā Hāfez rā be khatt-e sirilik mi-khānand.

That is why most Tajiks read Hafez in Cyrillic.

فارسی‌زبانان این سه کشور حرف یکدیگر را می‌فهمند، به‌ویژه وقتی آرام و رسمی صحبت می‌کنند.

fārsi-zabānān-e in se keshvar harf-e yekdigar rā mi-fahmand, be-vizhe vaghti ārām o rasmi sohbat mi-konand.

Persian speakers from the three countries understand each other, especially when they speak slowly and formally.

تفاوت‌ها بیشتر در تلفظ، در بعضی واژه‌های روزمره و در واژه‌های قرضی است.

tafāvot-hā bishtar dar talaffoz, dar ba'zi vāzhe-hā-ye ruzmarre o dar vāzhe-hā-ye gharzi ast.

The differences lie mostly in pronunciation, in some everyday words and in loanwords.

فارسی ایران واژه‌های زیادی از فرانسوی گرفته است، دری از انگلیسی و پشتو و اردو، و تاجیکی از روسی و ازبکی.

fārsi-ye Irān vāzhe-hā-ye ziyād-i az farānsavi gerefte ast, dari az engelisi o pashtu o ordu, o tājiki az rusi o ozbaki.

Iranian Persian has taken many words from French, Dari from English, Pashto and Urdu, and Tajik from Russian and Uzbek.

با این همه، شعر رودکی و فردوسی و مولوی و حافظ میراث مشترک هر سه کشور است.

bā in hame, she'r-e Rudaki o Ferdowsi o Mowlavi o Hāfez mirās-e moshtarak-e har se keshvar ast.

Even so, the poetry of Rudaki, Ferdowsi, Rumi and Hafez is the shared heritage of all three countries.

مولوی را «بلخی» هم می‌نامند، چون خانواده‌اش از بلخ بود؛ بلخ امروز در افغانستان است.

Mowlavi rā «Balkhi» ham mi-nāmand, chon khānevāde-ash az Balkh bud; Balkh emruz dar Afghānestān ast.

Rumi is also called Balkhi, because his family came from Balkh; Balkh is in Afghanistan today.

Three standards, one grammar

Think of English in Britain, America and Australia: one language, three standards, and nobody needs an interpreter. Persian is the same. The grammar is shared – the verb at the end, the ezafe, the present tense with می, the endings -am -i -ad, the object marker را – and written Dari is so close to written Iranian Persian that you can already read a newspaper from Kabul. What changes is the sound, a handful of everyday words, and the loanwords each country picked up from its neighbours and rulers.

The three names are not neutral. In Afghanistan the language has officially been called Dari since the 1960s, though many Afghans still simply say fārsi. In Tajikistan it is Tajik, تاجیکی tājiki, and it looks the most different of the three because it is written in Cyrillic. Afghanistan has a second official language, Pashto – a different Iranian language – and Dari has borrowed from it.

Tajik also has a few grammatical habits of its own: it often leaves out the be- of the subjunctive, joins را to the word as -ro, and has a 'right now' tense with the verb istodan 'to stand' – рафта истодаам (rafta istodaam) 'I am going'.

How the transliteration works in this lesson

  • In sentences, the transliteration shows the standard Iranian reading, which is also what the recordings use.
  • In tables and notes, Kabul words are written the way Kabulis say them: ē and ō for the two old vowels, w for و, q for ق, and -a for a final ه (khāna, not khāne).
  • Tajik is written in Cyrillic, followed by a Latin transliteration in italics: хона khona 'house'.

The 'unknown' vowels ē and ō

Classical Persian – the language of Rudaki and Ferdowsi – had five long vowels: ā, ī, ū and two more, ē and ō. The old grammarians called ī and ū معروف ma'ruf known and ē and ō مجهول majhul unknown, so you will read of یای مجهول yā-ye majhul and واو مجهول vāv-e majhul. The Persian script never had separate letters for them: ی writes both ī and ē, و writes both ū and ō.

In Iran the unknown vowels merged with the known ones a few centuries ago: ē became i and ō became u. That is why Tehranis say shir for both 'lion' and 'milk'. Kabul (and most of Afghanistan) and Dushanbe kept them apart – and Tajik Cyrillic even writes the difference: шер 'lion', шир 'milk', рӯз 'day'.

Other sounds to listen for:

  • و is w in Kabul (waqt 'time'), v in Tehran; Tajik writes в.
  • ق and غ are two different sounds in Kabul and Dushanbe – a deep q and a soft gh – but one sound in Tehran. Tajik writes қ and ғ.
  • A final silent ه is -e in Tehran (khāne) but -a in Kabul (khāna) and Tajik (хона).
  • Long ā stays ā in Kabul but is a rounded o in Tajik: нон non 'bread'.
  • The diphthongs ey and ow of Tehran are ai and au in Kabul: khaili 'very', nau 'new'.
  • The present prefix می is mi- in Tehran, mē- in Kabul and me- in Dushanbe – the old ē again.

25 everyday words: Tehran, Kabul, Dushanbe

Most Persian vocabulary is shared, and educated speakers know the other countries' words from films, books and the news. But daily life – the hospital, the bus ticket, the tomatoes – has its own words in each capital. They come from three sources:

  • Loanwords from different neighbours. Iran borrowed from French (ماشین māshin, پلیس polis), Afghanistan from English and Urdu (تکت tekat 'ticket', کچالو kachālu 'potato', چوکی chawki 'chair') and from Pashto (پوهنتون pohantun 'university'), Tajikistan from Russian (помидор, картошка, милитсия).
  • Old Persian words that took different jobs. مکتب maktab is 'school' in Kabul and Dushanbe, but in Iran it is the old Quran school of the past. دریا daryā is 'sea' in Iran but 'river' in Afghanistan and Tajikistan (дарё).
  • New words coined at different times. Iran's academy coined دانشگاه dāneshgāh in the 1930s; Tajik took the same word (донишгоҳ), while Kabul uses the Pashto پوهنتون pohantun.

Many Tajik loanwords come from Russian.

помидор 'tomato', картошка 'potato', мошин 'car' (Russian машина).

Reading Tajik: the Cyrillic alphabet

Tajik Cyrillic has 35 letters: the Russian alphabet minus a few letters, plus six with marks for Persian sounds – ғ (غ), ӣ, қ (ق), ӯ, ҳ (ه ح) and ҷ (ج). Because it writes every vowel, it actually shows more than Persian script does. Four correspondences unlock most words:

  1. Persian ā is Tajik о: нон = نان nān, об = آب āb, Тоҷикистон = تاجیکستان Tājikestān.
  2. Persian short e is и, and short o is у: дил = دل del, гул = گل gol.
  3. The old ē is е and the old ō is ӯ: шер, рӯз.
  4. The ezafe is written as -и joined to the word: забони тоҷикӣ = زبان تاجیکی zabān-e tājiki. A stressed final -i is ӣ: тоҷикӣ, форсӣ. And را is joined too, as -ро: китобро = کتاب را ketāb rā.

What Cyrillic cannot show is which Arabic letter a word had: س ص ث are all с, ز ذ ض ظ are all з, ت ط are both т. To write Tajik back in Persian script, you need to know the Persian spelling.

man dāneshju hastam.

I am a university student.

Tajik: Ман донишҷӯ ҳастам (man donishjū hastam). In Kabul: man mohassel hastam (محصل).

نمی‌دانم.

nemi-dānam.

I don't know.

Spoken نمی‌دونم. nemi-dunam.

Tajik: Намедонам (namedonam). In Kabul you will often hear namēfāmam (نمی‌فامم), from فهمیدن 'to understand'.

Spoken کجا می‌ری؟ kojā mi-ri?

fardā be Dushanbe mi-ravim.

Tomorrow we are going to Dushanbe.

Spoken فردا می‌ریم دوشنبه. fardā mi-rim Dushanbe.

Tajik: Пагоҳ ба Душанбе меравем (pagoh ba Dushanbe meravem) – Tajik 'we' ends in -ем where Tehran has -im.

Tajik: Забони модарии ман тоҷикӣ аст (zaboni modarii man tojikī ast) – the ezafe -и is written even after another и: модарии.

Tajiks read Hafez's Divan in Cyrillic.

Tajik: Тоҷикон девони Ҳофизро бо хати кириллӣ мехонанд. Look at девон (the old ē of دیوان), the ezafe -и, the plural -он (= -ān) and -ро for را.

Lunch at the university

Tom has lunch in the canteen of the University of Tehran with Nilufar, a master's student from Kabul. Her Persian is the same as his teachers' – almost.

Tom

سلام نیلوفر. این‌جا جای کسی است؟

salām Nilufar. in-jā jā-ye kas-i ast?

Hi Nilufar. Is this seat taken?

Spoken سلام نیلوفر. این‌جا جای کسیه؟ salām Nilufar. in-jā jā-ye kas-iye?

Nilufar

نه، بفرمایید. نان خوردید؟

na, befarmāyid. nān khordid?

No, please sit down. Have you eaten?

In Afghanistan نان خوردن, literally 'to eat bread', means to have a meal.

Tom

نان؟ نه، من برنج و خورش گرفتم!

nān? na, man berenj o khoresh gereftam!

Bread? No, I got rice and stew!

Spoken نون؟ نه، من برنج و خورش گرفتم! nun? na, man berenj o khoresh gereftam!

Nilufar

ببخشید! در کابل «نان خوردن» یعنی غذا خوردن.

bebakhshid! dar Kābol «nān khordan» ya'ni ghazā khordan.

Sorry! In Kabul 'eating bread' just means having a meal.

Tom

جالب است. هفته‌ی پیش هم گفتید «موتر» و من نفهمیدم.

jāleb ast. hafte-ye pish ham goftid «motar» o man na-fahmidam.

Interesting. Last week you said 'motar' too, and I didn't understand.

Spoken جالبه. هفته‌ی پیش هم گفتین «موتر» و من نفهمیدم. jālebe. hafte-ye pish ham goftin «motar» o man na-fahmidam.

Nilufar

موتر یعنی ماشین. به بیمارستان هم «شفاخانه» می‌گوییم.

motar ya'ni māshin. be bimārestān ham «shafākhāne» mi-guyim.

Motar means car. And we call a hospital a shafākhāna.

Tom

ایرانی‌ها حرف شما را راحت می‌فهمند؟

irāni-hā harf-e shomā rā rāhat mi-fahmand?

Do Iranians understand you easily?

Spoken ایرانی‌ها حرفتون رو راحت می‌فهمن؟ irāni-hā harf-etun ro rāhat mi-fahman?

Nilufar

بیشترشان بله. ما هم با فیلم‌ها و آهنگ‌های ایرانی بزرگ شده‌ایم.

bishtar-eshān bale. mā ham bā film-hā o āhang-hā-ye irāni bozorg shode-im.

Most of them, yes. And we grew up with Iranian films and songs.

Tom

ولی لهجه‌تان فرق دارد. مثلاً «شیر» را جور دیگری می‌گویید.

vali lahje-tān fargh dārad. masalan «shir» rā jur-e digar-i mi-guyid.

But your accent is different. For example, you say 'shir' another way.

Spoken ولی لهجه‌تون فرق داره. مثلاً «شیر» رو یه جور دیگه می‌گین. vali lahje-tun fargh dāre. masalan «shir» ro ye jur-e dige mi-gin.

Nilufar

بله. شیری که می‌نوشیم با شیر جنگل فرق دارد.

bale. shir-i ke mi-nushim bā shir-e jangal fargh dārad.

Yes. The milk we drink is different from the lion in the jungle.

She says shīr for the milk and shēr for the lion – and rōz for 'day', not ruz.

Tom

مثل دوستم سهراب در دوشنبه! در تاجیکی این دو کلمه را با دو حرف مختلف می‌نویسند.

mesl-e dust-am Sohrāb dar Dushanbe! dar tājiki in do kalame rā bā do harf-e mokhtalef mi-nevisand.

Like my friend Sohrab in Dushanbe! In Tajik they write these two words with two different letters.

Spoken مثل دوستم سهراب تو دوشنبه! تو تاجیکی این دو تا کلمه رو با دو تا حرف مختلف می‌نویسن. mesl-e dust-am Sohrāb tu Dushanbe! tu tājiki in do tā kalame ro bā do tā harf-e mokhtalef mi-nevisan.

Tajik шер 'lion', шир 'milk'.

Tom

پس من کدام فارسی را یاد بگیرم؟

pas man kodām fārsi rā yād begiram?

So which Persian should I learn?

Spoken پس من کدوم فارسی رو یاد بگیرم؟ pas man kodum fārsi ro yād begiram?

Nilufar

هر کدام را دوست دارید! حافظ را همه با همان کلمه‌ها می‌خوانیم.

har kodām rā dust dārid! Hāfez rā hame bā hamān kalame-hā mi-khānim.

Whichever you like! We all read Hafez in the same words.

Tom

پس به قول شما: تشکر!

pas be ghowl-e shomā: tashakkor!

Then, as you would say: tashakkor!

Neighbours who share a poet

Iran is home to one of the largest Afghan communities in the world – millions of people, many of them born in Iran. Afghan students fill the libraries of Tehran and Mashhad, and Afghan poets and novelists who write in Persian are part of the literary life of both countries. Tajikistan, for its part, has made the poets and princes of the Samanid age its national heroes: a main avenue of Dushanbe is named after رودکی Rudaki, and the currency, the somoni, after Ismail Samani, the ruler of Bukhara at whose court New Persian poetry was born. Even the capital's name is Persian: دوشنبه Dushanbe means 'Monday' – the city grew out of a village with a Monday market.

One poet ties the three countries together especially neatly. Nasir Khusraw (1004 – about 1088) was born in Qobadian, in today's Tajikistan, travelled through Iran to Cairo and Mecca, and died in the mountains of Badakhshan, in today's Afghanistan. He was proud of his language:

من آنم که در پای خوگان نریزم / مر این قیمتی دُر لفظ دری را man ān-am ke dar pā-ye khugān na-rizam / mar in gheymati dorr-e lafz-e dari rā I am not one to scatter at the feet of swine this precious pearl, the Dari word

خوگان khugān is an old spelling of خوکان khukān swine, and the poet plays on دُر dorr pearl and دری dari. For him dari simply meant fine Persian – whether it was spoken in Balkh, Nishapur or Isfahan.

Tips

  • Speak your Iranian Persian with confidence in Kabul or Dushanbe: Iranian films, songs and TV are followed there and you will be understood. The hard part is understanding them – listen for ē and ō, w for v, a deep q, and -a at the end of words where Tehran has -e.
  • False friends: دریا daryā is 'sea' in Iran but 'river' in Kabul and Dushanbe; بچه bacche is 'child' in Tehran but 'boy' in Kabul; مکتب maktab is 'school' in Kabul and Dushanbe but the old Quran school in Iran; مقبول maghbul means 'accepted' in Iran but 'beautiful' in Kabul.
  • To read Tajik, turn о into ا, и into a kasra, у into a damma and a final -и into the ezafe – then read it as Persian: хонаи ман = خانه‌ی من khāne-ye man my house.
  • Tajik grammar has a few habits of its own: it often drops the be- of the subjunctive (бояд равам 'I must go', Iran باید بروم), joins را to the word (китобро), and has a 'right now' tense with истодан: рафта истодаам 'I am going'.
  • Never 'correct' an Afghan's or a Tajik's Persian into Tehrani. Kabul and Dushanbe have their own standards – every bit as correct as Tehran's – and people are proud of them.
  • Afghanistan's other official language is Pashto, a different Iranian language. Words such as پوهنتون pohantun university come from it, and many Afghans speak both.
