دیروز چه کار کردی؟Yesterday: the simple past

diruz che kār kardi?

Good news: the Persian past tense is the most regular thing in the language. Every verb has a past stem, the past stem is simply the infinitive without -an, and the endings are ones you already know. In this lesson you learn to say what you did yesterday, where you were and what you didn't do – and to ask the question every Iranian asks after a weekend: خوش گذشت؟

In this lesson you will learn to

  • say what you did with the simple past: رفتم، خوردم، دیدم
  • say where you were with the past of بودن: بودم
  • say what you didn't do: نرفتم، نبودم
  • use past time words: دیروز، پریروز، دیشب، هفته‌ی پیش، دو روز پیش

What did you do on Friday?

Saturday morning, the first day of the Iranian working week. Sara meets Amir at the office.

Sara

سلام امیر! دیروز کجا بودی؟

salām Amir! diruz kojā budi?

Hi Amir! Where were you yesterday?

Amir

سلام! دیروز جمعه بود. با دوستانم به دربند رفتم.

salām! diruz jom'e bud. bā dustān-am be Darband raftam.

Hi! Yesterday was Friday. I went to Darband with my friends.

Spoken سلام! دیروز جمعه بود. با دوستام رفتم دربند. salām! diruz jom'e bud. bā dust-ām raftam Darband.

Sara

چه خوب! کوه رفتید؟

che khub! kuh raftid?

How nice! Did you go up the mountain?

Spoken چه خوب! کوه رفتین؟ che khub! kuh raftin?

Amir

بله، صبح زود رفتیم و تا ظهر راه رفتیم.

bale, sobh-e zud raftim o tā zohr rāh raftim.

Yes, we went early in the morning and walked until noon.

Spoken آره، صبح زود رفتیم و تا ظهر راه رفتیم. āre, sobh-e zud raftim o tā zohr rāh raftim.

Sara

ناهار چه خوردید؟

nāhār che khordid?

What did you have for lunch?

Spoken ناهار چی خوردین؟ nāhār chi khordin?

Amir

در یک رستوران کوچک کباب و دوغ خوردیم. خیلی خوشمزه بود!

dar yek resturān-e kuchak kabāb o dugh khordim. kheyli khoshmaze bud!

We had kebab and doogh in a small restaurant. It was delicious!

Spoken تو یه رستوران کوچیک کباب و دوغ خوردیم. خیلی خوشمزه بود! tu ye resturān-e kuchik kabāb o dugh khordim. kheyli khoshmaze bud!

Amir

تو چه کار کردی؟

to che kār kardi?

What did you do?

Spoken تو چی کار کردی؟ to chi kār kardi?

Sara

من هیچ جا نرفتم. خانه بودم و درس خواندم.

man hich jā na-raftam. khāne budam o dars khāndam.

I didn't go anywhere. I was at home and studied.

Spoken من هیچ جا نرفتم. خونه بودم و درس خوندم. man hich jā na-raftam. khune budam o dars khundam.

Amir

پریروز هم درس خواندی!

pariruz ham dars khāndi!

You studied the day before yesterday too!

Spoken پریروز هم درس خوندی! pariruz ham dars khundi!

Sara

بله، امتحان دارم. اما شب با خانواده‌ام شام خوردیم و فیلم دیدیم.

bale, emtehān dāram. ammā shab bā khānevāde-am shām khordim o film didim.

Yes, I have an exam. But in the evening I had dinner with my family and we watched a film.

Spoken آره، امتحان دارم. ولی شب با خانواده‌م شام خوردیم و فیلم دیدیم. āre, emtehān dāram. vali shab bā khānevāde-m shām khordim o film didim.

Amir

خوش گذشت؟

khosh gozasht?

Did you have a good time?

Sara

بد نبود. اما دفعه‌ی بعد من هم با شما به کوه می‌آیم!

bad na-bud. ammā daf'e-ye ba'd man ham bā shomā be kuh mi-āyam!

It wasn't bad. But next time I'm coming to the mountains with you!

Spoken بد نبود. ولی دفعه‌ی بعد منم باهاتون میام کوه! bad na-bud. vali daf'e-ye ba'd man-am bā-hātun miyām kuh!

The easiest tense in Persian

Every Persian verb has two stems. The present stem (rav- in می‌روم mi-ravam) can be irregular, but the past stem never is: it is the infinitive without -an. رفتن raftan to go → رفت raft, خوردن khordan to eat → خورد khord, دیدن didan to see → دید did.

To make the simple past, add the person endings you know from to be in lesson 1 – -am, -i, -im, -id, -and – with one exception: he / she has no ending at all. رفتم raftam I went, رفتی rafti you went, رفت raft he / she went. There is no mi- and no be-.

Say it with the stress on the stem, not on the ending: RAF-tam, KHOR-dim, ā-MA-dand. And to say what you did not do, put na- in front – it takes the stress: NA-raftam, نرفتم na-raftam I didn't go.

The simple past: رفتم، خوردم، دیدم

Past stem + ending. That is all, for every verb in the language:

  • آمدن āmadan → آمدم āmadam I came
  • کردن kardan → کردم kardam I did, so کار کردم kār kardam I worked
  • گفتن goftan → گفتم goftam I said
  • خریدن kharidan → خریدم kharidam I bought
  • شدن shodan → شدم shodam I became

The subject pronoun is usually dropped, because the ending already says who did it: رفتیم raftim can only mean we went. In speech -id becomes -in and -and becomes -an: raftin, raftan.

I (man)رفتم raftamخوردم khordamدیدم didamI went / ate / saw
you (to)رفتی raftiخوردی khordiدیدی didiyou went / ate / saw
he / she (u)رفت raftخورد khordدید didhe / she went / ate / saw
we (mā)رفتیم raftimخوردیم khordimدیدیم didimwe went / ate / saw
you (shomā)رفتید raftidخوردید khordidدیدید dididyou went / ate / saw
they (ān-hā)رفتند raftandخوردند khordandدیدند didandthey went / ate / saw

دیروز به بازار رفتم.

diruz be bāzār raftam.

Yesterday I went to the bazaar.

Spoken دیروز رفتم بازار. diruz raftam bāzār.

in speech the place often comes after the verb, without be

دیشب پیتزا خوردیم.

dishab pitzā khordim.

Last night we had pizza.

پارسال به اصفهان رفتند.

pārsāl be Esfahān raftand.

Last year they went to Isfahan.

Spoken پارسال رفتن اصفهان. pārsāl raftan Esfahān.

تام هفته‌ی پیش یک فرش خرید.

Tām hafte-ye pish yek farsh kharid.

Tom bought a carpet last week.

شما کی آمدید؟

shomā key āmadid?

When did you come?

Spoken شما کی اومدین؟ shomā key umadin?

I was, I wasn't, I didn't: بودم، نبودم، نرفتم

The past of بودن budan to be follows the same pattern: بودم budam I was, بودی budi you were, بود bud he / she / it was. It is used for where you were and what something was like: خسته بودم khaste budam I was tired, هوا سرد بود havā sard bud it was cold.

For the negative, join na- to the front of any past verb: نبودم na-budam I wasn't, نخوردم na-khordam I didn't eat. In a compound verb na- goes on the verb part: کار نکردم kār na-kardam I didn't work. Before the long ā of آمدن āmadan, na- becomes nay-: نیامدم na-yāmadam I didn't come.

دیروز خیلی خسته بودم.

diruz kheyli khaste budam.

Yesterday I was very tired.

هوا سرد بود و ما بیرون نرفتیم.

havā sard bud o mā birun na-raftim.

It was cold and we didn't go out.

دیشب شام نخوردم.

dishab shām na-khordam.

I didn't have dinner last night.

سارا دیروز سر کار نبود.

Sārā diruz sar-e kār na-bud.

Sara wasn't at work yesterday.

رضا به مهمانی نیامد.

Rezā be mehmāni na-yāmad.

Reza didn't come to the party.

Spoken رضا نیومد مهمونی. Rezā nayumad mehmuni.

When? Words for the past

Time words usually come first in the sentence, or right after the subject. پیش pish ago comes after the amount of time, the opposite of English: دو روز پیش do ruz pish two days ago, سه سال پیش se sāl pish three years ago.

For last week, last month use پیش pish with an ezafe: هفته‌ی پیش hafte-ye pish, ماه پیش māh-e pish. Last year has its own word, پارسال pārsāl. To ask when?, use کی؟ key?

کی به تهران آمدی؟ سه ماه پیش.

key be Tehrān āmadi? se māh pish.

When did you come to Tehran? Three months ago.

Spoken کی اومدی تهران؟ سه ماه پیش. key umadi Tehrān? se māh pish.

دیروز صبح نان سنگک خریدم.

diruz sobh nān-e sangak kharidam.

Yesterday morning I bought sangak bread.

پریروز تولد مادرم بود.

pariruz tavallod-e mādar-am bud.

The day before yesterday was my mother's birthday.

او یک ساعت پیش به من زنگ زد.

u yek sā'at pish be man zang zad.

He rang me an hour ago.

A day in Isfahan

Leila and Dariush took a trip last week. Every verb in the story is in the simple past.

هفته‌ی پیش لیلا و داریوش به اصفهان رفتند.

hafte-ye pish Leylā o Dāriush be Esfahān raftand.

Last week Leila and Dariush went to Isfahan.

آن‌ها با قطار رفتند و ساعت نه صبح رسیدند.

ān-hā bā ghatār raftand o sā'at-e noh-e sobh residand.

They went by train and arrived at nine in the morning.

اول به میدان نقش جهان رفتند.

avval be meydān-e Naghsh-e Jahān raftand.

First they went to Naqsh-e Jahan Square.

ظهر در یک رستوران سنتی بریانی خوردند.

zohr dar yek resturān-e sonnati beryāni khordand.

At noon they ate beryani, Isfahan's famous lamb dish, in a traditional restaurant.

عصر روی سی‌وسه‌پل راه رفتند.

asr ru-ye si-o-se-pol rāh raftand.

In the late afternoon they walked on Si-o-se Pol, the bridge of thirty-three arches.

شب خسته بودند، اما خیلی خوش گذشت.

shab khaste budand, ammā kheyli khosh gozasht.

In the evening they were tired, but they had a great time.

لیلا گفت: «اصفهان نصف جهان است!»

Leylā goft: «Esfahān nesf-e jahān ast!»

Leila said: "Isfahan is half the world!"

Friday: the Iranian weekend

In Iran the week starts on شنبه shanbe Saturday and the day off is جمعه jom'e Friday; for many people Thursday afternoon is free too. So the Monday-morning question of other countries is asked on Saturday: جمعه چه کار کردی؟ jom'e che kār kardi? What did you do on Friday?

A classic Tehran Friday starts early in the mountains. At dawn thousands of people walk up the valley of دربند Darband or the slopes of Tochal, stop for tea, dates and a breakfast of عدسی adasi lentil soup at a café by the stream, and are back down before the sun gets hot. Lunch is a long family meal at the grandparents' – often آبگوشت ābgusht – and the evening is for visits and television.

If you missed a good outing, your friends will tell you جای شما خالی بود! jā-ye shomā khāli bud! your place was empty – we missed you. It is the polite reply to khosh gozasht? as well.

Tips

  • The past stem is the infinitive minus -an – always. Learn every new verb by its infinitive and you get its past for free.
  • Stress the stem, not the ending: RAF-tam, KHOR-dim, ā-MA-dand – the endings are unstressed. With na- the stress moves to the front: NA-raftam. (At A2 you will see why this matters: raf-TAM, stressed at the end, is spoken Persian for 'I have gone'.)
  • na- is written joined to the verb: نرفتم، نخوردم، نبودم. Before آ it becomes nay-: نیامدم na-yāmadam, spoken nayumadam.
  • In speech -id becomes -in and -and becomes -an: raftin, raftan. Some stems change too: āmad- → umad-, khānd- → khund-.
  • After a trip or a party everyone asks khosh gozasht? The happy answer is kheyli khosh gozasht! – and if your friend wasn't there, add jā-ye shomā khāli!
